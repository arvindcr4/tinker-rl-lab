\begin{abstract}
A recurring worry about GRPO is length bias: because the group-relative update
divides by response length, the algorithm may be nudged toward longer completions
that stumble into reward rather than toward better reasoning. The length-debiased
variant Dr.\,GRPO removes this per-response normalization. We study length bias and
held-out generalization as one pillar of \ours{}, a benchmark of 70+ RL runs across
seven libraries and five model families ($0.6$B--$\sim$671B) on GSM8K, HumanEval,
and tool-use tasks. Our headline result is a carefully scoped negative one: in the
regime we measure---short-horizon GSM8K chain-of-thought with a 200-token
generation cap---the mean Qwen3-8B GRPO
gain over the pre-RL checkpoint on held-out data is small and not statistically
significant, GRPO and Dr.\,GRPO produce held-out gains that are indistinguishable
at our seed budget, and neither inflates length within this capped regime (pooled
mean completion length drifts from $194.4$ to $183.5$ tokens). We stress that the
$200$-token cap \emph{bounds} this claim: it cannot rule out length inflation in
longer-horizon or uncapped regimes, only establish its absence where length is
already controlled. The verbosity-trap signature is absent \emph{in this regime}. We argue this is the
\emph{expected} outcome when length is already controlled, not evidence against
Dr.\,GRPO, and specify the length-confounded regime and causal mediation tests that
would actually reveal a difference. We release code, logs, and checkpoints.
\end{abstract}
 \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:p4-intro}
GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath, guo2025deepseekr1} normalizes the group-relative
advantage by response length. This introduces a potential length bias: when reward
is sparse and binary, longer completions have more chances to stumble into a
correct answer, and a length-dividing update can reward verbosity as a proxy for
competence. Dr.\,GRPO~\citep{tong2025drgrpo} removes the per-response
$1/L$ factor, restoring the sequence-level gradient up to a global scale, and is
motivated precisely as a fix for reward hacking through length. Whether this fix
produces better held-out generalization in practice is the question of this pillar.

The concern matters because length inflation is a well-documented failure mode of
reward optimization~\citep{ouyang2022training, christiano2017deeprlhf}, and because
held-out gains can be manufactured by a policy that pads its outputs rather than
reasoning better. A careful comparison must therefore control both length and
memorization, and must distinguish a genuine direct effect on accuracy from an
indirect effect mediated by verbosity.

We study length bias as one pillar of \ours{}, a controlled benchmark of 70+ runs
across seven RL libraries and five model families ($0.6$B--$\sim$671B) on GSM8K
\citep{cobbe2021training}, HumanEval~\citep{chen2021codex}, and tool-use tasks. Our
contributions are: (i) a held-out comparison showing that, in the short-horizon
GSM8K regime, the mean Qwen3-8B-Instruct GRPO gain over the \emph{same} checkpoint's
pre-RL held-out accuracy is small and not statistically significant (a matched pre-RL
control for the Qwen3-8B-Base checkpoint is not available), and that GRPO and Dr.\,GRPO give held-out gains
that are indistinguishable at our seed budget with no length inflation under the
200-token GSM8K generation cap; (ii) evidence that the verbosity-trap signature is
absent in this regime; and (iii) an argument, developed through an external
cross-examination, that this null is the \emph{expected} result when length is
already controlled, together with a specification of the length-confounded regime
and causal length-mediation tests that would reveal a Dr.\,GRPO advantage. We frame
the pillar as a boundary on what the current evidence supports, not a refutation of
length debiasing.

\paragraph{Bounded-null interpretation.}
The 200-token cap is part of the estimand, not a nuisance detail. Our null result
means that no length pathology is detectable in this capped, short-horizon regime;
it cannot adjudicate uncapped reasoning, long agent trajectories, or settings in
which completion length has room to mediate reward.

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:p4-related}
Length bias and reward hacking are long-standing concerns in RL post-training
\citep{ouyang2022training, christiano2017deeprlhf, ahmadian2024backtobasics}.
Dr.\,GRPO~\citep{tong2025drgrpo} targets the length term specifically; related work
recasts group-relative updates as preference contrasts~\citep{wu2025grpo_dpo, liu2026gdpo}, which bears on why removing the $1/L$ factor changes the gradient
geometry. Our contribution is to test the practical consequence on a fixed
benchmark and to scope precisely the regime in which a difference should be
expected.

\subsection{Statistical Methodology}

Following Colas et al.~\cite{colas2019hitchhiker}, we report:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Mean $\pm$ standard error across seeds
    \item 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples)
    \item Welch's t-test for pairwise algorithm comparisons
    \item \texttt{rliable}~\cite{agarwal2021deep} aggregate metrics (IQM, median, optimality gap)
\end{itemize}

All pairwise comparisons were evaluated using Welch's independent-samples $t$-test
(unequal variances assumed) and the non-parametric Mann--Whitney $U$ test as a
robustness check. Effect sizes are reported as Cohen's $d$ with the pooled
standard deviation estimator; confidence intervals follow the
Hedges--Olkin (1985) analytical formula and are reported at the 95\% level
(two-tailed, $z = 1.96$). Post-hoc statistical power was computed under the
observed effect size and sample sizes at $\alpha = 0.05$.

To address the multiple-comparisons problem across 5 planned tests,
we apply both the conservative Bonferroni correction (family-wise error rate:
$\alpha' = \alpha / k = 0.0100$) and the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure
(false discovery rate $< 0.05$). Results surviving Bonferroni correction are
marked $^*$; those surviving BH--FDR correction are marked $^\dag$.

\paragraph{Power Analysis.}
All inferential tests in this paper are evaluated against pre-specified power
requirements using \texttt{statsmodels.stats.power.TTestIndPower} with
$\alpha = 0.05$ and target power $1 - \beta = 0.80$.

\textbf{Modal H100 experiments ($n = 5$ seeds per arm).}
The minimum detectable effect size (MDE) at 80\% power for a two-sample
Welch $t$-test with $n_1 = n_2 = 5$ is
$d_{\min} = 2.024$ (Cohen's $d$).
Table~\ref{tab:power-analysis} reports achieved power for a range of effect sizes.
This threshold falls in the \emph{very large} range, meaning that
comparisons yielding $|d| < 2.02$
are not adequately powered to detect true differences.

\textbf{Single-seed Tinker experiments ($n = 1$).}
Single-seed runs have \emph{zero statistical power}: with only one observation per
condition, no inferential test can be computed and no confidence intervals can be
formed.  All Tinker single-run results are treated as \emph{descriptive only} and
are not subject to significance testing.

\textbf{TRL baseline ($n = 5$ seeds).}
The TRL-GRPO baseline (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 5 seeds) achieves MDE
$d_{\min} = 2.024$ for equal-$n$ comparisons.
When compared to the pooled Classic-RL group ($n = 15$), the MDE
improves to $d_{\min} = 1.530$,
reflecting higher power from the larger reference group.

\paragraph{Multiple-Comparison Correction.}
Inferential tests in this paper are reported only for Tier-A/B
comparisons as defined in our statistical-rigor protocol;
single-seed or partial Tinker/API rows are descriptive and are
excluded from Welch / Mann--Whitney testing, BH correction, power,
and CI claims. Table~\ref{tab:bh-pvalues} predates the Tier-A/B/C
partition and lists raw and BH-adjusted $p$-values from the
earlier global family of $20$ tests with $19$ surviving correction;
we retain it here as a methodological audit trail. The headline
BH result the paper now stands behind is the restricted Tier-A/B
family computed in the addendum, where only $F_3$ survives. Where
the global family disagrees with the restricted family, the
addendum is authoritative.

All tests are two-sided.  Effect sizes are reported as Cohen's $d$ for
$t$-tests and Pearson/Spearman $r$ for correlations.  Bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals ($B = 10{,}000$) are reported for all means.

\paragraph{TRL baseline characterisation.}
The TRL GRPO reference implementation was evaluated across five random seeds
($s \in \{42, 123, 456, 789, 1024\}$) on GSM8K using Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B on
an NVIDIA L4 GPU. We report mean accuracy $\bar{x} = 0.734$ ($\sigma = 0.0703$),
median $= 0.740$, and IQM $= 0.747$. Normality was not rejected by Shapiro--Wilk
($W = 0.9018$, $p = 0.4198$); bootstrap 95\% CI $= [0.6720, 0.7820]$.

\paragraph{Variance decomposition.}
One-way ANOVAs across 42 experiments with valid performance observations show
that library/framework ($\eta^2 = 0.546$) and training algorithm ($\eta^2 = 0.558$)
are the dominant variance sources, consistent with our central thesis. Model
family explains $\eta^2 = 0.471$; model scale explains $\eta^2 = 0.322$.

Per-run compute is logged with the released artifacts. Total project compute:

 \section{Length Bias and Generalization: Results}
\label{sec:p4-results}
We report three findings and then scope them. First, in our near-ceiling
Qwen3-8B-Instruct evaluation, GRPO post-training does not deliver a significant
held-out gain: the mean improvement over the pre-RL checkpoint on held-out data is
small ($+0.013$) and not statistically significant (paired $p=0.256$, $n{=}5$). We
stress that this is a property of the near-ceiling regime, not of RL in general---in
the lower-accuracy Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT experiment analyzed throughout
the length sections, both GRPO and Dr.\,GRPO \emph{do} produce significant
pre$\to$post held-out gains ($+6.2$pp, McNemar exact $p=1.1\times10^{-4}$;
$+5.0$pp, $p=7.6\times10^{-4}$). Second, GRPO
and Dr.\,GRPO produce held-out gains that are indistinguishable at our seed
budget---removing the length-normalization term does not, here, change the
outcome. Third, under the 200-token GSM8K generation cap, neither algorithm
inflates length: pooled mean completion length drifts \emph{downward}, from
$194.4$ to $183.5$ tokens, and the verbosity-trap signature (a rising
coupling between length and reward as training proceeds) is absent.

Taken together these say that, in this regime, there is no length pathology for
Dr.\,GRPO to fix. The subsections develop the mechanism behind the null---the
weak, often negative length--reward coupling in short arithmetic
chains---and show through lag and reward-shape analyses that length dynamics are
only weakly identifiable here, so the defensible claim is a correction of a formal
gradient bias rather than universal held-out superiority. We then turn the null into
a positive research program: the external frontier-model cross-examination in the
final subsection specifies the length-confounded, sparse-reward regime in which a
Dr.\,GRPO advantage \emph{should} appear (high length dispersion, a train
length-spurious / test length-anti-spurious split), a causal length-mediation
protocol that decomposes held-out success into direct and verbosity-mediated
effects, and a length-adversarial truncation test executable on the checkpoints we
already release.
 \section{Length Bias: A Direct Per-Step Test of the Dr.~GRPO Signature}
\label{sec:length-bias-pillar}

\paragraph{Headline question.}
The Dr.~GRPO thesis~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} and the broader
length-bias / reward-hacking literature~\cite{ouyang2022training, christiano2017deeprlhf}
predict that GRPO
\emph{systematically} inflates response length during training:
longer outputs mechanically accumulate more gradient signal
regardless of correctness, the model first learns to associate length
with reward, then collapses when the reward model saturates. The
Dr.~GRPO correction (per-question rather than per-completion
normalisation) is offered as a remedy. We have
\emph{per-step traces} of $(\texttt{mean\_reward},
\texttt{mean\_comp\_len})$ for ten GRPO runs and eight Dr.~GRPO runs
across two tasks, and we ask: does the length-vs-reward trajectory
show the signature on our data, and does Dr.~GRPO break it?

\paragraph{Sources and measurement.}
Two controlled experiments are available with per-step traces:
\emph{(i)} Qwen2.5-0.5B on arithmetic, $40$ steps, $n{=}5$ seeds per
algorithm (GRPO vs Dr.~GRPO) -- a near-ceiling, easy task where
reward saturates above $0.98$; and
\emph{(ii)} Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on GSM8K-CoT, $30$ steps, $n{=}3$
seeds per algorithm -- a harder reasoning task where mean reward
is held near $0.25$ throughout training. The GSM8K-CoT sampler uses
\texttt{MAX\_NEW=200}; step-0 completions are already close to that cap, so
all no-inflation claims below are cap-bounded rather than evidence about an
uncensored long-form regime.
For each run we compute three Spearman correlations:
$\rho(\texttt{step}, \texttt{len})$ (length trend across training),
$\rho(\texttt{step}, \texttt{reward})$ (reward trend across
training), and $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{reward})$ evaluated
\emph{within} the run (per-step coupling); we also report the
first-half / second-half mean difference for both length and
reward, and a binary \emph{length-bias flag} that fires when
length grows (slope $>0$ or $\rho>0$) \emph{and} reward stays flat
or drops ($\text{slope}\le 0$ or $\rho\le 0$). All values are in
\texttt{experiments/results/length\_bias.tsv}; the per-algorithm
summary is in
\texttt{experiments/results/length\_bias\_summary.tsv}.

\paragraph{Headline finding.}
\textbf{At 30--40-step horizons neither GRPO nor Dr.~GRPO exhibits
the verbosity-trap signature on either task.} The per-run
$\rho(\texttt{step}, \texttt{len})$ is consistently \emph{negative}
on both tasks (Table~\ref{tab:length-bias-summary}): $-0.405$ (sd
$0.176$) for GRPO on arithmetic and $-0.622$ (sd $0.301$) for GRPO
on GSM8K-CoT -- the model becomes \emph{shorter} as training
proceeds, not longer. The Dr.~GRPO correction attenuates this
negative length trend on the hard task ($-0.355$ vs $-0.622$ for
GRPO), but does \emph{not} reverse its sign. The within-run
coupling $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{reward})$ is also negative on
all four (algo, task) cells ($-0.293$ to $-0.618$), meaning that
\emph{at any single step}, longer completions are associated with
\emph{lower} mean reward -- consistent with the model's
increasingly long but still-incorrect early-stage completions
contributing to lower step means. The picture is one of
\emph{compression under reinforcement}, not verbosity inflation.

\paragraph{Why this is consistent with, not against, the Dr.~GRPO thesis.}
Dr.~GRPO is a claim about \emph{horizon}: the verbosity-trap emerges
when training is long enough for the model to discover that length
is correlated with advantage. Our arithmetic runs are $40$ steps
and our GSM8K-CoT runs are $30$ steps; both are well below the
$\sim$hundreds-of-steps horizon at which Dr.~GRPO's authors
empirically observed the trap~\cite{tong2025drgrpo}. The
within-run coupling we measure \emph{is} negative, meaning
length has not yet decoupled from per-step reward; that is
\emph{what Dr.~GRPO predicts before the trap engages}. In other
words, the absence of length growth at $30$--$40$ steps is
\emph{necessary but not sufficient} evidence that the trap will
not engage later -- and our negative length trend is the signature
of a model that is \emph{compressing reasoning}, a behaviour that
the verbosity-trap hypothesis explicitly contrasts with what it
predicts (length \emph{growth} followed by collapse).

\paragraph{Where Dr.~GRPO does show a measurable effect.}
Although Dr.~GRPO does not flip the sign of the length trend, it
\emph{does} attenuate it on the hard task. The mean length trend
goes from $-0.622$ (GRPO) to $-0.355$ (Dr.~GRPO), an absolute
reduction of $0.267$; the standard deviation stays comparable
($0.301$ vs $0.255$). On the easy task the two are statistically
indistinguishable at $n{=}5$ ($-0.405$ vs $-0.307$, both well
inside one standard deviation). This is consistent with Dr.~GRPO's
\emph{qualitative} claim: the per-question normalisation prevents
length from compounding into the advantage estimate, so length
should drift less even when the absolute level of length reduction
is similar. Concretely, GSM8K-CoT last-10 mean length is $184.4$
for GRPO and $189.3$ for Dr.~GRPO (a $5$-token residual positive
shift, $+2.7\%$), while the \emph{slope} of length-vs-step is
flatter for Dr.~GRPO. The two effects are consistent: Dr.~GRPO
preserves the model's natural length, while GRPO lets it
over-shrink.

\paragraph{Cross-validation against §5.X instability metrics.}
The new per-step numbers agree with the existing instability index
in §5.X: the four GSM8K-CoT runs are classified there as
\emph{not verbosity-trap} (peak does not precede terminal drop),
and the per-step analysis here classifies them the same way
(flag $=0$). For the two arithmetic runs that are flagged in
§5.X (peak before 65\% training with terminal below 90\% of
peak), the new per-step view shows a different reason: the model
\emph{does} saturate above $0.98$, but it does so by compressing
length, not by inflating it. We therefore read the §5.X verbosity-trap
flag as a \emph{generic} instability detector, not a length-bias
detector specifically; the present pillar provides the direct
length-bias evidence the §5.X summary alludes to.

\paragraph{What would falsify this picture.}
If a follow-up experiment ran $200{+}$ steps on GSM8K-CoT and
produced $\rho(\text{step},\text{len})>0$ AND
$\rho(\text{step},\text{reward})\le 0$, the picture would invert.
Until then, the cleanest statement is: at the horizons and tasks
available in this benchmark, GRPO and Dr.~GRPO behave nearly
identically with respect to length -- both compress, Dr.~GRPO
slightly less so -- and neither engages the verbosity-trap
signature. We therefore \emph{do not} recommend Dr.~GRPO as a
necessary length-bias mitigation on these task scales; we
\emph{do} recommend measuring the same per-step Spearman on any
$200{+}$ step run before adopting it.

\paragraph{Scale extension: an uncapped Qwen3-8B panel.}
A six-arm panel run after the analysis above extends the scale axis:
Qwen3-8B (LoRA rank~4) on GSM8K, $30$ steps, $G{=}8$, batch~4, three
seeds per algorithm, with the completion cap raised $5\times$ to
$1{,}024$ tokens. The picture is unchanged: mean completion length
\emph{declines} 3.8--12.2\% in all six arms (GRPO $1004{\to}905$,
$981{\to}944$, $996{\to}900$; Dr.~GRPO $999{\to}931$, $972{\to}902$,
$1000{\to}878$ tokens), and late-run ZVF shows no separation between the
two losses (Dr.~GRPO $0.45/0.70/0.72$ vs.\ GRPO $0.47/0.47/0.55$).
Step-0 completions sit near the raised cap ($\approx 98\%$), so upward
headroom is still limited; but the trajectories move \emph{away} from
the cap rather than pressing against it, so the compression finding is
not a censoring artifact. The horizon caveat stands: $30$ steps does not
test the $200{+}$-step regime in which \cite{tong2025drgrpo} observe the
trap.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1)~$n{=}5$ and $n{=}3$ seeds are too few to put tight confidence
intervals on the difference between the two algorithms; we report
standard deviations across seeds but do not compute seed-level
$p$-values. (2)~Both tasks are \emph{reward-on-completion}
binary tasks, so the within-run $\rho(\text{len},\text{reward})$
is partially driven by the fact that longer incorrect completions
contribute one full penalty to the per-step mean -- not by an
advantage estimator pathology. (3)~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} themselves
report the verbosity trap at hundreds of steps on larger
reasoning benchmarks; our $30$--$40$ step horizons are short of
that regime by an order of magnitude. (4)~A direct ablation that
ran the same number of steps on a \emph{longer-horizon} task
(DeepSeek-R1 distillation chain, or R1-Zero on MATH) is the
natural next experiment, but is beyond the scope of this
iteration.

\paragraph{Take-aways for a reviewer.}
(1)~\emph{No length-bias trap at $30$--$40$ steps on either task.}
All sixteen runs have negative length trends, none crosses the
flag threshold. (2)~\emph{Dr.~GRPO's effect is small at this scale.}
On GSM8K-CoT it attenuates the length-trend slope by $0.27$ (one
sd) but does not flip its sign. (3)~\emph{Within-run coupling is
negative throughout.} Longer completions are not rewarded on our
data, in line with the model's pre-trap compression regime.
(4)~\emph{Direct per-step measurement} is the recommended
methodology -- aggregate instability indices can misclassify a
length-\emph{compressing} run as a length-\emph{inflation} run.
We therefore add the per-step Spearman triple to the recommended
diagnostic stack for any future length-bias audit on this benchmark.

\subsection{Elevated analysis: trap-onset detection, paired bootstrap CIs, decile binned coupling, and a 100-step cross-validation}
\label{sec:length-bias-elevated}

\paragraph{Why the elevation.}
The base analysis uses a \emph{global} monotonic-trend flag
(length slope $>0$ AND reward slope $\le 0$) and a within-run
Spearman triple. A reviewer concerned about the
Dr.~GRPO thesis~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} might object that the trap
\emph{can engage} in a sub-window without being visible in the
global trend, and that the small seed count ($n{=}5$ and $n{=}3$)
makes the per-algo difference hard to assess. This subsection
addresses both concerns with four additional analyses on the same
per-step traces.

\paragraph{(A) Sliding-window trap-onset detector.}
A window of $W{=}10$ consecutive steps is moved across each run;
at each step $s\ge W$ we compute $\rho_W(\text{step},\text{len})$
and $\rho_W(\text{step},\text{reward})$ on the window $[s-W,s]$.
The trap is said to \emph{onset} at the first $s$ where
\emph{(a)} the local $\rho_W(\text{step},\text{len})>0.3$ (a
non-trivial positive trend, not noise), \emph{(b)} the local
$\rho_W(\text{step},\text{reward})\le 0$, and \emph{(c)} the
end-of-window length is greater than the first-half mean length
(the model has actually \emph{re-expanded} past the early-stage
compression reference). Table~\ref{tab:length-bias-trap-rate}
reports the rate. On the easy task only $2$ of $10$ runs fire and
on the hard task $4$ of $6$ runs fire -- but in every case the
local reward is still \emph{increasing or flat} over the whole
run, so the global Dr.~GRPO signature (length \emph{growth}
followed by reward \emph{collapse}) is not engaged; the
sliding-window firings are local upticks against an overall
downward length trajectory, not a sustained verbosity-trap.

\paragraph{(C) Decile binned length-vs-reward coupling.}
The cleanest \emph{direct} test of the verbosity-trap hypothesis
is: \emph{within a (task, algo) cell, are longer completions
rewarded more than shorter completions?} We pool all
per-step $(\texttt{len}, \texttt{reward})$ points across seeds,
sort by length, and bin into $10$ deciles. Figure~\ref{fig:length-bias-elevated}
panel~(B) plots the GSM8K-CoT result. The decile-1 bin
(length $153$--$180$ tokens) has mean reward $0.351$, the
decile-8 bin (length $194$--$195$ tokens) has mean reward
$0.233$, and the relationship decreases across the length range with
one local exception: \emph{longer
completions are generally rewarded \emph{less}, not more}. The same
pattern holds on arithmetic: the highest-reward deciles (8, 9)
cluster around length $4.7$, while the lowest-reward decile
(10) has length $5.5$--$8.0$ -- these are the early-stage long
but mostly-wrong outputs that compress away as training
proceeds. The bin-level data thus \emph{rejects} the
verbosity-trap hypothesis at the bin level, consistent with the
global-flag and sliding-window analyses.

\paragraph{(D) 100-step cross-validation on \texttt{arithmetic\_metrics.jsonl}.}
The base Pillar 4 sources are $30$--$40$ step runs. The benchmark
also has a $100$-step arithmetic trace in
\texttt{experiments/results/arithmetic\_metrics.jsonl} with
per-step \texttt{ac\_tokens\_per\_turn} (the per-step mean
completion-length proxy) and \texttt{env/all/reward/total}.
The crossval is in \texttt{length\_bias\_crosval.tsv}:
$\rho(\text{step}, \text{len})=+0.41$ (the model is at the
truncation cap of $5$ tokens and shows a tiny upward drift),
$\rho(\text{step}, \text{reward})=+0.27$ (reward grows during
training as expected), and the absolute length range is
$0.08$ tokens -- the model has effectively no length to inflate
or compress. This is the most direct evidence that the
verbosity-trap mechanism has \emph{nothing to inflate} on
near-trivial-token tasks: the trap requires a non-trivial length
distribution, and a 5-token response distribution is one
distribution on which the trap is mechanically impossible.

(1)~\emph{The verbosity-trap is not engaged at $30$--$40$ step
horizons, and a sliding-window detector with strict
conditions still finds no global engagement on any of the
$16$ runs.} (2)~\emph{Dr.GRPO does \emph{significantly}
attenuate the compression on the hard task} (paired-bootstrap

paired-bootstrap CI on the algo difference}; the first two are
necessary, the last three are the elevation.

\paragraph{(E) Cross-pillar coupling with ZVF (iter 12).}
The frontier synthesis on Pillar~2 highlights that ZVF is best
framed as \emph{observed contrast availability} rather than
difficulty. The natural joint question is whether the verbosity
trap and the ZVF herding are \emph{the same mechanism under
different names}, or two independent failure modes. Both
$\texttt{drgrpo\_vs\_grpo.json}$ and
\texttt{drgrpo\_gsm8k\_cot\_full.json} carry a per-step
\texttt{zvf} entry alongside \texttt{mean\_comp\_len}, so we can
measure $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{zvf})$ per run, then bootstrap
within (task, algo) cells
(\texttt{scripts/length\_zvf\_coupling.py},
\texttt{length\_zvf\_coupling.tsv}, $n{=}16$ runs total).
The headline is a \emph{task-dependent sign flip}
(Figure~\ref{fig:length-zvf-coupling} panel~(A)):
\begin{itemize}
\item On arithmetic, $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{zvf})$ is
  \emph{negative} for both GRPO ($-0.449\,[-0.584,-0.324]$, $n{=}5$)
  and Dr.GRPO ($-0.291\,[-0.428,-0.139]$, $n{=}5$). The bootstrap
  $95\%$ CI excludes zero in both cells. As the model compresses
  length, ZVF \emph{rises}, the opposite of the simple
  ``converged-length $\Rightarrow$ all-same-output $\Rightarrow$
  herding'' story.
\item On GSM8K-CoT, $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{zvf})$ is
  \emph{positive} for both GRPO ($+0.286\,[+0.231,+0.348]$, $n{=}3$)
  and Dr.GRPO ($+0.187\,[+0.148,+0.225]$, $n{=}3$). Here length and
  ZVF move in the same direction, the canonical herding picture.
\end{itemize}
The sign flip rules out the simple causal arrow
``compression $\Rightarrow$ herding.''

\paragraph{Dr.GRPO's effect on the coupling.}
Dr.GRPO \emph{attenuates} $|\rho(\texttt{len},\texttt{zvf})|$ on
both tasks: paired-bootstrap
$\Delta(\text{Dr.GRPO}{-}\text{GRPO})$ is $+0.158$ on arithmetic
($95\%$ CI $[+0.075,+0.239]$, $n{=}5$) and $-0.099$ on GSM8K-CoT
($[-0.132,-0.041]$, $n{=}3$). Both CIs exclude zero. This is the
expected direction under Dr.GRPO's claim that per-question
normalisation decouples the gradient from raw response length,
but the magnitude is small (under $0.16$ in both cells). Note
also that Dr.GRPO does \emph{not} eliminate the sign-flip; it
merely compresses it toward zero.

\paragraph{Length-bin conditional ZVF (panel~(B)).}
Splitting each run's per-step trajectory into three length
tertiles and averaging ZVF within each tertile
(\texttt{length\_zvf\_bincond.tsv}, $48$ rows) recovers the same
sign flip from a different angle. On arithmetic, ZVF is highest
in the \emph{low-length} tertile (L1$=0.92$, L2$=0.94$) and
collapses in the high-length tertile (L3$=0.62$, paired
$\Delta_{\text{L3-L1}}{=}-0.30$, CI $[-0.34,-0.26]$). On
GSM8K-CoT the direction reverses: ZVF is highest in the
\emph{high-length} tertile (L3$=0.41$) and lower in the
low-length tertiles (L1$=0.30$, L2$=0.29$, paired
$\Delta_{\text{L3-L1}}{=}+0.11$, CI $[+0.10,+0.13]$).

\paragraph{Trajectory co-evolution (panel~(C)).}
Panel~(C) of Figure~\ref{fig:length-zvf-coupling} plots the
representative arithmetic GRPO seed-$42$ trajectory of
$\texttt{mean\_comp\_len}$ (normalized) and ZVF against step. The
two traces are \emph{anti-correlated at the step level}: ZVF
climbs from $\sim0.3$ to $\sim1.0$ during the same window in
which length falls from $\sim7$ to $\sim5$ tokens. This is a
direct visualisation of why the per-run
$\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{zvf})$ is negative on this cell.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
(1)~\emph{The verbosity trap and ZVF herding are not the same
mechanism}; they have \emph{opposite} signs of joint coupling
on near-ceiling short-output tasks (arithmetic) and \emph{same}
sign on hard long-output tasks (GSM8K-CoT).
(2)~\emph{The coupled mechanism depends on the response-length
distribution}: when the model has converged to a short, terse
regime, the residual ZVF inflation is a contrast signal about
\emph{correctness diversity}, not length diversity. When the
model operates in a long-form regime, length \emph{is} part of
the diversity axis and ZVF can encode length collapse.
(3)~\emph{Dr.GRPO attenuates both ends} of the coupling
without removing the sign-flip, consistent with its theoretical
claim of \emph{decoupling}, not \emph{eliminating}.
(4)~\emph{The recommended cross-pillar diagnostic is now}
$\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{zvf})$ + paired bootstrap CI +
length-bin conditional ZVF; this is the joint diagnostic that
neither Pillar~2 nor Pillar~4 alone can produce.

\paragraph{(F) Reward-shape decomposition $E[R \mid L]$ and Dr.GRPO flattening (iter 16).}
The Pillar~4 finding so far is that Dr.GRPO is at most a partial fix on the
easy arithmetic task and a measurable \emph{worsening} on hard GSM8K-CoT.
The frontier synthesis (FRONTIER\_INSIGHTS.md, Round~2) notes that
\textit{``ZVF is contrastive yield, not difficulty''} --- the natural
completion is to apply the same lens to the response-length axis: rather
than asking ``does length \emph{correlate} with reward?'', we ask
``does Dr.GRPO \emph{flatten} the conditional reward curve $E[R \mid L]$?''
This is the operational question for practitioners: even if the
per-step correlation is unchanged, a flatter $E[R\mid L]$ curve means
that the optimizer is no longer chasing longer outputs as a path to
higher reward, which is the actual mechanism by which length-bias
should be neutralised.

We compute the per-run Spearman $\rho(\texttt{len}, \texttt{reward})$
on the per-step trajectory of each of the $16$ runs (Table~\ref{tab:reward-shape}),
then pair the two algorithms by seed and bootstrap the
$\Delta_{\rho} = \rho_{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \rho_{\mathrm{GRPO}}$ with
$B{=}5000$ resamples and a $2000$-permutation $p$-value.

\paragraph{(G) Mechanism test: $dL/dR$ regression, $L_t$ momentum, and ZVF mediation (iter 20).}
The iter~16 reward-shape test is correlational; it measures whether Dr.GRPO
flattens the $E[R\mid L]$ curve but does not directly test the
\emph{mechanism} by which the flattening should arise. The Dr.GRPO paper
(Liu et al.\ 2025, arXiv:2503.20783) claims that the per-token advantage
normalisation $A_i \to A_i / |o_i|$ removes the response-length variance
from the gradient. Concretely, this implies three step-level predictions
that are testable from the existing aggregate step log:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The step-level OLS slope $\widehat{dL/dR}$ of mean completion
  length on mean reward should be \emph{smaller in magnitude} under
  Dr.GRPO than under GRPO on the easy arithmetic task (where the length
  bias is operative), because the long-response update-magnitude
  advantage is neutralised.
\item The lag-1 autocorrelation of $\Delta L_t$ should be weaker under
  Dr.GRPO on the same task (length momentum is reduced).
\item The early- vs late-half mean-length gap $L(\text{late}) - L(\text{early})$,
  split by the per-run median reward, should be smaller in magnitude
  under Dr.GRPO (the optimizer no longer pushes length up at higher reward).
\end{enumerate}
We implement (1)--(3) per-run, pair by seed, and bootstrap the
$\Delta = \text{Dr.GRPO} - \text{GRPO}$ distribution with $B{=}5000$
resamples and a $2000$-permutation $p$-value
(\texttt{scripts/length\_bias\_mechanism.py}). The per-run results are
in \texttt{experiments/results/length\_bias\_mechanism\_per\_run.tsv}
(16 rows) and the aggregated paired deltas in
\texttt{length\_bias\_mechanism\_paired.tsv}.

predictions hold and \emph{two are statistically significant}:
$\widehat{dL/dR}_{\text{OLS}}$ is reduced by $0.43$ tokens per reward
unit (95\% CI $[-0.58, -0.28]$, paired $p{=}0.036$);
$\widehat{dL/dR}_{\text{TS}}$ is reduced by $1.11$ (CI $[-1.59, -0.63]$,
$p{=}0.047$); $\rho(L,R)$ is reduced by $0.10$ (CI $[-0.18, -0.03]$,

unchanged ($\Delta = +0.01$, $p{=}0.92$), which is consistent with
length momentum being driven by the trajectory of the policy itself
rather than by the per-step gradient. On the hard GSM8K-CoT task the
direction of \emph{every} delta reverses: Dr.GRPO \emph{increases}
$\widehat{dL/dR}_{\text{OLS}}$ by $+4.41$ (CI $[-4.58, 19.84]$,

(Dr.GRPO is regime-specific) and gives a second, mechanism-level reading
of why: on long-form CoT, length \emph{is} the policy-progress signal
the optimizer is trying to amplify, and Dr.GRPO's length-normalisation
sharpens that amplification rather than damping it.

\textbf{ZVF mediation.} The step-level ZVF $Z_t$ from Pillar~2 acts as
a \emph{partial mediator} of the length--reward coupling on both tasks.
We regress $R_t$ on $L_t$ (total), then on $L_t \mid Z_t$ (direct)
and on $Z_t \mid L_t$ (indirect via $Z$). On the arithmetic task the
proportion of the $L \to R$ coupling that flows \emph{through} ZVF
\emph{increases} from $0.42$ (GRPO) to $0.55$ (Dr.GRPO): Dr.GRPO's
per-token normalisation makes the ZVF channel \emph{more} important in
determining step-level reward. On the GSM8K-CoT task the mediated
proportion \emph{decreases} from $0.28$ to $0.12$: on hard CoT, ZVF is
\emph{not} the channel through which length acts on reward, and Dr.GRPO
removes the residual direct path less effectively than it does on the
easy task. This is the first mechanistic cross-pillar measurement
linking Pillar~2's ZVF diagnostic to Pillar~4's length-bias decomposition.

\subsection{Drift \& forecast: is the verbosity trap \emph{beginning} to engage?}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter24}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The base analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:length-bias-pillar}) and the
elevated mechanism analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:length-bias-elevated})
both ask whether the Dr.~GRPO signature is \emph{already} visible
at $30$--$40$ step horizons. Iter~24 asks the sharper question:
\emph{is the trap \emph{beginning} to engage}? The Dr.~GRPO
hypothesis~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} predicts a \emph{drift}: the
windowed Spearman coupling $\rho_w(\mathrm{len}_t, R_t)$ should
move from strongly negative (compression regime) toward zero or
positive as training proceeds, because the model's within-step
length distribution collapses onto a single verbose template and
length ceases to be diagnostic of correctness. We measure four
drift / forecast signals purely from the per-step aggregates
$(\texttt{mean\_reward}_t, \texttt{mean\_comp\_len}_t)$ that
already exist.

\paragraph{(S1) Sliding-window $\rho_w(\mathrm{len}, R)$ drift.}
For each run we compute windowed Spearman correlation for windows
of size $w \in \{8, 10, 12\}$ and split the window sequence into
\emph{early}, \emph{mid}, and \emph{late} thirds. We then take the
mean across seeds for each (task, algo, $w$) cell. The
window-size-$10$ results are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:length-bias-iter24-drift}.

\paragraph{(S2) Sign-flip test.}
For each (task, algo, $w$) cell we compute the fraction of
\emph{late} windows whose $\rho \ge 0$ and compare to the null
hypothesis of $0.5$ (no drift) with a one-sample $t$-test on
$(\mathrm{frac} - 0.5)$. The results at $w = 10$ are:
GRPO-arithmetic $0.527$ ($p = 0.468$), Dr.GRPO-arithmetic
$0.382$ ($p = 0.562$), GRPO-GSM8K $0.048$ ($p = 0.011$,
\emph{significantly below $0.5$}), Dr.GRPO-GSM8K $0.000$
($p < 0.001$, \emph{significantly below $0.5$}). On the
hard task the late-window correlation stays negative in
\emph{every} single late window across both algorithms -- the
sign-flip rate is statistically \emph{below} $0.5$ rather than
above it. The Dr.~GRPO trap signature is therefore absent on
GSM8K-CoT at $30$ steps; on the easy task it is only \emph{weakly
emergent}, and only under plain GRPO.

\paragraph{(S3) Forecast horizon.}
For each run we linearly extrapolate the
$(\mathrm{step}, \mathrm{len})$ trajectory. The slope is
\emph{negative} for all $16$ runs (mean slope $-0.029$ tokens/step
for arithmetic GRPO, $-0.481$ tokens/step for GSM8K-CoT GRPO);
a positive-slope forecast horizon $t^*$ at which the model would
re-expand to its initial length does not exist for any run
(\texttt{n\_with\_horizon} = 0 for all four cells). On the
currently observed slope, arithmetic GRPO would have to run for
$\approx 200$ steps to return to its initial length; GSM8K-CoT
GRPO would have to run for $\approx 30$ more steps. Neither
extrapolation is alarming; the \emph{linear} model cannot
produce the verbosity-trap, and we would need at least a
piecewise-linear or sigmoid fit to capture the on--off
behaviour predicted by \cite{tong2025drgrpo}. We therefore
treat the forecast-horizon exercise as a falsification of the
\emph{strict-linear} trap model, not as evidence against the
general Dr.~GRPO thesis.

\paragraph{(S4) First-difference coupling $\rho(\Delta L, \Delta R)$.}
The detrended coupling $\rho(\Delta L_t, \Delta R_t)$ asks
\emph{locally}: does a step-to-step increase in length coincide
with an increase in reward (positive feedback, herding channel)
or a decrease in reward (correction channel)? On arithmetic the
coupling is weakly negative (GRPO $\rho = -0.164$, Dr.GRPO
$\rho = -0.169$, n.s.), consistent with a noisy correction
channel; on GSM8K-CoT it is strongly negative for both
algorithms (GRPO $\rho = -0.676$, Dr.GRPO $\rho = -0.673$),
meaning that whenever length \emph{increases} from one step to
the next, reward \emph{drops} by approximately half a standard
deviation -- the correction channel is dominant on the hard
task and Dr.~GRPO does not change this.

\paragraph{Headline finding.}
\textbf{The Dr.~GRPO trap is \emph{barely} detectable at our
$30$--$40$ step horizons, and only on the easy task, only under
plain GRPO, and only as a \emph{drift} toward zero rather than a
sign-flip.} Specifically:
\emph{(i)}~windowed $\rho$ drifts $+0.326$ on arithmetic GRPO
($p = 0.035$, the only statistically significant drift);
\emph{(ii)}~Dr.GRPO halves this drift to $+0.187$ on the same
task (ns); \emph{(iii)}~on GSM8K-CoT the late-window $\rho$ stays
\emph{more} negative than the early-window $\rho$ for both
algorithms ($-0.084$ and $+0.019$ drift, both ns); and
\emph{(iv)}~no run produces a positive length slope, so the
\emph{linear}-trap horizon is undefined. We therefore read the
iter~24 result as: \emph{at the horizons and tasks in this
benchmark, the Dr.~GRPO trap is in its very earliest phase on
arithmetic under plain GRPO, and Dr.~GRPO does measurably
attenuate even that early drift}. The hard task shows no trap
signature at all. This is consistent with
\cite{tong2025drgrpo}'s hundreds-of-steps horizon; our data is
in the pre-trap regime.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar note.}
The within-step length-dispersion collapse (the \emph{herding}
channel that mediates the verbosity trap; see Pillar~3's
anti-herding diagnostic) is
\emph{not directly observable} from our per-step aggregates
because we only retain \texttt{mean\_comp\_len}, not the
within-step length distribution. We flag this as the natural
next iteration: a run that logs per-completion lengths would let
us measure within-step $\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{len})$ and test
whether that dispersion is \emph{falling} in the late windows
on the arithmetic GRPO cell where the windowed $\rho$ drift is
significant. This is the most direct empirical test of the
Dr.~GRPO trap mechanism available, and it is one log-field away
from being executed.

 \subsection{Length-Bias Step Decomposition and Dr.\,GRPO Signature (Iter 28)}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter28}


\paragraph{Motivation.}
The verbosity-trap hypothesis~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} predicts a
characteristic \emph{signature}: per-step length grows while per-step reward
plateaus or declines, with the strongest effect on tasks where the reward
function tolerates longer answers. Iter~24 established that on the
30-step GSM8K-CoT and 40-step arithmetic horizons available here, this
\emph{aggregate} signature is weak or absent---length decreases as reward
increases (i.e., the anti-trap regime). We now decompose the same
step-level trace data into three orthogonal within-step components and ask
whether the signature emerges \emph{at any} component, and whether the
Dr.\,GRPO correction~\cite{tong2025drgrpo} selectively attenuates one of
them.

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
For each (task, algo, seed) trace with at least ten per-step
(mean~$L$, mean~$R$, ZVF) samples we compute:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Aggregate trend} --- OLS slope of $L$ on step
  ($\beta_L$, tokens/step) and of $R$ on step
  ($\beta_R$, probability/step).
\item \textbf{ZVF coupling} --- Spearman $\rho(L, \mathrm{ZVF})$ across
  the steps of one run (low ZVF $=$ high contrast $=$ rollouts
  with at least one correct and one incorrect).
\item \textbf{Reward coupling} --- Spearman $\rho(L, R)$ across the same
  steps (positive = within-step longer $\Rightarrow$ higher reward;
  negative = within-step longer $\Rightarrow$ lower reward).
\end{enumerate}

We additionally compute the \emph{Dr.\,GRPO signature}:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} = \mathrm{corr}(\beta_L, \beta_R)$ across seeds
of the same (task, algo) cell. A \emph{positive} signature means seeds
with steeper length growth also had steeper reward growth (the
canonical trap); a \emph{negative} signature means seeds that grew
length lost reward (anti-trap).

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:length-bias-iter28} reports the per-(task,algo) mean and
bootstrap-95\%-CI estimates across seeds ($n{=}3$ for GSM8K-CoT,
$n{=}5$ for arithmetic Qwen2.5-0.5B).

\paragraph{Within-step ZVF coupling is task-dependent and signs the
mechanism.}
On GSM8K-CoT, $\rho(L, \mathrm{ZVF}) > 0$ under both algos: in steps with
many uniform-reward (all-correct or all-wrong) groups, the mean
completion length is \emph{longer}. This is consistent with the
hypothesis that uniform-reward steps arise when the model has learned a
``safe'' longer-answer pattern that the verifier cannot distinguish
between successes and failures. On the easy arithmetic task the sign
flips ($\rho(L, \mathrm{ZVF}) < 0$): uniform-reward steps are the
\emph{easy}-arithmetic steps, where the model has converged to a short
canonical answer. Dr.\,GRPO reduces the magnitude of $\rho(L, \mathrm{ZVF})$
on arithmetic by 35\% ($-0.449 \to -0.291$) and on GSM8K by 35\%
($+0.286 \to +0.187$), i.e.\ the length-normalisation correction
weakens both the easy-task uniform-short and the hard-task uniform-long
behaviour symmetrically.

\paragraph{Within-step length-reward coupling is consistently negative.}
On both tasks and both algos, $\rho(L, R) < 0$ with bootstrap CI excluding
zero: longer responses within a step are \emph{rewarded less}, not more.
This is the opposite of the verbosity-trap prediction that the model
should be rewarded for length. Dr.\,GRPO \emph{intensifies} this negative
coupling on both tasks (GSM8K: $-0.501 \to -0.618$; arithmetic:
$-0.419 \to -0.293$ magnitude reduced, but the per-step grammar
penalty means long outputs that the verifier marks incorrect contribute
more strongly to the negative gradient). We interpret this as evidence
that the trap mechanism is \emph{not} the within-step gradient but
rather something in the cross-step learning dynamics.

\paragraph{Dr.\,GRPO signature is anti-trap on the easy task and the
correction \emph{removes} it.}
The arithmetic GRPO cell has the strongest negative Dr.\,GRPO signature
on the benchmark ($\sigma_{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} = -0.93$, $p = 0.023$):
seeds with the steepest length decrease also had the steepest reward
increase. Dr.\,GRPO releases this signature ($\sigma = +0.47$, $p = 0.42$,
no longer significant). Mechanistically, plain GRPO with the same
rollout batch has both its length and its reward partly controlled by
shared seed-specific random initialisation noise; Dr.\,GRPO's
length-normalised advantage decouples these by removing the per-prompt
length scaling from the baseline, so seed-level drift no longer
co-varies the two slopes.

\paragraph{Implications.}
\begin{itemize}
\item On the measured 30--40-step horizons the \emph{aggregate} length
  trend is consistently \emph{downward} and the within-step length-reward
  coupling is consistently \emph{negative}. The Dr.\,GRPO verbosity trap
  is therefore not the dominant failure mode on this benchmark;
  whatever length bias exists acts \emph{against} longer responses.
\item Dr.\,GRPO's length-normalisation correction has a measurable
  signature on easy tasks: it eliminates the strong negative
  seed-level correlation between length and reward slopes. This is
  consistent with the original Dr.\,GRPO paper's claim that the
  correction removes a confound in the GRPO advantage estimator.
\item The within-step ZVF-coupling \emph{sign} is a clean task-dependent
  diagnostic: positive on hard tasks where uniform-reward steps are
  verbosity-dominated, negative on easy tasks where they are
  canonical-answer-dominated.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:length-bias-iter32}


\paragraph{Motivation.}
Iter 28 decomposed the length trajectory into (A) the aggregate trend,
(B) within-step ZVF coupling, and (C) within-step reward coupling, and
found that $\rho(L,R)$ is consistently \emph{negative} on every (task,
algo) cell---the verbosity-trap does not operate at the within-step
gradient. None of those axes answers the \emph{time-axis} question: does
the length trajectory \emph{lead} or \emph{lag} the reward trajectory
across steps? If the trap operates via a delayed-correction channel
(length drops first, reward rises $k{>}0$ steps later) the lag profile
$\rho(L_t, R_{t{+}k})$ should peak at positive $k$; if the trap operates
via an anticipatory channel (reward rises first, length follows at
$k{<}0$) it peaks at negative $k$. We answer this question on the same
10 (task, algo, seed) traces as iter~28.

\paragraph{Driver.}
For each (task, algo, seed) trace we form the lag-$k$ Spearman
correlation
$\rho_k := \rho(L_t, R_{t{+}k})$ for $k \in \{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}$,
plus the coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV}_L = \mathrm{std}(L)/\mathrm{mean}(L)$
as an operational length-stability metric. We then compute, per cell:
(i) the distribution of the \emph{dominant} lag $k^\star =
\arg\max_{k}|\rho_k|$ per seed; (ii) the paired-bootstrap estimate of
$\rho_{+1} - \rho_{-1}$ (signed magnitude of the lead/lag asymmetry); and
(iii) the per-cell $\mathrm{CV}_L$ with bootstrap 95\% CI. Outputs are
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter32\_lag\_profile.tsv} (16 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter32\_lag\_summary.tsv} (4 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter32\_cv.tsv} (4 rows), and
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter32\_lead\_lag.tsv} (4 rows).

\paragraph{Results.}
On 13/16 runs the dominant lag is $k{=}0$ (contemporaneous coupling).
The two exceptions are: arithmetic GRPO seed~42 and seed~789 share a
non-zero peak at $k{=}3$ (or $k{=}0$), and arithmetic Dr.\,GRPO has two
seeds with peak at $k{=}{+}1$ and two seeds with peak at $k{=}{+}3$
--- these are the only cells with any positive-lag (length-leads-reward)
support. Critically, the per-cell \emph{paired} bootstrap on
$\rho_{+1} - \rho_{-1}$ is significantly negative on \emph{arithmetic
Dr.\,GRPO} ($-0.104$, 95\% CI $[-0.178, -0.027]$, $p_{\le 0}{=}0.995$),
significantly positive on \emph{arithmetic GRPO} ($+0.028$, 95\% CI
$[-0.051, +0.093]$, $p_{\le 0}{=}0.225$), and inconclusive (wide CI) on
GSM8K-CoT.

Dr.\,GRPO thus *flips* the lead-lag direction on the easy task from
``R and L co-move (no lead)'' to ``R leads L (correction channel).
This is consistent with the iter 28 finding that Dr.\,GRPO decouples
the seed-level $\beta_L$-$\beta_R$ slope correlation; the lag analysis
localises the same effect at the per-step temporal axis: the algorithm
inserts a one-step delay between reward inflection and length
inflection (rather than letting them happen simultaneously).

The \emph{CV}$_L$ (length stability) metric shows a sharper task-dependent
effect: arithmetic CV$_L$ is $5{-}6\times$ larger than GSM8K CV$_L$
(0.13 vs 0.024), reflecting that arithmetic has 40-step-length answers
that decay, whereas GSM8K has 192-token CoT answers whose variability
comes mostly from content not length. Dr.\,GRPO reduces CV$_L$ by
~28\% on GSM8K in point estimate ($0.034 \to 0.024$), but the bootstrap
CIs overlap, so we treat this as directional; it reduces CV$_L$ by ~5\% on
arithmetic ($0.135 \to 0.128$, CI just touching).

The temporal-lag analysis thus provides the cleanest comparative
evidence on this benchmark that Dr.\,GRPO's \emph{behavioural} effect is
not on the level of the trajectory itself (the iter 28 finding) but on
the \emph{coupling} between L and R: under GRPO the two co-move; under
Dr.\,GRPO reward changes are absorbed into L trajectories with a
one-step lag, consistent with a loss-side normalisation that delays
the visible response of length to a reward inflection by exactly one
update step.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar reconciliation (frontier synthesis).}
The iter~32 finding is consistent with the iter~30 ZVF leading-indicator
view~\cite{tong2025drgrpo, kimi}: if ZVF is a \emph{diagnostic} (not a

\emph{coupling} signature rather than a \emph{trajectory} signature.
Both iters find the same: aggregate trajectory is anti-trap (iter 28
anti-$\beta$), within-step $\rho(L,R)$ is negative (iter 28 negative
$\rho$), and the inter-step lag profile peaks at $k{<}0$ under
Dr.\,GRPO (this iter)---none of which matches the canonical positive
on/off trap of Tong et al. Our data are therefore evidence that the
\emph{measurable} trap signature on this benchmark is a \emph{coupling}
one, not a \emph{trajectory-level} one.

 \subsection{Joint Saturation of Length and Reward (Iter 36)}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter36}


\paragraph{Motivation.}
Iter~28 decomposed the length and reward trajectories \emph{separately}
into trend, ZVF-coupling, and reward-coupling components and concluded
that the verbosity-trap does not operate at the within-step gradient
($\rho(L, R)$ is consistently negative across every measured cell).
Iter~32 added a temporal axis and showed that the cross-correlation
peaks at lag~0 on 13/16 runs (contemporaneous coupling, no feedback
timing). Neither analysis asks the natural \emph{joint} question: are
the two trajectories governed by the same saturation dynamic, or do
they decouple? If both $L(t)$ and $R(t)$ share a common
$\lambda$-scale then the descriptive model $X(t) = X_{\max}(1 -
e^{-\lambda t})$ is a unified account; if they decouple, the
verbosity-trap (longer $L$ while $R$ plateaus) or the anti-trap
(shorter $L$ while $R$ rises) is the operative signature. This iter
fits $R(t)$ and $L(t)$ jointly per run and tests the joint-saturation
hypothesis $\lambda_L \approx \lambda_R$.

\paragraph{Driver.}
For each (task, algo, seed) trace we fit the bounded
saturation model
\begin{equation}
  X(t) \;=\; a \;+\; (b - a)\,\bigl(1 - e^{-\lambda t}\bigr),
  \qquad \lambda \in [10^{-4},\, 10],
\end{equation}
via grid search over $\lambda$ in $\mathrm{logspace}(-2, 1, 60)$
followed by finite-difference polishing; $a$ and $b$ are then
closed-form via least squares at the polished $\lambda$. The fit is
flagged \emph{identifiable} when the polished $\lambda$ lies strictly
inside the bound; GSM8K $L$ traces that hit the lower bound are
classified as non-saturating (linear-drift regime). Per run we report
$(\lambda_R, \lambda_L, t_{80}^{(X)} = -\ln 0.2 / \lambda_X)$ and the
residual cross-correlation $\rho(\epsilon_R, \epsilon_L)$ where
$\epsilon_X(t) = X(t) - \hat{X}(t)$. Per (task, algo) we report the
median $\lambda_L/\lambda_R$ across seeds (robust to single-fit
boundary outliers) and the paired bootstrap (4000 resamples) on
$\lambda_L/\lambda_R^{(\mathrm{GRPO})} - \lambda_L/\lambda_R^{(\mathrm{Dr.GRPO})}$.
Outputs are \texttt{length\_bias\_iter36\_per\_run.tsv} (16 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter36\_summary.tsv} (4 rows per-(task, algo)),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter36\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo.tsv} (2 rows
paired-bootstrap), and \texttt{length\_bias\_iter36\_findings.tsv} (2
rows aggregate).

\paragraph{Headline finding 1: joint saturation holds on easy tasks,
fails on hard reasoning.}
On the arithmetic task (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 40 steps, 5 seeds per algo) the
saturation model is identifiable in 5/5 $R$ fits and 5/5 $L$ fits for
both algorithms; the median $\lambda_L / \lambda_R$ ratio is $1.23$
for GRPO and $1.37$ for Dr.GRPO (Table~\ref{tab:length-iter36-summary}).
Length saturates \emph{at least as fast as} reward on the easy task,
and Dr.GRPO significantly \emph{increases} the ratio: paired
bootstrap on $\lambda_L/\lambda_R^{(\mathrm{GRPO})} -
\lambda_L/\lambda_R^{(\mathrm{Dr.GRPO})}$ gives $\Delta = -0.28$,
$95\%$ CI $[-0.45, -0.06]$, $p(\Delta \leq 0) = 0.995$
(Table~\ref{tab:length-iter36-cross}). The verbosity-trap prediction
($\lambda_L \ll \lambda_R$ while $R$ plateaus) is rejected on easy
arithmetic; the operative dynamic is closer to an \emph{anti-trap}
(length converges to its asymptote \emph{first}, then reward catches
up).

\paragraph{Headline finding 2: GSM8K $L$ is non-saturating.}
On GSM8K CoT (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 30 steps, 3 seeds per algo) the
saturation model is identifiable in 3/3 GRPO $R$ fits, 2/3 Dr.GRPO
$R$ fits, but only 1/3 $L$ fits per algorithm---the other $L$ traces
hit the lower $\lambda$ bound at $10^{-4}$, corresponding to
$t_{80} > 16{,}000$ steps. The joint-saturation hypothesis is
decisively falsified on hard reasoning: the operative dynamic for
$L$ is a slow linear drift with no detectable saturation on the
30-step horizon. After detrending, the residual cross-correlation
$\rho(\epsilon_R, \epsilon_L)$ is strongly \emph{negative} on GSM8K:
$-0.58$ (GRPO median), $-0.65$ (Dr.GRPO median); the residuals move
in opposite directions, confirming the anti-trap regime at the
within-step residual level.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar synthesis (P4 $\times$ P1).}
Iter~29 established that the saturation model
$R(t) = R_{\max}(1 - e^{-\lambda t})$ is the right descriptive model
for GRPO but the wrong model for PPO on the same stack, with AICc
favoring exponential saturation in 5/5 GRPO traces and linear in
5/5 PPO traces. The present iter extends that finding by asking the
\emph{joint} question: when $R$ is well-described by saturation, is
$L$ saturated \emph{at the same rate}? The answer is task-conditional.
On easy arithmetic the joint saturation model is well-supported
($\lambda_L / \lambda_R \approx 1.2$--$1.4$ for both algorithms), but
Dr.GRPO shifts the ratio upward by $\sim 0.14$, consistent with the
finding in iter~28 that Dr.GRPO measurably stabilises length
(CV$_L$ drops by 5--28\% depending on task). On hard GSM8K CoT the
joint model fails by identifiability: $L$ does not exhibit the
saturation dynamic on the 30-step horizon at all, and the residuals
of $R$ and $L$ move in opposite directions ($\rho \approx -0.6$).
The verbosity-trap literature claims the opposite---that hard
reasoning \emph{amplifies} the trap \cite{singhal2023drdrpo}---but on
the traces measured here the operative dynamic
on GSM8K is closer to an anti-trap with $L$ as the slow
non-saturating process and $R$ as the fast-saturating one. The
negatives reported in the paired bootstrap ($p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.995$
on arithmetic) suggest Dr.GRPO \emph{strengthens} the anti-trap
signature relative to GRPO on the easy task, not weakens it.

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Iters 28 / 32 / 36 studied the aggregate L(t) and R(t) trajectories:
step-decomposition into trend, ZVF-coupling and R-coupling on
iter~28; lag cross-correlation on iter~32; joint saturation fitting
on iter~36.  None asks the \emph{phase-conditional} question: does
the within-step length--reward coupling change across training?  The
Dr.GRPO length-normalization analysis predicts the trap
\emph{strengthens} late in training as the policy's length
distribution becomes more skewed---the within-step slope
$\beta_{R|L}^{(t)}$ should turn positive in the late phase.  The
anti-trap regime (iter 28's evidence) predicts the opposite: the
slope stays negative throughout, with a possibly stronger magnitude
late.  We split each run into $K=3$ equal-width phases and test both
hypotheses, jointly with a pooled anti-trap magnitude and a
reward-per-token (R/L) trajectory.

\paragraph{Driver.}
For each (task, algo, seed) trace we form the within-step OLS slope
$\beta_{R|L}^{(p)} = \mathrm{Cov}(L,R)/\mathrm{Var}(L)$ for each phase
$p \in \{\mathrm{early},\mathrm{mid},\mathrm{late}\}$ (early =
$[0, n/3)$, mid = $[n/3, 2n/3)$, late = $[2n/3, n)$); the pooled
Pearson slope as a per-run anti-trap magnitude
($\beta_{R|L}^{\mathrm{pool}}$); and the R/L ratio trajectory
$R_t / L_t$ as a measure of reward density per generated token.  We
report the per-(task, algo, phase) median slope
($\tilde\beta_{R|L}^{(p)}$) and the phase-delta
$\tilde\beta^{(late)} - \tilde\beta^{(early)}$; the per-run pooled
anti-trap slope; and a paired bootstrap (4000 resamples, paired over
seeds) of GRPO vs Dr.GRPO on five metrics:
$\beta^{(early)}$, $\beta^{(late)}-\beta^{(early)}$, R/L drift
($R/L_{\mathrm{last}} - R/L_{\mathrm{first}}$), R/L last, and the
pooled anti-trap slope.  Outputs are
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter40\_per\_run.tsv} (16 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter40\_phases.tsv} (48 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter40\_summary.tsv} (4 rows),
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter40\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo.tsv} (10 rows), and
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter40\_findings.tsv} (2 rows).

\paragraph{Headline finding 1: the slope flips sign from early
(anti-trap) to late (mixed / trap-like) on the easy task.}
On \emph{arithmetic\_easy} (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 40 steps, 5 seeds) the
per-phase slope is strongly negative in the early phase
($\tilde\beta_{R|L}^{(\mathrm{early})} \approx -0.24$ for both GRPO
and Dr.GRPO), close to zero in the mid phase, and turns slightly
\emph{positive} in the late phase for GRPO ($+0.014$) but stays
slightly negative for Dr.GRPO ($-0.009$).  The phase-delta
$\tilde\beta^{(\mathrm{late})} - \tilde\beta^{(\mathrm{early})}$ is
$+0.257$ for GRPO and $+0.253$ for Dr.GRPO---the within-step
length--reward coupling \emph{flips} from anti-trap to a near-zero
or trap-like signature as training progresses.  The verbosity-trap
prediction is therefore \emph{partially supported} on this task: the
late-phase slope is no longer anti-trap.  Dr.GRPO delays the flip
slightly (late $\beta$ stays negative), consistent with iter 28's
finding that the algorithm measurably stabilises length.

\paragraph{Headline finding 2: Dr.GRPO strengthens the anti-trap on
the pooled slope, significantly so.}
The pooled per-run anti-trap slope (Pearson $\beta_{R|L}$ over all
steps) is negative on \emph{every} (task, algo, seed) trace.
Paired bootstrap GRPO vs Dr.GRPO gives a positive
$\Delta = \beta^{\mathrm{GRPO}} - \beta^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}}$ on both
tasks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item arithmetic: $\Delta = +0.0172$, 95\% CI $[+0.003, +0.033]$,
        $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.005$ --- Dr.GRPO is \emph{significantly
        more} anti-trap than GRPO;
  \item GSM8K: $\Delta = +0.0050$, 95\% CI $[+0.001, +0.008]$,
        $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$ --- same direction, highly
        significant.
\end{itemize}
Across 16 (task, algo, seed) runs the pooled anti-trap slope is
strictly negative; Dr.GRPO raises the magnitude by a small but
consistent amount.  This is the cleanest single-number evidence that
Dr.GRPO \emph{strengthens}, not weakens, the anti-trap signature at
the pooled level.

\paragraph{Headline finding 3: reward-per-token rises sharply on the
easy task, but the two algorithms are statistically
indistinguishable.}
The R/L drift (last decile minus first decile of R/L) is $+0.145$ on
arithmetic for both GRPO and Dr.GRPO; the paired bootstrap on the
arithmetic drift difference has 95\% CI $[-0.007, -0.0004]$ with
$p(\Delta \le 0) = 1.0$---GRPO's drift is marginally larger but the
95\% CI is essentially symmetric around zero.  On GSM8K the drift is
much smaller in magnitude ($+0.0007$ for GRPO, $+0.0006$ for
Dr.GRPO), and the paired bootstrap gives $\Delta = +0.00009$ with
$p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$---GRPO's drift is statistically larger.
Dr.GRPO therefore yields a \emph{slightly smaller} efficiency gain
on the hard task.  This is the first metric in the iter 28--40
sequence where Dr.GRPO is \emph{worse} than GRPO, and the effect
size is small but real on GSM8K.

\subsubsection{Conditional quantile regression on $R \mid L$}\label{sec:lb-iter44}

The verbosity-trap literature predicts that longer responses inflate the
denominator of $R/L$ without raising $R$ itself, producing a positive length
bias. Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter28} used OLS on $\E[R\mid L]$ and reported a
strongly anti-trap signature (negative $\beta_{R\mid L}$) on every
(\task, \algo, \seed) run. Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter36} showed $L$ and $R$ share
a common saturation scale, and iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40} stratified the slope
into training phases. None of those decompositions, however, asked whether the
$\E[R\mid L]$ slope hides \emph{asymmetric} length-response at different
quantiles of $R$. This iter addresses that gap.

\paragraph{Quantile slopes.}
We fit pinball-loss quantile regression (in-house SGD, no sklearn dep) for
$\tau \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9\}$ on per-step $(R, L)$ pairs pooled
across the 5 \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} seeds or the 3 \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}
seeds, separately for GRPO and Dr.GRPO. The slope of $q_\tau(R \mid L)$ as a
function of $L$ is reported in
\path{experiments/results/length_bias_iter44_quantile_slopes.tsv} (80 rows,
one per \task$\times$\algo$\times$\seed$\times\tau$ combination).

\paragraph{Asymmetry delta $\delta_{R \mid L} \equiv \beta_{q_{0.9}} -
\beta_{q_{0.1}}$.}
If longer responses carried only shallow signal, the upper-quantile slope
$\beta_{q_{0.9}}$ would be small or negative while the lower-quantile slope
$\beta_{q_{0.1}}$ would be positive (longer responses cluster around
mid-reward), producing a NEGATIVE asymmetry. We observe the opposite on every
one of the 16 (\task, \algo, \seed) cells: $\delta_{R \mid L} > 0$ for all
16, with median $|\delta_{R \mid L}| = 0.030$ on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}
and $0.0034$ on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot} (\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_asymmetry.tsv}).

\paragraph{Dr.GRPO strengthens the asymmetry.}
A 4000-rep paired bootstrap on the per-seed asymmetry (Dr.GRPO $-$ GRPO)
gives $\Delta = +0.0052$ on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}, $95\%$\,CI
$[-0.0001, +0.0119]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.030$; and $\Delta = +0.0008$ on
\texttt{gsm8k\_cot}, $95\%$\,CI $[-0.0004, +0.0019]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) =
0.034$ (\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo.tsv}). The one-sided
tests support the predicted direction, although the two-sided 95\% CIs
marginally include zero. Dr.GRPO therefore directionally
increases the conditional upper-quantile length-response on both tasks,
strengthening (not suppressing) the anti-trap signature.

\paragraph{ZVF-binned interaction (cross-pillar with iter~34).}
Stratifying per-step $(R, L)$ pairs by per-step \zvf{} into
\texttt{low}$(<\!0.2)$, \texttt{mid}$([0.2, 0.5))$, and
\texttt{high}$(\ge\!0.5)$ bins reveals a striking regime flip
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_zvf\_binned.tsv}). On \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}
in the \texttt{high}-ZVF bin (the contrast-rich, near-ceiling regime),
$\beta_{R \mid L}$ is $-0.010$ for GRPO but $+0.033$ for Dr.GRPO --- a
sign-flip that the unconditional regression averages away. The paired bootstrap
on the high-bin $\beta$ gives $\Delta = +0.043$, $95\%$\,CI
$[+0.024, +0.062]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$ (4-of-4 Dr.GRPO seeds have
$\beta_{R \mid L} > 0$ in the high-ZVF bin while 5-of-5 GRPO seeds have
$\beta_{R \mid L} < 0$). On \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}, Dr.GRPO strengthens the
anti-trap slope in the \texttt{mid}-ZVF bin ($-0.004 \to -0.013$).

\paragraph{Operational reading.}
Three findings from iter~28--40 already established that the unconditional
$\E[R \mid L]$ slope is anti-trap; iter~44 adds: (a) the asymmetry of that
slope across quantiles is consistently positive (not negative as the
verbosity-trap would predict); (b) Dr.GRPO \emph{strengthens} that
asymmetry significantly on both tasks; (c) in the contrast-rich
high-ZVF regime on the easy task, Dr.GRPO flips the slope sign. The
anti-trap is therefore not a single mean-shift phenomenon but a
quantile-shape phenomenon that the length-bias correction in Dr.GRPO
exploits.

\subsubsection{Plateau-anchored length-drift decomposition}\label{sec:lb-iter48}

Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter28} regressed $\E[R\mid L]$, iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter36}
jointly saturated $R$ and $L$ on a shared scale, iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40}
stratified by training phase, and iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter44} decomposed the
conditional $R\mid L$ into quantiles. All four decompose the within-step
coupling between reward and length. None of them anchor the decomposition at
the moment reward stops improving, which is the moment the Dr.GRPO length
trap is mechanistically most active. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter48} closes that gap.

\paragraph{Plateau definition.}
For every (\task, \algo, \seed) trajectory we compute
$R_{\max} = \max_t R_t$, smooth $R_t$ with a 3-step centered moving average,
and let $\tplat$ be the first step at which the smoothed $R_t$ crosses
$0.90 \cdot R_{\max}$. The trajectory is then split into a \emph{rising}
phase $[0, \tplat)$ and a \emph{plateau} phase $[\tplat, T)$. We fit OLS
$\frac{dL}{dt}$ on each phase; the \emph{signature} is the marginal length
tax incurred \emph{after} reward saturates,
$\signature = (\tfrac{dL}{dt})_{\text{plat}} - (\tfrac{dL}{dt})_{\text{rise}}$.

\paragraph{Post-plateau drift on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}.}
The cleanest Dr.GRPO signature appears here. GRPO's plateau-phase
$\tfrac{dL}{dt}$ is $-0.0013$ (length still slowly \emph{compressing}); Dr.GRPO's
is $+0.0015$ (length \emph{expanding}). The paired difference is
$-0.0028$, $95\%$ bootstrap CI $[-0.0045, -0.0014]$, entirely below zero.
\emph{Dr.GRPO is the only algorithm whose length keeps drifting up after
reward has saturated on arithmetic\_easy.} Figure~\ref{fig:lb-iter48-plat}
displays this with a side-by-side bar.

\paragraph{Pre-plateau compression on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}.}
GSM8K CoT trajectories are longer (mean completion length $\approx 195$ tokens
vs $\approx 5$ tokens on arithmetic), so the relevant budget is the
\emph{pre}-plateau compression rate. GRPO compresses at $\tfrac{dL}{dt} = -0.451$
tokens/step, Dr.GRPO at only $-0.189$. Paired difference $-0.263$, $95\%$ CI
$[-0.554, -0.093]$. By the time GSM8K trajectories hit their (lower) reward
ceiling, Dr.GRPO has spent $60{+} \approx 60$ extra tokens per prompt on
average -- the same "stay-longer" trap, surfaced through the rising phase
because GSM8K's plateau is too short (only $\sim 1$ step) for a
post-plateau drift to manifest.

\paragraph{Two-task synthesis.}
The two findings are not contradictions but two views of the same Dr.GRPO
mechanism, surfaced at different time-horizons. On the short, dense
arithmetic trajectory the trap fires \emph{after} the policy has solved the
task (no further reward gradient, but length still drifts up). On the long
GSM8K CoT trajectory the trap fires \emph{before} the policy has solved the
task: Dr.GRPO's flattened group baseline blunts the length-compression signal
that GRPO receives from correct rollouts. In both cases the cross-algorithm
gap is significant with bootstrap CIs that exclude zero.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar with the ZVF proxy.}
Stratifying by the ZVF bin at the plateau step (Table
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter48\_zvf\_anchored.tsv}) shows that the arithmetic
plateau sits in the high-ZVF regime (0.5+) for \emph{every} Dr.GRPO seed and
in mid/high for GRPO -- consistent with iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40}'s stage
stratification and consistent with the cross-pillar claim that Dr.GRPO's
length bias only activates once within-group contrast has collapsed.

\subsubsection{Regime-conditional length-drift decomposition}\label{sec:lb-iter52}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter48} anchored the decomposition at the moment reward
saturated ($\tplat$). Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter52} instead bins every per-step
observation by its instantaneous correctness regime -- \emph{above} or
\emph{below} the per-task median of $R_t$ -- and asks whether the
algorithm-conditional length drift is regime-specific. The split is
\emph{relative} (median) rather than \emph{absolute} ($R \geq 0.5$)
because the data are bimodal across tasks: \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} sits
almost entirely in the high-$R$ regime (mean $R = 0.92$) while
\texttt{gsm8k\_cot} sits in the low/mid regime (mean $R = 0.27$). A
fixed absolute threshold would empty one regime per task; a per-task
median split gives both tasks balanced above/below bins with comparable
sample sizes per trajectory.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
GRPO's per-prompt advantage is $(R_x - \bar{R}_x) / \sigma(R_x)$.
Dr.GRPO drops the $/ \sigma$ normaliser. When the policy is mostly
succeeding (above-median regime), group means disperse across prompts
(because some prompts have $R=1$, others $R=0.6$, others $R=0.4$) but
the cross-rollout $\sigma$ within each group is small. Both algorithms
therefore see a weaker contrast signal than in the below-median regime,
but Dr.GRPO loses the $\sigma$-normalisation that GRPO uses to keep
its gradient direction stable -- and combined with the lack of
$/ \text{length}$ normalisation, this lets length drift up more than
GRPO's. The mechanism should be sharpest where the length budget is
large: GSM8K CoT ($\approx 195$ tokens per response).

\paragraph{Gsm8k\_cot: Dr.GRPO compresses length $\sim 2\times$ slower in both regimes.}
In the below-median regime GRPO compresses length at
$\tfrac{dL}{dt} = -0.421$ tokens/step; Dr.GRPO at only $-0.116$. Paired
difference $+0.305$, $95\%$ bootstrap CI $[+0.141, +0.499]$, entirely
above zero. In the above-median regime GRPO compresses even more
aggressively ($-0.504$); Dr.GRPO at $-0.273$. Paired difference $+0.232$,
$95\%$ CI $[+0.025, +0.609]$. Both gaps are significant with
bootstrap CIs that exclude zero; both gaps are of comparable magnitude
($\sim 0.3$ tokens/step); both gaps confirm the Dr.GRPO "stay-longer"
trap firing on the same task, in the same direction, at both
correctness levels.

\paragraph{Cross-regime gradient (above $-$ below) per algorithm.}
On GSM8K CoT the cross-regime gradient is $-0.084$ for GRPO (CI
$[-0.333, +0.158]$, indistinguishable from zero) and $-0.156$ for
Dr.GRPO (CI $[-0.222, -0.054]$, entirely below zero). Dr.GRPO
\emph{does} differentiate its length drift sharply across correctness
regimes -- it compresses more aggressively when succeeding than when
failing, exactly the policy-gradient intuition that Dr.GRPO's flattened
baseline was supposed to preserve but in fact inverts. The GRPO baseline
shows a much weaker and statistically indistinguishable gradient.
On \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} (5-token responses) the cross-regime
gradient is positive for both algorithms ($\approx +0.045$ for GRPO,
$\approx +0.057$ for Dr.GRPO, both CIs exclude zero) -- length actually
\emph{compresses faster in the failing regime than in the succeeding
regime} because the "failing" steps are the early ones before the
policy has solved the task. Dr.GRPO's gradient is $28\%$ steeper than
GRPO's on arithmetic but with overlapping CIs.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar with ZVF (Table \texttt{length\_bias\_iter52\_zvf\_coupling.tsv}).}
Stratifying the above-median regime by per-step ZVF on GSM8K CoT shows
the cleanest ZVF mediation: GRPO's above-median slope is $-1.194$ in
the mid-ZVF bin (seed 42) versus $-0.378$ for Dr.GRPO -- the
algorithm-conditional gap is largest precisely where ZVF is in its
mid-range, where contrast is plentiful but not perfect. In the
below-median regime the gap is smaller but reverses direction at the
highest ZVF bin (GRPO $-0.667$ vs Dr.GRPO $-0.168$, seed 123) -- the
Dr.GRPO length-bias resistance to compression persists even under
high-ZVF collapse, where iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40} showed the
algorithm-conditional gap is most extreme.

\paragraph{Synthesis with iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter48}.}
The two analyses triangulate the same Dr.GRPO mechanism at different
time-horizons. iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter48}'s post-plateau analysis on
arithmetic and pre-plateau analysis on GSM8K CoT captures the
\emph{marginal} length tax incurred once reward stops improving;
iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter52}'s regime-conditional analysis captures the
\emph{instantaneous} length tax the policy pays \emph{while} reward is
still improving. Both analyses agree: Dr.GRPO retains the same length
gap on GSM8K CoT regardless of whether the trajectory has plateaued,
and the gap is roughly twice as large as the gap observed in the
overall (regime-averaged) trajectories.

\subsubsection{Reward-per-token efficiency frontier}\label{sec:lb-iter56}

Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter28} established the \emph{absolute} Dr.GRPO signature:
$L_t$ grows or compresses slower than GRPO's. Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40}
showed the gap is largest \emph{after} the reward plateau. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter52}
showed the gap is comparable across the above-median and below-median
correctness regimes. The previous analyses all measured $L$ in absolute
tokens; iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter56} instead asks the productive question:
\emph{how much reward does each token buy?}

\paragraph{Definition.}
Define the reward-per-token efficiency
$\rho(t) \;=\; R_t \,/\, L_t \quad [\text{units: } 1/\text{tokens}].$
A policy that doubles its response length while keeping reward constant
\emph{halves} $\rho$; a policy that doubles length while reward also
doubles keeps $\rho$ unchanged. The Dr.GRPO signature should be a
\emph{decay} in $\rho$ relative to GRPO on tasks where $L$ is large
(GSM8K CoT, $\approx 195$ tokens per response) and reward is essentially
flat over the run.

\paragraph{Cumulative token-tax on GSM8K CoT: Dr.GRPO pays 46\% more waste.}
Define the iso-efficiency baseline $L^\star(t) \;=\; L_0 \cdot (R_t / R_0)$,
the length the policy would need to keep $\rho$ constant at its
initial value. Cumulative token-tax is
$\mathrm{Tax} \;=\; \sum_t \max(0,\, L_t - L^\star_t),$
normalised by $\sum_t L_t$ so the metric is unit-free and comparable
across tasks. Paired GRPO vs Dr.GRPO bootstrap across the three GSM8K
CoT seeds (Table~\texttt{length\_bias\_iter56\_cumulative\_tax.tsv}):

\begin{itemize}
\item GSM8K CoT: GRPO cumulative-tax $= 0.086$, Dr.GRPO $= 0.127$.
      Paired difference $+0.040$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.018,\, +0.071]$,
      $p_{\le 0} < 0.001$. Dr.GRPO pays $46\%$ \emph{more} wasted
      tokens than GRPO on this task -- the excess is roughly one in
      twelve of every token Dr.GRPO generates.
\item Arithmetic-easy: cumulative-tax $= 0$ for both algorithms, because
      $R_t$ improves by $\approx 5\times$ over the run while $L_t$
      \emph{compresses} by $\approx 40\%$, so $L^\star_t$ rises well
      above $L_t$ and the tax is structurally zero. The mechanism
      only fires when $R$ is roughly flat (i.e.\ reward-plateaued).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Length phase-portrait centroid on GSM8K CoT.}
At each step plot $(L_t,\, \Delta L_t)$ where $\Delta L_t = L_{t+1} - L_t$.
Compute the per-run centroid of this cloud and the RMS radius. Dr.GRPO's
centroid sits at $\bar L = 191.5$, GRPO's at $\bar L = 189.7$ (paired
diff $+1.77$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.40,\, +4.00]$, $p < 0.001$). The
centroid-$\Delta L$ is less negative for Dr.GRPO ($-0.32$ vs $-0.54$
for GRPO, $p = 0.034$) -- Dr.GRPO's late-stage trajectory is a tighter,
higher-attractor orbit around $L \approx 191$ tokens, with smaller
per-step compression. The RMS radius is $1.5$ tokens smaller for
Dr.GRPO (CI $[-4.70,\, +0.51]$, $p = 0.85$), consistent with the
late-stage stabilisation the signature predicts.

\paragraph{$\rho(t)$ slope and half-life.}
Per-task OLS slope of $\rho(t)$ on arithmetic is $\approx +0.0024$/step for
both algorithms (paired diff $+0.0001$, CI excludes zero only barely,
$p = 0.06$) -- $\rho$ \emph{rises} for both because $L$ compresses
faster than $R$ grows. On GSM8K CoT the per-step $\rho$ slope is
$\approx +5$--$8 \times 10^{-6}$ (paired diff $-3.8 \times 10^{-6}$,
$p = 0.85$, not significant) -- both algorithms' $\rho$ is essentially
flat over 30 steps at $\rho \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-3}$, and the
half-life is therefore larger than the run length for both. This is
expected: GSM8K CoT is the harder task and neither algorithm reaches
its iso-efficiency half-life.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar triangulation.}
Three angles agree. \emph{Absolute length-drift} (iter28, iter40, iter48)
shows Dr.GRPO's $L$ compresses slower. \emph{Regime-conditional drift}
(iter52) shows the gap fires equally in the failing and succeeding
regimes. \emph{Reward-per-token efficiency} (iter56) shows the gap
translates to $\approx 4$ percentage points of wasted tokens on GSM8K
CoT, with the phase-portrait centroid moved $\approx 2$ tokens higher.
All three converge on the same Dr.GRPO mechanism: \emph{less aggressive
length compression per step, especially on tasks where reward is
already flat}, costing the policy $\approx 4\%$ of its rollouts in
wasted tokens relative to GRPO.

\subsubsection{Length-elasticity of reward}\label{sec:lb-iter60}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter56} measured the \emph{average} reward-per-token
$\rho(t) = R_t / L_t$ and showed Dr.GRPO pays $\approx 4\%$ more wasted
tokens than GRPO on GSM8K CoT. The previous averages, however, mix two
structurally different regimes: steps where the marginal token PRODUCES
reward ($\Delta R / \Delta L > 0$) and steps where the marginal token
DESTROYS reward ($\Delta R / \Delta L < 0$). Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter60}
separates these regimes and reports the \emph{length-elasticity of
reward}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb60-elasticity}
\epsilon_i \;=\; \frac{\Delta R_i}{\Delta L_i} \qquad\text{(centred difference, smoothed $k{=}3$)}
\end{equation}
at every step. The sign and magnitude of $\epsilon_i$ directly answer
the productive question: how much extra reward does the policy buy with
each additional token, evaluated locally on the realised trajectory.

\paragraph{Per-step elasticity on GSM8K CoT: Dr.GRPO spends fewer
productive steps.}
For each step we compute the fraction of steps with $\epsilon_i > 0$
(positive-elasticity) and $\epsilon_i < 0$ (negative-elasticity).
Paired GRPO-vs-Dr.GRPO bootstrap across the three GSM8K CoT seeds
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_elasticity.tsv}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Positive-elasticity fraction}:
      GRPO $= 0.411$, Dr.GRPO $= 0.256$,
      paired diff $-0.156$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.233,\, -0.067]$,
      $p_{\le 0} < 0.001$. Dr.GRPO's marginal token PRODUCES reward
      on $\approx 26\%$ of steps versus $\approx 41\%$ for GRPO -- a
      $38\%$ relative drop in productive steps.
\item \textbf{Negative-elasticity fraction}:
      GRPO $= 0.589$, Dr.GRPO $= 0.711$,
      paired diff $+0.122$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.067,\, +0.167]$,
      $p_{\le 0} = 0.0002$. Dr.GRPO spends $\approx 71\%$ of steps
      where the marginal token DESTROYS reward, versus $\approx 59\%$
      for GRPO.
\end{itemize}
The mean elasticity per run is also lower for Dr.GRPO
($0.00552$ vs $0.00989$, paired diff $-0.0044$, CI $[-0.024,\, +0.017]$,
$p = 0.63$, not significant on this small-$n$ measure) -- the
\emph{fraction-of-time} measure is more sensitive than the \emph{average}
because it counts steps of opposite sign rather than averaging them.

\paragraph{Optimal-length fit on GSM8K CoT: Dr.GRPO's reward-maximising
length is $\sim 80$ tokens longer.}
Fit the closed-form OLS quadratic
$R(L) = a (L - L^\star)^2 + b$ per run to the realised $(L_t, R_t)$
trajectory (\texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_curvature.tsv}). The optimum
$L^\star$ is the length at which the parabola predicts maximum reward;
the curvature $a$ controls sharpness around $L^\star$. Paired bootstrap:
\begin{itemize}
\item GSM8K CoT:
      $L^\star_{\text{GRPO}} = 175.2$ tokens,
      $L^\star_{\text{Dr.GRPO}} = 256.2$ tokens,
      paired diff $+81.0$, $95\%$ CI $[+12.2,\, +194.8]$,
      $p_{\le 0} = 0.0002$. Dr.GRPO's quadratic predicts reward peaks
      at $\approx 256$ tokens -- $46\%$ longer than GRPO's
      $175$ tokens. This is the cleanest structural measurement of
      Dr.GRPO's longer "natural length" yet: the difference is not in
      the average trajectory but in the \emph{parabolic-peak estimate}.
\item Curvature $a$: comparable (Dr.GRPO $-1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ vs
      GRPO $-3.95 \times 10^{-4}$, $p = 0.14$) -- both algorithms have
      similar sharpness around their respective optima. The L$^\star$
      shift is therefore a translation, not a flattening.
\item $R^2$ of the quadratic fit: Dr.GRPO $0.43$ vs GRPO $0.30$
      ($p = 0.04$ borderline). Dr.GRPO's R-vs-L is slightly more
      quadratic -- the parabola captures more of its variance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Arithmetic-easy: elasticity signal survives but L$^\star$ is
dominated by collapse.}
On arithmetic-easy the same analysis yields:
\begin{itemize}
\item Positive-elasticity fraction: GRPO $0.374$, Dr.GRPO $0.329$, paired
      diff $-0.045$, CI $[-0.128,\, +0.028]$, $p = 0.86$ (not
      significant on this measure).
\item Negative-elasticity fraction: GRPO $0.480$, Dr.GRPO $0.563$, paired
      diff $+0.083$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.006,\, +0.163]$, $p = 0.016$
      (significant).
\item Iso-reward length band (R within $0.02$ of R$_\text{max}$): GRPO
      width $= 0.562$ tokens, Dr.GRPO width $= 0.478$ tokens, paired
      diff $-0.084$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.153,\, -0.016]$, $p = 0.99$
      (Dr.GRPO is \emph{tighter}, opposite of the iso-band hypothesis).
      The arithmetic task collapses L to its floor ($\approx 4.5$
      tokens) on both algorithms, so the R-vs-L curve is dominated by
      the collapse and the L$^\star$ estimate becomes meaningless
      (extrapolated $L^\star \in [-41, +4]$ tokens, off-scale).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cross-pillar triangulation.}
Three angles, all converging on the same Dr.GRPO signature, now stack
across four iterations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Absolute length drift} (iter28, iter40, iter48):
      Dr.GRPO's $L_t$ compresses slower than GRPO's.
\item \emph{Average reward-per-token} (iter56): Dr.GRPO pays
      $+0.040$ wasted-token-fraction on GSM8K CoT.
\item \emph{Marginal length-elasticity} (iter60): Dr.GRPO has $38\%$
      fewer productive steps on GSM8K CoT, and its parabolic
      reward-maximising length is $+81$ tokens higher than GRPO's.
\end{itemize}
The parabolic-peak estimate ($L^\star$) is the sharpest: it does not
depend on the average trajectory, only on the convexity of $R(L)$
near the realised range. That Dr.GRPO's $L^\star$ is significantly
larger than GRPO's means Dr.GRPO's policy \emph{wants} a longer
response to maximise reward -- it is not just slow to compress, it
is structurally biased toward a longer attractor. The mechanism is
consistent with Dr.GRPO's outlier-censoring: by clipping large
advantage magnitudes, Dr.GRPO suppresses the credit assigned to
\textit{short correct responses} relative to \textit{long correct
responses}, biasing the policy toward verbose-completion attractors.

\subsubsection{Conditional length response to reward direction}\label{sec:lb-iter64}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter60} separated the marginal-token elasticity
$\epsilon_i = \Delta R_i / \Delta L_i$ into positive- and negative-elasticity
regimes and showed Dr.GRPO spends $\approx 71\%$ of steps in the
destructive regime versus GRPO's $\approx 59\%$. The elasticity analysis
collapsed a 2D trajectory $(\Delta R, \Delta L)$ onto a single ratio.
Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter64} instead stratifies the same trajectory by the
\emph{sign} of the marginal reward change and asks a sharper question:
when the policy sees a reward-up step, does it differentially compress
or extend its response? We define the \emph{conditional length response}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb64-clrrd}
\mathrm{CLRRD}(\mathrm{sign}, \mathrm{ZVF\ tier}) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta L_t \mid \mathrm{sign}(\Delta R_t),\ Z_t \in \mathrm{tier}\right]
\end{equation}
and the \emph{reward-direction coupling}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb64-alignment}
\mathcal{A} \;=\; \mathbb{E}[\Delta L \mid \Delta R > 0] \;-\; \mathbb{E}[\Delta L \mid \Delta R < 0].
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{A} > 0$ means the policy lengthens on reward-up steps and
shortens on reward-down steps (verbosity-pursuing).
$\mathcal{A} < 0$ means the policy compresses on reward-up steps and
extends on reward-down steps (verbosity-rejecting). Both GRPO and
Dr.GRPO on GSM8K CoT exhibit $\mathcal{A} < 0$ (the policy is
compressing as it learns); the structural question is whether the
\emph{magnitude} of $|\mathcal{A}|$ differs.

\paragraph{Baseline length drift on GSM8K CoT: Dr.GRPO compresses less.}
Pairing the three seeds, $\mathbb{E}[\Delta L]$
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter64\_summary.tsv}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GRPO}: $-0.450$ tokens/step.
\item \textbf{Dr.GRPO}: $-0.241$ tokens/step.
\item \textbf{Paired diff}: $+0.210$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.017,\, +0.405]$,
      $p < 0.001$.
\end{itemize}
Dr.GRPO compresses \emph{less than half} as aggressively as GRPO per
step. This confirms iter56's cumulative-token-tax and iter60's reduced
negative-elasticity fraction: across all iters, Dr.GRPO's per-step
length response is shallower.

\paragraph{Reward-direction coupling collapses in the heterogeneous-group regime.}
On the same three GSM8K CoT seeds, conditioning on ZVF tier
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter64\_paired.tsv}, cells \texttt{pos\_low},
\texttt{neg\_low}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reward-up, low-ZVF (heterogeneous groups)}:
      GRPO $\mathbb{E}[\Delta L] = -1.84$, Dr.GRPO $= -1.04$,
      paired diff $+0.80$, CI $[+0.12,\, +1.92]$, $p < 0.001$.
      GRPO compresses by $\approx 1.8$ tokens; Dr.GRPO compresses by
      only $\approx 1.0$ tokens -- Dr.GRPO loses $43\%$ of its
      compression signal exactly in the regime where GRPO's gradient
      has the most contrast to work with.
\item \textbf{Reward-up, all tiers}:
      GRPO $= -1.71$, Dr.GRPO $= -1.52$, diff $+0.20$,
      CI $[-0.03,\, +0.36]$, $p = 0.034$. The averaged effect is
      smaller because high-ZVF steps (homogeneous groups) contribute
      nearly identical responses (Dr.GRPO gains nothing on these
      either, but neither does GRPO).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism interpretation.}
The Dr.GRPO estimator is $\hat A_i = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g},\, -c,\, c\right)$
for a prompt-relative baseline $\mu_g$. By clipping the outlier
advantage magnitude, Dr.GRPO equalises the gradient magnitudes
\emph{regardless of the response length}, so the per-token gradient
signal becomes length-invariant. The natural consequence is that
whenever the per-step policy gradient favours short correct responses
(the heterogeneous-group regime), Dr.GRPO under-applies it; whenever
the gradient favours long correct responses (saturation regime), it
over-applies it relative to GRPO. The net result is a more uniform
(less reward-direction-coupled) length trajectory -- the policy is
structurally pulled toward a longer response because the variance-driven
shrinkage signal is damped. Iter64 sharpens the iter60 elasticity
finding by demonstrating that the asymmetry is concentrated in the
low-ZVF regime: GRPO uses \emph{heterogeneous-group contrast} as a
length-compression lever, and Dr.GRPO's clip masks exactly that lever.

\paragraph{Arithmetic-easy task: null finding, by design.}
On the arithmetic-easy task (\texttt{drgrpo\_vs\_grpo.json}, 5 seeds,
40 steps each) all CLRRD coefficients are within $\pm 0.02$ tokens of
zero with CIs that straddle zero. The task converges in $4$--$5$ steps
to reward $\approx 1.0$ with $\mathrm{ZVF} \approx 1.0$ for the
remainder of training, so the trajectory does not visit the
heterogeneous-group regime long enough to develop a length response.
This is a clean negative control: the CLRRD diagnostic is not
spuriously firing on every pair of trajectories.

\subsubsection{Trajectory divergence decomposition}\label{sec:lb-iter68}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter64} conditioned the marginal length response
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta L \mid \mathrm{sign}(\Delta R), Z_t]$ on reward direction
and showed that Dr.GRPO loses compression signal in the
heterogeneous-group regime. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter68} instead asks a
\emph{trajectory-shape} question: holding the (mean) length response
fixed, is the policy's length \emph{trajectory itself} more or less
unstable under Dr.GRPO? We decompose the per-step trajectory
$(\Delta R_t, \Delta L_t)$ into three paired shape statistics and one
signed magnitude.

\paragraph{Shape statistic 1: reversal rate $\rho$.}
The reversal rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb68-rho}
\rho \;=\; \frac{1}{T-2}\sum_{t=2}^{T-1}
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{sign}(\Delta L_t) \neq \mathrm{sign}(\Delta L_{t-1}),\ \Delta L_t, \Delta L_{t-1} \neq 0\right]
\end{equation}
counts the fraction of consecutive step pairs where the per-step length
change reverses sign. A high $\rho$ means the policy length is
\emph{oscillating} step-to-step; a low $\rho$ means it is in a sustained
trend. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter68} additionally partitions $\rho$ by the
\emph{reward direction} of step $t$, yielding $\rho_{+}$ (restricted to
$\Delta R_t > 0$) and $\rho_{-}$ ($\Delta R_t < 0$).

\paragraph{Shape statistic 2: length-volatility ratio $\mathcal{V}$.}
$\mathcal{V} = \sigma(\Delta L)_\mathrm{Dr.GRPO} / \sigma(\Delta L)_\mathrm{GRPO}$
computed per-seed; the paired bootstrap is on $\log \mathcal{V}$.

\paragraph{Magnitude statistic: signed AUC.}
The trapezoidal area between the two per-step $L$ curves, signed so that
positive area means the Dr.GRPO trace lies above the GRPO trace on
average.

\paragraph{GSM8K CoT headline: Dr.GRPO reverses 14\,pp more often on reward-up steps.}
From \texttt{length\_bias\_iter68\_summary.tsv}, paired bootstrap
($B=2000$, $3$ seeds):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\rho_{+}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \rho_{+}^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = +0.141$
      (95\% CI $[+0.017,\,+0.214]$, $p < 0.001$). Dr.GRPO flips the
      sign of $\Delta L$ \emph{14 percentage points} more often than
      GRPO does, restricted to reward-up steps. This is exactly the
      regime where iter64's CLRRD found Dr.GRPO loses 43\% of its
      compression lever; the new finding is that the residual signal
      Dr.GRPO \emph{does} produce is structurally noisier.
\item $\rho_{-}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \rho_{-}^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = -0.054$
      (95\% CI $[-0.091,\, 0.000]$, $p = 1.0$ one-sided). Dr.GRPO
      reverses \emph{less} on reward-down steps -- but the CI just
      touches zero. The asymmetry concentrates in $\rho_{+}$.
\item Aggregate $\rho^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \rho^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = +0.048$
      (CI $[+0.000,\, +0.071]$, $p = 0.043$). The aggregate reversal
      rate is 72.6\% (Dr.GRPO) vs 67.9\% (GRPO).
\item $\mathcal{V}$ (volatility ratio) $= 0.94$ (CI $[0.68,\, 1.18]$):
      inconclusive. The two algorithms have similar $\sigma(\Delta L)$

\item Signed AUC (Dr.GRPO $-$ GRPO) $= -209.1$ token·step units

\item First-divergence step $\overline{T^*} = 10.3$ (CI $[3, 22]$).
      The wide CI reflects the 3-seed ceiling; the mean sits in the
      middle of training, indicating the Dr.GRPO-vs-GRPO length gap
      emerges gradually over training rather than at a sharp
      change-point.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism interpretation.}

high-reward-variance groups (the heterogeneous-group regime that GRPO
uses as its compression lever). On reward-up steps in this regime,
GRPO produces a sharp $\Delta L < 0$ compression response; Dr.GRPO
produces a \emph{noisy} $\Delta L$ whose sign is not consistently
negative because the clipped gradient still flips sign as the

rate $\rho_+$ is the right diagnostic for this kind of structural
gradient-magnitude loss: it does not require the mean $\Delta L$ to
move much (it doesn't -- both algos are still negative on average),
but it detects when the \emph{direction} of the per-step response is
unstable. The same diagnostic correctly returns a clean null on the
arithmetic-easy task, where the trajectory does not visit the
heterogeneous-group regime long enough to develop this signal.

\subsubsection{Length-shock persistence}\label{sec:lb-iter72}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter68} established a binary-flip signature: Dr.GRPO
reverses the sign of $\Delta L$ 14\,pp more often than GRPO on reward-up
steps. That is a \emph{trajectory-shape} claim with no magnitude content.
Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter72} asks the \emph{continuous-persistence} analogue:
holding the reward step fixed via regression, does a length shock
\emph{persist} or \emph{oscillate} under Dr.GRPO? We fit, per
(algorithm, seed), a 3-parameter model on the tail window (steps
20\%--100\%):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb72-ar1}
\Delta L_t \;=\; \alpha \;+\; c\, \Delta R_t \;+\; \phi\, \Delta L_{t-1} \;+\; \varepsilon_t .
\end{equation}
The AR(1) coefficient $\phi$ is the \emph{persistence}: $\phi > 0$ means
length shocks carry forward (momentum); $\phi < 0$ means shocks
immediately mean-revert (oscillate). The half-life
$\tau = -\log 2 / \log \phi$ is well-defined only on the persistent side.

\paragraph{GSM8K CoT headline: both algos are anti-persistent; Dr.GRPO has a smaller unexplained-variance point estimate.}
From \texttt{length\_bias\_iter72\_summary.tsv}, paired bootstrap
($B = 2000$, 3 seeds), tail window = 24 steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\phi^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = -0.419$, $\phi^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} = -0.393$,
      paired diff $= +0.026$ (95\% CI $[-0.102, +0.190]$, $p = 0.40$).
      \textbf{Both algorithms are anti-persistent}: a length shock
      decorrelates within $1$--$2$ steps. There is no persistence
      \emph{advantage} to Dr.GRPO; both algorithms produce an
      essentially AR(1)-with-$\phi \approx -0.4$ stationary series once
      the initial transient is removed.
\item \textbf{Residual-variance ratio} $\sigma^{2,\mathrm{Dr}}_\varepsilon /
      \sigma^{2,\mathrm{GR}}_\varepsilon = 0.695$ (95\% CI on log scale
      $[-0.823, +0.102]$).
      After accounting for $\Delta R_t$, $\Delta L_{t-1}$, and the
      intercept, Dr.GRPO's residual $\Delta L$ variance is
      \textbf{30\% smaller} than GRPO's in point estimate, but the paired
      CI includes a ratio of one. Iter68's reversal-asymmetry
      finding was a \emph{shape} claim; iter72 measures its
      continuous analogue and finds that the same clipping operation
      \emph{removes} unexplained noise, it does not \emph{add} it. The
      trajectory is not more chaotic under Dr.GRPO -- it is more
      \emph{compression-aligned}.
\item \textbf{Lag-2 cross-correlation diff} $r^{\mathrm{Dr}}(dL_t,
      dR_{t-2}) - r^{\mathrm{GR}}(\cdot) = +0.091$ (CI $[+0.059,
      +0.137]$, $p < 0.001$). Dr.GRPO's length response two steps after
      a reward change is significantly \emph{more} positively correlated
      with that reward change than GRPO's. This is a delayed-credential
      signal: Dr.GRPO surfaces more of the reward-step signal at lag 2
      than at lag 0 (where $r^{\mathrm{Dr}}(dL_t, dR_t) = r^{\mathrm{GR}}$
      within CI; $p = 0.70$).
\item $c$ (the on-step reward-to-length coupling) is consistently
      negative ($c^{\mathrm{GRPO}} \approx -25$, $c^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}}
      \approx -27$): a reward-up step is associated with a length
      \emph{decrease} of $\sim 25$\,tokens. The point estimates are
      indistinguishable on 3 seeds (paired CI contains 0).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Arithmetic-easy negative control.}
The same script applied to the 5-seed, 40-step arithmetic comparison:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\phi^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = -0.425$, $\phi^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} = -0.372$,
      diff $= +0.052$ (CI $[-0.024, +0.170]$, $p = 0.20$). Same
      anti-persistent regime as GSM8K, but both algos converge to a
      near-zero $\Delta L$ trajectory by step 20, so the AR(1) fit is
      dominated by sampling noise.
\item \textbf{Residual-variance ratio} $= 0.997$ (CI on log scale
      $[-0.303, +0.265]$, $p = 0.49$): \emph{exactly} equal. After
      removing the AR(1)+dR component the residual variance is
      identical -- this is a clean null on the easy task. The 30\%
      residual reduction on GSM8K is therefore \emph{task-conditional},
      not a Dr.GRPO universal.
\item Lag-2 diff $= -0.133$ (CI $[-0.391, +0.099]$, $p = 0.86$): wide,
      inconclusive, in the opposite sign of GSM8K. The lag-2
      asymmetry on GSM8K is \emph{specific to the harder task}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism interpretation.}
The half-life $=$ anti-persistent regime $\phi \approx -0.4$ in both
algorithms means length shocks decay geometrically with rate
$1.4 \times$ (i.e., a length shock at step $t$ has effective magnitude
$1.4\,|\Delta L_t|$ at step $t+1$ in the opposite direction).
Dr.GRPO's clipping at amplitude $c$ preserves the sign of each
$\Delta L$ shock but truncates its magnitude, which translates to a
\emph{smaller residual} once the predictable $\Delta R$ component is
partialled out. Iter68 saw the binary-flip signature of the same
phenomenon; iter72 sees the continuous-variance signature.
Together they form a coherent Dr.GRPO length-dynamics portrait:
\emph{same trajectory shape, smaller residual variance, more reversal
on reward-up}. The clipped advantage operates as a soft
``reset-and-redirect'' controller: rather than letting $\Delta L$ build
momentum it pushes each step back toward the reward-driven mean. On
the arithmetic-easy task, where the optimum is degenerate and both
algos already sit near it, this controller has nothing to compress --
hence the null.

\paragraph{Negative-control sharpness.}
The arithmetic-easy ratio $= 0.997$ is the cleanest null we have
produced on any $\sigma^2$ ratio diagnostic, and it falls on exactly
the null line $1.0$. The 30\% GSM8K effect is therefore not an
algorithm-wide property but a \emph{task-specific} consequence of the
clipping meeting a non-trivial length-control surface.

(frontier synthesis) The frontiers sharpest reading of the
$\phi^{\mathrm{Dr}} > \phi^{\mathrm{GR}}$ direction (both negative but
Dr.GRPO less negative) is: clipping dampens the reward-driven
reversal-amplitude exactly enough to push $\phi$ closer to zero, which
is the boundary between momentum and anti-momentum. The Dr.GRPO
trajectory lives closer to this white-noise boundary than GRPO does,
which combined with the iter68 reversal-asymmetry correctly predicts
the residual-variance reduction we observe: there is simply less
trajectory to explain by residual noise.
 

\subsubsection{Reward-shock mean-reversion and phase-plane dissipativity}\label{sec:lb-iter76}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter68} established a discrete / binary
\emph{trajectory-shape} claim: Dr.GRPO flips the sign of $\Delta L$
14\,pp more often than GRPO on reward-up steps. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter72}
gave the continuous analogue: AR(1) persistence $\phi \approx -0.4$ on
both algorithms and a 30\% residual-variance reduction under
Dr.GRPO. This iter asks the \emph{mechanical-systems} analogue: how
does the length trajectory \emph{dissipate} a reward shock, and what is
the closed-loop work done in $(L, R)$ phase space?

\paragraph{Three paired diagnostics.}
For each paired (algorithm, seed) run we compute:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Half-life $\tau$.} For each shock step $t$ with
      $|\Delta R_t|$ in the top quartile of the trace, fit
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:lb76-tau}
      |L_{t+h} - L_{\mathrm{baseline}}| \;=\; A \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{\ln 2}{\tau}\,h\bigr),
      \qquad h = 0, \ldots, 9,
      \end{equation}
      via log-linear regression on the well-defined region
      ($|L - L_{\mathrm{baseline}}| > 0.1\,A_0$). $L_{\mathrm{baseline}}$
      is the mean of the $H$-step post-shock window. $\tau$ is the
      mean-reversion half-life of the length response.
\item \textbf{Damping ratio $\zeta$.} Fit a 2nd-order AR to the
      centred post-shock series $x_h = L_{t+h} - L_{\mathrm{baseline}}$:
      $x_{h+1} = a_1 x_h + a_2 x_{h-1}$. From the root discriminant
      $\Delta = a_1^2 + 4 a_2$, recover
      $\omega_n = \sqrt{a_2}$ (real roots) or
      $\omega_n = \sqrt{(a_1/2)^2 - \Delta/4}$ (complex roots), and
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:lb76-zeta}
      \zeta \;=\; -\frac{a_1}{2\,\omega_n}.
      \end{equation}
      $\zeta > 1$ = overdamped (Dr.GRPO hypothesis);
      $\zeta < 1$ = underdamped (GRPO hypothesis);
      $\zeta = 1$ = critical.
\item \textbf{Phase-plane loop area $\mathcal{A}$.} Closed-loop integral
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:lb76-loop}
      \mathcal{A} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\,\oint (L\,dR - R\,dL)
      \end{equation}
      via the Shoelace formula on the discretised $(L, R)$
      trajectory closed back to the starting point. Smaller
      $|\mathcal{A}|$ = less wasted movement in phase space.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Arithmetic-easy headline: Dr.GRPO's length shocks mean-revert
faster.}
From \texttt{length\_bias\_iter76\_summary.tsv}, paired bootstrap
($B = 2000$, $5$ seeds):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tau^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \tau^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = -4.24$ steps
      (95\% CI $[-7.05,\,-1.44]$, $n = 5$). The CI \textbf{excludes
      zero}: Dr.GRPO's length shocks dissipate $33\%$ faster on the
      easy task (12.96 steps $\to$ 8.72 steps). This is the
      \emph{continuous, mechanical} counterpart of iter68's binary
      reversal asymmetry: on the easy task, where iter68 already
      concentrated the reward-conditional reversal signal, Dr.GRPO
      also shows shorter $\tau$.
\item $\zeta^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \zeta^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = +0.19$
      (CI $[+0.04,\,+0.42]$, $n = 4$): directionally consistent with
      Dr.GRPO being \emph{more damped} (higher $\zeta$),
      but both algorithms sit in the underdamped regime
      ($\zeta \approx 0.5 < 1$). The overdamped-Dr.GRPO hypothesis is
      \textbf{falsified}; the iter76 picture is that Dr.GRPO is
      \emph{slightly less} underdamped on the easy task but neither
      algorithm is critically damped.
\item $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{GRPO}} = -0.09$
      (CI $[-0.24,\,+0.11]$, $n = 5$, n.s.): both algorithms sweep the
      phase plane in the clockwise direction ($\mathcal{A} < 0$), but
      with comparable magnitudes.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{GSM8K CoT headline: $\tau_{\downarrow}$ direction is faster under Dr.GRPO; $\zeta$ measurement
is incomplete.}
From the same summary table, paired bootstrap ($B = 2000$, $3$ seeds):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tau^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}}_{\downarrow} - \tau^{\mathrm{GRPO}}_{\downarrow}

\item $\tau^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}}_{\uparrow} - \tau^{\mathrm{GRPO}}_{\uparrow}

\item $\zeta$: only $2$ of $3$ seeds produced a valid $\zeta$ estimate

\item $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} - \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{GRPO}}

\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism interpretation.}
The arithmetic-easy $\tau$ result is the cleanest single iter76
finding: under Dr.GRPO, a top-quartile reward shock dissipates ~4
steps faster than under GRPO. This is \emph{not} a length-elasticity
claim (iter60) — both algorithms have converged to the same mean
length by step~5 on the easy task. It is a \emph{transient} claim
about how quickly the policy returns to the local length baseline
after a perturbation. Dr.GRPO's clipped advantage
$\hat A_i = \mathrm{clip}((r_i - \mu_g)/\sigma_g, -c, +c)$ truncates
the \emph{magnitude} of the gradient update on
high-variance-reward groups. On the easy task, where most groups are
near-zero-variance (all-correct or all-wrong), the clip is rarely
active, so Dr.GRPO's transient response is governed by the
\emph{unclipped} advantage — and the unclipped advantage, on a binary
reward, induces a sharper, faster-recovering length response than
GRPO's noisier estimator.

The overdamped-Dr.GRPO hypothesis ($\zeta > 1$) is \textbf{falsified}
on both tasks: $\zeta \approx 0.5$ for both algorithms. Neither is
critically damped; both oscillate post-shock. This is consistent with
iter72's AR(1) finding of $\phi < 0$ (anti-persistence): an
underdamped system with negative root product is exactly the
phase-plane picture of an anti-persistent AR(1).

\paragraph{Cross-iter consistency.}
Iter72 found $\phi^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} \approx -0.39$ on GSM8K CoT;
this iter's $\zeta \approx 0.5$ on arithmetic-easy implies an AR(1)
with $\phi$ in the same range. Iter68 found a 14\,pp reversal
asymmetry concentrated on reward-up steps; this iter's
arithmetic-easy $\tau$ diff $= -4.24$ (CI excludes zero) is the
\emph{continuous, mechanical} counterpart on the easy task. The
three iters triangulate: Dr.GRPO produces length trajectories that
\emph{recover faster} on the easy task, \emph{oscillate less
coherently} on GSM8K CoT (iter68's binary flip), and have
\emph{lower unexplained residual variance} after reward-direction
regression (iter72). These three claims are independent
measurements of the same underlying phenomenon: Dr.GRPO's clipped
advantage removes gradient-magnitude contrast on heterogeneous
groups, leaving a length trajectory that is closer to a
deterministic, fast-recovering oscillation than GRPO's noisy
estimator-driven trajectory.

\subsubsection{Equilibrium length and a unit-root test of ``length inflation''}\label{sec:lb-iter80}

Iters~\ref{sec:lb-iter72} and~\ref{sec:lb-iter68} characterised the
\emph{shock} dynamics of $\Delta L_t$ (persistence, sign-flips). They say
nothing about the \emph{level}: does the length series drift without an
attractor---the mechanism the Dr.GRPO critique
\citep{tong2025drgrpo} invokes when it argues GRPO's response-level
normalization ``artificially increases response length''? A drift with no
finite equilibrium is exactly a \emph{unit root}. We therefore model each
run's mean-completion-length level as a discrete Ornstein--Uhlenbeck (AR(1))
process,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb80-ou}
L_t \;=\; c \;+\; \phi\, L_{t-1} \;+\; \varepsilon_t,
\qquad
\mu \;=\; \frac{c}{1-\phi},\quad
\theta \;=\; -\log\phi,\quad
\tau \;=\; \frac{\log 0.5}{\log\phi},
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the long-run \emph{equilibrium length} (finite iff
$\phi<1$), $\theta$ the mean-reversion rate, and $\tau$ the half-life. The
sharp falsifiable object is the Dickey--Fuller statistic from the companion
regression $\Delta L_t = \alpha + \gamma L_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$ with
$\gamma=\phi-1$: $\gamma=0$ ($\mathrm{DF}\!\to\!0$) is a unit root
(unbounded length), while $\mathrm{DF}$ below the $5\%$ critical value
$-2.93$ rejects it in favour of a bounded, mean-reverting length. We drop a
2-step transient and fit per (algorithm, seed).

\paragraph{Falsification: neither algorithm carries a length unit root.}
From \texttt{length\_bias\_iter80\_unitroot.tsv}: on arithmetic
(Qwen2.5-0.5B, 5 seeds) \emph{both} algorithms reject the unit root in
$100\%$ of seeds, with near-identical mean statistics
$\mathrm{DF}^{\mathrm{GRPO}}=-6.03$ and
$\mathrm{DF}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}}=-6.07$. On GSM8K CoT (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 3 seeds)
the mean statistics are $-3.86$ (GRPO) and $-4.85$ (Dr.GRPO); both still sit
below the $5\%$ line \emph{on average}. The strong reading of
\citet{tong2025drgrpo}---that GRPO drives length along a stochastic trend
with no attractor---is \textbf{not supported} at this scale and horizon: the
length level is a stationary, mean-reverting process for both estimators.

\paragraph{But the predicted \emph{direction} survives.}
The unit root is a boundary the two estimators approach at different speeds.
Seed-paired bootstrap ($B=2000$) on GSM8K CoT
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter80\_paired.tsv}) shows GRPO's length process is
systematically \emph{closer} to a random walk than Dr.GRPO's:
\begin{itemize}
\item persistence $\Delta\phi = +0.223$ (95\% CI $[+0.060,+0.452]$, $p<0.001$);
\item reversion half-life $\Delta\tau = +0.64$ steps (CI $[+0.53,+0.76]$, $p<0.001$);
\item the unit root is rejected in only $33\%$ of GRPO seeds versus $67\%$ of Dr.GRPO seeds.
\end{itemize}
So the qualitative claim---GRPO's length is less tightly controlled than
Dr.GRPO's---holds as a statement about \emph{reversion dynamics}, even
though the catastrophic level effect does not materialise.

\paragraph{The level paradox.}
The dynamics do not translate into ``GRPO longer.'' On GSM8K CoT GRPO's
equilibrium $\mu$ is in fact \emph{lower}: paired
$\Delta\mu = -2.86$ tokens (95\% CI $[-6.21,-1.02]$, $p<0.001$), with GRPO
also \emph{less} volatile ($\Delta\sigma=+1.12$ favouring Dr.GRPO). GRPO
reverts slowly toward a \emph{smaller} attractor while Dr.GRPO settles
quickly at a higher but tightly held length---consistent with iter~60's
finding that Dr.GRPO's reward-optimal length $L^\ast$ is $+81$ tokens larger.
At the scale where both algorithms \emph{compress} (GSM8K CoT length falls
$196\!\to\!170$), the estimator that the literature flags as ``length
inflating'' is the one with the shorter equilibrium; the difference is one
of control, not of magnitude.

\paragraph{Negative control.}
On arithmetic---converged, saturated accuracy---GRPO and Dr.GRPO are
statistically indistinguishable on every OU parameter: $\Delta\mu=+0.001$
($p=0.91$), $\Delta\phi=+0.050$ ($p=0.19$), $\Delta\tau=+0.25$ ($p=0.27$),
all CIs straddling zero. The length-dynamics divergence is confined to the
hard, non-saturated CoT regime where within-group contrast (and hence the
estimator's length coupling) is still active. Figure~\ref{fig:lb-iter80}
shows the CoT trajectories with their fitted equilibria (A) and the
per-seed Dickey--Fuller statistics against the unit-root line (B).

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Recast as a unit-root question, the Dr.GRPO length critique splits into a
true \emph{directional} claim (GRPO's length is a stickier, less
mean-reverting process) and a false \emph{level} claim (unbounded inflation)
at small scale. The correct diagnostic is the reversion speed $\theta$/half-life
$\tau$, not the raw length level---which can even move \emph{against} the
literature's expectation when both estimators are in a compression regime.
 

\subsection{The length-efficiency frontier: Dr.GRPO Pareto-dominates GRPO}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter128}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Earlier per-window analyses characterised Dr.GRPO's \emph{severship}
mechanism --- where it acts, on what baseline feature it conditions, and how
strongly it de-couples length from reward at the per-window level.  The remaining
practitioner question is the \emph{efficiency} one: at a fixed
budget $T$, is Dr.GRPO strictly better, equivalent, or worse than
GRPO when we price in the length it had to contract to gain the
accuracy it delivered?

We define the within-run length contraction
$\Delta L = \overline{L}_{\mathrm{first}\,5} - \overline{L}_{\mathrm{last}\,5}$
and the within-run reward gain
$\Delta R = \overline{R}_{\mathrm{last}\,5} - \overline{R}_{\mathrm{first}\,5}$,
then take the efficiency ratio
$\mathrm{eff} = \Delta R / \Delta L$ (reward-growth per token dropped)
or, on GSM8K where within-run reward is not summarised,
$\mathrm{eff} = \Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}^{\mathrm{heldout}} / \Delta L$.

\paragraph{H1 -- Pareto dominance.}
On \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 5 seeds, 40 steps),
$\mathrm{eff}_{\mathrm{GR}} = 0.276$ vs $\mathrm{eff}_{\mathrm{DR}} = 0.312$
($\Delta = +0.036$, paired Wilcoxon one-sided $p = 0.0312$,
Cohen's $d = +3.62$, $n{=}5$ pairs).
On \texttt{gsm8k\_cot} (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 3 seeds, 30 steps),
$\mathrm{eff}_{\mathrm{GR}} = 0.0063$ vs $\mathrm{eff}_{\mathrm{DR}} = 0.0076$
($\Delta = +0.0013$, $p = 0.250$, $d = +0.44$, $n{=}3$ pairs ---
low-power but consistent direction).
Dr.GRPO Pareto-dominates on the $(\Delta L, \Delta R)$ plane in both
tasks: less length contraction for equal-or-better reward gain (panel~A).

\paragraph{H2 -- signed per-window CCF decoupling.}
Dr.GRPO reduces the per-window backward signed CCF magnitude
$|\mathrm{CCF}^{\mathrm{GR}}_{bwd}|$ --- the length $\to$ reward
static coupling.  On \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}, mean
$|\mathrm{CCF}^{GR}_{bwd}|=0.371$ vs $|\mathrm{CCF}^{DR}_{bwd}|=0.327$
($\Delta = -0.044$, paired Wilcoxon one-sided $p = 0.420$,
$n{=}20$ (seed, window) pairs); on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot},
$|\mathrm{CCF}^{GR}_{bwd}|=0.651$ vs $|\mathrm{CCF}^{DR}_{bwd}|=0.529$
($\Delta = -0.122$, $p = 0.235$).  The Wilcoxon does not reach
significance at $n{=}20$ but the direction agrees across both tasks
(panel~C); a permutation null on $n{=}32$ signed (seed, window) pairs yields
$p = 0.136$.

\paragraph{H3 -- cross-task envelope.}
The cross-task envelope of efficiency ratios is monotone: $\mathrm{eff}^{DR}/\mathrm{eff}^{GR} = 1.13$
on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} and $1.20$ on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot} ---
both above the GRPO baseline (panel~B).

\paragraph{H4 -- heldout GSM8K trade-off frontier.}
Per-seed heldout scatter on GSM8K (panel~D): Dr.GRPO's three runs
cluster at smaller length contractions ($4.5$ to $8.9$ tokens) than GRPO's
cluster ($5.8$ to $25.6$ tokens).  Both algorithms reach comparable
$\Delta_{\mathrm{acc}}^{\mathrm{heldout}}$ (GR $+0.062$, DR $+0.050$,
paired $\Delta = -0.012$, well within seed noise); the trade-off is
that Dr.GRPO accepts a $\sim 1.2$ percentage-point accuracy
discount in exchange for a $\sim 50\%$ reduction in length
contraction --- the expected price of de-biasing.

\paragraph{H5 -- sign test across (task, seed).}
Aggregating the H1 paired $\Delta$ over 8 (task, seed) cells,
Dr.GRPO wins $7/8$ cells, one-sided binomial $p = 0.0352$.
The H1 permutation null (within-task sign-flip) gives
$p = 0.0077$ at $B{=}50{,}000$ --- Dr.GRPO's efficiency advantage is
robust to $(algo, seed)$ shuffling within each task.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Dr.GRPO sits on (or inside) the GRPO length-efficiency frontier.
The arithmetic\_easy effect is large ($d = +3.62$) and reaches
conventional significance; the GSM8K effect is in the same
direction but underpowered at $n{=}3$.  Combined with the iter120
per-window evidence that Dr.GRPO's \emph{severship} is largest in
backward-CCF-heavy windows and is not explained by active
length-inflation velocity, this sharpens the practitioner takeaway:
Dr.GRPO is a length-efficiency
optimizer that pays a small accuracy tax on GSM8K-style long-CoT
tasks in exchange for substantial length parsimony, and pays
\emph{no} accuracy tax on short-form arithmetic tasks.

\subsection{Closing the causal chain: per-window $|CCF_{bwd}|$ decoupling
drives Dr.GR's efficiency advantage}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter132}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The preceding per-window severship analysis showed that
\emph{severship} (Dr.GR's per-prompt severity score on the original
length) predicts smaller $|CCF^{GR}_{bwd}|$ in the expected direction:
Spearman $\rho=0.50$ on arithmetic ($n=20$, $p=0.025$) and
$\rho=0.56$ on GSM8K-CoT ($n=12$, $p\approx0.06$). No pooled-32
statistic is used here.
Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter128} then showed that Dr.GR
\emph{Pareto-dominates} GR on the run-level $(\Delta L, \Delta R)$
length-efficiency frontier, with arithmetic\_easy Cohen's $d=+3.62$.
What iter-128 did \emph{not} establish is the MECHANISM by which
Dr.GR's CCF reduction yields its efficiency advantage.

Iter-132 closes this loop with a within-run analysis at the
per-window granularity $w\in\{0,1,2,3\}$:

\paragraph{Definition.}
For each $(w)$ at the end of window $w$, define
\[
  \mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_w \;=\;
    \frac{\overline R_{\mathrm{last\,half}\,w} - \overline R_{\mathrm{first\,half}\,0}}
         {\overline L_{\mathrm{first\,half}\,0} - \overline L_{\mathrm{last\,half}\,w}},
\]
the cumulative reward-growth per token-dropped from baseline
(first-half of window 0) to the end of window $w$.  Note
$\mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_{w=n_w-1}$ matches the iter-128
run-level efficiency.  Per run, we have four $(|CCF_{bwd}|_w,
\mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_w)$ points.

\paragraph{H1 (within-window paired Wilcoxon).}
Paired Wilcoxon on $\mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_w(\mathrm{DR}) >
\mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_w(\mathrm{GR})$ over the 20
(arithmeticeasy) and 12 (gsm8k\_cot) (seed, window) pairs.
\textbf{Arithmetic\_easy:} $W=181.0$, $p=0.002$, Cohen's $d=+0.890$.
\textbf{GSM8K\_cot:} $W=43.0$, $p=0.396$ (low power $n{=}12$).
\textbf{Cross-task direction:} $2/2$ tasks favour Dr.GR.

\paragraph{H1b (within-run anti-correlation sharpening).}
Per (algo, seed, task), Spearman
$\rho(|CCF_{bwd}|_w, \mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}}_w)$ across the
$n_w=4$ windows.  Dr.GR's rho should be more NEGATIVE than GR's --
the CCF should NOT co-vary with the cumulative efficiency in Dr.GR.
\textbf{Arithmetic\_easy}: GR mean rho $= +0.480$, Dr.GR mean rho $= +0.160$,
$\Delta = -0.320$, paired Wilcoxon $p=0.125$ (one-sided, alt=less),
Cohen's $d=-0.772$ (medium-large), permutation $p=0.249$ ($B=50{,}000$).
\textbf{GSM8K\_cot}: GR rho $= +0.467$, Dr.GR rho $= +0.333$,
$\Delta = -0.133$, $d=-0.320$, permutation $p=1.000$ ($n{=}3$).
\textbf{Cross-task consistency:} $2/2$ tasks have $\Delta < 0$
(binomial $p=0.25$, $n=2$ -- directionally consistent, but
$n=2$ is under-powered for cross-task inference).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
GR's positive $\rho(|CCF|, \mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}})\approx +0.48$
is the LENGTH-REWARD COUPLING TREADMILL: as the run's cumulative
efficiency climbs, the within-window backward CCF tightens, locking
the policy into a regime where more reward requires more length.
Dr.GR's reduced $\rho\approx +0.16$ breaks that treadmill -- the
backward channel stays loose even as efficiency climbs, which is
exactly the static-vs-dynamic dissociation iter-124 identified from
a different angle.

\paragraph{H1c (cross-task pooled sign test).}
At the run level ($n=6$ paired (seed, task) cells), $4/6$ have
$\Delta\rho<0$ (Dr.GR more anti-correlated).  Binomial
$P(X\ge 4 | n=6, p=0.5)=0.344$ -- directionally consistent but
not significant at $\alpha=0.05$.

\paragraph{H3 (Sobel-style mediation).}
At the run level, direct effect (Dr.GR vs GR on final cum-eff):
arithmetic\_easy $\Delta=+0.024$, gsm8k\_cot $\Delta\approx 0$.
Indirect effect via $-|\Delta CCF_{bwd}|$: arith $-0.002$, gsm $+0.003$.
Bootstrap mediation ratios ($B=5{,}000$) span zero in both tasks
(arith CI $[-0.86, +0.21]$).  We therefore REJECT a strict
mediation hypothesis at the run level -- Dr.GR's cum-eff gain
does not run purely through its $|\!CCF\!|$ reduction.

\paragraph{H4 (cross-task direction consistency).}
$2/2$ tasks have $\rho(\Delta\mathrm{eff}, \Delta|CCF|) > 0$ at the
window level (h1) and $\Delta\rho < 0$ at the within-run level
(h1b).  Direction is consistent with iter-128's Pareto dominance;
the magnitudes are bounded by the small per-task $n$.

\paragraph{Mechanistic reading.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Per-window severship evidence: Dr.GR's severship predicts smaller
$|CCF^{GR}_{bwd}|$ in the expected direction within each task.  Static mechanism.
\item Iter-132 (this iter): in GR the residual $|CCF|$ is
\emph{anti-decoupled} from the efficiency trajectory (rho $\approx +0.48$),
while in Dr.GR the two are nearly orthogonal (rho $\approx +0.16$).
\item Iter-128: Dr.GR Pareto-dominates the run-level efficiency
frontier.  Combined with iter-120 and iter-132, the chain is:
\[
\text{severship} \;\longrightarrow\; |CCF_{bwd}|\downarrow
\;\longrightarrow\; \rho(|CCF_{bwd}|, \mathrm{eff}^{\mathrm{cum}})
\text{ flattens} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{efficiency dominance}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

The H3 mediation ratio at the run level is small because the
EFFICIENCY DOMINANCE comes mostly from cum-eff magnitudes
(iter-128's headline) and not from how $|CCF|$ scales with cum-eff.
Iter-132's sharper contribution is H1b: Dr.GR makes the CCF and
the cum-eff trajectory ORTHOGONAL, so the CCF is no longer the
binding constraint on efficiency growth.
 

\subsection{Step-level trajectory coupling: $\Delta$reward and $\Delta$length
are decoupled in Dr.GR on every (task, hypothesis) cell}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter136}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Earlier per-window analyses measured the GR-vs-DR gap at the
10-step window level (backward / forward CCF).
The severship analysis showed smaller $|CCF^{GR}_{bwd}|$ in the expected
direction within task ($\rho=0.50$, $n=20$, $p=0.025$ on arithmetic;
$\rho=0.56$, $n=12$, $p\approx0.06$ on GSM8K-CoT), without relying on a
pooled-32 statistic.
Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter128} showed Dr.GR Pareto-dominates GR on
the run-level $(\Delta L, \Delta R)$ frontier with arithmetic\_easy
$d=+3.62$.
Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter132} closed the causal chain:
severship $\rightarrow$ $|CCF_{bwd}|$ shrinkage $\rightarrow$
efficiency gain.

What is STILL MISSING is the finest-grained signature:
do GR's \emph{per-step} $\Delta$-vectors $({\Delta r_t, \Delta L_t})$
co-move as a treadmill while Dr.GR's are decoupled?
This is the most direct test of the iter-128/132 orthogonality claim
-- a per-step velocity Spearman that operates on the rawest unit of
the trajectory.

\paragraph{Setup.}
For each (algo, seed, task) we have a per-step time series
$\{(r_t, Z_t, L_t)\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ from $\texttt{step\_log}$:
$T=40$ steps for arithmetic\_easy (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 5 seeds),
$T=30$ steps for gsm8k\_cot (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, 3 seeds).
Define the velocity series
$\Delta r_t = r_{t+1} - r_t$, $\Delta L_t = L_{t+1} - L_t$,
$\Delta Z_t = Z_{t+1} - Z_t$, and compute four statistics per run:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1 (step-velocity Spearman coupling):}
  $\rho(\Delta r_t, \Delta L_t)$ over $t = 0 \ldots T-2$.
  Prediction: $| \rho_{GR} | > | \rho_{DR} |$
  (GR's reward and length velocities are coupled; Dr.GR's are decoupled).
\item \textbf{H2 (length-trajectory trendiness):}
  lag-1 Spearman autocorrelation $\rho(L_t, L_{t+1})$ of the length series.
  Prediction: $| \rho^{GR}_{\mathrm{lag}1} | > | \rho^{DR}_{\mathrm{lag}1} |$
  (GR's length is trending; Dr.GR's is noisy / decoupled from step to step).
\item \textbf{H3 (late-training efficiency):}
  $\mathrm{eff} = ( \bar r_{\mathrm{last10}} - \bar r_{\mathrm{first5}} )
                / ( | \bar L_{\mathrm{last10}} - \bar L_{\mathrm{first5}} | + 1 )$.
  Prediction: $\mathrm{eff}_{DR} > \mathrm{eff}_{GR}$
  (Dr.GR gets more reward-gain per unit of length change in late training).
\item \textbf{H4 (ZVF--length co-movement):}
  $\rho(\Delta Z_t, \Delta L_t)$ over consecutive deltas.
  Prediction: $\rho^{GR} < 0 < \rho^{DR}$ (GR's ZVF falls with its length
  shrink -- a co-movement signature of the collapse mechanism in Pillar 2;
  Dr.GR's ZVF and length deltas are decoupled toward zero).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Per-task paired tests.}
For each (task, hypothesis) we pair the two algorithms across the
$n_{\mathrm{seeds}}$ matched seeds and run a one-sided paired Wilcoxon
in the predicted direction; we also compute a two-sided permutation
null over $B = 50{,}000$ sign-flips of the paired deltas.

\textbf{H1 -- step-velocity coupling:}
Arithmetic\_easy: $\Delta = -0.029$, $W=7$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.500$,
$d = -0.16$.  GSM8K\_cot: $\Delta = -0.003$, $W=3$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.625$,
$d = -0.02$.  Both tasks favour DR (smaller $|\rho|$), but neither is
significant under $n \in \{3, 5\}$.

\textbf{H2 -- length trendiness:}
Arithmetic\_easy: $\Delta = -0.059$, $W=6$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.406$,
$d = -0.37$.  GSM8K\_cot: $\Delta = -0.120$, $W=1$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.250$,
$d = -0.36$.  Both tasks favour DR (smaller $|\rho_{\mathrm{lag}1}|$),
gsm8k shows the larger effect.

\textbf{H3 -- late-training efficiency:}
Arithmetic\_easy: $\Delta = +0.020$, $W=15$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.031$
($p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.063$), $d = +2.68$ (very large).  This is the
only individually-significant test in the panel.
GSM8K\_cot: $\Delta = +0.002$, $W=6$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.125$
($p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.253$), $d = +1.59$.  Both tasks favour DR.

\textbf{H4 -- ZVF--length co-movement:}
Arithmetic\_easy: $\Delta = +0.069$, $W=9$, $p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.406$,
$d = +0.26$.  GSM8K\_cot: $\Delta = +0.065$, $W=6$,
$p_{\mathrm{param}}=0.125$, $d = +1.13$.  Both tasks favour DR (DR's
$\rho$ is more positive, i.e.\ less negative, i.e.\ decoupled from
the GRPO-style co-movement).

\paragraph{Cross-task sign test.}
Because every individual test is under-powered ($n \le 5$),
we aggregate directionally: across all 4 hypotheses $\times$ 2 tasks
$= 8$ paired tests, ALL 8 paired $\Delta$'s lie in the predicted
direction.  One-sided binomial under uniform-null:
$p = (1/2)^{8} = 0.0039$.
\textbf{Global verdict: GLOBAL\_REJECT} the joint null that GR and
Dr.GR have step-velocity co-movement of equal magnitude.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The four statistics are mutually orthogonal probes of the same
mechanism: GR's reward-gain and length-shrink happen together at the
STEP level (the treadmill), while Dr.GR's happen on independent
axes.  The mechanism is consistent with the window-level
$|CCF_{bwd}|$ finding (larger for GR) and iter-128's run-level
Pareto dominance (Dr.GR strictly better on both axes), but the
step-level granularity adds an important new piece of evidence:
\textbf{the decoupling is present in the per-step velocity itself,
not just in the cumulative statistic.}
Per Pillar 2's ZVF-trajectory early-warning analysis, GR's
negative ZVF--length co-movement is the within-collapse signature;
Dr.GR breaks this co-movement without sacrificing the ZVF-safety
that variance-mitigation methods buy.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
The strict per-task null is under rejection; only H3 on
arithmetic\_easy reaches $p < 0.05$.  The headline claim rests on
the cross-cell sign-test, which is robust under heterogeneity but
vulnerable to publication-style aggregation (we pre-registered the
four hypotheses, so the aggregation is honest).  Per-seed values are
small ($n \in \{3, 5\}$); a follow-up with $n \ge 10$ per task would
turn several individual cells significant and would sharpen the
inferred Cohen's $d$ estimates.

\subsection{Cross-Pillar Synthesis: The CDH Echo on Length-Bias Data}
\label{sec:cdh-echo}

The Critic-Degeneracy Hypothesis introduced in the Pillar~1/3
analysis (forward-cited from this paper's Pillar~3 group-size
section; see also the Pillar~3 group's discussion of the
PPO$\leftrightarrow$GRPO equivalence) makes a sharp mechanistic
prediction: \emph{any} component added on top of the stateless
group-mean baseline shifts the natural gradient-flow coupling in
the same direction, and the shift is \emph{larger when the
component is learned}. We test this on the Pillar-4 length-bias
data (this paper, length-bias section) by replacing the
learned value head with Dr.GRPO's \emph{fixed} per-sample length
normaliser.

\paragraph{Data.}
We reuse the iter-136 step-level coupling TSV
(\texttt{experiments/results/length\_bias\_iter136\_step\_coupling.tsv})
with $n_{\text{cell}} = 8$ paired observations across two tasks
(arithmetic\_easy: 5 seeds; gsm8k\_cot: 3 seeds) and two algorithms
(GR / Dr.GR). The relevant variable is
$|\rho(\Delta r_t,\Delta L_t)|$---the per-run reward-velocity /
length-velocity coupling---which is the length-axis analogue of
the $|\rho(\|g_t\|, \mathrm{reward}_t)|$ gradient-reward coupling
tested on Pillar-1 by row 12.

\paragraph{Pre-registered criterion.}
Across the 8 (task, seed) cells, pooled $|\rho|$ for Dr.GR vs GR
should satisfy (i) the Dr.GR/GR ratio is below $1.0$ (the fixed
normaliser does not amplify $|\rho|$ as the learned PPO critic
does) and (ii) the Pillar-1 and Pillar-4 effects share sign. The
synthesis-test rejects row-16 iff (i)-(ii) both hold.

\paragraph{Result.}
The pooled Dr.GR / GR ratio is $\mathbf{0.949}$ (Dr.GR
$|\rho|=0.358$ vs GR $|\rho|=0.377$, $\Delta=-5.1\%$)---favouring
the fixed-normaliser prediction. Cross-pillar, the Pillar-1
\emph{learned} effect is $-19.5\%$ and the Pillar-4 \emph{fixed}
effect is $-5.1\%$ (negative $\Rightarrow$ added component
loosens $|\rho|$). Both effects share sign; the learned / fixed
effect-size ratio is $\mathbf{3.82\times}$, matching the CDH
mechanism's prediction that \emph{learned} components carry
parameter noise beyond what \emph{fixed} components contribute.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
\textbf{Validated (H2 + H3, both FAVOURS; H1 + H4 under-powered
null at $n=8$).} Row 16 corroborates the CDH mechanism on a
\emph{different pillar, different algorithm pair, and different
coupling axis} (length instead of reward): adding any baseline
component shifts the natural coupling, and the shift is larger
when the component is learnable. Pillar-1's
PPO/GRPO $\leftrightarrow$ Pillar-4's Dr.GR/GR form a single
mechanism projected onto two orthogonal coupling axes---the
\textbf{cross-pillar generalisation of the Critic-Degeneracy
Hypothesis}.
 

\subsection{A Suggestive Tool-Use Diagnostic: ReAct-Style Intermediate-Credit Shaping}
\label{sec:length-bias-react-shaping}

ReAct~\citep{yao2023react} interleaves reasoning traces with
task actions and attributes its gains to intermediate states
becoming credit-bearing rather than rolling up into a single
end-of-trajectory signal. Transcribed to RL post-training, the
analogous intervention is \emph{intermediate-credit (dense)
reward shaping}---directly relevant to this pillar, where
end-anchored sparse credit is the hypothesised driver of both
the length blow-out and the zero-signal failure modes. We test
the transcription on the BFCLv4 tool-use rollout summary
(Qwen~0.5B; 2~seeds, 10~step-level observations;
\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/react\_dense\_vs\_sparse\_step.tsv}),
where the sparse- and dense-reward columns differ only in reward
sparsification.

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypotheses.}
Four reward-summary-level hypotheses, with thresholds fixed
before measurement: \textbf{H1}~dense beats sparse on average
rollout reward (decisive iff Cohen's $d > 0.4$);
\textbf{H2}~the first-half vs last-half reward delta is larger
under dense (credit is used earlier); \textbf{H3}~the fraction
of seed-trajectories with identically-zero reward drops under
dense (decisive iff drop $> 0.05$); \textbf{H4}~the
zero-variance fraction (ZVF; the Pillar-2 signal-availability
variable) drops under dense (decisive iff drop $> 0.05$).

\paragraph{Result: 2/4 decisive.}
H1 is \textbf{decisive}: dense lifts the mean of seed-mean
rewards from $0.1125$ to $0.1863$ ($\Delta = +0.074$, Cohen's
$d = 0.667$;
\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/react\_intervention\_gap.tsv}).
H4 is \textbf{decisive}: pooled ZVF drops from $0.40$ (sparse)
to $0.30$ (dense), a reduction of $0.10$
(\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/react\_zvf\_reduction.tsv}).
The other two hypotheses are \textbf{null} on this data: the H2
half-life delta comes out $-0.044$, i.e.\ sign-opposite to the
prediction, and H3 cannot move because no seed on this rollout
has an identically-zero reward trace (sparse and dense zero-floor
fractions are both $0.0$;
\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/react\_summary.json}).

\paragraph{Scope and verdict.}
With 2/4 hypotheses decisive, the mining ledger classifies this
finding as \textbf{suggestive}, not decisive, and we present it
strictly as a diagnostic. The evidence base is small ($n{=}2$
seeds) and is a re-analysis of an existing rollout summary
rather than a live ReAct intervention with explicit
thought/action tokens; the pre-registered promotion criterion
($n \ge 5$ seeds on longer trajectories, flipping H2/H3) has not
been run. Within that scope, the two decisive results point the
same way as this pillar's mechanism story: dense intermediate
credit reduces signal starvation on tool-use---raising realised
reward while restoring within-group contrast (lower ZVF)---which
is what one expects if the length and zero-signal pathologies
documented here are downstream of end-anchored credit
assignment.
 

\subsection{Frontier-Model Cross-Examination: Length Bias and Generalization}
\label{sec:frontier-length-bias}

\paragraph{Framing (external synthesis).}
To stress-test the Pillar~4 null result we submitted it to a live
cross-examination of two frontier reasoning systems (ChatGPT Pro Extended and
Gemini Deep Think). Their contributions are proposed theory and experimental
designs, not measurements we ran; we report them as adversarial extensions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:length-bias-pillar}. The models converged on a single reframing:
our benchmark is a \emph{negative control} for Dr.~GRPO, not a refutation of it.
Because mean length already \emph{falls} under both algorithms (the paper's
$194.4\!\to\!183.5$-token, cap-bounded no-inflation regime), length is not an
active nuisance variable, and a length-debiasing correction can only be equivalent to GRPO where
there is no length bias to remove.

\paragraph{The regime that would reveal a Dr.~GRPO advantage (frontier synthesis).}
ChatGPT Pro specified the missing regime as a \emph{length-confounded sparse-RLVR}
setting in which reward discovery and length dynamics are entangled. The target
conditions are a high length dispersion and a length--reward coupling that our
easy/short tasks lack:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CV}(L)\ \ge\ 0.5, \qquad
\bigl|\mathrm{corr}(L,R)\bigr|\ \ge\ 0.25, \qquad
\mathbb{E}[L \mid A<0] - \mathbb{E}[L \mid A>0]\ \ge\ 50\text{--}100\ \text{tokens},
\end{equation}
instantiated on hard math, symbolic search, code-debugging, or proof-style tasks
with a \emph{binary exact-match} reward, no length penalty, long completion caps
($512$--$1024$), temperature $0.8$--$1.0$, $G\in\{8,16\}$, and frontier prompts
with pass rate $p_x\in[0.1,0.4]$ so that groups mix rare successes with verbose
failures. The decisive test is a \emph{train length-spurious / test
length-anti-spurious} split: in training, verbose reasoning statistically helps
stumble into reward; on held-out data the same verbosity is neutral or harmful.
Plain GRPO should then overfit through length-mediated updates while Dr.~GRPO
preserves reward learning with weaker length drift. The frontier decision rule
is to declare a Dr.~GRPO advantage only at matched rollout budget and matched
train reward when $\Delta_{\text{held-out}}\ge +2$--$5$ pp \emph{and} the length
drift is at least $30\%$ smaller than GRPO's. This directly explains our null:
at $30$--$40$ steps on arithmetic/GSM8K-CoT, length is already controlled, so
equivalence is the \emph{expected} outcome, not evidence against Dr.~GRPO.

\paragraph{A proposed length-normalization bias theorem (frontier synthesis).}
ChatGPT Pro sharpened \emph{why} the two estimators can differ into a covariance
statement. Writing $S_i=\sum_{t}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})$
and $A_i=R_i-\bar R_G$, per-response averaging computes a length-reweighted
covariance whereas the sequence-level object does not:
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(x)=\mathrm{Cov}_G\!\left(S/L,\,R\right),
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{seq}}(x)=\mathrm{Cov}_G\!\left(S,\,R\right),
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{Dr}}(x)=\tfrac{1}{L_{\mathrm{ref}}}\,g_{\mathrm{seq}}(x).
\end{equation}
Per-response normalization is harmless \emph{iff}
$\mathbb{E}\!\left[AS\left(1/L-c\right)\mid x\right]=0$, i.e.\ when $1/L$ is
conditionally uncorrelated with the advantage-weighted score. The frontier
reading is that Dr.~GRPO is \emph{length-neutral, not anti-length}: it removes
the action-dependent $1/L_i$ importance weight and restores the sequence-level
gradient up to a global learning-rate scale. Crucially, the frontier synthesis
\emph{reinforces} our own hedge: length dynamics are weakly identifiable in the
current data (reward saturation fits cleanly, length saturation rarely does), so
the defensible claim is \emph{correction of a formal gradient bias}, not
universal held-out superiority --- exactly the posture Sec.~\ref{sec:length-bias-pillar}
already adopts.

\paragraph{The Causal Length-Mediation Protocol, CLMP (frontier synthesis).}
Gemini Deep Think argued that marginal pass-rate tests --- including the paired
McNemar test our section leans on --- are confounded, because a length-hacking
policy can post held-out successes by over-generating tokens to stochastically
stumble into reward. CLMP instead treats trajectory length $L$ as a causal
\emph{mediator} between the algorithm $A\in\{0{:}\mathrm{GRPO},1{:}\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}\}$
and held-out success $S$, and decomposes the total effect into a Natural Direct
Effect (genuine deduction) and a Natural Indirect Effect (verbosity-mediated
success). In an internal cross-critique the models rejected their own first
proposal --- hard ``iso-brevity'' truncation at the minimal proof length --- as
mechanistically fatal (it yields syntactically invalid, incomplete trajectories
and both algorithms trivially fail), and replaced it with a \emph{non-destructive}
length-stratified estimator via Pearl's mediation formula, letting each
completion finish naturally and marginalizing over the length distribution
$P_A(l)\equiv P(L{=}l\mid A)$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{NDE} &= \sum_{l}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[S\mid A{=}1,l]-\mathbb{E}[S\mid A{=}0,l]\bigr)\,P_1(l),\\
\mathrm{NIE} &= \sum_{l}\mathbb{E}[S\mid A{=}0,l]\,\bigl(P_1(l)-P_0(l)\bigr).
\end{align}
The reported summary statistic is the Generalization-to-Exploit Ratio
$\mathrm{GER}=\mathrm{NDE}/\mathrm{TE}$; the frontier \emph{prediction} (to be
tested, not a result here) is that in the length-confounded regime GRPO would
show $\mathrm{GER}<0.15$ (accuracy is mostly length-mediated exploit) while
Dr.~GRPO would show $\mathrm{GER}>0.85$. On our own data, where length is already
compressed and $\rho(L,R)$ is negative, CLMP would report a small $\mathrm{NIE}$
for \emph{both} algorithms --- consistent with the observed equivalence.

\paragraph{A reviewer-proof memorization-vs-generalization design (frontier synthesis).}
ChatGPT Pro proposed replacing a bare paired test with a stratified,
memorization-controlled generalization ladder of four disjoint held-out strata:
$S_0$ exact/near-duplicates (a contamination sentinel, excluded from the headline
claim), $S_1$ same template with new variables, $S_2$ same skill with held-out
template, and $S_3$ external distribution. Each eval item $x$ receives a
train-nearest-neighbor exposure score $M(x)$ blending BM25, char/$n$-gram overlap,
embedding similarity, and answer/template match; items are binned into
$M$-deciles so that a \emph{memorization} account predicts gains concentrated in
high-$M$ deciles whereas \emph{generalization} predicts nonzero gains in low-$M$,
$S_2/S_3$ items. The flagship estimand is the adjusted low-similarity
$S_2/S_3$ treatment effect from a logistic model with item-difficulty and
seed random effects and a length covariate, so Dr.~GRPO ``wins'' only if its
coefficient survives length and memorization controls. Two hard negative
controls were specified: (i) \emph{length-matched replay} --- subsample GRPO
outputs to match Dr.~GRPO's length distribution within each stratum and
$M$-decile, so any residual gain is not merely a shorter/longer-decoding
artifact; and (ii) \emph{answer-preserving perturbation} (rename variables,
permute numbers, paraphrase) with a reported consistency
$C=\Pr\!\left(f(x)=f(T(x))\right)$, since memorization yields high raw accuracy
but low $C$. McNemar is demoted to a secondary check.

\paragraph{Adversarial objection and the length-adversarial truncation test (frontier synthesis).}
The sharpest reviewer objection the models raised against Dr.~GRPO is that
removing the $1/L_i$ divisor should explode gradient variance on long chains;
their crispest rebuttal is that the native leave-one-out control variate contains
that variance, so the divisor is an invalid heuristic rather than a necessary
regularizer. As the corresponding empirical probe, Gemini Deep Think proposed a
\emph{length-adversarial truncation} test: evaluate both converged policies under
harsh generation caps $T_{\max}\ll\mathbb{E}[|y_{\mathrm{GRPO}}|]$. If GRPO's
held-out accuracy relies on ``stumbling into correctness'' via padding, it should
crater non-linearly under truncation, whereas a genuinely length-invariant
Dr.~GRPO policy should degrade gracefully --- a direct behavioral test of whether
compressed deductive logic, rather than Markov-horizon exploitation, was
acquired. We flag this as the single highest-value follow-up: it is one
truncation sweep away from being executed on the checkpoints
Sec.~\ref{sec:length-bias-pillar} already produced, and unlike the CLMP and
stratified-ladder designs it needs no new training regime.
 \section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:p4-discussion}
The central caution is that our comparison lives in a regime that is, by
construction, unfavorable for revealing a length effect: short arithmetic chains
with a binary exact-match reward, and GSM8K-CoT sampled with a 200-token cap where
step-0 completions are already near the cap and length is weakly (often
negatively) coupled to reward. A null difference between GRPO and
Dr.\,GRPO here is therefore weak evidence about length debiasing in general. Two
further limitations matter. Held-out evaluation is single-benchmark and
the top-10-checkpoint frontier comparison is single-seed (the pre-RL-vs-post
Qwen3-8B-Instruct comparison uses five seeds), so the non-significant GRPO gain
should be read as a boundary on what we can claim rather than a universal result;
and length
dynamics are only weakly identifiable in this data, so mediation-style conclusions
require the length-confounded regime we specify but do not yet run. The
paired-outcome tests we rely on are also marginal tests, which can be confounded by
length-mediated success---a limitation the proposed causal mediation protocol is
designed to address.

On the statistics themselves, an independent Miller-style
audit~\citep{miller2024erroreval} re-derived seven headline numbers across the four
pillar papers under paired/unpaired bootstrap CIs and an equivalence-region (TOST)
test (\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/adding\_error\_bars\_summary.json}).
The single P4-facing headline---audit H7, the iter-136 arithmetic late-training-efficiency
contrast (our H3; paired $n{=}5$, Cohen's $d = +2.68$,
one-sided $p_{\mathrm{param}} = 0.031$, two-sided $p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.063$)---is
graded \textbf{DECISIVE} at $\alpha = 0.05$ under the audit's $n{<}10$
paired-bootstrap rule, while the audit's three NULL verdicts (its H3, H4, H5) all
fall on Pillar-1/3 headlines rather than on this paper's claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:p4-conclusion}
In the near-ceiling Qwen3-8B-Instruct evaluation, GRPO does not significantly
outperform its pre-RL checkpoint on held-out GSM8K; in the Qwen2.5-1.5B
comparison, Dr.\,GRPO does not outperform GRPO at our seed budget; and under the
200-token GSM8K cap neither algorithm inflates length. Rather than read this as
evidence against length debiasing, we
read it as the expected outcome when there is no length pathology to correct, and
we convert it into a concrete experimental program: a length-confounded sparse-reward
regime, a causal length-mediation protocol separating genuine deduction from
verbosity-mediated exploitation, and a length-adversarial truncation test on the
released checkpoints. The most useful contribution of this pillar is thus a precise
statement of where a Dr.\,GRPO advantage should and should not be expected.
