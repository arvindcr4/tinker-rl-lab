## The port

NIAH places a *needle* fact at depth `d` in a *haystack* context of length `L` and
asks whether it is recoverable as a function of `(d, L)`. We port that eval onto the
GRPO **training trajectory**: the 40-step per-run trace is the haystack; the
**needle is the terminal-performance signal**. From only a window of `W` consecutive
steps starting at position `p`, is the needle recoverable? Two needles:

- **per-run needle** = the run's own terminal reward level `R_T` (last-10-step mean);
  recovered iff `|mean_reward(window) − R_T| ≤ TOL` (TOL = 0.05).
- **cross-run needle** = the terminal group-size *reward ranking*, recovered via
  `Spearman(window_reward, terminal_reward)` across the 12 runs.

**Data (real, same-stack).** `experiments/results/groupsize_zvf_sweep.json` —
4 group sizes × 3 seeds × 40 steps (Qwen2.5-0.5B, arithmetic), per-step
`{zvf, mean_reward, entropy, advantage_variance, grad_norm}`.

**Prototype.** `scripts/berkeley/niah_training_signal.py`. No new training; pure
re-analysis. Window grid `W ∈ {1,2,3,5,8,13,21}`, fixed-`W`=5 for position curves.

## What the results mean

**H1 + H5 — the training haystack is "lost-at-the-start", not "lost-in-the-middle."**
Liu et al.'s signature U-shape (worst-middle) does **not** transfer. A monotone
position model beats a U-shape model in **12/12** runs, and the median early→late
recovery gap is a maximal **1.00** (early windows *never* land within TOL of terminal;
late windows *always* do). The reason is structural: a static retrieval context is
symmetric, but a **training haystack is generated by a directional optimization
process**, so its needle is pinned to the terminal end. This is a clean, falsifiable
methodological contrast — NIAH position effects invert under a dynamic haystack.

**H2 — the effective window is UNIMODAL, a genuine contrast to RULER.** Unlike static
long-context where recovery *saturates* past an effective length, the training-haystack
ranking signal **peaks at W*≈13 and then degrades** (ρ 0.857→0.415 as W→21). An
over-long end-anchored window reaches back into the **exploration phase** and
re-absorbs its noise, *corrupting* the ranking. Practical rule: for recovering the
terminal group-size ordering from a partial trace, use a **bounded ~13-step terminal
window**; longer is worse, not neutral. (Recorded as NULL under a saturation criterion,
but the unimodality is the finding.)

**H3 — the NIAH grid is a clean late/long triangle.** `p*(W)` drops monotonically from
step 14 (W=1) to step 7 (W=21): longer windows push the recoverable frontier earlier,
tracing a connected recoverable region in the upper-right of the (p,W) map — the
canonical NIAH heatmap shape, reproduced for training signal.

**H4 reconciles with StateFlow row-23 via the variance channel.** `ρ(log₂G, mean p*)
= −0.80`: **larger G makes the needle recoverable earlier.** This is *not* a
contradiction of row-23's finding that "G does not reshape the state schedule"
(conv-entry ≈ step 10 for all G). Recoverability is **variance-limited**, not
schedule-limited: larger G shrinks group-mean-reward variance, so a fixed-length
window's mean lands inside TOL sooner *even though the underlying reward schedule is
unchanged*. Row-23 said **"G moves variance, not schedule"**; H4 is exactly that
statement projected onto the recoverability axis.
