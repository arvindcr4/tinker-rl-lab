\begin{abstract}
Comparative claims about reinforcement-learning post-training of language
models are usually made between method labels: ``GRPO beats PPO'', ``DAPO beats
GRPO''. We report a set of negative and cautionary results showing that, in
the GRPO family, the label is not the unit of evidence: the training stack is
part of the result. A matched same-stack PPO/GRPO contrast on synthetic
arithmetic is inconclusive ($\Delta=-0.002$, $p=0.374$, five seeds). A
same-stack audit of four labelled variants on GSM8K is inconclusive in every
contrast against baseline GRPO (eight seeds). Under one label, last-10
training reward spans 5.0\% to 85.6\% (0.050 to 0.856) across two stacks
that also differ in base checkpoint, so it is not a backend-only effect; one
label (``DAPO'') yields opposite telemetry on two stacks, one of which is our
own custom loss without dynamic sampling; and four labels implemented as our
own simplified losses on one managed stack land within a 0.034
training-reward band. Two historical exhibits whose source analyses cannot be
reproduced are withdrawn. We distil these exhibits into an eight-item
minimum reportable stack and a machine-readable registry whose diff tool flags
such label collisions from manifests alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1}
replaces PPO's learned critic \citep{schulman2017proximal} with a baseline
computed from a group of $G$ sampled completions per prompt, and has spawned a
family of variants such as DAPO \citep{yu2025dapo}, Dr.\ GRPO
\citep{liu2025understanding} and GSPO \citep{qwen2025gspo}. Each is available
in several libraries, including TRL \citep{vonwerra2022trl}, veRL
\citep{sheng2024verl}, OpenRLHF \citep{hu2024openrlhf} and managed services such
as Tinker \citep{thinkingmachines2024tinker}, which differ in answer parser,
sampler, KL handling, clip range, LoRA targets \citep{hu2022lora}, precision
and evaluator. Deep-RL and RLHF results are known to be sensitive to such
details \citep{henderson2018deep,engstrom2020implementation,huang2024nplus}, but papers in this area are not asked to report
them, so a gain attributed to a method label may belong to the stack.

This short paper asks a narrow question: \emph{how much of a reported GRPO-family
difference survives once the stack is held fixed?} Our answer, from our own
short-horizon, low-seed GSM8K experiments, is: little that we could detect.
We contribute (i) five measured exhibits
of stack sensitivity (E-2, E-4--E-7), including the E-2 failure to detect and
E-4 inconclusive contrasts, not equivalences, and (ii) an eight-item minimum reportable stack with an
auditor and a diff tool that operationalise the lesson.

\section{Setting and Protocol}

All runs use verifiable rewards: GSM8K exact match
\citep{cobbe2021training} except E-2, which uses synthetic $a{+}b$
arithmetic, on open models from the Qwen \citep{yang2024qwen25,yang2025qwen3} and Llama \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} families.
Where possible, each comparison fixes model, prompts, verifier, decoding
configuration, step budget and held-out evaluation, and varies one factor.
The unit of inference is the training seed. Paired $t$-tests across seeds
are reported with df; E-4 follows its preregistered analysis (paired seed
bootstrap 95\% intervals, Benjamini--Hochberg \citep{benjamini1995controlling} over its four contrasts, exact
80\%-power MDE and a $\pm0.010$ equivalence margin). Exhibits with one or
three seeds per arm are descriptive. An inconclusive test is reported as
inconclusive; it is not read as equivalence. Where we use the
zero-variance fraction (ZVF), it is the share of a training batch's
(post-filtering) prompt groups in which every completion received the same
outcome reward, so the mean-centred group-relative advantage is identically
zero (with an $\epsilon$-stabilised or omitted standard-deviation divisor).

\paragraph{Withdrawn historical exhibits (E-1, E-3).}
Earlier drafts reported marginal one-way $\eta^2$ values over a 42-experiment
corpus (E-1) and cross-backend PPO/GRPO statistics for Qwen3-8B and
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (E-3). No per-run table or analysis script for E-1
survives, and the E-3 statistics were step-level summaries of single runs
whose source aggregates cannot be reproduced; recomputing from the retained
step logs gives different values. We therefore report neither, and make no
direction-reversal or framework-attribution claim.

\paragraph{Matched inconclusive contrast (E-2).}
With the stack held fixed (one minimal trainer, Qwen2.5-0.5B, synthetic
$a{+}b$ arithmetic, 40 steps, five seeds), PPO and GRPO differ by $-0.002$ in
held-out accuracy (GRPO 0.990, PPO 0.992; paired $t_4=-1.0$, $p=0.374$): a
failure to detect a difference, not equivalence, near a 0.99 ceiling. The
stored runs drew evaluation items from a shared random stream, so the two
arms of a seed saw different items and PPO's items overlapped its training
draws more often; the pairing is by seed only.

\paragraph{Same-stack variants do not separate (E-4).}
Holding one stack fixed (TRL GRPOTrainer, Qwen3-8B, GSM8K, $G=4$, 30 steps),
we compared DAPO, GSPO, Dr.\ GRPO and AERO \citep{zhang2026aero} against GRPO
over eight paired seeds, evaluating each final checkpoint on 500 GSM8K test
items (GRPO mean 0.633). All four contrasts are inconclusive, e.g.\ GSPO
$+0.00500$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.00125, +0.01200]$, raw paired $t_7$
$p=0.210$) and AERO $-0.00075$ ($[-0.00825, +0.00675]$, $p=0.858$);
Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment rejects none. Our own aggregation script had
earlier labelled the differences ``disappear''; we retract that verdict
because each contrast's exact 80\%-power MDE (0.0101 for DAPO, up to 0.0148
for Dr.\ GRPO) exceeds the preregistered $\pm0.010$ margin, which the
preregistered equivalence rule requires. No arm produced a significant
held-out difference, and none met that rule. The runs also deviate from
parts of the preregistered run contract, so this is a reanalysis of
finished runs rather than a validated confirmatory audit.

\paragraph{Labels hide stacks (E-5, E-6).}
Under the GRPO label, last-10 training reward was 85.6\% on the managed
service (Qwen3-8B-Base) and 5.0\% with TRL on an H100 (Qwen3-8B), one seed
and 30 steps each. We do not report this as a backend effect, because the run
configurations bind different base checkpoints; that bundling is itself the
finding. Similarly, the label ``DAPO'' produced mean ZVF 0.00 with TRL's
dynamic sampling (Qwen3-8B, eight seeds of E-4) and 0.58 on the managed
service (Qwen3.5-4B, three seeds, 15 steps), where our own loss under that
label clips the standardised advantage to $[-2,3]$ and has neither an
importance ratio nor dynamic sampling. Loss, model and budget all differ, so
the telemetry gap cannot be attributed to a single mechanism.

\paragraph{Labels on one managed stack at short horizons (E-7).}
Running losses labelled GRPO, DAPO, Dr.\ GRPO and GSPO on one managed stack
(Qwen3.5-4B, GSM8K, $G=8$, 15 steps, three seeds per arm), mean last-10
training reward spans 0.710--0.744, a band of 0.034, smaller than the
within-label seed SD (0.090--0.116); the label term gives one-way
$\eta^2=0.025$ over the 12 runs. These losses are our own simplified
implementations (e.g.\ the GSPO label has no sequence-level ratio), so E-7
shows labels that do not separate, not a comparison of the published
algorithms. An earlier group-size variance comparison is withdrawn.

\section{A Minimum Reportable Stack}

The exhibits and complementary reporting practice motivate eight proposed
items: (1)~the loss form (ratio level, clip
bounds, token mask, advantage normalisation, dynamic sampling), since E-6
shows one label with and without dynamic sampling, and E-7 shows labels
attached to simplified losses; (2)~reference policy and
KL handling; (3)~sampler, backend and precision, including base-checkpoint
identity, motivated by E-5; (4)~the per-step ZVF and gradient-utilisation
trajectory, since a single mean conflates opposite regimes; (5)~the
group-size schedule; (6)~a held-out split disjoint from the reward
environment; (7)~a contamination check and parser-robustness probe; and
(8)~held-out pass@$k$ curves attached to a standalone held-out evaluation record,
not merely in-training rollout telemetry.
The items extend general reporting norms
\citep{pineau2020improving,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets} to the
specifics of group-relative RL.

The current implementation is in \texttt{platform\_hybrid/registry/}
of \url{https://github.com/arvindcr4/tinker-rl-lab}: \texttt{schema.json},
\texttt{entries/} and \texttt{query.py}. This is a repository locator, not
a frozen release or proof of historical execution. Stack records describe
configurations; variant-delta records describe declared changes, not new runs.
The current badge averages substantive non-null leaf coverage equally over
seven schema blocks, then rounds $100$ times that mean. Explicit false counts
as reported; disabled clipping excludes the two clip-bound leaves.
The blocks cover loss form (ratio, clip bounds, length/advantage normalisation,
mask, aggregation, shaping, filter, clipping flag), reference/KL (policy,
coefficient, estimator), sampler (backend, precision, temperature, top-$p$),
telemetry (ZVF, GU), group schedule (initial $G$, schedule, rule), held-out
split (disjointness, $n$), and decontamination (performed, parser probe).
Pass@$k$ is the eighth recommendation, not an eighth scored block.

Current stackdiff assigns the maximum severity of known differences:
R0 no known differences; R1 seed/version/reporting; R2 runtime;
R3 hyperparameters/budget; R4 loss/KL/variant changes; R5 outcome-critical
checkpoint/reward/prompt/data/evaluation/execution differences. Unknown
fields lower coverage and flag underspecification; they do not automatically
raise severity. Thus R0 is not proof of a matched stack. We withdraw the
obsolete null-implies-R5 interpretation and historical 43--96 badge claim
as a description of this current implementation.

\paragraph{Proposal versus compliance.} The eight items are a proposed reporting target, not a claim that all historical exhibits satisfy it. Reference/KL disclosure, dynamic schedules, contamination/parser probes and held-out pass@$k$ are complementary reporting recommendations rather than experimentally validated levers in every exhibit. Several historical runs lack checkpoint bindings, trajectories, independent held-out evaluations or contamination evidence. Initial registry manifests are not independent training seeds, and neither their count nor a reporting badge validates scientific quality or hidden implementation semantics.

\section{Discussion}

None of these exhibits shows that the GRPO variants are equivalent. They show
that, at the scale and power available to us, the comparisons that are
reported here could not have distinguished a method effect from a stack
effect. We think the productive response is procedural rather than
algorithmic: report the stack, audit it mechanically, and treat a label-only
comparison as unfalsifiable.

\paragraph{Inferential boundary.} No direction-reversal or framework-attribution claim is made; E-1 and E-3 are withdrawn. E-5 bundles checkpoint changes and is single-seed. E-7 compares simplified losses under published labels; its narrow training-reward band establishes neither equivalence nor a variance decomposition. These short-horizon (15--40 step), low-seed exhibits cannot test the long-horizon dynamics that DAPO, Dr.\ GRPO and GSPO target.

\section*{Limitations}

Our evidence comes from small-to-mid open models (0.5B--8B in the reported exhibits),
mathematics-style verifiable rewards and short training horizons (15--40
steps in the exhibits), so it says nothing about asymptotic behaviour,
preference-model rewards or frontier-scale training. Several comparisons are
single-seed or low-seed. The managed service's sampler and optimiser internals
are closed, so results on it describe that platform plus our loss code rather
than an abstract algorithm. The 5.0\%--85.6\% exhibit mixes backend and
checkpoint changes. The
historical twenty-record, ten-fingerprint and 23-sub-field summaries are not
revalidated against the current schema and are not current registry statistics.
Manifest coverage is not experimental independence or outcome validation.
The standard's eight items are motivated by our exhibits and complementary
reporting practice and have not
been validated by independent groups.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses public models and public mathematics benchmarks and involves
no human subjects or personal data. Its main risk is over-reading: our nulls
are underpowered and should not be cited as evidence that the methods are
equivalent.
