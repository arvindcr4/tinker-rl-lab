\begin{abstract}
We study zero-variance groups in short-horizon GRPO-family training.
Uniform outcome rewards give zero centred advantages, but a thresholded
Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) is only a diagnostic proxy. A single-seed,
four-arm GSM8K training panel ($G=8$, 40 steps) gives ZVF 0.71--0.77;
three separately stored GSM8K sampling evaluations give pooled 0.1583 and
are not the same population. Pairwise contrast density (PCD) is
a variance-scale proxy, not a pair-disagreement fraction or a signed mastery
signal. The Bernoulli identity $h_G(p)=p^G+(1-p)^G$ assumes conditionally
i.i.d. rewards; pooled-rate comparisons and resampling uniform observed
vectors do not independently validate it. A live adaptive arm records the
same point gain as a fixed recipe at higher rollout cost. A separate offline
trace has 92.3\% of escalations on observed all-correct groups, not proven
true boundary prompts. No controller superiority or general optimum is established.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
reinforcement learning, GRPO, large language models, verifiable rewards,

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains a language model
against a programmatic checker, such as exact match for mathematics or unit
tests for code~\cite{lambert2024tulu3}, instead of a learned reward model~\cite{ouyang2022training}.
GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1} has become the default
optimiser for this setting because it removes the value network of
PPO~\cite{schulman2017proximal}. It samples $G$ completions per prompt and
standardises their rewards within the group:
\begin{equation}
A_i = \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon},\;
\mu_g = \tfrac{1}{G}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{G} r_j,\;
\sigma_g^2 = \tfrac{1}{G}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{G} (r_j-\mu_g)^2 .
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
with stabiliser $\varepsilon>0$. If every completion in the group receives the same reward, $\sigma_g = 0$ and
every $A_i$ is zero. Any KL term still acts, but the group-relative part of
the update, which is the only part driven by the task, vanishes. Standard
training logs (reward, loss, gradient norm, entropy, length) do not show how
often this happens: a rising reward curve looks the same whether a fifth or
four fifths of the batch is starved.

This paper measures that quantity and asks what, if anything, it tells a
practitioner to do. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a precise, recomputable definition of the Zero-Variance Fraction and
its measurement on real GRPO reward tensors;
\item evidence that starvation in our regime is \emph{easy-driven}, and that
ZVF aliases mastery with incapacity, which motivates the pairwise contrast
density (PCD) as a more robust signal;
\item a signal model for group size with three falsifiable predictions, and
the measurements that test them;
\item a counterfactual audit of an adaptive group-size controller, together
with null results for curriculum and re-baselining interventions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

DAPO~\cite{yu2025dapo} addresses degenerate groups by dynamic sampling,
discarding and resampling groups whose rewards are uniform.
GSPO~\cite{qwen2025gspo} moves the importance ratio to the sequence level,
and Dr.\ GRPO~\cite{liu2025understanding} removes the length and
standard-deviation normalisations that it shows to be biased. Open-source
training stacks such as TRL~\cite{vonwerra2022trl}, veRL~\cite{sheng2024verl}
and OpenRLHF~\cite{hu2024openrlhf} implement these variants with different
defaults. Other work acts on zero-variance prompts directly, by pre-rollout
filtering of prompts predicted to be uninformative~\cite{zheng2025greso} or by
shaping advantages on zero-variance prompts~\cite{le2025rlzvp}; we did not
evaluate these methods, so our intervention nulls apply only to the heuristics
audited here. Henderson et al.~\cite{henderson2018deep} showed how sensitive deep
RL conclusions are to seeds and implementation details, which shapes our
statistical protocol. Unlike this work on new estimators, we do not propose
an optimiser; we measure how much of the group-relative signal is available
in the first place, and test whether acting on that measurement helps.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Zero-Variance Fraction}
For a batch $\mathcal{B}_t$ of prompt groups at step $t$, each with $G$
sampled completions and rewards normalised to $[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ZVF}_t = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_t|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{B}_t}
\mathbb{1}\!\left[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(r_{g,1},\dots,r_{g,G}) \le \tau\right],
\end{equation}
with $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}$ the unbiased sample variance and
$\tau = 10^{-6}$ (distinct from the stabiliser $\varepsilon$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:adv})). Gradient utilisation is its complement,
$\mathrm{GU}_t = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}_t$. For objectives with KL, clipping or
auxiliary losses this is an accounting identity, not a claim that the total
gradient is zero. Our measured runner has no KL or clip term, so there
$\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 1$ guarantees a zero policy-term gradient only when groups
are exactly uniform; a positive variance threshold also counts near-uniform groups.
For binary rewards any $\tau<1/G$ is equivalent to an exact-zero test, since the smallest non-zero unbiased variance is $1/G$. The definition does not apply to
dense rewards, $G = 1$, or non-outcome rewards.

\subsection{Pairwise contrast density}
For rewards $r_1,\ldots,r_G$ in $[0,1]$ and $G\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{PCD}}=\frac1G\sum_{i=1}^G(r_i-\bar r)^2
=\frac{1}{2G^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^G(r_i-r_j)^2.
\end{equation}
For conditionally i.i.d.\ Bernoulli rewards its expectation is
$(G-1)p(1-p)/G$. This is a variance-scale contrast proxy, not the
fraction of distinct pairs that disagree: the latter has expectation
$2p(1-p)$. PCD, like ZVF, is unsigned and invariant under $p\mapsto1-p$;
mean reward is needed to distinguish mastery from incapacity.

\subsection{A signal model for group size}
For a prompt with per-rollout success probability $p$ and $G$
conditionally independent Bernoulli rewards, the probability that the group
is degenerate is $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$. We model usable signal per
prompt as
\begin{equation}
S(p,G) = p(1-p)\bigl(1 - h_G(p)\bigr).
\label{eq:signal}
\end{equation}
The first factor is a heuristic contrast proxy, not a normalised-gradient identity; the second
is the probability that any contrast survives centring. Both vanish at
$p \in \{0,1\}$ and peak at $p = \tfrac12$. Three consequences follow:
\textbf{(T1)} the expected ZVF indicator of a group equals $h_G(p)$;
\textbf{(T2)} for $0<p<1$, $h_G$ strictly decreases in $G$, with asymptotic exponential rate
$-\ln\max(p,1-p)$; and \textbf{(T3)} with unnormalised group-centred
advantages, the mean absolute advantage is $2\hat p(1-\hat p)$, so gradient
magnitude should track $p(1-p)$.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
Training runs use LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} adapters on Qwen-family models
from 0.5B to 32B parameters, together with Llama-3 models, against GSM8K
exact-match~\cite{cobbe2021training} and synthetic arithmetic verifiers. The
training loop logs per-step ZVF, gradient utilisation, entropy, completion
length and KL, and persists the raw per-group reward tensors so that every
diagnostic can be recomputed rather than read from a log line. The same
task, reward and decoding specification is dispatched to a managed training
API and to an open trainer; comparisons hold the stack fixed and vary one
factor. Panels differ in model, task, seed count and held-out size; we
state these per result where the retained artifact records them, and say so
where it does not.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Starvation is the typical, easy-driven regime}
On a stored GSM8K training panel (one seed per arm, 16 prompts $\times$
$G=8$, 40 steps; GRPO and three variants), recomputing ZVF from the raw
per-group reward tensors gives 0.71--0.77 per arm: about three quarters of
each rollout batch contributes no group-relative gradient. A separate check
recomputes ZVF from three stored Qwen3-8B GSM8K sampling evaluations
(200 problems, $G=8$): 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155 (pooled 0.1583), matching the
logged values; these are evaluation samples, not training groups. A
matched-budget panel of 2,560 rollouts per arm on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K (two seeds
per arm) shows the mechanism: over the last ten steps the $G=2$ arms reach
mean training reward 0.90 and 0.96 with mean ZVF 0.975, while the $G=16$ arms
average ZVF 0.10--0.15 (one step reaches 0.375). When the policy solves the task, nearly every
group becomes all-correct. Starvation here is driven by ease, not
difficulty.

\subsection{ZVF is a diagnostic, not a score}
Two observations argue against optimising ZVF directly. First, it aliases
mastery with incapacity. In a 368-run audit (W\&B summaries for 350 runs
with logged ZVF; nine models; 2--12 steps per run, mostly 5), run-mean ZVF is
0.96 for runs ending at reward below 0.05 ($n=201$) and 0.90 for runs ending
at 0.95 or above ($n=30$), against 0.17--0.54 in the 0.2--0.65 reward bins
($n=11$ runs in total). These are very short single-run cells, so this is a
descriptive pattern only. Second, ZVF depends on its variance convention. On
600 stored $G=8$ groups, adding $\delta\sim U(0,10^{-4})$ to the rewards
takes an exact-zero-variance count from 0.158 to 0.000 but leaves the
thresholded ZVF ($\tau=10^{-6}$, unbiased variance) at 0.158, while PCD
moves by $3\times10^{-7}$. Jitter below $\sqrt{\tau}$ cannot cross the
threshold (an interval of width $10^{-4}$ gives sample variance at most
$5\times10^{-9}$); an exact-tie implementation can.

\subsection{Testing the signal model}
\textbf{T1.} A group's empirical success fraction mechanically determines
whether that same group's binary rewards are uniform. Binning by that fraction
therefore is not an independent test of $h_G(p)$. Prompt heterogeneity requires
an average of $h_G(p_g)$, not $h_G$ of a pooled empirical rate. The historical
theory column below uses unreconciled rate inputs, so it is withdrawn as a
quantitative validation; its residual cannot identify dependence or a Jensen gap.

\textbf{T2.} In the 368-run audit, cell-mean ZVF falls across $G=4/8/16$
for Qwen3-32B (0.33 $\to$ 0.18 $\to$ 0.08; $n=2/2/1$ runs) and
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (0.76 $\to$ 0.65 $\to$ 0.47; $n=3/1/1$).
Llama-3.2-1B, near $p\approx0$, stays at 0.98 $\to$ 0.97 ($G=4/8$), and
Qwen3-4B-Instruct is non-monotone (0.89, 0.84, 0.91). The single-run $G=6$
cells break the ordering for Qwen3-32B (0.42) and Llama-3.1-8B (0.50), so
these cells are descriptive, not a test. A controlled Qwen2.5-0.5B sweep on
synthetic $a{+}b$ arithmetic (three seeds per $G$) gives a clean decline
(Table~\ref{tab:gsweep}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gu}), with held-out accuracy flat
near 0.98 on 200 freshly sampled problems per run.
Raising $G$ buys signal, but on this near-saturated task it does not buy
outcome.

\textbf{T3.} Per-group gradients are not exposed by the managed stack. One
logged run of an open backward pass (0.5B model, synthetic arithmetic)
reports $\mathrm{corr}(\lVert\nabla\rVert, \hat p(1-\hat p)) = +0.71$. Only
this scalar survives: no per-group rows, the number of groups or the
advantage normalisation are retained. It is directional evidence at most.

\subsection{Group size: no optimum established}
The marginal ZVF reduction shrinks with $G$: 0.0745 from $G=2$ to 4, but
only 0.0594 from $G=8$ to 16. On the near-ceiling sweep, mean held-out
accuracy is 0.982, 0.988, 0.990 and 0.978 at $G=2,4,8,16$ (3 seeds,
200 problems each). Three seeds do not establish equivalence; the $G=2$ to
$G=16$ point-estimate ratio is 100.3\%. Eq.~(\ref{eq:signal})
gives group size a mechanism, but the three-seed sweep establishes no
optimum.

\subsection{Acting on the diagnostic}
\textbf{Adaptive group size.} We audited an adaptive-$G$ controller that
escalates group size on ZVF spikes, using PCD as an aliasing guard, in a
four-arm open-trainer comparison (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, synthetic
arithmetic, seeds 0 and 1) with shared data and seeds but unequal rollout
costs (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). Only arm means survive; per-seed values and
the held-out set size are not retained. Dynamic sampling reaches perfect telemetry
(ZVF 0.00) at a 45\% rollout premium, without beating the cheaper fixed
recipe. Adaptive-$G$ ties the best held-out gain at 186 rollouts.
This is a tie of reported point gains, not evidence of statistical
competitiveness, equivalence or superiority; the comparison is small-$n$
and single-task.

\textbf{Counterfactual controller trace.} The separate 2,560-decision
trace includes 1,867 escalation events, of which 1,723 (92.3\%) occur on
observed all-correct groups and 144 on observed all-wrong groups. The GRPO
subset contains 640 prompt-step pairs, 461 uniform (427 correct, 34 wrong).
Resampling a finite uniform reward vector necessarily stays uniform: zero
restorations at $G=16$ are a bootstrap identity, not evidence that fresh
independent rollouts cannot restore contrast. Observed $\hat p=0$ or 1 does
not establish a true boundary probability. The trace is not the live
four-arm experiment and supplies no measured controller gain.

\textbf{Curriculum and re-baselining.} A difficulty curriculum that filters
collapsed groups drives the zero-gradient waste fraction from 0.50 to 0.00,
but at 4.81$\times$ the sampling cost, and its held-out gain equals the
baseline's (+0.05 in both arms; one 8-step run per arm). At a matched
30k-token budget the two tie at a mean +0.028 over three seeds per arm. A
single-seed $G=4$ ``win'' of +0.125 (held-out 0.875 to 1.000) did not survive
re-testing: in a three-seed group-size panel the $G=4$ gain is 0.0 in every seed. Re-baselining is not specified sufficiently here to assess its
estimator or gradient effect. Equal reported point gains in these panels
do not establish that interventions cannot improve held-out outcomes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
These are short, heterogeneous telemetry panels, not a single confirmatory
experiment. The 0.71--0.77 training-panel values and the stored
sampling-evaluation checks (0.1583 pooled) have different cohorts; their difference is not
a replication discrepancy resolved by matching a log. The exact main-panel
configuration, intervention definitions, seed/item counts, control law and
uncertainty records are incomplete here. Equal reported point gains do not
establish equivalence. The live four-arm audit uses unequal rollout costs
(120, 120, 174 and 186), and is separate from the offline controller trace.
No matched-cost superiority follows. The heuristic signal model is not an
expected normalised-gradient identity. T2's strict decrease holds only for
$0<p<1$, with the stated rate an asymptotic exponential rate; at true
boundaries $h_G=1$. Toy gradient correlation lacks a fully specified
normalisation, norm and sampling unit and is descriptive only. Dense rewards
and positive variance thresholds require their own calibration. No fixed
operational alarm threshold is validated by this evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
ZVF and variance-scale PCD can describe reward contrast, but both are unsigned
and require mean reward and source-bound configuration alongside them.
The historical sweep shows declining measured ZVF with $G$; it establishes
neither an outcome optimum nor independent validation of the pooled-rate
model. Uniform-vector bootstrap failures are mechanical, not fresh-rollout
impossibility results. A pre-registered, fully specified, matched-cost
controller comparison remains future work.
