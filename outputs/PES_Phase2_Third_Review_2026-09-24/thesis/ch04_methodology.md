# 4. Proposed Methodology

This chapter sets out the methodology of the work rather than its results. It states four things in order: the design commitment to attribution and the four de-confound pillars that operationalise it (§4.1); the per-step telemetry that makes a run's internal state observable at the granularity at which the mechanism acts (§4.2); the formalisation of the Zero-Variance Fraction as a descriptive diagnostic rather than a metric (§4.3); the eight-study portfolio P1–P8 and the question each study asks (§4.4); and the design of the E1–E14 held-out evaluation campaign, including its single trained actor, pinned native evaluators, frozen sampling, receipt model, terminal-state taxonomy and decontamination handling (§4.5). Throughout, the reporting discipline is part of the method: figures are given only where a repository file supports them, and where a lane produced no admissible result, it is recorded as blocked rather than written around.

## 4.1 The Design Commitment to Attribution

The premise of the project is that an RL-for-LLM result is not identified by the name of its algorithm. Two runs both labelled GRPO may differ in loss form, reference-policy handling, sampler, precision, base-checkpoint identity, data order and group-size schedule, and the label alone does not say which of those differences produced the outcome. The project's response is a design commitment rather than a post-hoc covariate adjustment: hold the whole experimental stack fixed and vary exactly one factor at a time, so that an observed difference is attributable to the factor that was varied rather than to the several things that changed alongside it (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). This commitment is the methodological spine of the whole thesis, and it has a practical corollary that shapes every experiment reported later: a study is defined by the single axis it varies and is only interpretable to the extent that every other axis was genuinely pinned.

The commitment is implemented in a unified launcher, `platform_local/unified/launcher.py`, which consumes a single configuration object and dispatches it to thin per-framework adapters — `trl_integrations/`, `verl/`, `platform_modal/openrlhf/`, `platform_hybrid/skyrl/` and `platform_tinker/atropos/` — with the reference GRPO loop implemented once in `platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The reward grader and the decoding path are shared code. That sharing is the load-bearing part of the design: because the grader and the sampler are the same objects across back-ends, a difference in outcome between two frameworks is attributable to the framework, and not to a divergent reward parser or a differently configured decoder masquerading as an algorithmic difference. The commitment also bounds what a result may claim. A completed run supports a statement about that stack, at that revision, with that grader; it does not license a statement about "GRPO" in the abstract, and the project's reporting standard (§4.4, P5) is built around that distinction.

Four pillars carry the commitment, each holding the remainder of the stack fixed while varying one factor (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/submissions/mtech-final-review/build_review_pptx.py, slide 7):

| Pillar | Factor isolated | Design |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · PPO versus GRPO, same stack | Advantage estimator only | Identical model, data and stack |
| 2 · Zero-Variance Fraction | Signal starvation and its confounders | Per-step ZVF measured across nine libraries |
| 3 · Trainability and group size | Contrast density against the variance knob | G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} × seeds, held out |
| 4 · Held-out generalisation | Whether GRPO adds real capability | Dr. GRPO versus GRPO, pre-to-post, McNemar |

: The four de-confound pillars, the single factor each isolates, and the design that holds the remainder fixed.

Pillar 1 asks whether a reported GRPO advantage survives when the estimator is the only thing that changes; on the measured Qwen3-8B comparison the paired difference is small and not statistically significant (paired Δ = −0.002, p = 0.374; source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/frontier_synthesis_scaling.tex, backed by `samestack_ppo_grpo.json`), while the same-stack Welch test for that comparison is recorded in the historical twenty-test family at p = 0.7605 and flagged there as a provenance conflict rather than an estimate (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Pillar 2 turns a run's internal signal state into a first-class measurement; it is formalised in §4.3. Pillar 3 varies the group size G while holding model, data, reward and schedule fixed, and is the axis on which the preference-density argument of P3 rests. Pillar 4 asks the question the other three cannot answer — whether any of this changes held-out behaviour — by comparing a pre-RL checkpoint with its post-RL successor on data the training loop never saw.

Held fixed across the experiment programme are the training protocol constants: LoRA rank 32; Adam with β₁ = 0.9 and β₂ = 0.95; learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴ for the headline configuration; and the seed set {42, 123, 456, 789, 1024} (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The model roster spans the Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Llama-3.x, DeepSeek-V3.1 and Nemotron-120B families, from 0.6B to approximately 671B total parameters, including mixture-of-experts anchors at the frontier end of that range (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The statistical apparatus is fixed in advance of the comparisons it supports: Welch t-tests and Mann–Whitney U tests for two-sample contrasts; Cohen's d with Hedges–Olkin intervals; 95% bootstrap confidence intervals with 10,000 resamples; rliable aggregate metrics (interquartile mean, median and optimality gap); and Bonferroni and Benjamini–Hochberg corrections over a declared test family (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Power was computed rather than assumed: at 80% power the minimum detectable effect for an equal-n comparison of five seeds against five is d_min = 2.024, improving to d_min = 1.530 when a five-seed group is compared against a pooled group of fifteen (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Those figures are a standing reminder of how coarse a five-seed design is, and the project reports several null results that are null precisely because the design could not have detected anything smaller.

Two limitations belong in this section rather than in a later discussion. First, the framework roster is broader than the framework evidence: although seven RL libraries are named in the study design, only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs, and the veRL and OpenRLHF entries in `framework_comparison.json` are dry-run placeholders rather than measurements (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Cross-library claims are therefore narrower than the roster implies. Second, the variance decomposition that motivates the whole attribution commitment is itself a one-way analysis over 42 experiments with valid performance observations, where library/framework explains η² = 0.546 and training algorithm η² = 0.558, ahead of model family at η² = 0.471 and model scale at η² = 0.322 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). The historical twenty-test multiple-comparison family that accompanies it pre-dates the Tier-A/B/C partition, silently includes single-seed rows whose Welch statistics are mathematically undefined, and reduces to a single surviving comparison under the restricted family; it is retained as a reproducibility audit trail and explicitly not as a corrected survival claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex).

## 4.2 Per-Step Telemetry

Attribution is only as good as the run's observability. Every training step therefore emits a record containing the Zero-Variance Fraction; gradient utilisation; the collapse decomposition that separates all-correct from all-wrong zero-variance groups; and, alongside them, reward, loss, entropy, completion length and the KL divergence to the reference policy (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). Records are written to JSONL and mirrored to Weights & Biases, so a run can be analysed locally without a network dependency and reviewed remotely without a local environment. The reason ZVF and its decomposition are logged as separate fields rather than collapsed into a single starvation number is that the same aggregate fraction can describe opposite situations: a group of completions that are all correct and a group that are all wrong both contribute zero within-group variance, and the remedy for each is different. Logging the decomposition preserves that distinction at the point of collection instead of trying to recover it afterwards.

The quantity being logged is the group-relative advantage actually used by the runner,

A_i = (r_i − μ_g) / (σ_g + ε),  μ_g = (1/G) Σ_j r_j,  σ_g = sqrt((1/G) Σ_j (r_j − μ_g)²)

(source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). When σ_g = 0 every A_i is zero, so the whole group contributes no gradient through the advantage (surrogate) term. The parenthetical qualification matters and is preserved throughout this thesis: it is the advantage term that vanishes, not necessarily the total update, because the KL penalty and any auxiliary loss or clipping term are unaffected (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex).

Two further design choices distinguish this telemetry from conventional training curves. The first is that the raw per-group reward tensors are retained, not only the summaries computed from them. This makes a published number auditable by recomputation: any downstream diagnostic, such as `p2_collapse_analysis.py`, can reproduce a reported statistic from the stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The convention has been exercised. Study P2 recomputed three stored GSM8K group tensors from their raw values and obtained 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155, pooling to 0.1583 — figures reproduced from the tensors rather than read back from a log line (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The second is that the logging layer is treated as part of the apparatus and is expected to fail. An early Weights & Biases integration used the library's thread-unsafe global run object from multiple worker threads, which produced repeated "Run is finished" summary-write failures; the fix was to give each run its own process in `campaign.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The defect is recorded because telemetry that silently drops writes is indistinguishable from telemetry showing a healthy run, and the remedy — process-level isolation per run — is a structural property of the campaign rather than a patch.

## 4.3 Formalising the Zero-Variance Fraction

The Zero-Variance Fraction is the share of prompt groups in a batch whose reward variance is zero. Formally, for a batch B_t of prompt groups at step t, each group g contributing K sampled completions with rewards r_{g,1}, …, r_{g,K},

ZVF_t = (1 / |B_t|) Σ_{g ∈ B_t} 1[ Var̂(r_{g,1}, …, r_{g,K}) ≤ ε ]

where Var̂ is the unbiased sample variance and ε = 10⁻⁶ for rewards normalised to [0, 1] (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). A prompt-set formulation used elsewhere in the paper sources writes the same quantity as ZVF_t = (1 / |P_t|) Σ_{p ∈ P_t} 1[ Var_{c ~ C(p)} r(c) = 0 ], with C(p) the set of sampled completions for prompt p (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The reference implementation is deliberately small, so that the diagnostic cannot acquire hidden behaviour:

```python
def zvf(rewards_2d: np.ndarray, eps: float = 1e-6) -> float:
    # rewards_2d shape: [num_groups, K]
    var_per_group = rewards_2d.var(axis=-1, ddof=1)
    return float((var_per_group <= eps).mean())
```

(source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). Gradient utilisation is then defined as its complement, GU_t = 1 − ZVF_t (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The complement is a descriptive accounting identity, not a claim about optimiser behaviour: when ZVF_t = 1, every prompt has zero centred reward-contrast coefficient in this group-standardised runner, and the source is explicit that this "does not assert a zero total gradient for objectives with KL, auxiliary losses, clipping, or token masking" (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex).

Three properties make the definition more than a restatement of the training loop. First, it is non-tautological under a simple null: if completions were independent Bernoulli draws with per-prompt success probability p_g, then E[ZVF_t] = E_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1 − p_g)^K], a quantity that depends on the model's accuracy distribution and the group size rather than on the observed batch (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). The identity pass@G − p^G = 1 − ZVF follows algebraically under the same i.i.d.-Bernoulli model and is therefore reported as an algebraic remark rather than as evidence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Second, it was validated against that null on instrumented runs: a Qwen2.5-0.5B sweep over G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}, three seeds and 40 steps — twelve runs and 480 logged steps — found per-step observed ZVF correlating at r = 0.92 with the Bernoulli-null prediction p^G + (1 − p)^G, with mean ZVF of 0.84, 0.76, 0.69 and 0.63 at G = 2, 4, 8 and 16 respectively (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). Third, at frontier scale the same instrument was run on Qwen3-8B against held-out GSM8K through the Tinker managed API at G = 8, temperature 1.0, over 200 problems and three seeds, yielding a mean ZVF of 0.16 composed of 12.7% all-correct groups, 3.2% all-wrong groups and 84% mixed groups; the raw correlation with the mechanical null is approximately r = 0.93 with rank ρ ≈ 0.65, and the i.i.d. null over-predicts (approximately 0.28 against the observed 0.16), which is the expected direction when the reward signal is real rather than noise (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex).

That the quantity is portable across libraries is a separate, weaker claim, and the project states its limits explicitly. A normaliser at `scripts/zvf_compute_cross_framework.py` maps each framework's reward and mask fields onto the common definition — TRL's `rewards` with `batch_size / group_size` and `completion_mask`; Tinker's `rollout.reward` with `rollout.group_id` and `rollout.eos_mask`; OpenRLHF's `reward_score` with `prompt_index` and `attention_mask`; veRL's `data_source/reward` with `uid` and `response_mask` — and emits JSONL records of the form `{step, zvf, batch_reward_mean, n_groups, K}` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). The scope correction attached to that parser is that it normalises reward logs and does not make its runner canonical GRPO; because the project does not bundle raw per-group rollout JSONL for every framework, no fresh four-way pointwise ZVF overlay is claimed, only the narrower implementation-level portability claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). Sensitivity to the threshold ε is bounded: the reported ε-sensitivity is at most 0.4% (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). Boundary conditions are declared where the diagnostic does not apply: dense or continuous reward signals, K = 1, SFT-saturated baselines and non-outcome-reward RL all fall outside the setting in which the definition is meaningful (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex).

The methodological point of this section is that ZVF is a diagnostic and not a metric, and the project defends that position with its own negative evidence rather than with a general caution. Four observations support it. First, the same ZVF value can describe mastery or incapacity: in a 368-run Weights & Biases audit across seven models, ZVF sits at 0.95–0.97 at both reward extremes and at 0.25–0.29 at mid-range rewards of 0.35–0.50, so a high value is as consistent with a solved task as with an unsolved one (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Second, the pooled association between ZVF and performance, Pearson r = −0.769 (p = 0.0008) with Spearman ρ = −0.784 (p = 0.0005) over N = 15 experiments spanning five model families, is confounded by task type: tool-use experiments reach ZVF = 100% at approximately 0% reward while GSM8K experiments average ZVF = 8.5%, and within GSM8K the association weakens to r = +0.40 (p = 0.09, N = 19) and then to r = +0.13 (p = 0.79) when restricted to the seven model-varying runs (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). A statistic whose sign is set by which tasks are included in the pool is not a score. Third, ZVF does not track final accuracy (r ≈ 0.09, n = 12), and P2 reports only a weak association with final held-out outcome (ρ ≈ 0.27) while deliberately declining to promote ZVF to a causal or incrementally predictive statistic (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Fourth, no step-level p-value is computed on the 480 autocorrelated steps of the instrumented sweep, because treating correlated within-run steps as independent samples would manufacture significance (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). One further boundary is recorded for the same reason: a variance-mitigation table in the paper sources is a declared simulation projection and not evidence, and its rows should be read as synthetic rather than as independent library deployments (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex). The result of these choices is a diagnostic that is useful for describing where within a batch the learning signal is and is not, and that is explicitly not proposed as a quantity to be maximised, ranked or reported as a headline score.

## 4.4 The Eight-Study Portfolio

Semester 3 delivered the shared code and the initial experimental foundation; the Semester 4 programme — the research direction, the expanded experiments, the analysis and the P1–P8 papers — is the present author's solo continuation, and the roster records 206 analysis iterations behind the numbers reported in this thesis (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md). The portfolio is organised as eight studies, each asking one question and each carrying its own claim boundary. The questions and boundaries are stated here because they are methodological commitments: a study's question determines which comparisons it may make, and the boundary states what it declines to conclude.

P1, "Scaling Laws for GRPO Post-Training of Language Models: A Cross-Library, Cross-Scale Study", asks whether GRPO post-training outcomes scale with model scale in a way that can be fitted and extrapolated (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). Its answer is a conservative negative one: across more than seventy runs and seven RL libraries over five model families from 0.6B to 1T parameters, the cross-scale slope is flat over roughly 2.4 orders of magnitude; four of five frontier-scale traces have their saturation rate pinned at the fit boundary and are unidentifiable; the internal three-phase geometric check matches only 2 of 12 anchors; and the Nemotron-120B trace shows a zero-reward step fraction of 0.55 against at most 0.067 elsewhere (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). The frontier anchors — Qwen3-235B-MoE, DeepSeek-V3.1, Kimi-K2 and Nemotron-120B — were run through the Tinker managed API in a single-seed, descriptive-only regime, and the paper's claim boundary is a descriptive limits-and-identifiability audit rather than a positive scaling law; matched multi-seed cross-scale runs are an explicit non-claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex).

P2, "The Zero-Variance Fraction: A Descriptive Diagnostic for Signal Starvation in GRPO", asks how much of a training batch carries usable group-relative signal, and whether that fraction predicts the final outcome (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The study characterises ZVF descriptively, establishes the algebraic identity of §4.3 as algebraic rather than empirical, reports the weak association with final held-out outcome (ρ ≈ 0.27), and marks the variance-mitigation table as a simulation projection; the retired roster item that previously carried a causal framing now lives under `archive/absorbed/R02_main_zvf/` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex).

P3, "Group Size in GRPO: Contrast Density and the Bridge to DPO", asks how trainability varies with the group size G and whether GRPO admits an all-pairs preference interpretation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex). Two results anchor it. The first is that trainability varies non-monotonically with G, with intermediate sizes often outperforming both the smallest and the largest settings, although the data do not justify a universal optimum; measured equivalence tests extend to G = 16, retained held-out accuracy is essentially flat across G ∈ {2, …, 16} on the near-ceiling arithmetic task with a retention ratio of approximately 1.00 between G = 2 and G = 16 (a ratio, not an accuracy above 100%), and effective signal-to-noise rises only sublinearly with G, at approximately 52% of the √G ideal; no directly trained matched-budget G = 32 cell exists, and the G = 32 values come from an explicitly labelled reconstructed token-budget grid and are hypotheses rather than measurements (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex). A 505-task conditional utility audit using (1 − ZVF)/√G selects G = 4 over G = 5 by 0.00177 with a 95% bootstrap interval of [0.00082, 0.00270], conditional on cohort and objective (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex). The second is that GRPO admits an all-pairs preference interpretation resembling DPO, offered as an interpretation and not as a proven equivalence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex).

P4, "Length Bias and Held-Out Generalization in GRPO and Dr. GRPO", asks whether the verbosity trap associated with GRPO appears in the measured regime and whether either method changes held-out accuracy (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex). The headline is a carefully scoped negative: in the short-horizon GSM8K chain-of-thought regime with a 200-token generation cap, the mean Qwen3-8B GRPO gain over its pre-RL checkpoint on held-out data is small and not statistically significant, and GRPO and Dr. GRPO are indistinguishable at the seed budget used; neither inflates length within the capped regime, with pooled mean completion length drifting from 194.4 to 183.5 tokens. The 200-token cap bounds the claim, and the absence of the verbosity signature is the expected outcome when length is already controlled rather than a positive finding (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex).

P5, "Report the Stack, Not the Label: RL-for-LLM Results Are Stack-Conditioned", asks how much of a reported result is attributable to the stack rather than to the algorithm label, and what a reportable stack must contain (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). Three exhibits carry the argument: a nominally matched-configuration backend audit spanning final training rewards from 5.0% to 85.6%, a seventeen-fold same-label swing, in which the managed backend also silently pinned a different base checkpoint — undisclosed bundling being part of the exhibit rather than a footnote to it; a "DAPO" comparison in which true dynamic sampling on an open trainer produced a mean ZVF of 0.00 while the same label approximated on a closed stack through an asymmetric-clip surrogate produced a mean ZVF of 0.58; and a twelve-cell head-to-head of four labelled algorithms on one fixed stack landing within a 0.034 band (mean last-ten reward 0.710–0.744) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). The study proposes an eight-item minimum reportable stack — loss form; reference-policy and KL handling; sampler, backend and precision including base-checkpoint identity by revision or hash; per-step ZVF and gradient-utilisation trajectory; group-size schedule; a disjoint held-out split; decontamination plus a parser-robustness probe; and held-out pass@k curves alongside pass@1 — and formalises an RL-reproducibility threat model with flip-risk grades R0–R5 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). This is the standard this thesis is written to.

P6, "GRPO-Registry: A Machine-Readable Catalog of Group-Relative RL Stacks and Their Variant Deltas", asks whether stacks can be compared mechanically rather than through prose (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). It defines seven-field stack records corresponding to the MIN-REPORT-RL manifest block, a per-component variant-delta status, three variant-delta records verified against the DAPO, Dr. GRPO and GSPO papers, twenty seed stack entries, and a reference CLI with a 0–100 MIN-REPORT auditor badge spanning 43–96 across the seed entries and a `stackdiff` command that maps entries to an R0–R5 flip-risk verdict, flagging the open-versus-closed "DAPO" pair as R5 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). The study is explicitly a resource paper rather than a second ranking, and it cites the seventeen-fold exhibit only to motivate the schema (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex).

P7, "From Diagnostic to Controller: A Signal-Starvation Theory of GRPO and an Adaptive Group-Size Intervention", asks whether the diagnostic of §4.3 can be turned into a control signal (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). It models usable learning signal per prompt as S = p(1 − p)(1 − h_G(p)) with h_G(p) = p^G + (1 − p)^G, yielding three testable consequences: that the expected ZVF indicator equals p^G + (1 − p)^G; that larger G lowers ZVF; and that gradient magnitude tracks p(1 − p) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). The empirical checks reproduce the predicted U-shape in the 368-run audit; a model-by-group-size grid confirms the monotone G effect at interior accuracy (Qwen3-32B: 0.33, 0.18 and 0.08 at G = 4, 8 and 16); and a toy-scale open-backward-pass experiment finds corr(‖∇‖, p(1 − p)) = +0.71 on a 0.5B model with synthetic arithmetic, reported as directional only (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). The study promotes pairwise-contrast density, PCD = (G − 1)/G · E[p(1 − p)], as the control signal, and validates it by falsification: a micro-jitter intervention collapses batch ZVF from 0.158 to 0.000 while leaving PCD unchanged, showing that the two quantities are not measuring the same thing (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Adaptive group size matches the best fixed-recipe held-out gain (+0.575) at 186 rollouts, while DAPO-style dynamic sampling drives ZVF to 0.00 at a 45% rollout cost increase; in the 2,560-observation controller trace, 1,723 of 1,867 escalation events (92.3%) occur on all-correct groups and only 144 on all-wrong groups (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). The methodological commitment that governs this study is stated at its head: adaptive group size is not promoted, and no result establishes prospective superiority over static G = 16, Dr. GRPO or dynamic sampling (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

P8 occupies the eighth slot with a study outside the RL programme, "LLM vs. XGBoost in Credit-Card Fraud: Sensor and Scribe, Not Scorer" (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md). Its question is whether a large language model should be used as the fraud scorer or as a component around one, and it is answered with tabular baselines reported at AUC 0.7955, F1 0.356, precision 0.723 and recall 0.236 for the boosted-tree model, against a Qwen3.5-4B scorer at accuracy 0.792 with AUC 0.483, i.e. chance-level ranking, at roughly eighty-five times lower cost as a triage component (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/submissions/mtech-final-review/README.md; platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_abstract.tex). The roster evolved after the semester-4 title list was fixed, and the present position is recorded honestly rather than smoothed over: the fraud study is parked outside the ZVF thesis, contributes no RL evidence, and is retained for its measurement discipline; the active P8 slot in the current paper roster is an exploratory workshop artifact (source: platform_hybrid/paper/PAPERS_README.md). No result from P8 is used anywhere in this thesis to support an RL claim.

## 4.5 The Design of the E1–E14 Held-Out Evaluation Campaign

The evaluation campaign is the applied half of the programme and is deliberately narrow in a way that makes its results interpretable: it evaluates one actor against fourteen held-out benchmark suites under a receipt, budget and decontamination regime, instead of training many actors and comparing them. The single actor is the base model `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at commit `995ad96e…`, with adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal-tinker-cf0ad8c1…` at commit `64444133…`, in bfloat16 (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). One actor fixes the object of study: every reported per-suite figure describes the same frozen policy, so a difference between two suites is a property of the suite and not of a model swap. The design consequence is that the campaign cannot support model-comparison claims at all, and it makes none.

Evaluators are the suites' own scorers, used natively rather than reimplemented, and they are pinned by revision. The surviving native runners live under `zvf-program/flagship/`, including `e1_swe_bench_pro_full_eval.py`, `e11_verilog_eval_decontamination.py`, `eval_pavlov_sdab.py`, `eval_pavlov_xlam.py`, `mle_bench_eval.py` and `eval_apex_agents.py` (source: zvf-program/flagship/). The evaluation-side revisions pinned for the decontamination audit are LAB-Bench `998a8e0a40cf116c80e1b0e7a805ebb5fb9fa838`, AgentDojo `089ed468cf3ed0322acc66b0211f26d9d90dbf60` and Omni-MATH `40ba231d8f16e29ecd40e6407e2c8640145a8f62`, with the training-side envelope pinned at API-Bank-RLVR `bf67c42626f02c305514b1df16dcabc5fc616333` (597 rows, MIT) and SWE-Gym `bb94ed9e39bbeb96a7fcbfb533b80f25a7fd59cb` (2,438 train rows, MIT) (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Using the native scorer is a substantive choice and not a convenience: when the Omni-MATH lane produced two rows whose judged outputs were truncated before any equivalence verdict, the native scorer's omission of missing-verdict rows was adjudicated as correct official behaviour rather than a parser bug, and the lane closed with no re-judge because re-judging would deviate from the official scorer and generate new evidence with no effect on the score (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

Sampling is frozen rather than merely recorded: temperature 0.1, top-p 0.95, one sample per row, a maximum of 128 response tokens, with the service seed fixed where the provider supports it and otherwise recorded in the receipt (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Prompt and response limits are 1,200 and 128 tokens respectively, and the same source requires the strict verifier implementation to be hashed before paid work, with a perfect call defined as a verifier score of exactly 1.0 and no parser, target, split or verifier change permitted mid-campaign (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Freezing the sampler addresses the failure mode in which a benchmark score quietly improves because decoding became more favourable between runs; it also fixes the comparison between the base reference and the adapter, which is evaluated side by side on untouched manifests (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

The receipt model is freeze-before-paid-work, and it is the part of the campaign design that carries the most methodological weight. Before any paid phase a run must record an immutable base-model revision, tokenizer revision, container or environment digest and adapter revision; the selection slice and the disjoint holdout are re-created from a pinned dataset revision and sealed, are write-once, are not mounted in training, and the word "holdout" is reserved for a suite or manifest only once its split, task-ID, license, runtime and decontamination receipts are complete (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The selection slice is used for arm decisions and is therefore unsuitable for confirmatory claims; the final evaluation manifests are never used to choose an arm, alter a prompt, or re-run a failed suite with a more favourable split (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Tracking is verified before spend rather than after: an online Weights & Biases run must be initialised and its Tinker run identifier recorded before the first paid step, every step records loss, reward, configuration, seed, split identity and cumulative cost, and every checkpoint is published to Hugging Face with a unique repository or revision URL, with a failure of any of these stopping the run fail-closed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). If a receipt is missing, the phase is blocked rather than silently shortened (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). At handoff the protocol requires four separate evidence classes to be reported apart: completed receipts; operational and provenance receipts; prospective gates and budget projections; and admissible final results, the last existing only if every gate passed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

That last distinction has teeth in practice, and the campaign's disposition record shows it. The portfolio-level protocol defines a predeclared equal-domain macro average over sixteen domains, a conjunctive coverage gate, an integrity gate, a global improvement gate requiring the lower bound of a one-sided 95% interval for the portfolio difference to exceed zero, a domain no-regression guard with a predeclared five-percentage-point tolerance, and a safety guard bounding any violation increase by two percentage points — and it states that a cost cap may not be met by dropping domains or suites, and that a suite or domain which is missing, underpowered, contaminated or outside the cost cap must be labelled incomplete or blocked, "not negative and not positive" (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The portfolio training phase was never launched, so the campaign does not report a portfolio result; the recorded outcome is a set of per-lane evaluations of the one adapter, and the protocol's own instruction that a partial subset is not a portfolio result and may not be relabelled as one is honoured throughout (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

Lane dispositions are recorded in a seven-state taxonomy rather than as pass or fail: COMPLETE, for lanes with verified full-coverage results and no open gate (E8 public, E10 benign, E11); COMPLETE_TERMINAL_NOTE, for a lane complete under the native protocol with a recorded terminal note (E14 replacement); CLOSED_EXTERNAL, for lanes with no local or paid path, closed by written closure records (E3, E7, E8-private, E10-private, E12, E14-private); PENDING_QUOTA, for lanes gated on cloud quota resolution alone (E6, E9); AMENDMENT_ACCEPTED_LAUNCH_PENDING (E2, E13); REBUILD_READY_LAUNCH_PENDING (E1 wave10, E5 successor27); and CLOSED_PARTIAL (E4) (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md). The taxonomy exists so that a lane which has not been measured is not silently absorbed into a percentage. Carried alongside it is the project's evidence rule, stated in the ledger of record and repeated in every results artefact: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md). The rule is not cosmetic. The original-contract E1 lane and its replacement scope are different suites with different task populations, and a table that added them would produce a number that corresponds to no benchmark.

Decontamination is handled as a declared deterministic procedure with its own stated limits. The check hashes complete UTF-8 fields and then normalised fields with SHA-256; for fields of at least eight tokens it hashes each consecutive eight-token sequence and computes exact set Jaccard similarity for every training and evaluation pair sharing a shingle; and it tests whether an entire normalised evaluation field is a token-bounded contiguous substring of a training field (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Against the pinned training envelope, the per-suite inventories were 1,967 LAB-Bench tasks over 13,113 text fields with 5,105 eligible for eight-token near checks, 97 AgentDojo tasks over 71,966 fields with 9,382 eligible, and 4,428 Omni-MATH tasks over 13,284 fields with 9,394 eligible; the count of near pairs at or above 0.80 was zero in all three suites, and the maximum eight-gram Jaccard similarity was 0 for LAB-Bench and AgentDojo and 0.13414634146341464 for Omni-MATH, below the declared 0.80 threshold, with all 1,338 candidate pairs sharing an eight-gram examined (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Short exact matches of zero to three normalised tokens were retained and enumerated rather than dropped — the longest examples being seven characters in the LAB and Omni inventories and at most seventeen raw characters in AgentDojo, such as "Paris" and "Conference Room A" — and the audit states plainly that these collisions do not demonstrate duplication of a task but do make an unqualified "zero overlap" claim incorrect (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit's scope limits are recorded with equal care: it does not reconstruct the exact 512 consumed training records, because the dirty training transform and consumed task inventory were not recorded; it establishes a content comparison against the verified historical source envelope rather than proving global pretraining cleanliness, semantic disjointness, character-edit disjointness or image cleanliness; ASCII normalisation loses mathematical punctuation and non-ASCII text; and fields shorter than eight tokens fall outside the near-match and containment rule (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit itself is receipted: it re-verifies Weights & Biases run `bsv8vx04` against `training_identity_verified.json`, matches the 23,733 original source-field document hashes to the historical E11 receipt, adds 2,507 decoded nested API-Bank strings to reach 26,240 source documents, and records 43,977,748 bytes downloaded against a 500 MiB cap, with the checker not having enumerated the roughly 2.58 billion possible document pairs (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md).

Two features of the campaign's execution are methodological rather than incidental. The first is that a lane stops when its receipts are incomplete, and the record says why. A single inventory pass on 2026-09-21 checked every blocked lane live and found concrete, named failures: E1 lacked W&B authentication, a sealed reservation and a live actor endpoint; E2 awaited an owner IAM binding that only the project owner can create; E5 lacked both a bound authorization receipt and any real native runtime adapter in the repository; E13's request remained a draft with launch authorisation false; E6 and E9 were refused by cloud quota in both regions (source: outputs/UNBLOCK_CARRYOUT_2026-09-21.md). Recording a lane as blocked-with-reason, rather than reporting a shortened run, is the same principle as the terminal-state taxonomy applied to execution. The second is that the campaign's largest lane is reported as a negative result. E1 on SWE-bench Pro produced 2/731 resolutions, or 0.274% pass@1, with 731 terminal generations, 713 native evaluations, and 14 generation failures and 4 artifact losses retained in the denominator rather than excluded (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A separate authorized rerun of the base model on the E4 finance suite completed 100 of 100 trials with a mean reward of 0.0, and the audit of all one hundred trajectories attributed the result to a mechanism rather than to reasoning: agents terminate after two to fourteen steps with a median of about six and no deliverables, so the verifier short-circuits to zero, and 27 or more trajectories end in role-token degeneration; the source's own reading is that this zero measures tool-dialogue collapse in the base model and not finance reasoning (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). Reporting those outcomes as findings about the actor, with the mechanism identified, is the behaviour the whole apparatus is designed to produce.

The campaign also carries its integrity accounting in the same register. Eleven of eleven deterministic checks passed on 2026-09-19, covering exact divisions, receipt presence, sealed-artifact hash equality and lost-source absence probes; the recorded spend was $5.6301 against the $50 additional cap counted at that date, with later budget-mode records moving to an unlimited mode whose per-run resource and timeout bounds remain in force (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A judgment layer was added for classification, faithfulness and value-ordering questions and then partly invalidated by an external service outage: both blocker-classification batteries returned degenerate distributions after the incident, their receipts are retained as incident evidence, and the per-lane blocker column therefore rests on document evidence and not on model judgment (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A design rule was banked from the healthy window and is followed in this thesis: arithmetic and string lookups stay in code, and model judgment is reserved for classification, faithfulness and value ordering, because a parity probe was answered incorrectly even while the service was healthy (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

What the campaign does not claim follows directly from the design. It does not claim improvement over any baseline anywhere, because no lane carries a test establishing it: the campaign's role in this thesis is to fix the evaluation protocol, the receipt discipline and the disposition taxonomy that any future improvement claim would have to satisfy. It does not claim that the fourteen suites constitute a portfolio result. It does not pool original-contract with replacement-scope figures, and it does not average across suites. Its one trained actor means it says nothing about model comparisons. And where the record is incomplete — a lost execution source, an unanswered external access request, a run whose budget exceeded the authorised envelope — the incompleteness is written into the ledger rather than resolved by inference.
