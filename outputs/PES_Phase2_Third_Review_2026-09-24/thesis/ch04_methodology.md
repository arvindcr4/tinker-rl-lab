# 4. Proposed Methodology

This chapter sets out the methodology of the work rather than its results. It states four things in order: the design commitment to attribution and the four de-confound pillars that operationalise it (§4.1); the per-step telemetry that makes a run's internal state observable at the granularity at which the mechanism acts (§4.2); the formalisation of the Zero-Variance Fraction as a descriptive diagnostic rather than a metric (§4.3); the eight-study portfolio P1–P8 and the question each study asks (§4.4); and the design of the E1–E14 evaluation campaign, including its single trained actor, pinned native evaluators, frozen sampling, receipt model, terminal-state taxonomy and decontamination handling (§4.5). Throughout, the reporting discipline is part of the method: figures are given only where a repository file supports them, and where a lane produced no admissible result, it is recorded as blocked rather than written around.

## 4.1 The Design Commitment to Attribution

The premise of the project is that an RL-for-LLM result is not identified by the name of its algorithm. Two runs both labelled GRPO may differ in loss form, reference-policy handling, sampler, precision, base-checkpoint identity, data order and group-size schedule, and the label alone does not say which of those differences produced the outcome. The project's response is a design commitment rather than a post-hoc covariate adjustment: hold the whole experimental stack fixed and vary exactly one factor at a time, so that an observed difference is attributable to the factor that was varied rather than to the several things that changed alongside it (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). This commitment is the methodological spine of the whole thesis, and it has a practical corollary that shapes every experiment reported later: a study is defined by the single axis it varies and is only interpretable to the extent that every other axis was genuinely pinned.

The commitment is implemented in a unified launcher, `platform_local/unified/launcher.py`, which consumes a single configuration object and dispatches it to thin per-framework adapters — `trl_integrations/`, `verl/`, `platform_modal/openrlhf/`, `platform_hybrid/skyrl/` and `platform_tinker/atropos/` — with the reference GRPO loop implemented once in `platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The reward grader and the decoding path are shared code. That sharing is the load-bearing part of the design: because the grader and the sampler are the same objects across back-ends, a difference in outcome between two frameworks is attributable to the framework, and not to a divergent reward parser or a differently configured decoder masquerading as an algorithmic difference. The commitment also bounds what a result may claim. A completed run supports a statement about that stack, at that revision, with that grader; it does not license a statement about "GRPO" in the abstract, and the project's reporting standard (§4.4, P5) is built around that distinction.

Four pillars carry the commitment, each holding the remainder of the stack fixed while varying one factor (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/submissions/mtech-final-review/build_review_pptx.py, slide 7):

**Table 4.1 — The four de-confound pillars.**

| Pillar | Factor isolated | Design |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · PPO versus GRPO, same stack | Advantage estimator: group baseline versus learned value head | Same model, runner, clipped surrogate and step budget on a saturated 0.5B addition task; the arms also differ in prompts per step (16 × 8 versus 128) |
| 2 · Zero-Variance Fraction | Signal starvation and its confounders | Per-step ZVF logged on every run; the nine-row method panel, vanilla GRPO row included, is a declared simulation projection (§6.3.2) |
| 3 · Trainability and group size | Contrast density against the variance knob | G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} × seeds, held out |
| 4 · Held-out generalisation | Whether GRPO adds real capability | Dr. GRPO versus GRPO, pre-to-post, McNemar |

Pillar 1 asks whether a reported GRPO advantage survives when the advantage estimator is the main thing that changes. The comparison is designed on `Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B` two-digit addition over 40 steps and five paired seeds, evaluated on 200 prompts drawn fresh from the training generator rather than from a disjoint split, and the exact loss form of both arms is in Table 4.2 (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/samestack_ppo_grpo.json). Because that task is near ceiling, the design can bound an estimator effect but cannot separate "no effect" from "no headroom"; the result is reported in Chapter 6 §6.2.3. The Qwen3-8B row in the project's own effect-size table is excluded from Pillar 1 as a provenance conflict and is not treated as an estimate (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex; its status is recorded in §6.2.3). Pillar 2 turns a run's internal signal state into a first-class measurement; it is formalised in §4.3. Pillar 3 varies the group size G while holding model, data, reward and schedule fixed, and is the axis on which the preference-density argument of P3 rests. Pillar 4 asks the question the other three cannot answer — whether any of this changes held-out behaviour — by comparing a pre-RL checkpoint with its post-RL successor on data the training loop never saw.

The headline configuration of the internal report sources is LoRA rank 32, Adam with β₁ = 0.9 and β₂ = 0.95, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴, and the seed set {42, 123, 456, 789, 1024} (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). It is not held fixed across the programme. The canonical dispatch matrix uses rank 16 and learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁶, the stack probe uses 1 × 10⁻⁵, the P4 scale extension uses rank 4, and the Tinker runners default to 3 × 10⁻⁵; Table 4.2 lists the values each study actually used. The instrumented 79-run roster described in that source spans the Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Llama-3.x, DeepSeek-V3.1 and Nemotron-120B families, from 0.6B to approximately 671B total parameters, including mixture-of-experts anchors at the frontier end of that range (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex); the wider P1 cross-scale roster adds a 1T anchor (Kimi-K2) and so spans 0.6B to 1T (Chapter 6 §6.1; source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). The statistical apparatus is declared in the shared methods source but was not pre-registered (Chapter 9 §9.2; the analysis log records 206 iterations). It consists of Welch t-tests and Mann–Whitney U tests for two-sample contrasts; Cohen's d with Hedges–Olkin intervals; 95% bootstrap confidence intervals with 10,000 resamples; rliable aggregate metrics (interquartile mean, median and optimality gap); and Bonferroni and Benjamini–Hochberg corrections over a declared test family (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Power was computed rather than assumed: at 80% power the minimum detectable effect for an equal-n comparison of five seeds against five is d_min = 2.024, improving to d_min = 1.530 when a five-seed group is compared against a pooled group of fifteen (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Those figures are a standing reminder of how coarse a five-seed design is, and the project reports several null results that are null precisely because the design could not have detected anything smaller.

Two limitations belong in this section rather than in a later discussion. First, the framework roster is broader than the framework evidence: although seven RL libraries are named in the study design, only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs, and the veRL and OpenRLHF entries in `framework_comparison.json` are dry-run placeholders rather than measurements (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Cross-library claims are therefore narrower than the roster implies. Second, the variance decomposition that motivates the whole attribution commitment is itself four separate marginal one-way ANOVAs — library/framework, training algorithm, model family and model scale — each run against the same 42 experiments with valid performance observations (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). On a corpus where the factors are collinear a marginal η² absorbs the variance of each factor's correlates, so the design reads the decomposition only as evidence that framework and algorithm vary a great deal across this corpus, which is what motivates holding them fixed — not as an estimate of any factor's independent contribution, which a marginal one-way analysis on a non-orthogonal design cannot identify. The values are reported in Chapter 6 §6.1. The historical twenty-test multiple-comparison family that accompanies it pre-dates the Tier-A/B/C partition and is retained as a reproducibility audit trail, explicitly not as a corrected survival claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex; §6.1).

## 4.2 Per-Step Telemetry

Attribution is only as good as the run's observability. Every training step therefore emits a record containing the Zero-Variance Fraction; gradient utilisation; the collapse decomposition that separates all-correct from all-wrong zero-variance groups; and, alongside them, reward, loss, entropy, completion length and the KL divergence to the reference policy (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). Records are written to JSONL and mirrored to Weights & Biases, so a run can be analysed locally without a network dependency and reviewed remotely without a local environment. The reason ZVF and its decomposition are logged as separate fields rather than collapsed into a single starvation number is that the same aggregate fraction can describe opposite situations: a group of completions that are all correct and a group that are all wrong both contribute zero within-group variance, and the remedy for each is different. Logging the decomposition preserves that distinction at the point of collection instead of trying to recover it afterwards.

The quantity being logged is the group-relative advantage actually used by the runner:

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon},\qquad \mu_g = \frac{1}{G}\sum_j r_j,\qquad \sigma_g = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_j (r_j - \mu_g)^2}$$

This form is taken from the Phase-1 report (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). Throughout this thesis $\sigma_g$ is the population standard deviation, with 1/G as written above. Every GRPO-style runner in Table 4.2 computes it that way. Two places use the unbiased 1/(G − 1) form instead: the reference `zvf()` of §4.3, whose `ddof=1` variance does not change a ≤ ε zero-variance test, and the batch-wide whitening of the PPO arm in the same-stack run. The ZVF-definition figure (Figure \ref{fig:zvfdef}) also shows the unbiased form, which gives $\sigma_g \approx 0.535$ for its example group where the population form gives 0.5. When $\sigma_g = 0$ every $A_i$ is zero, so the whole group contributes no gradient through the advantage term. Whether anything else acts depends on the loss form: a KL penalty, auxiliary loss or clipping term would be unaffected, but none of the runners in Table 4.2 has a KL penalty or a reference policy, so for them a zero-variance group contributes exactly zero gradient.

Two further design choices distinguish this telemetry from conventional training curves. The first is that the raw per-group reward tensors are retained, not only the summaries computed from them. This makes a published number auditable by recomputation: any downstream diagnostic, such as `p2_collapse_analysis.py`, can reproduce a reported statistic from the stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The convention has been exercised: study P2 recomputes three stored GSM8K group tensors (Qwen3-8B sampling runs, G = 8) from their raw values rather than reading the figures back from a log line, and the recomputed values are reported in Chapter 6 §6.3.1 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The second is that the logging layer is treated as part of the apparatus and is expected to fail. An early Weights & Biases integration used the library's thread-unsafe global run object from multiple worker threads, which produced repeated "Run is finished" summary-write failures; the fix was to give each run its own process in `campaign.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The defect is recorded because telemetry that silently drops writes is indistinguishable from telemetry showing a healthy run, and the remedy — process-level isolation per run — is a structural property of the campaign rather than a patch.

## 4.3 Formalising the Zero-Variance Fraction

The Zero-Variance Fraction is the share of prompt groups in a batch whose reward variance is zero. Formally, for a batch $B_t$ of prompt groups at step $t$, each group $g$ contributing $K$ sampled completions with rewards $r_{g,1}, \ldots, r_{g,K}$,

$$\mathrm{ZVF}_t = \frac{1}{|B_t|} \sum_{g \in B_t} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(r_{g,1}, \ldots, r_{g,K}) \le \varepsilon\right]$$

where $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}$ is the unbiased sample variance and ε = 10⁻⁶ for rewards normalised to [0, 1] (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). A prompt-set formulation used elsewhere in the internal report sources writes the same quantity as $\mathrm{ZVF}_t = \frac{1}{|P_t|} \sum_{p \in P_t} \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{Var}_{c \sim C(p)}\, r(c) = 0]$, with $C(p)$ the set of sampled completions for prompt p (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The reference implementation is deliberately small, so that the diagnostic cannot acquire hidden behaviour (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex):

```
def zvf(rewards_2d: np.ndarray, eps: float = 1e-6) -> float:
    # rewards_2d shape: [num_groups, K]
    var_per_group = rewards_2d.var(axis=-1, ddof=1)
    return float((var_per_group <= eps).mean())
```

Gradient utilisation is then defined as its complement, $\mathrm{GU}_t = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}_t$ (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The complement is a descriptive accounting identity, not a claim about optimiser behaviour. Its scope must be read against the runner actually used, so the loss form of every study is listed in Table 4.2, read from the runner code rather than from the internal reports' prose. Three facts in the table govern how ZVF is interpreted. First, no runner in the programme has a KL penalty or a frozen reference policy, so for every study a zero-variance group contributes exactly zero gradient through the policy term; the only escapes are optimiser momentum from earlier steps and the per-batch normalisers. Second, the Tinker runners, including the one that trained the E1–E14 actor, are REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline: the loss is $-A_i$ times the summed token log-probability, averaged over completions, with no importance ratio and no clipping, and this thesis calls them that rather than GRPO in the strict sense. Third, the runners labelled GRPO are not one loss: the same-stack and Dr. GRPO studies use a clipped ratio with two inner epochs, the Tinker runners do not, and the Phase-1 four-method run divides by no σ even in its "grpo" arm. The quoted sentence from paper_P8_workshop.tex, which disclaims objectives with KL, auxiliary losses, clipping or token masking, therefore applies to none of the measured configurations.

Three properties make the definition more than a restatement of the training loop. First, it is non-tautological under a simple null: if completions were independent Bernoulli draws with per-prompt success probability $p_g$, then $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ZVF}_t] = \mathbb{E}_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1-p_g)^K]$, a quantity that depends on the model's accuracy distribution and the group size rather than on the observed batch (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). The identity $\mathrm{pass@}G - p^G = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}$ follows algebraically under the same i.i.d.-Bernoulli model and is therefore reported as an algebraic remark rather than as evidence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Second, it is checked against that null on instrumented runs: a Qwen2.5-0.5B sweep over G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}, three seeds and 40 steps — twelve runs and 480 logged steps — compares per-step observed ZVF with the Bernoulli-null prediction $p^G + (1-p)^G$ (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). Because the null for each step is computed from the same step's mean reward, a high correlation is partly mechanical, so the comparison is designed as a shape check rather than a validation, and the baseline used is the per-step average of the closed form (results in Chapter 6 §6.3.3). Third, at larger scale the same instrument is run on Qwen3-8B against GSM8K test problems through the Tinker managed API at G = 8, temperature 1.0, over 200 problems and three seeds (sampling only, no training; these are the same three stored tensors whose ZVF P2 recomputes), with the zero-variance groups decomposed into all-correct and all-wrong and compared with the same null (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex; results in Chapter 6 §6.3.3). Deviations from the null in either direction are not read as evidence about dependence between completions.

That the quantity is portable across libraries is a separate, weaker claim, and the project states its limits explicitly. A normaliser at `platform_modal/scripts/zvf_compute_cross_framework.py` maps each framework's reward and mask fields onto the common definition — TRL's `rewards` with `batch_size / group_size` and `completion_mask`; Tinker's `rollout.reward` with `rollout.group_id` and `rollout.eos_mask`; OpenRLHF's `reward_score` with `prompt_index` and `attention_mask`; veRL's `data_source/reward` with `uid` and `response_mask` — and emits JSONL records of the form `{step, zvf, batch_reward_mean, n_groups, K}` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). The scope correction attached to that parser is that it normalises reward logs and does not make its runner canonical GRPO; because the project does not bundle raw per-group rollout JSONL for every framework, no fresh four-way pointwise ZVF overlay is claimed, only the narrower implementation-level portability claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). The threshold ε is fixed in advance, and its sensitivity is reported with the P2 results in Chapter 6 §6.3.1 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). Boundary conditions are declared where the diagnostic does not apply: dense or continuous reward signals, K = 1, SFT-saturated baselines and non-outcome-reward RL all fall outside the setting in which the definition is meaningful (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex).

The methodological point of this section is that ZVF is a diagnostic and not a metric. The design commits to that position before the results rather than inferring it from them, and it takes four precautions. First, because the statistic is unsigned, a high value is read as consistent with either a solved or an unsolved task and never as a score in one direction; the 368-run audit that shows both extremes is reported in Chapter 7 §7.1 and §7.3 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Second, a pooled association between ZVF and performance is always broken down by task type before it is read, because the task mix of a pool can set the sign of the association (results in Chapter 6 §6.3.4). Third, the association with final accuracy and with final held-out outcome is tested, but ZVF is not promoted to a causal or incrementally predictive statistic whatever that test returns (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex; results in Chapter 6 §6.3.4). Fourth, no step-level p-value is computed on the 480 autocorrelated steps of the instrumented sweep, because treating correlated within-run steps as independent samples would manufacture significance (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). One further boundary is recorded for the same reason: a variance-mitigation table in the internal report sources is a declared simulation projection and not evidence, and its rows are read as synthetic rather than as independent library deployments (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex; §6.3.2). The result of these choices is a diagnostic that is useful for describing where within a batch the learning signal is and is not, and that is explicitly not proposed as a quantity to be maximised, ranked or reported as a headline score.

## 4.4 The Eight-Study Portfolio

Semester 3 delivered the shared code and the initial experimental foundation; the Semester 4 programme — the research direction, the expanded experiments, the analysis and the P1–P8 internal reports — is the present author's solo continuation, and the roster records 206 analysis iterations behind the numbers reported in this thesis (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md). The portfolio is organised as eight studies, each written up as an unpublished internal report, each asking one question and each carrying its own claim boundary; the titles quoted below are the internal reports' working titles. This section states the questions and designs; the results are in Chapters 6–7 and, for P8, Appendix F. The questions and boundaries are stated here because they are methodological commitments: a study's question determines which comparisons it may make, and the boundary states what it declines to conclude.

P1, "Scaling Laws for GRPO Post-Training of Language Models: A Cross-Library, Cross-Scale Study", asks whether GRPO post-training outcomes scale with model scale in a way that can be fitted and extrapolated (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). Its evidence base is more than seventy runs across seven RL libraries and five model families from 0.6B to 1T parameters, fitted with saturating forms and tested for a cross-scale slope. The frontier anchors — Qwen3-235B-MoE, DeepSeek-V3.1, Kimi-K2 and Nemotron-120B — were run through the Tinker managed API in a single-seed, descriptive-only regime, so the report's claim boundary is a descriptive limits-and-identifiability audit rather than a positive scaling law; matched multi-seed cross-scale runs are an explicit non-claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 6 §6.2.

P2, "The Zero-Variance Fraction: A Descriptive Diagnostic for Signal Starvation in GRPO", asks how much of a training batch carries usable group-relative signal, and whether that fraction predicts the final outcome (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The study characterises ZVF descriptively, treats the identity of §4.3 as algebraic rather than empirical, tests the association with final held-out outcome without promoting ZVF to a predictive statistic, and marks the variance-mitigation table as a simulation projection; the retired roster item that previously carried a causal framing now lives under `archive/absorbed/R02_main_zvf/` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 6 §6.3.

P3, "Group Size in GRPO: Contrast Density and the Bridge to DPO", asks how trainability varies with the group size G and whether GRPO admits an all-pairs preference interpretation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex). Its measured sweep covers G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} on the near-ceiling arithmetic task, with seed-level equivalence tests against G = 2 and a one-way test of held-out accuracy across G. No directly trained matched-budget G = 32 cell exists, so every G = 32 value comes from an explicitly labelled reconstructed token-budget grid and is held as a hypothesis rather than a measurement. The claim boundary is that the data may not be read as a universal optimum, and the all-pairs preference reading resembling DPO is offered as an interpretation and not as a proven equivalence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex). Results, including a conditional utility audit over 505 tasks, are in Chapter 6 §6.4.

P4, "Length Bias and Held-Out Generalization in GRPO and Dr. GRPO", asks whether the verbosity trap associated with GRPO appears in the measured regime and whether either method changes held-out accuracy (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex). Its controlled comparison runs GRPO against Dr. GRPO on a near-ceiling arithmetic task and in a short-horizon GSM8K chain-of-thought regime with a 200-token generation cap, alongside a multi-seed Qwen3-8B pre-RL versus post-RL held-out control. The 200-token cap bounds the claim: under a cap that already controls length, absence of the verbosity signature would be the expected outcome rather than a positive finding (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 6 §6.5.

P5, "Report the Stack, Not the Label: RL-for-LLM Results Are Stack-Conditioned", asks how much of a reported result is attributable to the stack rather than to the algorithm label, and what a reportable stack must contain (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). Three exhibits carry the argument: a nominally matched-configuration backend audit, a same-label "DAPO" comparison between true dynamic sampling on an open trainer and an asymmetric-clip surrogate on a closed stack, and a twelve-cell head-to-head of four labelled algorithms on one fixed stack (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex); their results are in Chapter 7 §7.1. The study proposes an eight-item minimum reportable stack (seven run-manifest fields and one evaluation item) — loss form; reference-policy and KL handling; sampler, backend and precision including base-checkpoint identity by revision or hash; per-step ZVF and gradient-utilisation trajectory; group-size schedule; a disjoint held-out split; decontamination plus a parser-robustness probe; and held-out pass@k curves alongside pass@1 — and formalises an RL-reproducibility threat model with flip-risk grades R0–R5 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). This is the standard this thesis is written to.

P6, "GRPO-Registry: A Machine-Readable Catalog of Group-Relative RL Stacks and Their Variant Deltas", asks whether stacks can be compared mechanically rather than through prose (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). It defines stack records carrying the seven manifest fields of the MIN-REPORT-RL standard, a per-component variant-delta status, three variant-delta records verified against the DAPO, Dr. GRPO and GSPO papers, twenty seed stack entries, and a reference CLI with a 0–100 MIN-REPORT auditor badge and a `stackdiff` command that maps a pair of entries to an R0–R5 flip-risk verdict (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). The study is explicitly a resource study rather than a second ranking, and it cites P5's seventeen-fold exhibit only to motivate the schema (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 7 §7.2.

P7, "From Diagnostic to Controller: A Signal-Starvation Theory of GRPO and an Adaptive Group-Size Intervention", asks whether the diagnostic of §4.3 can be turned into a control signal (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). It models usable learning signal per prompt as $S = p(1-p)(1-h_G(p))$ with $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$, yielding three testable consequences: that the expected ZVF indicator equals $p^G + (1-p)^G$; that larger G lowers ZVF; and that gradient magnitude tracks p(1 − p) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Each consequence is paired with a measurement that could falsify it: the 368-run audit, a model-by-group-size grid, and a toy-scale open-backward-pass experiment whose result is admitted as directional only (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). The study promotes pairwise-contrast density, PCD = (G − 1)/G · E[p(1 − p)], as the candidate control signal, subject to a micro-jitter falsification test against ZVF, and then audits an adaptive group-size controller against fixed recipes and DAPO-style dynamic sampling on a four-arm open trainer and in a counterfactual controller trace (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 7 §7.3. The methodological commitment that governs this study is stated at its head: adaptive group size is not promoted, and no result establishes prospective superiority over static G = 16, Dr. GRPO or dynamic sampling (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

P8 occupies the eighth slot with a study outside the RL programme, "LLM vs. XGBoost in Credit-Card Fraud: Sensor and Scribe, Not Scorer" (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md). Its question is whether a large language model should be used as the fraud scorer or as a component around one, and it is designed as a comparison of a boosted-tree model with a Qwen3.5-4B scorer on held-out splits, with the scorer's cost as a triage component recorded alongside its accuracy (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/submissions/mtech-final-review/README.md; platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_abstract.tex). Results are in Appendix F. The roster evolved after the semester-4 title list was fixed, and the present position is recorded as it stands: the fraud study is parked outside the ZVF thesis, contributes no RL evidence, and is retained for its measurement discipline; the active P8 slot in the current report roster is an exploratory workshop artifact (source: platform_hybrid/paper/PAPERS_README.md). No result from P8 is used anywhere in this thesis to support an RL claim.

## 4.5 The Design of the E1–E14 Held-Out Evaluation Campaign

The evaluation campaign is the applied half of the programme and is deliberately narrow in a way that makes its results interpretable: it evaluates one actor against fourteen benchmark suites that were not part of its training data under a receipt, budget and decontamination regime, instead of training many actors and comparing them. The single actor is the base model `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at commit `995ad96e…`, with adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal-tinker-cf0ad8c1…` at commit `64444133…`, in bfloat16 (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). How that adapter was trained is reconstructed in Chapter 8 §8.1.1. "Held-out" is used here in a narrow sense: the actor was trained only on API-Bank-RLVR and SWE-Gym, and none of the fourteen suites is in that training data. It does not mean that every suite was decontamination-checked. The content check below covers LAB-Bench, AgentDojo and Omni-MATH, the E11 receipt records its own verified decontamination, and the E10 receipt itself declares `heldout_claim: false` (sources: checkpoints/grpo/pavlov_portfolio_api_swegym_qwen36_20260809_seed809.json; outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/agentdojo_native_receipt.json; outputs/modal_e1_e14/2026-08-16/e11_full_receipt.json). One actor fixes the object of study: every reported per-suite figure describes the same frozen policy, so a difference between two suites is a property of the suite and not of a model swap. The design consequence is that the campaign cannot support model-comparison claims at all, and it makes none.

Evaluators are the suites' own scorers, used natively rather than reimplemented, and they are pinned by revision. The surviving native runners live under `zvf-program/flagship/`, including `e1_swe_bench_pro_full_eval.py`, `e11_verilog_eval_decontamination.py`, `eval_pavlov_sdab.py`, `eval_pavlov_xlam.py`, `mle_bench_eval.py` and `eval_apex_agents.py` (source: zvf-program/flagship/). The evaluation-side revisions pinned for the decontamination audit are LAB-Bench `998a8e0a40cf116c80e1b0e7a805ebb5fb9fa838`, AgentDojo `089ed468cf3ed0322acc66b0211f26d9d90dbf60` and Omni-MATH `40ba231d8f16e29ecd40e6407e2c8640145a8f62`, with the training-side envelope pinned at API-Bank-RLVR `bf67c42626f02c305514b1df16dcabc5fc616333` (597 rows, MIT) and SWE-Gym `bb94ed9e39bbeb96a7fcbfb533b80f25a7fd59cb` (2,438 train rows, MIT) (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Using the native scorer is a substantive choice and not a convenience: when the Omni-MATH lane produced two rows whose judged outputs were truncated before any equivalence verdict, the native scorer's omission of missing-verdict rows was adjudicated as correct official behaviour rather than a parser bug, and the lane closed with no re-judge because re-judging would deviate from the official scorer and generate new evidence with no effect on the score (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

For the training-selection protocol, sampling is frozen rather than merely recorded: temperature 0.1, top-p 0.95, one sample per row, a maximum of 128 response tokens, with the service seed fixed where the provider supports it and otherwise recorded in the receipt (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Prompt and response limits are 1,200 and 128 tokens respectively, and the same source requires the strict verifier implementation to be hashed before paid work, with a perfect call defined as a verifier score of exactly 1.0 and no parser, target, split or verifier change permitted mid-campaign (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Freezing the sampler addresses the failure mode in which a benchmark score quietly improves because decoding became more favourable between runs; it also fixes the comparison between the base reference and the adapter, which is evaluated side by side on untouched manifests (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The 128-token setting belongs to that protocol only. The E1–E14 lanes were decoded under each suite's own settings, which range from 1,024 to 8,192 output tokens at temperatures 0 to 0.2, and are recorded per lane in the serving table of Chapter 8 §8.1.2. The actor's own training run also departed from the protocol in several fields (§8.1.1).

The receipt model is freeze-before-paid-work, and it is the part of the campaign design that carries the most methodological weight. Before any paid phase a run must record an immutable base-model revision, tokenizer revision, container or environment digest and adapter revision; the selection slice and the disjoint holdout are re-created from a pinned dataset revision and sealed, are write-once, are not mounted in training, and the word "holdout" is reserved for a suite or manifest only once its split, task-ID, license, runtime and decontamination receipts are complete (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The selection slice is used for arm decisions and is therefore unsuitable for confirmatory claims; the final evaluation manifests are never used to choose an arm, alter a prompt, or re-run a failed suite with a more favourable split (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Tracking is verified before spend rather than after: an online Weights & Biases run must be initialised and its Tinker run identifier recorded before the first paid step, every step records loss, reward, configuration, seed, split identity and cumulative cost, and every checkpoint is published to Hugging Face with a unique repository or revision URL, with a failure of any of these stopping the run fail-closed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). If a receipt is missing, the phase is blocked rather than silently shortened (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). At handoff the protocol requires four separate evidence classes to be reported apart: completed receipts; operational and provenance receipts; prospective gates and budget projections; and admissible final results, the last existing only if every gate passed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

That last distinction has teeth in practice, and the campaign's disposition record shows it. The portfolio-level protocol defines a predeclared equal-domain macro average over sixteen domains, a conjunctive coverage gate, an integrity gate, a global improvement gate requiring the lower bound of a one-sided 95% interval for the portfolio difference to exceed zero, a domain no-regression guard with a predeclared five-percentage-point tolerance, and a safety guard bounding any violation increase by two percentage points — and it states that a cost cap may not be met by dropping domains or suites, and that a suite or domain which is missing, underpowered, contaminated or outside the cost cap must be labelled incomplete or blocked, "not negative and not positive" (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The portfolio training phase was never launched, so the campaign does not report a portfolio result; the recorded outcome is a set of per-lane evaluations of the one adapter, and the protocol's own instruction that a partial subset is not a portfolio result and may not be relabelled as one is honoured throughout (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

Lane dispositions are recorded in a seven-state taxonomy rather than as pass or fail: COMPLETE, for lanes with verified full-coverage results and no open gate (E8 public, E10 benign, E11); COMPLETE_TERMINAL_NOTE, for a lane complete under the native protocol with a recorded terminal note (E14 replacement); CLOSED_EXTERNAL, for lanes with no local or paid path, closed by written closure records (E3, E7, E8-private, E10-private, E12, E14-private); PENDING_QUOTA, for lanes gated on cloud quota resolution alone (E6, E9); AMENDMENT_ACCEPTED_LAUNCH_PENDING (E2, E13); REBUILD_READY_LAUNCH_PENDING (E1 wave10, E5 successor27); and CLOSED_PARTIAL (E4) (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md). Those are the states of the 19 September ledger. The three pending states were emptied on 27 September, when the E1, E2, E5, E6, E9 and E13 replacement scopes were run to completion (§8.4). This report assigns them COMPLETE on coverage, with two qualifications recorded against the label: E6 still has 108 tasks awaiting a judge, and E1's coverage spans two contracts reported separately. The taxonomy exists so that a lane which has not been measured is not silently absorbed into a percentage. Carried alongside it is the project's evidence rule, stated in the ledger of record and repeated in every results artefact: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md). The rule is not cosmetic. The original-contract E1 lane and its replacement scope are different suites with different task populations, and a table that added them would produce a number that corresponds to no benchmark.

Decontamination is handled as a declared deterministic procedure with its own stated limits. The check hashes complete UTF-8 fields and then normalised fields with SHA-256; for fields of at least eight tokens it hashes each consecutive eight-token sequence and computes exact set Jaccard similarity for every training and evaluation pair sharing a shingle; and it tests whether an entire normalised evaluation field is a token-bounded contiguous substring of a training field (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Against the pinned training envelope, the per-suite inventories were 1,967 LAB-Bench tasks over 13,113 text fields with 5,105 eligible for eight-token near checks, 97 AgentDojo tasks over 71,966 fields with 9,382 eligible, and 4,428 Omni-MATH tasks over 13,284 fields with 9,394 eligible; the count of near pairs at or above 0.80 was zero in all three suites, and the maximum eight-gram Jaccard similarity was 0 for LAB-Bench and AgentDojo and 0.13414634146341464 for Omni-MATH, below the declared 0.80 threshold, with all 1,338 candidate pairs sharing an eight-gram examined (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Short exact matches of zero to three normalised tokens were retained and enumerated rather than dropped — the longest examples being seven characters in the LAB and Omni inventories and at most seventeen raw characters in AgentDojo, such as "Paris" and "Conference Room A" — and the audit states plainly that these collisions do not demonstrate duplication of a task but do make an unqualified "zero overlap" claim incorrect (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit's scope limits are recorded with equal care: it does not reconstruct which training records were consumed: 512 is the size of the training pool recorded in the E11 decontamination receipt (`selected_training_row_count`), from which the 40-step run drew 80 prompts at two per step, and the transform and consumed-row inventory that would identify them were not recorded; it establishes a content comparison against the verified historical source envelope rather than proving global pretraining cleanliness, semantic disjointness, character-edit disjointness or image cleanliness; ASCII normalisation loses mathematical punctuation and non-ASCII text; and fields shorter than eight tokens fall outside the near-match and containment rule (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit itself is receipted: it re-verifies Weights & Biases run `bsv8vx04` against `training_identity_verified.json`, matches the 23,733 original source-field document hashes to the historical E11 receipt, adds 2,507 decoded nested API-Bank strings to reach 26,240 source documents, and records 43,977,748 bytes downloaded against a 500 MiB cap, with the checker not having enumerated the roughly 2.58 billion possible document pairs (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md).

Two features of the campaign's execution are methodological rather than incidental. The first is that a lane stops when its receipts are incomplete, and the record says why. A single inventory pass on 2026-09-21 checked every blocked lane live and found concrete, named failures: E1 lacked W&B authentication, a sealed reservation and a live actor endpoint; E2 awaited an owner IAM binding that only the project owner can create; E5 lacked both a bound authorization receipt and any real native runtime adapter in the repository; E13's request remained a draft with launch authorisation false; E6 and E9 were refused by cloud quota in both regions (source: outputs/UNBLOCK_CARRYOUT_2026-09-21.md). Those blockers were cleared on 27 September by a single lead authorisation and a move from AWS to Google Cloud and Modal, and each lane recorded the change as a deviation (§8.4). Recording a lane as blocked-with-reason, rather than reporting a shortened run, is the same principle as the terminal-state taxonomy applied to execution. The second is that negative outcomes are reported with their full denominators and their mechanisms. Generation failures and artifact losses stay in a lane's denominator rather than being excluded, as in the E1 SWE-bench Pro lane, and a zero score is audited trajectory by trajectory before it is read, as in the base-model E4 rerun whose zero was traced to the serving bridge rather than to the model (results in Chapter 8 and Section 8.5; sources: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md; outputs/e1_e14_small_scale_2026-09-26/E4/BRIDGE_STOP_TOKEN_FINDING.md). Reporting outcomes with the mechanism identified is the behaviour the whole apparatus is designed to produce.

The campaign also carries its integrity accounting in the same register. Eleven of eleven deterministic checks passed on 2026-09-19, covering exact divisions, receipt presence, sealed-artifact hash equality and lost-source absence probes; the recorded spend was $5.6301 against the $50 additional cap counted at that date, with later budget-mode records moving to an unlimited mode whose per-run resource and timeout bounds remain in force (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A judgment layer was added for classification, faithfulness and value-ordering questions and then partly invalidated by an external service outage: both blocker-classification batteries returned degenerate distributions after the incident, their receipts are retained as incident evidence, and the per-lane blocker column therefore rests on document evidence and not on model judgment (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A design rule was banked from the healthy window and is followed in this thesis: arithmetic and string lookups stay in code, and model judgment is reserved for classification, faithfulness and value ordering, because a parity probe was answered incorrectly even while the service was healthy (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

What the campaign does not claim follows directly from the design. It does not claim improvement over any baseline anywhere, because no lane carries a test establishing it. The one trained-versus-base comparison is the paired small-scale rerun of Table 8.B, with 5 to 151 identical items per lane, and its outcome is reported in Chapter 8; the campaign's role in this thesis is to fix the evaluation protocol, the receipt discipline and the disposition taxonomy that any future improvement claim would have to satisfy. It does not claim that the fourteen suites constitute a portfolio result. It does not pool original-contract with replacement-scope figures, and it does not average across suites. Its one trained actor means it says nothing about model comparisons. And where the record is incomplete — a lost execution source, an unanswered external access request, a run whose budget exceeded the authorised envelope — the incompleteness is written into the ledger rather than resolved by inference.
