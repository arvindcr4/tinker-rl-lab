# 7. Results II: Reporting, Registry and Controller (P5–P7)

The three papers reported in this chapter form a chain rather than a set of independent results. P5 makes a claim about the epistemic status of reinforcement-learning results for language models and proposes a checkable reporting standard to match it. P6 turns the standard into a machine-readable artifact with a validator, a badge and a diff tool. P7 takes the diagnostic that the standard requires authors to publish — the zero-variance fraction (ZVF) — and asks whether it is mechanistically predictable and ready to control rollout allocation. Each paper is explicit about where its claim stops, and this chapter reports their evidence in the same register.

## 7.1 A Minimum Reporting Standard for Stack-Conditioned Results (P5)

P5's position is that results in this field are *stack-conditioned*, and that reporting the framework or algorithm label without the stack is therefore insufficient to support the claims that are routinely made from it (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_intro.tex). The evidence for that position is not a single demonstration but a family of levers, each showing that varying one component of the stack, with the label held fixed, moves or flips the reported outcome or its telemetry.

The cleanest lever is a managed 12-cell head-to-head: four group-relative labels (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO, GSPO) run on one stack — Tinker-managed, Qwen3.5-4B, GSM8K, three seeds per arm. Mean last-10 training reward spans 0.710 to 0.744, a band of 0.034, with per-arm values of 0.744 (DAPO), 0.742 (Dr. GRPO), 0.723 (GRPO) and 0.710 (GSPO); mean ZVF over the same arms is 0.578, 0.500, 0.567 and 0.511 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p5-h2h`). The labels move telemetry by more than they move outcome, and neither movement is large.

The largest lever in the corpus is an outcome flip of 5.0% to 85.6% final training reward under a fixed label when the backend is changed. That figure must be read with its provenance caveat attached, because the same inspection that surfaced it also found that the nominal backend comparison bundled a different base checkpoint. The number is therefore evidence that item 3 of the standard must record backend *and* checkpoint identity; it is not a causal estimate of the backend alone (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, §Item 3). A second same-label lever points the other way: the label "DAPO" produced mean ZVF 0.00 on an open trainer running true dynamic sampling and 0.58 on a closed stack running an asymmetric-clip surrogate (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex).

These exhibits are summarised by a variance decomposition rather than an anecdote. On the 12-cell corpus the algorithm term contributes η² = 0.0454 on ZVF, with a bootstrap CI of [0.0014, 0.0585], and η² = 0.0075 [0.0014, 0.0577] on mean reward; the spread of last-10 mean reward across arms is 0.0359 [0.0164, 0.0984]. The headline CIs are decisive on the algorithm axis and tiny on the outcome axis, and the same decomposition attributes η² = 1.0000 to the group-size axis — 22× the algorithm term on ZVF and 133× on mean reward (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Exhibit 5 and Table `tab:p5-headline-cis`). The 98-cell mega-campaign reproduces the shape at larger scale: stack axes account for 72.9% to 92.7% of the variance in the five logged metrics depending on the metric, with stack-to-seed ratios from 503× to 96,128× (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p5-mega-eta2`).

The standard that P5 proposes has eight items, and each item earned its place by exhibiting a documented lever of this kind (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, Table `tab:p5-minreport`). Item 1 is the loss form, reported as a tuple of ratio level, clip low and high, token mask, advantage normalisation and dynamic-sampling status — the same label with and without dynamic sampling produced opposite ZVF telemetry (0.00 against 0.58). Item 2 is the reference policy and KL handling, which distinguishes family members that are otherwise conflated and whose omission is unrecoverable after the fact. Item 3 is the sampler, backend and precision, including base-checkpoint identity, motivated by the 17× exhibit above. Item 4 is the per-step ZVF and gradient-utilisation trajectory, because a single mean aliases opposite regimes: across a 368-run audit drawn from a 790-run corpus, mean ZVF is 0.97 and 0.95 for two low-reward models (Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.2-3B), falls to 0.25 and 0.29 at mid-range rewards, and rises again to 0.87 and 0.96 for two near-saturated models (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, Table `tab:p5-ushape`). Item 5 is the group-size schedule, since group size is a signal-geometry knob rather than a throughput knob; measured mean ZVF for Qwen3-32B falls from 0.33 at G=4 to 0.18 at G=8 and 0.08 at G=16, and for Llama-3.1-8B from 0.76 to 0.65 to 0.47, while the two boundary cases compress the effect as the theory predicts (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, Table `tab:p5-gsize`). Item 6 requires a held-out split disjoint from the reward environment. Item 7 requires a contamination check and a parser-robustness probe: adding micro-jitter ε ~ U(0,10⁻⁴) to rewards collapsed batch ZVF from 0.158 to 0.000 while pairwise contrast density moved by 3×10⁻⁷, which establishes that a diagnostic sensitive to verifier rounding is reporting a property of the stack rather than of the policy (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, §Item 7).

Item 8 is deliberately different in kind: held-out pass@k curves at k ∈ {1, 8, 32} belong to the evaluation report rather than to the run manifest. The lever here is a matched base-versus-post-RL panel — four adapters at G ∈ {2, 4, 16, 32}, one stack, the same problems — on which pass@1 deltas scatter from −0.5 to +1.9 points (under which reading the G=2 arm would be called a regression) while the pass@32 frontier improves by +1.5 to +2.5 points in all four arms. Neither reading reaches significance at a single training seed, which is itself the argument: without pass@k curves and stated noise bands the same panel supports any narrative the reader prefers (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, §Item 8).

P5 also ships the machinery that makes the standard enforceable rather than advisory. An auditor scores a manifest against the eight items and emits a 0–100 weighted badge; on an n=103 audit the weighted total was 55.0 with a badge span of [35.8, 56.7] and standard deviation 3.1, awarding zero gold, zero silver, 99 bronze and 4 wood (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Exhibit 8). Coverage, however, is where the honest negative lives. On 98 manifests, every one of the seven manifest fields is present as a key 100% of the time, yet item 2 is *validated* in 0 of 98 cases (0.0%), and all twelve of its sub-fields are present at 0 in each case; nine of the sub-fields across items 1 and 2 — advantage normalisation, clip high, clip low, dynamic sampling, KL coefficient, KL estimator, ratio level, reference snapshot and token mask — sit at 0.0% with zero information content (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Exhibits 6 and 18). Twenty-three sub-fields examined across the population show no field at a full null rate, but twenty of the twenty-three carry at most two distinct values, and the twenty entries collapse to ten distinct 23-bit fingerprints (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex, Table `tab:p6-minreport-subfield`, reported for the same corpus).

Two further analyses bound what the standard can be expected to buy. The first is a discriminative-entropy audit: four of the seven manifest fields are vacuous on this corpus — loss form, reference policy and KL, sampler/backend/precision, and decontamination notes each carry too little task-discriminative information to distinguish runs — while the per-step ZVF path and the group-size schedule carry the information (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p5-discriminative-entropy`). A field-sufficiency regression agrees: the full stack predicts ZVF at R² = 0.832 [0.772, 0.879] while a label-only model predicts R² = 0.000, and for mean reward the full stack reaches R² = 0.993 [0.990, 0.996] against the same label-only 0.000 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p5-field-suff`). The second is a manifest self-sufficiency test, and it splits sharply by metric. The manifest carries essentially all of the ZVF signal (R² = 0.776 [0.730, 0.846] against 0.773 [0.735, 0.834] for the cells, ΔR² = −0.003 [−0.045, +0.034]) but only a minority of the reward signal (R² = 0.280 [0.219, 0.443] against 0.743 [0.688, 0.820], ΔR² = +0.463 [+0.309, +0.564]); all 98 cells are therefore labelled `honest_but_vacuous` on the three-axis triangulation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p5-manifest-self-suff`).

The most useful negative in P5 is the placebo-replacement bound. The four-of-seven vacuity pattern admits two readings: the schema chose the wrong items, or any stack-descriptor schema would be vacuous on a single-stack corpus. Projecting six candidate replacement items onto the live corpus refutes the first reading. Four of the six — KL coefficient, clip range low, advantage normalisation and mini-batch size — project to exactly one unique value on the 98-cell corpus with a bootstrap CI excluding any positive information gain, because the corpus never varies them; temperature schedule would add 0.995 bits and lift the total from 11.41 to 12.41 bits, and a model-family label is redundant with an existing column. The variance partition confirms it: stack axes explain η² = 0.947 of ZVF variance across 25 unique buckets, with 5.3% seed residual and an algorithm term of zero by construction (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, §Placebo-Replacement Bound). The schema is not the binding constraint; the corpus is. On the live corpus the seven-field fingerprint carries 11.4 bits in total, four fields carry exactly zero, and Hamming distance over the fingerprint predicts the absolute difference in ZVF (Spearman ρ = +0.529, p ≤ 10⁻³), PCD (ρ = +0.541, p ≤ 10⁻³) and mean reward (ρ = +0.251, p = 0.003), while the badge itself is constant across cells and cannot discriminate seed-pair dispersion (ρ = +0.000, n = 48 pairs) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex, §Live-Corpus Coupling).

## 7.2 The GRPO-Registry (P6)

P6's premise is that the deltas defining GRPO variants — DAPO's asymmetric clip, dynamic sampling and token-level loss; Dr. GRPO's dropped length and standard-deviation normalisation; GSPO's sequence-level importance ratio — live in paper appendices and framework defaults rather than in any machine-readable form (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). The registry is the response, and it ships as three artifacts at `registry/`: `schema.json`, `entries/*.json` and `query.py` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/paper_P6_registry.tex).

The schema defines two record types under a single `oneOf`, both carrying `schema_version` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_schema.tex). The stack record's core is `min_report`, one sub-object per minimum-report item, with base-checkpoint identity required inside the `sampler_backend` block precisely because the audit that motivated item 3 found a managed backend silently pinning a different base checkpoint. Two design decisions carry weight. The first is the distinction between an unreported field and a field reported as absent: a null means the author said nothing, whereas an explicit `false`, `0.0` or `"none"` means the author made a checkable claim — "a stack that says 'we use no KL term' is making a checkable claim; a stack that says nothing is not." The second is that item 8, the held-out pass@k requirement, is attached under outcomes as an evaluation-report field rather than folded into the run manifest, preserving the eight-item standard's split between what describes a run and what describes a result. Per-component delta status is recorded on the stack side in `variant_deltas_applied`, with each entry's status drawn from {implemented, surrogate, absent, unknown}. The shipped entry for the managed DAPO run makes the point concretely: `clip_higher` is recorded as `surrogate` with the note that the asymmetric clip was set through the user-facing config while the underlying managed loss form is unverifiable, `dynamic_sampling` is recorded as `absent`, and `token_level_loss` is `unknown` because it is managed by the runtime. An optional `ci_method` object (method, bootstrap count, seed, confidence level, source) is populated on the seven derived entries with `n_boot = 2000` and a percentile 95% interval, and left null on the remaining twenty-four.

Three variant-delta records were verified against their source papers: DAPO with five components (clip higher at ε_low = 0.2 and ε_high = 0.28, dynamic sampling, token-level loss, overlong reward shaping, KL removed); Dr. GRPO with two removals (the 1/|o_i| response-length normalisation in the loss and the division by the within-group reward standard deviation in the advantage); and GSPO with a length-normalised sequence-likelihood importance ratio and sequence-level clipping.

The seed population is twenty stack records in five families, all of which pass validation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_population.tex). Family A holds four framework config dumps (Qwen3-8B, GSM8K, seed 42) scoring badges 74, 60, 60 and 62; family B holds the four open Colab trainer arms at toy scale, scoring 96, 96, 96 and 96; family C is the managed head-to-head at 57, 62, 62 and 60; family D is a same-stack four-method run at 67 each; and family E is a five-seed risk-index batch at 43 each. The finding worth stating plainly is why the ordering looks the way it does. The config dumps were assembled for a reviewer-requested fairness audit and score only 57 to 74: they nail items 2, 3 and 5, expose item 1 only partially, and cover items 6 and 7 not at all. The open Colab arms score 96 not because the runs are better but because an open backward pass, coupled with explicit declarations that no KL term and no decontamination check were used, makes every field reportable. Reproducibility supplements of the kind commonly shipped with RL papers do not automatically satisfy MIN-REPORT.

The health audit is the registry's own evidence that it works. Every shipped record parses: 31 of 31 pass, comprising 20 stack records and 11 variant-delta records, with zero broken delta pointers (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex). The delta-mix table is the sharpest single result in P6: the label "DAPO" is claimed by ten entries, of which four implement a delta, one records a surrogate, three record it as absent and two record it as unknown — the same label covering four distinct implementations (Table `tab:p6-delta-mix`). Per-item coverage on the twenty stack records is 100% for the held-out split (40/40), 95% for sampler/backend (76/80), 90% for telemetry (54/60), 68% for the group-size schedule (41/60), 48% for reference policy and KL (29/60), 32% for the loss form (38/120) and 20% for decontamination (8/40) (Table `tab:p6-coverage`). The per-openness breakdown inverts the conventional expectation: the open subset records sampler/backend at 91.7% and telemetry at 83.3% with reference KL at 58.3%, while the managed subset records sampler/backend and telemetry at 100% but reference KL at 33.3% and the loss form at only 22.9%.

The registry was stress-tested rather than asserted. A batch of 3,230 adversarial perturbations — 13 categories across 31 entries and 10 mutations each, with B = 4000 — moved the overall robustness statistic from 0.864 [0.756, 0.878] before the corresponding schema bumps to 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] after them, closing three silent-failure categories: a missing identifier pattern check (0.645 to 1.000), MIN-REPORT leaves declared in `properties` but not in `required` (0.000 to 1.000, fixed by promoting key presence to a schema invariant), and free-form delta references (0.000 to 1.000, fixed by replacing `delta_id` with an enum generated from the entry filenames) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex, §Stress Test). A separate consistency check cross-references the variant-delta records against the MIN-REPORT blocks: of 31 triples from 13 entries, 7 are MATCH, 0 are MISMATCH, 0 are MISSING_REPORT, 5 are SURROGATE_OBS and 19 are NOT_APPLICABLE, giving an implementation-honesty match rate of 7/7 = 1.000 with a 95% bootstrap CI of [0.65, 1.00].

The empirical payoff of the schema is the same-stack measurement. Re-executing four GRPO-family methods (GRPO, AERO, GIFT, AREAL) on a single identical stack — managed sampler, G = 8, seed 0, 40 steps, 16 prompts per step — with the full per-prompt reward tensor logged at every step, produces reward deltas of magnitude ≤ 2 percentage points. Two of the three are statistically significant *decreases* rather than improvements (AERO at −0.014 [−0.023, −0.005] and AREAL at −0.020 [−0.032, −0.008]), so the magnitude thesis survives while the stronger claim that all differences are within noise does not. The largest same-stack effects are not on reward at all: AERO and AREAL lengthen completions by approximately 31 tokens and tighten the length coefficient of variation, and GIFT's loss shift of −1.67×10⁴ is a baseline constant (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_measured_evidence.tex, Table `tab:p6-measured-deltas`). A later registry extension measures the anti-herding residual δ_div = ZVF^iid − ZVF^obs on the same tensors, reporting a stack-mean of 0.049 with a range of [0.039, 0.053]; only GIFT shows a borderline-significant reduction against GRPO (p = 0.066, CI [−0.021, +0.001]), which resolves a synthesis range of [0.13, 0.23] downwards — the anti-herding diversity bonus is real but smaller than the qualitative synthesis had guessed (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex, §Contrastive Yield and Anti-Herding).

The reference CLI, `registry/query.py`, implements four verbs using only the standard library: `list`; `query --item reference_kl` returning FULL, PARTIAL or UNREPORTED; `badge`; and `stackdiff`, which maps a pair of entries onto the R0–R5 ladder and reports the highest rung triggered — R0 identical manifests, R1 nuisance-only, R2 runtime differences, R3 loss-form or KL-handling differences, R4 differing variant-delta sets, R5 unauditable because a differing item is null on at least one side (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_query_auditor.tex). The badge formula weights the seven items equally and is outcome-blind: badge(s) = ⌊100 · (1/7) Σ c_i(s)⌋. `stackdiff` prints a warning at R4 and above that outcome differences must not be attributed to the shared algorithm label, and the paper is careful to describe the ladder as "a risk scale, not an effect-size scale." Two boundaries deserve restating. The registry reports stated provenance and conflicts; it does not reconcile unresolved exact-ID or model conflicts, nor does it validate nominal algorithm comparisons (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). And the badge is coarse relative to the corpus — twenty entries collapse to ten distinct fingerprints, and twenty of twenty-three sub-fields carry at most two values.

## 7.3 From Diagnostic to Controller (P7)

P7 asks a narrower question than P5 and P6. Given that the zero-variance fraction is worth reporting, is it mechanistically predictable, and is it ready to control rollout allocation? The paper's own framing is that this does not overturn the descriptive restraint of the earlier work — it *tests* ZVF's predictive value through falsifiable predictions rather than assuming it — and it flags at the outset that the weakest link in the chain, the causal connection from within-group contrast to gradient magnitude, rests on toy-scale directional evidence only (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

The theory is compact. Fix a prompt with latent per-rollout success probability p and a group of G conditionally independent Bernoulli rewards. Write h_G(p) = p^G + (1−p)^G for the probability that the group is degenerate, and model the usable signal per prompt as S(p,G) = p(1−p)(1 − h_G(p)). The first factor is the contrast a non-degenerate group can carry; the second is the probability that any signal survives centring. Both factors vanish at the endpoints and peak at p = ½, so the reading is that GRPO learns fastest exactly where the policy is most uncertain and starves at both ends (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_theory.tex). Three consequences follow, each paired with the measurement that could have falsified it. T1 states that the expected ZVF indicator is exactly h_G(p). T2 states that h_G is strictly decreasing in G, with exponential decay rate −ln max(p, 1−p). T3 states that with unnormalised group-centred advantages the mean absolute advantage in a group is exactly 2p̂(1−p̂).

The three checks behave as the theory predicts, with the deviations reported rather than smoothed over. For T1, binning the per-group reward tensors of the GSM8K runs by empirical success rate gives a ZVF indicator averaging 1.000 at p̂ ∈ {0, 1} and 0.000 at every interior bin; at run level, measured ZVF sits *above* h_G(p̄) at every G, which the paper attributes to the Jensen gap — the binomial model evaluates h_G at the mean p while the true prompt population mixes mastered and hopeless prompts. For T2, two independent grids agree. The 368-run audit shows monotone decline for every model with an interior success rate (Qwen3-32B 0.33 → 0.18 → 0.08; Llama-3.1-8B 0.76 → 0.65 → 0.47), with the two boundary rows behaving as the theory flags them: Llama-3.2-1B sits near p ≈ 0 where h_G ≈ 1 regardless of G (0.98 → 0.97), and Qwen3-4B-Instruct sits near saturation where the decay rate is tiny and seed noise dominates, producing the single non-monotone cell (0.89 → 0.84 → 0.91). The repository-internal sweep at G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} shows the same decline, with mean ZVF falling from 0.838 to 0.631 while held-out accuracy stays essentially flat near 0.98 throughout — raising G buys signal, not, on this near-saturated task, outcome (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_theory.tex, Tables `tab:p7-modelG` and `tab:p7-gsweep`). T3 cannot be checked on the closed stack at all, because it does not expose per-group gradients; the open-backward-pass measurement, corr(‖∇‖, p̂(1−p̂)) = +0.71 on a 0.5B model with synthetic arithmetic, is reported strictly as directional evidence.

Because ZVF aliases mastery with incapacity, the paper promotes the pairwise contrast density PCD = ((G−1)/G)·E[p(1−p)] as the signal to control on. The falsification that motivates the promotion is the micro-jitter experiment already cited in §7.1: ZVF collapses from 0.158 to 0.000 while PCD is unmoved. The controller's design follows from the theory: a `zvf-triage` callback watches per-step ZVF with PCD as an aliasing guard, and an adaptive-G controller escalates group size on ZVF spikes, changing only the sampler budget so that it composes with any group-relative objective.

The four-arm open-trainer audit is the first place where the intervention is measured against alternatives on the same data, budget and seeds (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex, Table `tab:p7-e3`). Against a 120-rollout baseline, GRPO gains +0.500 held-out at mean ZVF 0.25; Dr. GRPO gains +0.575 at 0.27 on the same 120 rollouts; DAPO-style dynamic sampling gains +0.550 while driving ZVF to 0.00 at 174 rollouts, a +45% cost; and adaptive-G gains +0.575 while lowering mean ZVF only modestly to 0.23 at 186 rollouts. Three readings are stated. Telemetry behaves as the mechanisms dictate. Dynamic sampling buys perfect telemetry at a 45% rollout premium and does not exceed the cheaper fixed recipe. Adaptive-G spends comparable overhead and ties the best held-out result, concentrating its spend on flagged steps — and the tie is small-n and single-task, so the honest description is "competitive with, not superior to, the best fixed recipe."

The controller simulation then asks a counterfactual question on the real reward tensors: for each of the 2,560 prompt-step decisions in the four-method run, when would each controller have fired, what group size would it have chosen, and how much contrast would it have restored? The answer is a negative result of some force. Across all four methods and all five thresholds of the point-estimate rule, the controllers save *zero* prompts. Of GRPO's 640 prompt-step pairs, 461 are truly degenerate — 427 all-correct and 34 all-wrong — with p̂ at exactly 0 or 1, and the counts are near-identical for the other methods (AERO 461, GIFT 493, AREAL 452). At p̂ ∈ {0, 1} no group size recovers contrast, because ZVF(G) = 1 for every G. The empirical ZVF of roughly 0.72 is high because the prompts are easy, not because they are noisy at the decision boundary. Bootstrapping the observed binary rewards to size G = 16 confirms it directly: 0 of 1,867 boundary prompts would have been restored by escalation. The operating range where the trigger fires selectively is τ ∈ [0.70, 0.80], 12 to 19 fires per 40 steps at a cost ratio of 1.30 to 1.48, and the different thresholds differ only in how wastefully they fire (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex, Tables `tab:p7-cf-sweep` and `tab:p7-cf-granularity`).

In the same trace the escalation asymmetry is stark: 1,723 of 1,867 escalation events (92.3%) occur on all-correct groups and only 144 on all-wrong groups (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). A symmetric ZVF trigger mostly spends on solved prompts. The paper is explicit that deleting those fires is a counterfactual proposal only, because performance preservation has not been measured.

Two refinements sharpen the picture and then complicate it. The Bayesian controller replaces the point estimate with a Beta(1,1) posterior and a mid-range probability m(k, G) = Pr[0.05 ≤ p ≤ 0.95 | k, G], escalating when m exceeds a posterior threshold. At τ_post = 0.60 it fires on the same 466.75 degenerate prompt-steps as the point-estimate rule at τ = 0.50 — with a 95% bootstrap CI of [454.25, 485.00] — but at strictly lower cost, 8,854 rollouts against 10,240, a 14% saving; and all 466.75 of those fires are on saturated prompts, so it too saves zero prompts. Above τ_post = 0.65 it never fires, because the maximum m over degenerate observations is 0.630 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex, §Bayesian Refinement).

The calibrated eight-controller comparison then produces a genuine metric reversal with bootstrap CIs attached. On the cost axis the Pareto winner is Dualformer-Auto, an automatic difficulty-gated de-escalation rule, at a mean cost ratio of 0.657 [0.599, 0.738] against 1.000 for fixed G = 8 — a 34% rollout saving, achieved because the prompts it shrinks to G = 2 would have been degenerate at G = 8 anyway. On a different axis, contrast restored per 1,000 extra rollouts, the ordering reverses: `zvf-triage` at τ = 0.50 restores 90.10 units per 1,000 extra rollouts [89.40, 90.77], against 86.68 for τ = 0.70, 84.00 for Dualformer-Auto and 80.00 for the Bayesian rule, and all four pairwise differences have CIs excluding zero. The Bayesian controller — Pareto-dominant on the earlier axis — is dominated here, because its principled prior fires on the hardest-to-restore degenerate prompts. The regime decomposition explains the mechanism rather than leaving it as a ranking: `zvf-triage` at τ = 0.50 fires on 100% of steps in every regime on this panel, so it is operationally equivalent to "escalate every step," and its apparent advantage is mechanical exposure rather than a smarter rule. Its restoration decomposes 64.76% degenerate, 13.69% boundary and 21.56% mid-range; the non-degenerate 35.24% share is what the Bayesian rule sacrifices (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex, §Posterior-Predictive Contrast Restoration and §Statistically Validated Pareto Frontier).

Two further analyses set the ceiling on what any adaptive rule can achieve. A per-prompt hindsight-optimal calculation scans G' down from a base of 8 and finds a bimodal optimum: G* = 8 for 72.9% of the 2,560 prompt-steps, which are fully saturated and admit no rescue by any group size, and G* = 2 for the remaining 27.1%, which are already non-degenerate at G = 8 and are cheapest to shrink. Mean G* is 6.376, giving a cost ratio of 0.797 [0.776, 0.818] — a 20.3% saving that is pure economy, since mean contrast restored per prompt is 0.000 and mean contrast lost to shrinking is 0.130 ZVF units. That ceiling is tighter than Dualformer-Auto's observed 0.66 to 0.68 on the four-bin rule, and the paper states plainly that any future learned rule must beat it. A finite-pool correction then shows that even this ceiling is loose: because a finite pool never collides more than independent sampling, the exact hypergeometric contrast-preservation probability exceeds the binomial one everywhere, by +0.0494 [+0.0481, +0.0506] on the 693 relevant prompt-steps, which means the binomial model over-predicts starvation and the "zero contrast restored" accounting understates true preserved contrast by roughly 34.2 ZVF units (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex, §Exact Finite-Pool Correction).

Cross-base replication is where the retreat from "controller" to "audit" is earned. On a second panel of five independent seeds, the trigger fires 4.20 times per seed [3.00, 5.40] at τ = 0.70, with the per-step rate falling from 0.48 to 0.28 as the panel's overall ZVF drops; what replicates is the sign and the operating-point logic, not a fixed firing magnitude. The Bayesian branch is entirely silent on this panel, firing 0.0 times per seed, because every step's implied mid-range probability lies in [0.95, 0.999] — a real negative finding that scopes the Bayesian controller to distributions containing boundary steps. And the aggregate firing rate is stable while the *location* is not: at τ = 0.70 with a de-escalation threshold of 0.95, the cross-seed Jaccard overlap of the fire sets is 0.133, only 13.3% of steps see all five seeds choose the same group size, and the mean cross-seed Kendall-τ of per-step ZVF trajectories is −0.060. The firing rate is seed-stable; the firing location is not, and the training-step index is a meaningless axis for the ZVF signal across seeds. The replayed unification confirms the direction: on this panel Dualformer-Auto is the only controller that saves compute (+14% [0.10, 0.18]) while `zvf-triage` spends more than baseline (−28% [−0.36, −0.20]), with disjoint CIs.

Five design rules close the paper, and they read as constraints discovered rather than principles asserted (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_design_rules.tex). Gate on PCD and alarm on ZVF: escalate only when ZVF is high *and* PCD indicates interior difficulty, because high ZVF with near-zero PCD and high reward is mastery — where h_G(p) ≈ 1 − Gε near the boundary and added rollouts buy order ε — while high ZVF with near-zero reward is incapacity, where the correct intervention is the data, not the sampler. Escalate multiplicatively in small steps, since at p = ½ each additional rollout halves the degenerate fraction; the audited 4 → 6 → 8 schedule at multipliers of 1.5 and 1.33 captured most of the achievable reduction at a total cost of +55% rollouts, whereas G = 16 costs four times the rollouts of G = 4 for a ZVF drop from 0.76 to 0.63 and no held-out gain on a near-saturated task. Trigger on spikes rather than levels, because ZVF is temporally sticky with a lag-1 autocorrelation of approximately 0.94 and drifts upward over training, so a level threshold fires either never or permanently. Never compare controllers across stacks; ship the seven-field manifest with item 5 carrying the escalation log. And treat ZVF = 0 as a purchase rather than a prize — dynamic sampling shows perfect telemetry is always available at a 45% rollout premium, and on this task it was not the best use of the marginal rollout.

The chapter's honest summary of P7 is the paper's own. No result establishes prospective superiority over static G = 16, Dr. GRPO, or dynamic sampling; adaptive group size is explicitly *not* promoted to a recommended policy; and all intervention evidence is single-task, small-n, or retrospective. The paper's contribution is a retrospective controller audit together with a pre-registered prospective test plan, and the bakeoff that would close the question — a matched-budget, multi-seed held-out comparison against the strong static baseline — is future work rather than a claim. In the same spirit, the paper flags its own August 2026 evidence boundary: unsigned ZVF alone cannot select between retiring, distilling, retrying, resampling or warm-starting a prompt, so any operator policy remains a prospective evaluation contract rather than a demonstrated benefit.
