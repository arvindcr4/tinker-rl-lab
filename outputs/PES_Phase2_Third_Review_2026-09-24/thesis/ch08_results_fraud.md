# 8. Results III: Applied Study (P8)

This chapter reports the applied side study of the programme: a head-to-head comparison between a tuned gradient-boosted tree and a fine-tuned large language model on credit-card fraud detection. The study is deliberately cross-domain. It steps outside the GRPO post-training work that occupies the rest of this thesis and asks whether the programme's governing discipline — measure capability, do not trust the label — survives contact with a setting that already has a strong non-LLM baseline. Where the benchmark chapters ask whether reinforcement learning changed a model in a measurable way, this chapter asks a blunter question: when a lender is told that an LLM can detect fraud, what is the LLM actually doing that the existing tree cannot? Applied here, the label under test is "AI" itself.

The short answer is that the tree keeps the real-time scoring seat, that the head-to-head which produced this conclusion is in any case the least interesting fact in the study, and that the LLM's real contribution lies in four task categories the tree cannot enter at all. Those categories are compliance narration, document and image fraud via vision-language models, cold-start triage of novel typologies, and agentic investigation of the alert queue. The chapter reports the measured comparison first, then the operational arguments that survive even at accuracy parity, then the measured evidence beyond the headline, then the capability-gap taxonomy and the hybrid architecture that follow from it. It closes with the limitations, stated plainly, because several of them bound what any number in this chapter may be used to claim.

A scope note is owed at the outset. The study is parked outside the ZVF thesis and supplies no evidence for GRPO signal starvation, group-size control, PPO, or SAO; it contributes an example of the programme's measurement discipline and nothing to the RL claims (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_intro.tex). Its conclusions stand on the fraud artifacts and the cited domain literature alone.

## 8.1 Dataset, splits, and the two arms

The benchmark is a custom synthetic dataset generated with scikit-learn's `make_classification` (seed 42): 50,000 transactions, 20 anonymised numeric features V1–V20 of which 10 are informative and 2 redundant, two clusters per class, class separation 0.8, and a 1% label-noise setting (`flip_y=0.01`). The generator's 1% target positive rate realises as an actual fraud rate of 1.44% over the emitted rows. Four aggregates are appended to the twenty raw features — per-row mean, standard deviation, maximum, and minimum — giving a 24-feature design matrix (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_setup.tex). The released dataset at the repository root carries exactly this schema: `V1` through `V20`, a `Class` label, and the four aggregate columns, across 50,000 rows (source: fraud_data.csv).

The V-prefix convention mirrors the PCA-transformed public card-fraud datasets, but the data here is a stylised stand-in for one institution's post-processing feature view, and the study says so. It reproduces the class imbalance and the anonymised feature shape of single-institution card-fraud data and none of its temporal drift, verification latency, seasonality, or adversarial adaptation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_limitations.tex).

The split is 80/20 stratified with seed 42, giving 40,000 training rows and 10,000 held-out test rows containing 144 positives. The primary metric is held-out ROC-AUC; F1, precision, and recall at the default threshold are logged alongside.

The scorer arm is XGBoost with 200 estimators, maximum depth 6, learning rate 0.05, subsample and column-subsample of 0.8, and `scale_pos_weight=7` to counter the roughly 99:1 imbalance. No hyperparameter search was run beyond this single sensible configuration (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_setup.tex).

The challenger arm is a Qwen3.5-4B model fine-tuned on row-to-text serialisations in the TabLLM style: each transaction is rendered as a feature-name/value string and the model is trained to emit a fraud-or-legitimate answer. Because the natural-rate test split contains too few positives for a stable LLM AUC, evaluation uses a 500-row positive-enriched held-out subset at 20% fraud, disjoint from the training data (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/quick_20260704/qp8-fraud-sft_manifest.json). The fine-tune uses token-level cross-entropy with weight zero on prompt tokens and weight one on answer tokens, with no reference policy and no KL term — this is pure supervised fine-tuning, not RL. The run completes in 63 steps at batch size 32 and takes 309.1 seconds to reach its final evaluation (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/quick_20260704/qp8-fraud-sft.tsv).

## 8.2 The head-to-head result

On the tabular scoring task the released artifacts place the two arms as follows.

| Arm | Eval split | _n_ | Accuracy | ROC-AUC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| XGBoost (scorer) | full test | 10,000 | n/a | 0.7955 |
| Qwen3.5-4B SFT | positive-enriched test | 500 | 0.7920 | 0.4827 |

: Head-to-head result on the synthetic card-fraud set, by arm and evaluation split.

(source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/quick_20260704/qp8_fraud.tsv)

The LLM's accuracy is not evidence of useful ranking. At this class balance an AUC of 0.4827 means the SFT scores do not order positives ahead of negatives: the model is at chance-level discrimination and its apparently reasonable accuracy comes from the majority class. This is consistent with the broader tabular literature, where tree ensembles remain the reference class on medium-sized tabular problems and LLM row-serialisation approaches are most attractive in few-shot regimes, not at 40,000 labelled examples (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_scorer.tex).

Three provenance points must be stated with the table rather than buried beneath it. First, the two AUCs sit on different held-out splits and are not a like-for-like ranking comparison; the load-bearing point is only that the LLM scorer ranks at chance, not that it is worse than the tree by this exact margin (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_abstract.tex). Second, the precision, recall, and F1 figures belong to the released script's own output rather than to the quick artifact's 0.7955 run. That file logs AUC 0.7942 with precision 0.723, recall 0.236, and F1 0.356 at the default threshold, alongside a training time of 0.49 seconds and an inference time of 0.0062 seconds per 10,000 rows (source: xgboost_results.json). The paper's own setup section notes that this run uses a slightly different feature and library configuration from the quick artifact and that the two released artifacts agree to two decimals (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_setup.tex). The precision-recall asymmetry — precision 0.723 against recall 0.236 — is the class-imbalance signature at an untuned default threshold: the tree alerts on a small set of transactions it is mostly right about, and misses most fraud. It is a statement about the operating point, not about the model's ranking ability, which the 0.7955 AUC measures.

Third, an older internal record from 21 June 2026 listed XGBoost at AUC 0.975 and the LLM arm at 0.948. That environment could not be reconstructed, so those numbers survive only as historical records and are not headline evidence; the paper's architectural conclusions are insulated from them by design, since the seat assignment rests on arguments that apply at accuracy parity (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_limitations.tex).

## 8.3 Why the seat does not move even at parity

The artifact-backed ranking is only the first of five reasons the tree keeps the real-time scoring seat. Even at AUC parity the LLM would not be moved into the synchronous path, for four operational reasons (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_scorer.tex).

Latency is the first. Card authorisation is a hard-real-time loop whose score must arrive within a network-and-rules budget measured in tens of milliseconds. The committed run of the released script measures roughly 6 ms per 10,000 rows (source: xgboost_results.json) — a rounding error against that budget. An LLM forward pass over a serialised row, let alone an autoregressive generation, is orders of magnitude slower and jitter-prone, and the batching that would amortise its cost is exactly what a per-authorisation synchronous path cannot use.

Cost is the second. The released cost table prices the XGBoost scorer at $1.00 per 10,000 rows and the Qwen3.5-4B SFT path at $0.0035 per row on 120 input and 5 output tokens. The synchronous LLM path is about 35 times more expensive than the tree-only path per transaction, and several orders of magnitude slower on latency — approximately 300 ms against approximately 6 ms (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_evidence.tex). The tree's marginal cost per transaction is effectively zero at card-network volumes; the LLM's is a token bill that scales linearly with traffic.

Calibration is the third, and it is the reason the argument does not reduce to cost. Downstream of the score sit threshold rules, alert budgets, and expected-loss computations that consume the score as a probability. Boosted trees produce dense scores that calibrate well under standard post-hoc maps; verbalised LLM confidences inherit the miscalibration pathologies of modern neural networks, compounded by the quantisation of confidence into tokens. A scorer whose 0.9 does not mean ninety per cent silently corrupts every expected-loss decision behind it (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_scorer.tex).

Prompt-injection exposure is the fourth, and it is qualitative but decisive. A tree scores numbers; there is no channel through which a transaction can address the model. An LLM that reads serialised transaction fields — merchant names, memo strings, cardholder-supplied text — creates an attack surface in which adversarial instructions are embedded in attacker-controlled fields, the indirect prompt-injection pattern documented in the security literature. A fraudster who learns that the scorer reads free text will put text in front of it. Placing an instruction-following model in a synchronous decision loop over attacker-authored inputs is a security regression independent of accuracy, and it is the single strongest argument for keeping the scorer seat non-linguistic (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_scorer.tex).

The conclusion of this section is deliberately narrow: on fixed-schema tabular scoring with ample labels, the current artifacts score the LLM below the tree, and it would not get the seat even had it tied.

## 8.4 What the measured evidence adds

Beyond the headline pair, the released dataset supports a set of checks that sharpen the negative result and bound its interpretation.

The first is an empirical surrogate for the LLM-as-sensor idea. A tree fitted on only the four aggregate columns — a stand-in for what an LLM sensor returns — is worse than the 24-feature tree on every measured axis at 95% confidence under a paired bootstrap: AUC lower by 0.0245 with interval [0.0174, 0.0320], accuracy lower by 0.0135 with interval [0.0109, 0.0160], and Brier score worse by 0.0109 with interval [0.0099, 0.0120] (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p5p8/p8_headline_cis.tsv). Adding the four engineered aggregates to the twenty raw features does not move AUC or accuracy outside paired-bootstrap noise: the AUC difference is 0.0002 with interval [−0.0002, 0.0007], which contains zero (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p5p8/p8_headline_cis.tsv). The sensor is complementary information, not a substitute for the raw features.

The second is a leakage caveat that must travel with any tree number quoted from the wider evidence section. Several tables in that section report AUC between 0.998 and 0.999 for tree variants on the 10,000-row split with 144 positives. Those figures are not absolute operating numbers. The calibration and robustness pipeline that produces them fits on the full `fraud_data.csv` and scores `test_data.csv`, whose rows are a subset of `fraud_data.csv`; training therefore overlaps evaluation, which inflates the absolute AUC towards 1.0 and drives top-2% caught-recall to 1.000. The leakage-free release split reproduces the headline 0.7955 with top-2% caught-recall of approximately 0.40, and that is the tree's reproducible absolute performance. The 0.998–0.999 tables are within-protocol relative comparisons only (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_evidence.tex). Quoting 0.9988 as the tree's accuracy would be a straightforward misreading of the evidence.

The third is a noise budget for any deployed sensor. In the oracle frame, where the sensor emits the exact aggregates, the sensor-augmented tree breaks even at a missed-fraud loss of about $5.29. At the certified sensor-noise budget of σ ≤ 0.02, the break-even rises to $26.44, and at σ = 0.05 it reaches $36.40. The oracle break-even is a five-fold under-estimate of the realistic break-even a production LLM sensor would face, and the paper states this as the sharpest reviewer-facing claim of the noisy-sensor frame (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_noisy_sensor.tex). Notably, the qualitative cost signature is unchanged by noise: the sensor-augmented tree remains detectably costlier at low fraud loss, and confidence intervals straddle zero elsewhere.

The fourth is cost per fraud caught at a canonical cost cell. At an investigation cost of $0.50 per alert and a missed-fraud loss of $100, raw-features-only costs $2.68 per fraud caught, the sensor-augmented tree $3.28, and the sensor-only surrogate $14.92 — a 5.57× penalty whose direction is consistent across all 25 cells of the phase diagram. In the (σ, L) plane the sensor never pays for itself in any of the 25 cells (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_evidence.tex).

The fifth is threshold transfer. A cost-optimal threshold fitted on training data is a fingerprint of the training prior, not a portable decision rule: under class-prior shift the transfer gap is detectable for all three tree variants at the ρ = 500 cell, at +6.55, +6.67, and +2.19 cents per decision respectively (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_evidence.tex). Any deployment that carries a fitted threshold across a base-rate change inherits this penalty, LLM or not.

## 8.5 Where the LLM does contribute: four capability gaps

Framing the study as "LLM versus XGBoost" was a category error, and the error is instructive. The comparison was conducted on the tree's home ground — fixed-schema tabular scoring with ample labels — and the LLM lost there. The useful question is the inverse: on which tasks does the tree not compete at all? The study identifies four, each of which is a task category the tree cannot enter, not an accuracy improvement on a task the tree already performs (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_taxonomy.tex).

The first is document and image fraud: forged receipts, tampered identity documents, synthetic cheques, and manipulated screenshots. These inputs lie outside any tree's feature space by construction. The claim is not that a vision-language model is accurate enough to auto-decide, but that it is the only model family in the pipeline that can look at the evidence at all; in the proposed architecture its output is a feature vector and a written observation, not a verdict.

The second is compliance narration, and the study argues it is the paper's most durable claim because it survives any future accuracy result. A tree emits a float; it cannot enter this category at any accuracy. Suspicious Activity Report narratives are governed by 31 C.F.R. § 1020.320, which sets a $5,000 threshold and a 30-day filing window, and by FinCEN's narrative guidance requiring who, what, when, where, and why alongside the method of operation. Adverse-action statements under ECOA and Regulation B, 12 C.F.R. § 1002.9, must state the principal reasons for the action specifically, and CFPB Circular 2022-03 states that creditors may not rely on black-box complexity as a defence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_compliance.tex). Two design consequences follow: the scorer must remain attribution-extractable, which makes keeping XGBoost in the scorer seat a compliance posture and not only a performance one; and the scribe must narrate the scorer's actual attributions rather than invent them.

The third is cold-start triage of novel typologies, before labels exist. This is a few-shot regime, and it is precisely where row-serialisation LLM approaches are most attractive. The claim is not that the LLM is a better classifier but that it is a stopgap classifier that exists during the label vacuum (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_taxonomy.tex).

The fourth is agentic investigation of the alert queue, at roughly 85 times lower cost than a human analyst by the study's internal estimate — a figure reported to one significant figure, built on June-2026 token prices and assumed investigation times, and not validated against any production deployment. It should be read as "one to two orders of magnitude," not as a measured constant. Crucially, this role is post-score: the agent works the queue after the tree has ranked it (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_taxonomy.tex).

## 8.6 The hybrid architecture

These findings distil into a three-seat architecture. The LLM is the sensor: it reads unstructured evidence and emits typed features — document-consistency scores, extracted field values, tamper-likelihood indicators, mismatch flags — across a fixed numeric schema, with no tools, no memory across cases, and no authority. XGBoost remains the scorer: nothing in the real-time loop parses natural language, which preserves both the tree's calibration and the containment of the injection surface to per-case feature corruption. The LLM is also the scribe: it drafts Suspicious Activity Report narratives and adverse-action statements conditioned on the tree's own feature attributions, so that the stated reasons are the model's actual principal reasons, with provenance links on every draft and a human signature on every filing. An agentic triage stage sits between scoring and writing, working the alert queue asynchronously under a budget cap, so that token spend lands only on the small fraction of traffic that alerts. Analyst dispositions flow back as confirmed labels, closing the loop with the scorer (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_architecture.tex).

## 8.7 Limitations

Six limitations bound what this chapter may be used to claim, and they are not incidental (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_limitations.tex).

The benchmark is a custom synthetic dataset, not production transactions. It reproduces class imbalance and anonymised feature shape and none of the temporal drift, verification latency, seasonality, or adversarial adaptation of real card-fraud data. The artifact-backed AUCs of 0.7955 and 0.48268 are results on a stylised testbed and must not be quoted as production expectations. No number in the study should be read as an expected production AUC, and the margin between the arms is dataset- and environment-specific.

The historical numbers are records, not reproductions, as discussed above. There is no cross-institution validation: everything reported here is one configuration of one synthetic generator standing in for one institution's feature view, and the head-to-head has not been replicated on any second data distribution, public benchmark, or real institution. The cost estimates are internal; the 85× agentic-triage figure, like the latency and per-transaction cost contrasts, is an operational characterisation rather than a benchmarked service-level measurement. The challenger is a single LLM family under a single serialisation format and a single fine-tuning budget; stronger or differently trained models could narrow the accuracy gap, though the seat assignment does not hinge on the gap because the latency, cost, calibration, and injection arguments apply at parity. Finally, the capability gaps are argued rather than all measured: of the four taxonomy entries, only the scorer head-to-head carries a number of the study's own. The sensor, scribe, and cold-start seats are grounded in external literature and primary regulatory text, the hybrid architecture is a design contribution whose integrated performance is future work, and nothing in the compliance discussion is legal advice.

## 8.8 Relation to the thesis

The fraud study belongs in this report as a boundary test of the programme's central discipline rather than as a result about RL. It contributes one worked example of the programme's habit of measuring a claimed capability instead of accepting its label: the label happened to be "AI in fraud detection," the measurement happened to return a negative, and the negative is reported as found. Its scientific claims stand or fall on the fraud artifacts and the cited domain literature alone, and it supplies no evidence for the GRPO, PPO, or SAO results in the surrounding chapters (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_intro.tex).
