# 10. Cross-Study Synthesis, Threats to Validity, Conclusions and Future Work

## 10.1 Cross-Study Synthesis

This report has presented two bodies of work built with different instruments. The first is the P1–P8 corpus, which probes GRPO-style post-training across libraries, model scales, group sizes and datasets and ends in a reporting standard, a machine-readable registry and a controller audit. The second is the E1–E14 campaign, a governance-bound evaluation of one Tinker-trained actor against fourteen held-out agentic and reasoning suites. They are the same methodological commitment applied twice — once to *what does GRPO do*, once to *what does a GRPO-trained actor score*. That commitment is attribution: every comparative claim holds the entire training stack fixed except the one factor under test, and where that was unachievable the confound is reported alongside the number rather than absorbed.

The corpus's own synthesis, recorded at the close of Phase 1, is that on this compute scale the *measurement* layer is useful while most *algorithmic* levers are noise-limited. The dominant measured phenomenon is advantage-signal starvation, with the Zero-Variance Fraction running at approximately 0.72–0.77, and it is easy-driven: the collapsed groups are mostly prompts the model has already solved. That rules out the most tempting remedy, because re-baselining the advantages merely converts the signal into a difficulty proxy with no learning content, and the remedy that remains — a curriculum that removes the wasted rollouts — returned an honest null. The narrow systems observation survived better than any algorithmic one: per-step telemetry separated the available method traces more cleanly than reward-only features, though the classifier's integrity generalisation was never tested (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex).

The Semester 4 papers sharpen those threads rather than reversing them. P2's diagnostics are recomputable from stored raw tensors rather than read back from logged summaries: three stored GSM8K group tensors reproduce ZVF at 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155, pooling to 0.1583, and the correlation between ZVF and final held-out outcome is ρ ≈ 0.27 — real but weak, so ZVF is offered as a diagnostic and not a predictor. P4's matched comparison of GRPO against Dr. GRPO finds the two held-out gains indistinguishable, with pooled mean completion length drifting from 194.4 to 183.5 tokens under a 200-token cap that bounds what the comparison can say. P4's companion independent error-bar audit grades the single P4-facing headline — audit H7, the iter-136 arithmetic late-training-efficiency contrast, paired n = 5, Cohen's d = +2.68, one-sided p_param = 0.031, two-sided p_perm = 0.063 — as DECISIVE under its n < 10 paired-bootstrap rule, while its three NULL verdicts (H3, H4, H5) fall on Pillar-1 and Pillar-3 headlines rather than on this paper's claims. Across all seven audited headlines the aggregate is four DECISIVE, three NULL and none merely suggestive. The asymmetry is the useful part: the headline of ours that survived adversarial audit is a late-training efficiency contrast, not a capability claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_conclusion.tex; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/berkeley/adding_error_bars_summary.json).

P3 and P7 both treat group size as a lever and are best read as a pair, because they disagree in emphasis. P3 finds that under saturation the retention ratio between G = 2 and G = 16 is approximately 1.00, that the effective signal-to-noise ratio reaches only about 52% of the √G ideal, and that a 505-task conditional utility audit selects G = 4 over G = 5 by 0.00177 with a 95% bootstrap interval of [0.00082, 0.00270] — positive, small, and explicitly cohort- and objective-conditional. P7 models usable signal per prompt as S = p(1−p)(1 − h_G(p)) with h_G(p) = p^G + (1−p)^G, derives three falsifiable consequences, and checks them: a 368-run Weights & Biases audit across seven models reproduces the predicted U-shape in accuracy, with ZVF 0.95–0.97 at both reward extremes and 0.25–0.29 at mid-range rewards of 0.35–0.50; a model × G grid confirms the monotone effect at interior accuracy, Qwen3-32B moving 0.33 → 0.18 → 0.08 across G = 4/8/16; and a toy-scale open-backward-pass experiment finds corr(‖∇‖, p(1−p)) = +0.71 on a 0.5B model, which the paper labels directional only. Where P3's sweep could not find an optimum, P7 supplies a mechanism under which a shallow one should exist. That is a reconciliation, not a resolution: the mechanism is checked in telemetry, and its causal link to the gradient remains toy-scale (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

The same paper is clear about the control it does not have. A micro-jitter falsification collapses batch ZVF from 0.158 to 0.000 while leaving the pairwise-contrast density unchanged, which is why PCD rather than ZVF is promoted as the control signal. In a four-arm open-trainer audit, adaptive group size matches the best fixed-recipe held-out gain, +0.575, at 186 rollouts, while DAPO-style dynamic sampling drives ZVF to 0.00 at +45% rollout cost — an accuracy-versus-compute trade, not a controller win. In the larger 2,560-observation controller trace, 1,723 of 1,867 escalation events, 92.3%, occur on all-correct groups and only 144 on all-wrong, so a symmetric ZVF trigger spends most of its budget on prompts already solved; deleting those fires is a counterfactual only, because performance preservation was not measured. Adaptive group size is therefore not promoted to a recommended policy (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

P5 and P6 supply the corpus's methodological claim, and they contain its sharpest internal tension. P5's headline exhibits are a nominally matched-configuration backend audit whose final training rewards span 5.0% to 85.6% — a 17× same-label swing the paper declines to assign to the backend, because the managed stack also silently pinned a different base checkpoint, and that undisclosed bundling is itself part of the exhibit — and a pair of runs carrying the same "DAPO" label in which an open trainer with true dynamic sampling produced mean ZVF 0.00 while a closed stack running an asymmetric-clip surrogate produced mean ZVF 0.58. Alongside them sits a third exhibit: a 12-cell head-to-head of four labelled algorithms on one fixed stack, landing within a 0.034 band with mean last-10 reward 0.710–0.744, a spread the paper calls comparable to seed-level variation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). P5's framing is that a label-only comparison can hide stack-level variation, and that the converse also holds: when the stack is genuinely fixed, the labels stop separating. P6 reads the same exhibit in the reverse direction, using it to motivate a schema while explicitly declining to re-claim it as novel empirical science; on the twenty seed entries the MIN-REPORT auditor badge spans 43–96, and *stackdiff* flags the open-versus-closed "DAPO" pair as an R5 label-flip risk from manifests alone (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex).

Two gaps should be named. The first is that P7's controller audit and P5's stack-attribution programme have not been joined: the controller is evaluated on open trainers where the update rule is auditable, while the standard is motivated by a closed-stack divergence that cannot be audited. The second is P8, the credit-card fraud side-probe, absorbed here as a measurement-discipline case study and nothing more. Its two AUCs are not a like-for-like ranking: XGBoost's 0.7955 and the Qwen3.5-4B SFT arm's 0.48268 on a positive-enriched 500-row held-out evaluation sit on different held-out splits by construction (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_abstract.tex).

The E1–E14 campaign is a different instrument and should be read as one. A single Tinker-trained actor — base `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at commit `995ad96e…`, with adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal-tinker-cf0ad8c1…` at commit `64444133…`, in bfloat16 — was evaluated against fourteen held-out suites, each through its own native evaluator at a pinned revision. Four scopes closed strictly complete: LAB-Bench public at 1,967/1,967 across eight categories, AgentDojo benign utility at 97/97, VerilogEval at 312/312 with 129/312 = 41.35% pass@1 canonical, and Omni-MATH with all 4,428 dispositions recorded and the official accuracy of 2,271/4,428 = 51.31% reproduced. Two suites carry full verified scores under their original contracts: SWE-bench Pro at 2/731 = 0.274% pass@1 and VerilogEval. The rest are partial, quota-blocked or externally blocked, each with a terminal state and a named gate (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md; outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

What the campaign establishes is descriptive, and its scope matters. For *this* actor, at *this* revision, under *each suite's own* evaluator and denominator, these are the scores. It is not a post-training delta: no per-suite base-model comparator appears in the campaign ledger, so no campaign number, high or low, is evidence about what the GRPO step contributed. Where the campaign does carry a base-model arm it is the separately authorised E4 BankerToolBench rerun, and there the mean reward was 0.0 across 100/100 completed trials, with the audited mechanism recorded as agents dying after 2–14 steps with no `deliverables/` output so that the verifier short-circuits 0.0; the ledger's verdict is that this 0.0 "measures tool-dialogue collapse, not finance reasoning" (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §9.7).

What governance bought is visible in the ledger itself: eleven of eleven deterministic checks passing on 2026-09-19, and a historical snapshot recording E7 as "1/28 errored" sitting visibly beside the superseding table's "1/46 attempted" rather than being quietly reconciled (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

## 10.2 Rigour and Threats to Validity

**What rigour was exercised.** Diagnostics are recomputable from stored raw tensors, and headline structural claims were re-verified by deterministic code checks on 2026-09-19, 11/11 PASS, covering exact divisions, receipt presence, sealed-artifact hash equality and lost-source absence probes (source: outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json). Held-out *n* is reported alongside every gain, and failed runs are counted rather than hidden: approximately $30–35 of Tinker spend went to failed runs, disclosed because concealing it would understate reproduction cost by roughly 25% (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §3).

**Statistical power: single-seed Tinker experiments.** Each Tinker configuration ran once, because cost precluded repetition, so there are no variance estimates and no Tinker-side significance tests. The limitations document records that Henderson et al. (2018) recommends at least ten seeds, that this bar was met only for the TRL baseline and the held-out GSM8K evaluation, and that compute constraints then forced that evaluation to five seeds (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1); both are reported rather than one chosen. The 30B MoE scaling analysis uses three seeds; the bootstrap confidence intervals assume independent observations, so correlation from shared initialisation or data ordering could narrow the true intervals; and secondary analyses carry uncorrected p-values. Quantitative conclusions therefore come from the open-source side, where every hyperparameter is auditable, while Tinker figures are descriptive (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2).

**Horizons of 30–50 steps.** The Tinker runs are early-training snapshots; long-horizon effects such as reward hacking, catastrophic forgetting and late-stage policy collapse are unlikely to manifest, and the source conclusion is direct: "Asymptotic claims are unsupported" (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1). This bounds results rather than vitiating them: P1's failure to recover a scaling law across roughly 2.4 orders of magnitude is a bounded negative, and P4's drift from 194.4 to 183.5 mean completion tokens is bounded by the 200-token cap.

**Closed Tinker internals.** The Tinker SDK exposes a custom loss path, a limited optimiser surface and standard LoRA hyperparameters, but not the exact server-side GRPO loss formulation, the reward normalisation or baseline-subtraction scheme, minibatch or gradient-accumulation strategy, hardware configuration, or system-level telemetry (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.1). The consequence is best quoted: Tinker results measure "the **platform's** implementation of GRPO, not an abstract algorithmic specification". The illustrative case is a reader asking why Tinker GRPO scores 99.9% on GSM8K while TRL GRPO scores 73.4% on the same task; seed variance and a model-size confound can be ruled out through the same-model Qwen3-8B comparison, but the implementation itself cannot (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2). The threat propagates into the campaign, whose actor was trained on Tinker. The SDK is also a moving target: Phase 1 pinned `tinker==0.16.1`, while the September 2026 bridge required `tinker==0.30.0` after the older SDK produced a no-longer-supported error (source: outputs/TINKER_SAMPLER_STATUS_2026-09-20.json).

**Per-suite native denominators are not comparable.** Every E1–E14 result is expressed on its own suite's denominator, and those denominators differ in size, in what they count and in what they exclude. SWE-bench Pro is 2/731, whose denominator retains 14 generation failures and 4 artifact losses alongside 713 native evaluations; APEX-Agents is 7/480; E9 MLE-bench is 40/75 competitions natively graded, stated as 53.33% coverage with a null suite score, because coverage and score are different quantities; E13 BALROG is 13/255 episodes; E6 WebArena is 0/812; E9's replacement MLDevBench is 0/34 graded; E1's replacement SWE-bench Multilingual is 35/300 graded; and E14's Omni-MATH records 4,428 dispositions of which 4,426 were accepted, with the official accuracy computed over all 4,428 (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A 41.35% on one denominator and a 0.274% on another do not license a comparison, an average or a ranking, and none is offered; the rule holds within lanes too, and the ledger states it flatly: "N/A means unverified; no pooled results" (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

**No cross-suite aggregate.** No mean, median or composite index over suites appears anywhere in this report or in the campaign materials it draws on: an aggregate over native denominators would be a number with no referent, mixing suites that measure different capabilities at different difficulties under different scoring rules. The cost is that the campaign has no single headline; the four strictly complete scopes plus the two original-contract scores are the honest maximum.

**The E10 benign-utility scope says nothing about adversarial robustness.** The E10 lane's completed evidence is a benign-utility evaluation of 97/97 tasks under AgentDojo's benign scope; its original contract, private AgentHarm tasks, is externally blocked and carries a closure record with a named reopen condition. The completeness result therefore carries no adversarial-robustness measurement, and this report makes no such claim for the trained actor. The lane has a dual-use dimension, though the limitations analysis finds the marginal uplift of the released artefacts small, because the base model's capability is overwhelmingly pre-existing: the Qwen3-8B base-model control already scores 82.0% held-out on GSM8K, and full GRPO adds only +1.3 points to 83.3%, not significant at p = 0.26 (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §2.2, §2.3).

**The retained E11 result came through the original sampler.** The VerilogEval lane's paid-run receipt records `model.sampler_path = tinker://cf0ad8c1-1f1b-5ff3-8bd7-2a0bf232657b:train:0/sampler_weights/step_seed809_40` with `model.source = tinker_sampler_checkpoint`, so the scores come from sampling the trained adapter through the Tinker managed sampler rather than from a locally served copy of the weights (source: outputs/e11_verilog_eval/e11_trained_step40_receipt.json). That route no longer exists: the sampler status record of 2026-09-20 reports a bridge deployment booting to a sampler-weights 404, `list_checkpoints` on the `cf0ad8c1` run returning 404 with the run purged, no `last_sampler_checkpoint` on any of twenty training runs, and the status string `TINKER_SAMPLER_WEIGHTS_DELETED_E9_AND_BRIDGE_AFFECTED`; the recorded recovery path is to serve the HuggingFace mirror `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal` at commit `64444133…` through vLLM on Modal (source: outputs/TINKER_SAMPLER_STATUS_2026-09-20.json). The threat is not that the number is wrong — it is receipt-bound, provenance recorded — but that the sampling path is not re-runnable, so a re-run would be a same-weights, different-sampler comparison whose divergence is unmeasured. The receipt's `outstanding_blockers` field further records that the decontamination receipt ID does not exist and that the split-manifest validator still fails on it (source: outputs/e11_verilog_eval/e11_trained_step40_receipt.json).

**Known evidence loss in the finish-era recovery source.** The record is explicit: the `.codex-run` deletion recorded in `finish/STATE_RECONCILIATION_2026-09-19.md` removed the execution sources of the finish-era recovery and build code — the E1 wave-10 controller, the E9 restored dependencies and the E6 validator — while sealed requests, validation receipts and test logs survive with matching hashes, and the replacement lanes' native runners survive under `zvf-program/flagship/` (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §6). The lost-source policy was applied rather than waived: a faithful re-implementation was written into a tracked path under `zvf-program/e1_wave10/`, where it verifies 10/10 targeted regressions offline (`test_process_group.py` 4/4, `test_cleanup_regressions.py` 6/6) (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §9.5). Two consequences follow: a sealed request without its execution source is not a rerun, so the E9 MLDevBench runtime recipe reconstruction is still owed; and the policy response — execution sources henceforth live under tracked paths — cannot restore what was already lost. A related item: the Jev (TypeSafe) judgment harness lost reliability from roughly 13:10Z on 2026-09-19, both blocker-classification batteries returning degenerate distributions and a controlled yes-probe falling from about 1.0 to 0.3; the receipts are retained as incident evidence and the batteries must be re-run once the health check passes, so the ledger's first-uncleared-blocker column stands on document evidence rather than judgment output (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §8).

**Scope and confound inventory.** Several Phase-1 threats remain live. Many early runs logged train-set rewards only, so time-series comparisons spanning old and new runs must account for the instrumentation change. Initial phases used LoRA at ranks 8–64 exclusively; historical baseline comparisons use default hyperparameters from the Tinker cookbook and the TRL documentation; and no full learning-rate × rank × batch × group-size grid was swept. Tinker, Modal, Colab and the PES A100 node use different accelerators, memory hierarchies and training stacks, so observed differences in reward dynamics are not purely algorithmic, and the work was not pre-registered. The model-scale range is 0.6B–235B, so dense-70B and trillion-parameter MoE extrapolations are unreliable; benchmark coverage is narrow and biased toward verifiable rewards such as exact match, unit tests and schema validity, with no human preference data, so findings should not be extended to reward-model-based RLHF; and every author is based at one institution in Bengaluru, so benchmark choices and evaluation design reflect that context (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.2, §7.3).

The resource accounting is itself a threat. Authors' out-of-pocket spend was $130–140, with roughly 60 A100 GPU-hours of in-kind PES University compute valued at approximately $270 and projected carbon of about 776 kWh and about 296 kg CO₂-eq; the dominant uncertainty is the Tinker GPU-hour count, inferred because the platform exposes no hardware telemetry, giving a pessimistic Tinker contribution of about 540 kg against an optimistic one of about 130 kg. No offsets were purchased; the mitigation offered is reproducibility by artefact (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §3, §4).

## 10.3 Conclusions

This report set out to build a multi-framework benchmark for GRPO-style post-training of large language models and to study that post-training under a single methodological rule: hold the stack fixed, change one factor, report what happens. Both halves were completed, and the honest summary is less flattering, and more useful, than a positive result would have been.

On the analytical side the contribution is a measurement layer and a set of bounded negatives. The Zero-Variance Fraction is a reproducible diagnostic of advantage-signal starvation, recomputable from stored tensors, measured at roughly 0.72–0.77 with a weak ρ ≈ 0.27 association with final held-out outcome — descriptive, not predictive. Its theory has been made falsifiable, and two of its three consequences reproduced: the U-shape in accuracy across reward levels and the monotone group-size effect; the gradient link remains toy-scale and directional (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Group size therefore has a mechanism and a shallow optimum rather than a universal best value. Length bias is real at the level of run-level trajectories — four of eleven GRPO runs peak before 65% of training with terminal reward below 90% of peak (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §2.2) — but it does not separate GRPO from Dr. GRPO on held-out gain. The levers we could test — a naive curriculum, a matched-token-budget intervention, an integer group-size sweep — did not produce reliable held-out gains. The strongest original contribution remains P5, the reporting standard, whose status the record states precisely: a targeted search of the GRPO, model-reporting and provenance literature through July 2026 did not identify an equivalent GRPO-specific machine-readable run datasheet with rollout provenance, and this is "an evidence-bounded novelty claim, not proof of non-existence" (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The earlier second bet — predictive layer freezing from step-1 behaviour — is weakened rather than withdrawn: it held at 1.5B and collapsed to chance at 3B across four seeds, at a mean of 0.083 ± 0.048.

On the empirical side the E1–E14 campaign delivered what a governance-first campaign can deliver: complete, receipt-bound results on four suites; full verified original-contract scores on two; and an explicit, auditable terminal state for every remaining lane, with a named gate and a named reopen condition instead of an indefinite pending. Its discipline is negative, which is why it is credible: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled, no cross-suite aggregate is computed, and no claim of improvement over a baseline or over the base model is made anywhere in this work. The strongest complete positive result — 129/312 = 41.35% pass@1 on VerilogEval, at full 312/312 coverage — is reported as a measurement of one actor on one suite, with its provenance recorded down to the sampler path.

The report's overall conclusion is about method rather than algorithms. On this compute scale, and with these instruments, the measurements are informative while the algorithmic levers are noise-limited; the stack is a material part of the result, so a label without its stack is not a reproducible experimental specification; and the most durable artefacts produced here are the ones that make other people's results checkable — the ZVF/PCD diagnostic layer, the eight-item minimum reportable stack, the registry and its CLI, and a campaign ledger in which every number is bound to a receipt and every gap has a gate.

Beyond completing the campaign, the plan inherits five Phase-1 items, each answering a threat identified above: adequately powered evaluation, moving from held-out *n* of 8–20 to the full GSM8K test split or a large stratified subset at five or more seeds; a token-budget-optimal curriculum with staleness bounds, tested multi-seed; re-examining P1 to ask whether the emerged mid-to-late training band supports a FLOP-saving freeze schedule even though step-1 predictability did not survive scaling; hardening the P8 classifier on grouped run-level and temporal splits rather than row-wise cross-validation; and publication, consolidating the diagnostic findings and the reporting standard into a paper draft (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). Semester 4 adds three extensions: the pre-registered controller bakeoff against static G = 16 and naive boundary heuristics, the test P7 declines to claim in advance of; a cross-framework divergence study executing an identical task, reward, decoding and seed configuration across TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF and Tinker, converting P5's attribution argument from an audit of existing runs into a designed experiment; and the joint programme connecting them — dogfooding the registry schema on the campaign's own runs so that every future benchmark result ships with a verifiable provenance record.

Two commitments carry forward, because the threats above are structural rather than incidental. The first is the never-pool rule: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers stay separate, per-suite native denominators are never averaged, and launching a lane changes its coverage, not the meaning of the numbers already recorded. The second is candour about failure, which here is not a stylistic preference but the mechanism that made the ledger trustworthy: the E11 sampler path, the E4 zero, the lost execution sources, the seed-count discrepancy and the failed-run spend are all reported, and any Phase-2 result that turns out badly will be reported the same way.
