# 9. Cross-Study Synthesis, Threats to Validity, Conclusions and Future Work

## 9.1 Cross-Study Synthesis

This report has presented two bodies of work built with different instruments. The first is the P1–P8 corpus, which probes GRPO-style post-training across libraries, model scales, group sizes and datasets and ends in a reporting standard, a machine-readable registry and a controller audit. The second is the E1–E14 campaign, a governance-bound evaluation of one Tinker-trained actor against fourteen agentic and reasoning suites that were not part of its training data. They are the same methodological commitment applied twice — once to *what does GRPO do*, once to *what does a GRPO-trained actor score*. That commitment is attribution: every comparative claim holds the entire training stack fixed except the one factor under test, and where that was unachievable the confound is reported alongside the number rather than absorbed.

The corpus's own synthesis, recorded at the close of Phase 1, is that on this compute scale the *measurement* layer is useful while most *algorithmic* levers are noise-limited. The dominant measured phenomenon is advantage-signal starvation, with the Zero-Variance Fraction at approximately 0.72–0.77 on the Phase-1 main configuration, and it is easy-driven: the collapsed groups are mostly prompts the model has already solved. That rules out the most tempting remedy, because re-baselining the advantages merely converts the signal into a difficulty proxy with no learning content, and the remedy that remains — a curriculum that removes the wasted rollouts — returned a null. The narrow systems observation survived better than any algorithmic one: per-step telemetry separated the available method traces more cleanly than reward-only features, though the classifier's integrity generalisation was never tested (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex).

The Semester 4 internal reports sharpen those threads rather than reversing them. P2's diagnostics are recomputable from stored raw tensors rather than read back from logged summaries: three stored GSM8K group tensors reproduce ZVF at 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155, pooling to 0.1583, and the correlation between ZVF and final held-out outcome, ρ = 0.27, is not significant (95% CI [−0.37, 0.88], n = 23 pooled cells). ZVF is therefore offered as a diagnostic and not a predictor. Once mean reward is regressed out, the pooled measured residual is r = +0.21 (95% CI [−0.31, +0.62], n = 17), so ZVF is not an incremental predictor either (Chapter 6 §6.3.4). P4's matched comparison of GRPO against Dr. GRPO finds the two held-out gains indistinguishable, with pooled mean completion length drifting from 194.4 to 183.5 tokens under a 200-token cap that bounds what the comparison can say. P4's companion error-bar audit grades the single P4-facing headline as DECISIVE: audit H7, the iter-136 arithmetic late-training-efficiency contrast, paired n = 5 seeds, $d_z = +2.68$. Re-examined, it is *suggestive* only (Chapter 6 §6.5.4). Its "one-sided parametric p = 0.031" is the floor of an exact one-sided Wilcoxon test at n = 5. The two-sided exact sign-flip p is 0.0625, even though all five pairs point the same way. The direction was one of eight hypotheses in its family, and Holm correction across those eight raises the family's p to 0.25. The audit's other three DECISIVE grades do not survive either. Two (H1, H2) rest on an interval that excludes its own point estimate and on four reconstructed budgets (§6.4.2; Table E.1), and one (H6) is an AUROC computed on a synthetic and imputed panel (§6.3.4). Of the seven audited headlines, then, none is decisive, one is suggestive and the rest are null or hypothesis-generating. What survives is a consistent-direction late-training efficiency contrast on a training metric, not a capability claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_conclusion.tex; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/berkeley/adding_error_bars_summary.json).

P3 and P7 both treat group size as a lever and are best read as a pair, because they disagree in emphasis. P3 finds that under saturation the retention ratio between G = 2 and G = 16 is approximately 1.00. That ratio is of last-10 training reward, and a seed-level TOST (n = 3, margin ±0.02) does not establish G = 16 as equivalent to G = 2 (p = 0.056; Chapter 6 §6.4.1). P3 also finds that the effective signal-to-noise ratio reaches only about 52% of the √G ideal, and that a 505-task conditional utility audit selects G = 4 over G = 5 by 0.00177 with a 95% bootstrap interval of [0.00082, 0.00270] — positive, small, and explicitly cohort- and objective-conditional. P7 models usable signal per prompt as $S = p(1-p)(1-h_G(p))$ with $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$, derives three falsifiable consequences, and checks them: a 368-run Weights & Biases audit across seven models reproduces the predicted U-shape in accuracy, with ZVF 0.95–0.97 at the low-reward extreme and 0.87–0.96 at the high-reward extreme and 0.25–0.29 at mid-range rewards of 0.35–0.50; a model × G grid confirms the monotone effect at interior accuracy, Qwen3-32B moving 0.33 → 0.18 → 0.08 across G = 4/8/16; and a toy-scale open-backward-pass experiment finds corr(‖∇‖, p(1−p)) = +0.71 on a 0.5B model, which the P7 report labels directional only. Where P3's sweep could not find an optimum, P7 supplies a mechanism under which a shallow one should exist. That is a reconciliation, not a resolution: the mechanism is checked in telemetry, and its causal link to the gradient remains toy-scale (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

The same report is clear about the control it does not have. A micro-jitter falsification collapses batch ZVF from 0.158 to 0.000 while leaving the pairwise-contrast density unchanged, which is why PCD rather than ZVF is promoted as the control signal. In a four-arm open-trainer audit, adaptive group size matches the best fixed-recipe held-out gain, +0.575, at 186 rollouts, while DAPO-style dynamic sampling drives ZVF to 0.00 at +45% rollout cost — an accuracy-versus-compute trade, not a controller win. In the larger 2,560-observation controller trace, 1,723 of 1,867 escalation events, 92.3%, occur on all-correct groups and only 144 on all-wrong, so a symmetric ZVF trigger spends most of its budget on prompts already solved; deleting those fires is a counterfactual only, because performance preservation was not measured. Adaptive group size is therefore not promoted to a recommended policy (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex).

P5 and P6 supply the corpus's methodological claim, and they contain its sharpest internal tension. P5's headline exhibits are a nominally matched-configuration backend audit whose final training rewards span 5.0% to 85.6% — a 17× same-label swing the P5 report declines to assign to the backend, because the managed stack also silently pinned a different base checkpoint (Qwen3-8B-Base on Tinker against the instruction-tuned Qwen3-8B on TRL), and that undisclosed bundling is itself part of the exhibit — and a pair of runs carrying the same "DAPO" label in which an open trainer with true dynamic sampling produced mean ZVF 0.00 while a closed stack running an asymmetric-clip surrogate produced mean ZVF 0.58. Alongside them sits a third exhibit: a 12-cell head-to-head of four labelled algorithms on one fixed stack, landing within a 0.034 band with mean last-10 reward 0.710–0.744, a spread the P5 report calls comparable to seed-level variation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). P5's framing is that a label-only comparison can hide stack-level variation, and that the converse also holds: when the stack is genuinely fixed, the labels stop separating. P6 reads the same exhibit in the reverse direction, using it to motivate a schema while explicitly declining to re-claim it as novel empirical science; on the twenty seed entries the MIN-REPORT auditor badge spans 43–96, and *stackdiff* flags the open-versus-closed "DAPO" pair as an R5 label-flip risk from manifests alone (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex).

Two gaps should be named. The first is that P7's controller audit and P5's stack-attribution programme have not been joined: the controller is evaluated on open trainers where the update rule is auditable, while the standard is motivated by a closed-stack divergence that cannot be audited. The second is P8, the credit-card fraud side-probe, absorbed here as a measurement-discipline case study and nothing more. Its two AUCs are not a like-for-like ranking: XGBoost's 0.7955 and the Qwen3.5-4B SFT arm's 0.48268 on a positive-enriched 500-row held-out evaluation sit on different held-out splits by construction (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/P08_fraud/sections/p8_abstract.tex).

The E1–E14 campaign is a different instrument and should be read as one. A single Tinker-trained actor — base `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at commit `995ad96e…`, with adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal-tinker-cf0ad8c1…` at commit `64444133…`, in bfloat16 — was evaluated against fourteen suites that were not part of its training data, each through its own native evaluator at a pinned revision. Three scopes closed strictly complete: two replacement scopes, LAB-Bench public at 1,967/1,967 across eight categories and AgentDojo benign utility with 97/97 episodes completed and utility 88/97 = 0.907 (Wilson 95% CI [0.833, 0.950]), and one original-contract suite, VerilogEval, at 312/312 with 129/312 = 41.35% pass@1 canonical (Wilson 95% CI [36.0%, 46.9%]). Omni-MATH closed terminal-complete under its native protocol, with all 4,428 dispositions recorded, 4,426 accepted, and an accuracy of 2,271/4,428 = 51.29% with the two unjudged rows counted as failures (the official scorer reproduces 51.31% over the 4,426 judged rows) (source: outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json). Two suites carry full-coverage scores under their original contracts: SWE-bench Pro at 2/731 = 0.274% pass@1 (Wilson 95% CI [0.08%, 0.99%]) and VerilogEval, the same E11 result counted once. Six further replacement scopes were run in full on 27 September 2026 (Table 8.C): CORE-Bench hard at 27/45 = 0.600, Tau3 at 8/97 = 0.082, WebArena at 90/812 = 0.111 (a lower bound, with 108 judge-dependent tasks ungraded), ML-Dev-Bench at 10/34 = 0.294, BALROG at 26.1% progression over 255 episodes, and SWE-bench Multilingual at 1/190 on the tasks not attempted before, kept separate from the earlier 4/110. Lane scope and status are tabulated once, in Table C.3. The rest are partial or externally blocked, each with a terminal state and a named gate (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md; outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

What the campaign establishes is descriptive, and its scope matters. For *this* actor, at *this* revision, under *each suite's own* evaluator and denominator, these are the scores. It is not a post-training delta: no per-suite base-model comparator appears in the campaign ledger at the campaign's own scopes, so no campaign number, high or low, is evidence about what the GRPO step contributed. The one paired comparison of trained actor and base model is the 26 September small-scale rerun (Table 8.B): 14 lanes, 5 to 151 identical items each, one serving engine. It finds no significant difference in any lane, which at those sizes is inconclusive rather than evidence of no effect. The separately authorised E4 BankerToolBench base-model rerun returned a mean reward of 0.0 across 100/100 completed trials. That zero is attributed to the serving bridge, which passed no stop sequences, so agents ran into invented turns and ended after 2–14 steps with no `deliverables/` output (Section 8.5; source: outputs/e1_e14_small_scale_2026-09-26/E4/BRIDGE_STOP_TOKEN_FINDING.md). It measures the harness, not the model.

What governance bought is visible in the ledger itself: eleven of eleven deterministic checks passing on 2026-09-19, and a historical snapshot recording E7 as "1/28 errored" sitting visibly beside the superseding table's "1/46 attempted" rather than being quietly reconciled (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

## 9.2 Rigour and Threats to Validity

**What rigour was exercised.** Diagnostics are recomputable from stored raw tensors, and headline structural claims were re-verified by deterministic code checks on 2026-09-19, 11/11 PASS, covering exact divisions, receipt presence, sealed-artifact hash equality and lost-source absence probes (source: outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json). Held-out *n* is reported alongside every gain, and failed runs are counted rather than hidden: approximately $30–35 of Tinker spend went to failed runs, disclosed because concealing it would understate reproduction cost by roughly 25% (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §3).

**Statistical power: single-seed Tinker experiments.** Most Tinker configurations ran once, because cost precluded repetition, and those single-seed runs carry no variance estimates or significance tests. The exceptions are stated where they occur. They are the three-seed-per-arm managed head-to-head (Chapter 7 §7.1), the five-seed Qwen3-8B held-out control (p = 0.26, Chapter 6 §6.5.4) and the Qwen3-8B scale extension. Henderson et al. (2018) recommend at least ten seeds, and no result family reported here reaches that bar. The seed counts are: one seed for most Tinker runs and all P1 anchors; three for the P3 sweep, the P4 GSM8K-CoT and scale-extension arms and the managed head-to-head; five for the same-stack PPO/GRPO contrast, the P4 arithmetic arms and the Qwen3-8B pre/post control; and eight for the variant audit (P11). The limitations document says the ten-seed bar was met for the TRL baseline and the GSM8K evaluation and then that the evaluation was cut to five seeds; the run records in Appendix A show five seeds for both the TRL Modal campaign (Table A.3) and the GSM8K pre/post evaluation (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1; Table 1.1). The 30B MoE scaling analysis uses three seeds; the bootstrap confidence intervals assume independent observations, so any positive correlation from shared initialisation or data ordering would make the reported intervals too narrow, and the true intervals wider; and secondary analyses carry uncorrected p-values. Quantitative conclusions therefore come from the open-source side, where every hyperparameter is auditable, while Tinker figures are descriptive (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2).

**Horizons of 30–50 steps.** The Tinker runs are early-training snapshots; long-horizon effects such as reward hacking, catastrophic forgetting and late-stage policy collapse are unlikely to manifest, and the source conclusion is direct: "Asymptotic claims are unsupported" (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1). This bounds results rather than vitiating them: P1's failure to recover a scaling law across roughly 2.4 orders of magnitude is a bounded negative, and P4's drift from 194.4 to 183.5 mean completion tokens is bounded by the 200-token cap.

**Closed Tinker internals.** The Tinker SDK exposes a custom loss path, a limited optimiser surface and standard LoRA hyperparameters, but not the exact server-side GRPO loss formulation, the reward normalisation or baseline-subtraction scheme, minibatch or gradient-accumulation strategy, hardware configuration, or system-level telemetry (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.1). This applies unevenly: the custom-loss Tinker runners, including the one that trained the campaign actor, define their loss in client code (REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline, Table 4.2), whereas the runners that use Tinker's built-in `importance_sampling` loss leave its server-side details unrecorded. The consequence is best quoted: Tinker results measure "the **platform's** implementation of GRPO, not an abstract algorithmic specification". The illustrative case in the limitations document compares a pooled last-10 training reward of 99.9% for Tinker GRPO with 73.4% for TRL GRPO on GSM8K. Those figures are training rewards, not held-out accuracy, and they pool Tinker runs on 4B–235B models against TRL on Qwen2.5-0.5B, so model size and implementation are confounded; the source itself attributes part of the gap to the model difference (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2; platform_tinker/reports/combined/combined_capstone.tex). The only same-model pair, Qwen3-8B, used the Base checkpoint on Tinker and the Instruct checkpoint on TRL (Table A.2), so it does not remove the confound either. The threat propagates into the campaign, whose actor was trained on Tinker. The SDK is also a moving target: Phase 1 pinned `tinker==0.16.1`, while the September 2026 bridge required `tinker==0.30.0` after the older SDK produced a no-longer-supported error (source: outputs/TINKER_SAMPLER_STATUS_2026-09-20.json).

**Per-suite native denominators are not comparable.** Every E1–E14 result is expressed on its own suite's denominator, and those denominators differ in size, in what they count and in what they exclude. SWE-bench Pro is 2/731, whose denominator retains 14 generation failures and 4 artifact losses alongside 713 native evaluations; APEX-Agents is 7/480; E9 MLE-bench is 40/75 competitions natively graded, stated as 53.33% coverage with a null suite score, because coverage and score are different quantities; E1's replacement SWE-bench Multilingual is 1/190 on one contract and 4/110 on another, never summed to a 300-task figure; E6 WebArena is 90/812 with 108 tasks ungraded, so its denominator counts items that were never graded; E13 BALROG is a mean of six per-environment means rather than a rate over episodes; and E14's Omni-MATH records 4,428 dispositions of which 4,426 were accepted, with the headline accuracy computed over all 4,428 (the official scorer's 51.31% uses the 4,426 judged rows) (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A 41.35% on one denominator and a 0.274% on another do not license a comparison, an average or a ranking, and none is offered; the rule holds within lanes too, and the ledger states it flatly: "N/A means unverified; no pooled results" (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md).

**No cross-suite aggregate.** No mean, median or composite index over suites appears anywhere in this report or in the campaign materials it draws on: an aggregate over native denominators would be a number with no referent, mixing suites that measure different capabilities at different difficulties under different scoring rules. The cost is that the campaign has no single headline; the three strictly complete scopes, the terminal-complete Omni-MATH scope, the six replacement scopes completed on 27 September and the two original-contract scores are the most the record supports.

**The E10 benign-utility scope says nothing about adversarial robustness.** The E10 lane's completed evidence is a benign-utility evaluation of 97/97 completed tasks with utility 88/97 = 0.907 under AgentDojo's benign scope; its original contract, private AgentHarm tasks, is externally blocked and carries a closure record with a named reopen condition. The completeness result therefore carries no adversarial-robustness measurement, and this report makes no such claim for the trained actor. The lane has a dual-use dimension, though the limitations analysis finds the marginal uplift of the released artefacts small, because the base model's capability is overwhelmingly pre-existing: the pre-RL Qwen3-8B-Instruct checkpoint already scores 82.0% on held-out GSM8K, and full GRPO adds only +1.3 points to 83.3%, not significant at p = 0.26. The 82.0% is the same Instruct checkpoint before RL, not a separate base model (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §2.2, §2.3; platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex).

**The retained E11 result came through the original sampler.** The receipt of the 129/312 full run records `model.sampler_path = tinker://cf0ad8c1-1f1b-5ff3-8bd7-2a0bf232657b:train:0/sampler_weights/seed809_final`, so the scores come from sampling the trained adapter through the Tinker managed sampler rather than from a locally served copy of the weights (source: outputs/modal_e1_e14/2026-08-16/e11_full_receipt.json). That route no longer exists: the sampler status record of 2026-09-20 reports a bridge deployment booting to a sampler-weights 404, `list_checkpoints` on the `cf0ad8c1` run returning 404 with the run purged, no `last_sampler_checkpoint` on any of twenty training runs, and the status string `TINKER_SAMPLER_WEIGHTS_DELETED_E9_AND_BRIDGE_AFFECTED`; the recorded recovery path is to serve the HuggingFace mirror `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal` at commit `64444133…` through vLLM on Modal (source: outputs/TINKER_SAMPLER_STATUS_2026-09-20.json). The threat is not that the number is wrong — it is receipt-bound, provenance recorded — but that the sampling path is not re-runnable, so a re-run would be a same-weights, different-sampler comparison whose divergence is unmeasured. The full receipt's `outstanding_blockers` field is empty. The blocker that the decontamination receipt ID does not exist and that the split-manifest validator still fails belongs to the separate four-task step-40 pilot, whose receipt records the `step_seed809_40` sampler path (Chapter 8 §8.1; source: outputs/e11_verilog_eval/e11_trained_step40_receipt.json).

**Known evidence loss in the finish-era recovery source.** The record is explicit: the `.codex-run` deletion recorded in `finish/STATE_RECONCILIATION_2026-09-19.md` removed the execution sources of the finish-era recovery and build code — the E1 wave-10 controller, the E9 restored dependencies and the E6 validator — while sealed requests, validation receipts and test logs survive with matching hashes, and the replacement lanes' native runners survive under `zvf-program/flagship/` (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §6). The lost-source policy was applied rather than waived: a faithful re-implementation was written into a tracked path under `zvf-program/e1_wave10/`, where it verifies 10/10 targeted regressions offline (`test_process_group.py` 4/4, `test_cleanup_regressions.py` 6/6) (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §9.5). Two consequences follow: a sealed request without its execution source is not a rerun, so the E9 MLDevBench runtime recipe was never reconstructed (the 27 September run built a fresh environment on Google Cloud instead); and the policy response — execution sources henceforth live under tracked paths — cannot restore what was already lost. A related item: the Jev (TypeSafe) judgment harness lost reliability from roughly 13:10Z on 2026-09-19, both blocker-classification batteries returning degenerate distributions and a controlled yes-probe falling from about 1.0 to 0.3; the receipts are retained as incident evidence and the batteries must be re-run once the health check passes, so the ledger's first-uncleared-blocker column stands on document evidence rather than judgment output (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §8).

**Scope and confound inventory.** Several Phase-1 threats remain live. Many early runs logged train-set rewards only, so time-series comparisons spanning old and new runs must account for the instrumentation change. Initial phases used LoRA at ranks 8–64 exclusively; historical baseline comparisons use default hyperparameters from the Tinker cookbook and the TRL documentation; and no full learning-rate × rank × batch × group-size grid was swept. Tinker, Modal, Colab and the PES A100 node use different accelerators, memory hierarchies and training stacks, so observed differences in reward dynamics are not purely algorithmic, and the work was not pre-registered. The model-scale range is 0.6B–1T, with the anchors above 235B single-seed and descriptive (Chapter 6 §6.1); the limitations record's "reliable" range of 0.6B–235B is the multi-run part of that roster, so dense-70B and trillion-parameter MoE extrapolations are unreliable; benchmark coverage is narrow and biased toward verifiable rewards such as exact match, unit tests and schema validity, with no human preference data, so findings should not be extended to reward-model-based RLHF; and the work was carried out at one institution in Bengaluru, so benchmark choices and evaluation design reflect that context (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.2, §7.3).

The resource accounting is itself a threat. The recorded out-of-pocket spend was $130–140, with roughly 60 A100 GPU-hours of in-kind PES University compute valued at approximately $270 and projected carbon of about 776 kWh and about 296 kg CO₂-eq; the dominant uncertainty is the Tinker GPU-hour count, inferred because the platform exposes no hardware telemetry, giving a pessimistic Tinker contribution of about 540 kg against an optimistic one of about 130 kg. No offsets were purchased; the mitigation offered is reproducibility by artefact (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §3, §4).

## 9.3 Conclusions

This report set out to build a multi-framework training harness for GRPO-style post-training of large language models and to study that post-training under a single methodological rule: hold the stack fixed, change one factor, report what happens. The harness dispatches one configuration to several back-ends, but only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs, on different base checkpoints, so it is not a completed multi-framework benchmark. The four research questions of §1.4 have the following answers.

- **RQ1 (ZVF).** Yes, it is recomputable: the three stored Qwen3-8B tensors reproduce 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155 exactly. Its information about outcome is limited: its reported association with catastrophic collapse is stronger than with final held-out accuracy, and the latter is not distinguishable from zero (§6.3; Table 1.1). Its magnitude is regime-dependent, from 0.16 to 0.84 across the measured runs.
- **RQ2 (algorithm labels on a fixed stack).** Unanswered at this budget. PPO versus GRPO, GRPO versus Dr. GRPO and G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} all sit at evaluation accuracy 0.98–0.99 on 0.5B two-digit arithmetic, and nulls at ceiling are not evidence of equivalence: they cannot separate no effect from no headroom. The unsaturated GRPO/Dr. GRPO comparisons (Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K-CoT; the eight-seed variant audit of §1.2) are inconclusive and establish no equivalence either (§6.2.3, §6.4, §6.5; Table 1.1). The same-stack rerun on the Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K-CoT configuration set out in §9.4 is what would answer it.
- **RQ3 (manifest-level flip risk).** Yes, for the one pair tested: `stackdiff` flags the open-versus-closed "DAPO" pair as R5 from manifests alone (§7.2). Whether it flags risk that matters on outcome was not tested beyond that pair.
- **RQ4 (governance-first evaluation).** It yields receipt-bound per-suite scores and an explicit terminal state for every lane (Table C.3). It does not yield a post-training delta: the actor had 40 training steps, and the paired Table 8.B comparison is inconclusive in every lane.

On the analytical side the contribution is a measurement layer and a set of bounded negatives. The Zero-Variance Fraction is a reproducible diagnostic of advantage-signal starvation, recomputable from stored tensors, regime-dependent in magnitude (0.72–0.77 on the Phase-1 main configuration, 0.16 to 0.84 across the measured runs; Table 1.1), with an association with final held-out outcome that is not distinguishable from zero (ρ = 0.27, 95% CI [−0.37, 0.88], n = 23). It is descriptive, not predictive. Its theory has been made falsifiable, and two of its three consequences reproduced: the U-shape in accuracy across reward levels and the monotone group-size effect; the gradient link remains toy-scale and directional (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Group size therefore has a candidate mechanism, but the measured three-seed sweep found its arms statistically indistinguishable and establishes no optimum (Chapter 6 §6.4). Length bias is not established: the roster-level flag that marks four of eleven GRPO runs — peak before 65% of training, terminal reward below 90% of peak (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §2.2) — has post hoc thresholds, is not specific to length, and fires on most runs in the wider trace pool, so it is not evidence of length bias; in the controlled comparison the same rule fires on both GRPO and Dr. GRPO (five of six GSM8K-CoT runs) while no run shows length growing, completion length drifts shorter, and the two are indistinguishable on held-out gain (Chapter 6 §6.5). No claim is made that Dr. GRPO is uniquely free of the pattern. The levers we could test — a naive curriculum, a matched-token-budget intervention, an integer group-size sweep — did not produce reliable held-out gains. The same-stack de-confounds (PPO versus GRPO, GRPO versus Dr. GRPO, and group size) were all measured on a saturated 0.5B arithmetic task with evaluation accuracy near 0.99, so their nulls say that no difference was detectable there, not that the methods are equivalent; the one unsaturated configuration (Qwen2.5-1.5B on GSM8K-CoT, +5 to +6 points pre-to-post) was not used for a PPO/GRPO or group-size contrast. The strongest original contribution remains P5, the reporting standard, whose status the record states precisely: a targeted search of the GRPO, model-reporting and provenance literature through July 2026 did not identify an equivalent GRPO-specific machine-readable run datasheet with rollout provenance, and this is "an evidence-bounded novelty claim, not proof of non-existence" (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The earlier second bet — predictive layer freezing from step-1 behaviour — is weakened rather than withdrawn: it held at 1.5B and collapsed to chance at 3B across four seeds, at a mean of 0.083 ± 0.048 (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p1_layerfreeze/scaled_4seed_result.json).

The E1–E14 campaign is an evaluation-governance contribution, not evidence about GRPO: the actor received 40 steps of critic-free policy-gradient training (§8.1.1), so its scores describe a checkpoint rather than a training method. It delivered what a governance-first campaign can deliver: complete, receipt-bound results on three suites (two replacement scopes and one original contract), with Omni-MATH terminal-complete; full-coverage original-contract scores on two (E1 and E11); and an explicit, auditable terminal state for every remaining lane, with a named gate and a named reopen condition instead of an indefinite pending. Its discipline is negative, which is why it is credible: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled, no cross-suite aggregate is computed, and no claim of improvement over a baseline or over the base model is made anywhere in this work (the one paired trained-versus-base comparison, Table 8.B, is inconclusive in every lane). The strongest complete positive result, 129/312 = 41.35% pass@1 on VerilogEval at full 312/312 coverage, is an end-to-end harness score (150 of its 183 failures are extraction failures) and is reported as a measurement of one actor on one suite, with its provenance recorded down to the sampler path.

The report's overall conclusion is about method rather than algorithms. On this compute scale, and with these instruments, the measurements are informative while the algorithmic levers are noise-limited; the stack is a material part of the result, so a label without its stack is not a reproducible experimental specification; and the most durable artefacts produced here are the ones that make other people's results checkable — the ZVF/PCD diagnostic layer, the eight-item minimum reportable stack (seven manifest fields and one evaluation item), the registry and its CLI, and a campaign ledger in which every number is bound to a receipt and every gap has a gate.

## 9.4 Future Work

The work leaves three kinds of open question: evaluation lanes whose measurement is incomplete, analytical claims that the present evidence could not settle, and methodological tools that have been built but not yet tested at scale.

**Closing the remaining evaluation gaps.** The six replacement lanes that were launch-pending or quota-blocked on 21 September were run to completion on 27 September 2026 for \$44.25 in lane spend (Chapter 8 §8.4). Two measurement gaps remain in them. The 108 WebArena tasks that need an LLM judge are ungraded because the judge account had no credit, and the native judge model has since been retired, so a regrade would record a judge substitution (source: `outputs/finish_pending_2026-09-27/E6/result.json`). SWE-bench Multilingual has been attempted on all 300 tasks, but under two serving contracts, so a single 300-task figure would need the first 110 tasks rerun under the 27 September contract (32,768-token context and the re-implemented source collector). Six original-contract scopes are recorded `CLOSED_EXTERNAL` with written closure records: E3 (SDAB private bundle), E7 (BinaryAudit private payload), E8-original (LifeSciBench package), E10-original (AgentHarm private tasks), E12 (AppBench deployment) and E14-original (FrontierMath hosted evaluation). A seventh original contract, E13's OpenReward held-out games, is also blocked on external access but has no closure record, because the lane continues through its BALROG replacement scope (Table C.3). Each reopens only on a provider response, not on an internal milestone (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §5; outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md). A full BankerToolBench score for E4 would be a new-runtime experiment with a grader-only lower bound of approximately $59.45, and the 100/100-trial base-model rerun at mean reward 0.0 provides its calibration point (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/Pending_Experiments.md; outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §9.7).

**Turning the campaign into a post-training measurement.** The campaign reports scores for one actor, not the contribution of the training step. The paired same-engine comparison of §8.7 (Table 8.B) is the first base-model arm; scaling it from 5–151 items per lane to the full native scopes, with more than one training seed and a longer training run than the actor's 40 steps, would turn each lane from a descriptive score into a post-training delta. Separately, the retained E11 result was sampled through a Tinker sampler route that no longer exists. Re-serving the published adapter mirror and re-running E11 would measure the same-weights, different-sampler divergence that is currently unmeasured (source: outputs/TINKER_SAMPLER_STATUS_2026-09-20.json).

**Settling the analytical questions.** Five Phase-1 directions remain open, each answering a threat identified above (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex):

- adequately powered evaluation, moving from the Phase-1 probes' held-out *n* of 8–20 (the multi-seed controlled comparisons already use 200–500 items, Chapter 1 §1.5) to the full GSM8K test split or a large stratified subset at five or more seeds;
- a token-budget-optimal curriculum with staleness bounds, tested multi-seed;
- testing whether the mid-to-late band in the per-layer gradient profile supports a FLOP-saving freeze schedule, even though step-1 predictability did not survive scaling;
- hardening the Phase-1 method-trace classifier on grouped run-level and temporal splits rather than row-wise cross-validation;
- consolidating the diagnostic findings and the reporting standard for publication.

Three more follow from the Chapter 6 results. The first is a length-confounded regime, with the completion cap lifted and longer horizons, in which a Dr. GRPO length advantage would be expected to appear if it exists. The second is the head-to-head protocol between ZVF and the pairwise-contrast density, to test whether PCD meets its target association. The third is a harder, unsaturated task on which algorithm and group-size effects could be detected at all: rerunning the same-stack PPO/GRPO contrast and G ∈ {2, 8} on the Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K-CoT configuration, with five paired seeds and a disjoint evaluation split, and reporting the difference with its interval against a stated equivalence margin in accuracy points.

**Testing the methodological tools.** Three extensions come from the Semester-4 work:

- a pre-registered controller bakeoff against static G = 16 and naive boundary heuristics, the test P7 declines to claim in advance of;
- a cross-framework divergence study that runs an identical task, reward, decoding and seed configuration across TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF and Tinker, turning P5's attribution argument from an audit of existing runs into a designed experiment;
- dogfooding the registry schema on the campaign's own runs, so that every future benchmark result ships with a verifiable provenance record.

Two commitments carry forward, because the threats above are structural rather than incidental. The first is the never-pool rule: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers stay separate, and per-suite native denominators are never averaged. The second is that failures are reported with their mechanism, as the E11 sampler path, the E4 zero, the lost execution sources, the seed-count discrepancy and the failed-run spend are here.
