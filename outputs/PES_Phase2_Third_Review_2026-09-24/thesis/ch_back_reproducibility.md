# Appendix B. Reproducibility and Provenance

## B.1 What this appendix establishes

This appendix records three things that should not be confused with one another. The first is what a reviewer can independently re-run: a pinned container, a pinned dependency set, a headline training command, and the tolerance within which a rerun is expected to land. The second is what the repository verifies about itself without re-running any model: an audit suite driven by a single entry point, and a deterministic ledger check that recomputes the reported arithmetic directly from surviving evidence files. The third is provenance: which parts of this work were produced by the Semester 3 group, which by the individual contribution reported here, and what was lost along the way.

## B.2 The pinned runtime

The container definition is `Dockerfile`, built from `nvidia/cuda:12.4.1-cudnn-devel-ubuntu22.04`. It installs Python 3.12 through the deadsnakes archive rather than the distribution default, with the in-file comment that Python 3.12 "matches the frozen runtime validated 106/106". It sets `PYTHONHASHSEED=42` and a fixed `LANG`/`LC_ALL` of `C.UTF-8`; stamps the build commit into an OCI `org.opencontainers.image.revision` label and a `.build_info` file; fixes `SEED=42`, `WANDB_MODE=offline`, `HF_HUB_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` and `OMP_NUM_THREADS=8`; self-checks by importing the core stack at build time; and runs under `tini`.

The authoritative pin set is `requirements.txt`, headed as "pinned to the frozen E1 runtime lock … validated 106/106 on this stack". Its exact pins include `torch==2.7.1`, `numpy==2.2.6`, `transformers==5.5.4`, `trl==1.2.0`, `datasets==4.8.4`, `accelerate==1.13.0`, `peft==0.19.1`, `huggingface-hub==1.11.0` and `wandb==0.21.0`, with bounded ranges for scipy, safetensors, tokenizers, rliable, math-verify, latex2sympy2-extended and pytest, alongside the framework clients `tinker`, `tinker-cookbook` and `atroposlib`.

The 106/106 figure is the recorded outcome of the frozen-runtime validation, which `execution-notes.md` states as passing the complete pinned pilot/preregistration suite on Python 3.12.13 with the pinned versions listed above, and the focused amendment, protocol, launcher and verifier suite passing 55/55. The same record binds five artefacts by SHA-256 — protocol document, screening manifest, launch manifest, launch fingerprint and source binding — and states that a mismatch in package, Python, A100, FLOP, token or gradient conditions fails closed rather than proceeding.

## B.3 The reviewer's entry point

`REPRODUCE.md` is the reviewer-facing document. Its fastest path is the structural smoke test at `platform_modal/scripts/smoke_test.sh`, which runs in about two minutes offline and about eight when a live Tinker key is supplied so that the wire protocol is exercised; the offline path needs no GPU and no account.

The headline result is GRPO on Qwen3-8B over GSM8K for 30 steps at LoRA rank 32, group size 8 and seed 42, recorded as a peak accuracy of 62.5 % with a last-10 average of 34.4 %. Reviewer-side reruns are accepted within ±5 percentage points on the last-10 figure and ±10 points on peak, justified in the same document by the residual non-determinism discussed in §B.10. Per-seed reference values and W&B run identifiers are tabulated for seeds 42, 123, 456 and 789, with seed 1024 marked as a reviewer-only rerun; the canonical multi-seed set is `{42, 123, 456, 789, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384, 32768}`. The group-size ablation is expected to reproduce a peak of 50.0 % at G=2, 75.0 % at G=4, 100 % at G=8 and 71.9 % at G=16, with last-10 values of 37.5 %, 52.1 %, 84.4 % and 38.0 %. Five-seed aggregation runs through `utils/stats.py` with rliable intervals and 10,000 bootstrap samples; `utils/verify_results.py` checks a rerun against the recorded expectations at those tolerances.

The cost is stated plainly: about five GPU-hours on a single A100-80GB for the headline run, against roughly 446 GPU-hours for a full paper reproduction. Dataset provenance is tabulated with pinned revisions — GSM8K at `740312a` with 7,473 training and 1,319 test rows, plus MATH-500, HumanEval, ArenaHard Auto, NoRobots and OpenThoughts3 each at a named snapshot — with exact shas in `ARTIFACT.md` §3.4.

## B.4 Where the documentation has drifted from the code

Four discrepancies exist between the repository's prose and its live tree, and a reviewer following the prose literally would hit them. The training driver has moved: `REPRODUCE.md` and `ARTIFACT.md` both invoke `grpo_gsm8k_base.py` at the repository root, and the file now lives at `platform_tinker/grpo_gsm8k_base.py`, though the documented command still works because the CLI in `platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo_cli.py` carries a compatibility entry point, `legacy_main`, that translates the documented flag spellings onto the current ones — what changed is the path a reviewer must type, not the flags the documents specify. `ARTIFACT.md` names the smoke test at `scripts/smoke_test.sh` rather than at `platform_modal/scripts/smoke_test.sh`, though `REPRODUCE.md` gives the correct path. The pins quoted in prose are stale: `REPRODUCE.md` §9 states that `requirements.txt` pins `transformers>=4.56.2,<4.60.0` and a `datasets<3.2` bound, whereas the live file pins `transformers==5.5.4` and `datasets==4.8.4`, and `ARTIFACT.md` §3.3 quotes a third, older set; similarly, `ARTIFACT.md` describes the environment as Python 3.10 from the Ubuntu 22.04 archive, whereas the `Dockerfile` installs Python 3.12 via deadsnakes and the frozen-runtime record validates on Python 3.12.13. The `ARTIFACT.md` items are explained by that document's frozen status — it records a Semester 3 artefact at commit `9019b5c946f17ffe4761b5f4eac13b86d1e51cc0`, dated 19 April 2026 — but `REPRODUCE.md` §9 is live guidance, not a frozen record, and carries the same defect.

## B.5 The audit apparatus

The integrity suite is driven by a single entry point, `platform_local/run_all_audits.py`, which imports a shared result interface from `utils/audit_utils.py` and registers its audits as a tuple of name-and-function pairs. As it stands in this tree, that tuple contains **nine** audits: `claim_issues`, `sync_issues`, `anon_issues`, `strength_issues`, `package_issues`, `workflow_issues`, `export_guard_issues`, `caveat_issues` and `scientific_issues`. Each is implemented by a corresponding `platform_local/` module carrying a `__main__` block, so each can also run on its own; `tests/test_audit_runner.py` asserts that the registered modules and the `platform_local/*_audit.py` files are the same set.

Some repository documents give different counts, and the difference is historical. At the blind-review sweep the suite was thirteen audits, registered as a list of script filenames at the repository root and executed as subprocesses; the committed log of that run records `METRIC audits_total=13` with ten passing and three failing, and the same sweep states that all seventeen `*_audit.py` scripts then at the root were executed, with the suite covering thirteen of them. The registry now holds nine audits, all passing: a pruning commit removed obsolete audits, and a later restructuring replaced the subprocess runner with the in-process interface, so the seventeen-script description has narrowed by the same route. No script now prints a metric line directly; the line is constructed in one place, `AuditResult.metric_line()` in `utils/audit_utils.py`, and emitted by the nine audit modules plus the runner. A repository-wide scan finds several dozen files matching `*_audit.py`, but that population is not a suite: it includes unrelated analyses, test modules and a third-party file in the virtual environment.

The output contract is uniform. `AuditResult.metric_line()` emits `METRIC <name>=<issue_count>`, so every audit reports one machine-readable line whose right-hand side is the number of issues it found, and an audit passes exactly when that count is zero. `render_suite` prints each audit's metric line and issue codes, then three suite-level lines — `METRIC suite_issues`, `METRIC audits_total` and `METRIC audits_passing` — before either listing the failing audits by name or stating that all audits are passing. The entry point exits `0` when the suite passes and `1` otherwise, which makes it usable as a gate; the packaging step runs the suite before exporting and refuses to package if it fails. The runner's own test asserts this rendering against a synthetic two-audit suite, checking for `METRIC audits_total=2` and `METRIC suite_issues=1`.

## B.6 The deterministic ledger check

Where the suite above audits the submission package's internal consistency, a second instrument audits the campaign's reported figures. `outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json`, under schema `ledger-code-check-v1`, records eleven checks of which eleven passed and none failed. Each recomputes a claim from the underlying evidence rather than trusting a summary of it, and the four classes of check prove four different things.

| Class of check | Specific checks | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Exact divisions | E1 `2/731`; E11 `129/312`; E11 `67+62=129`; E14 `4426/4428` and accuracy `0.5131043831902395`; E9 `40 of 75` | That each reported ratio is the quotient of the two integers the report claims, and that a partition sums to its whole |
| Receipt presence | E8 eight categories summing to 1967 with every `evaluated == expected_total`; E9 graded-row counts; the strict-completion set | That every claimed unit of coverage has a surviving graded row behind it, and that the set of strictly complete suites is exactly `[E8, E10, E11]` |
| Sealed-artefact hash equality | E1 v6 sealed request and fixture receipt | That the sealed request and the validation receipt are byte-identical to the artefacts recorded at sealing time |
| Lost-source absence probes | `codex_run_execution_sources_absent` | That four `.codex-run` paths are genuinely absent, so a later re-creation cannot be mistaken for the lost original |

: The deterministic ledger check by class, listing each specific check and what it establishes.

The distinction matters under questioning. An exact division proves the arithmetic is not a rounding convenience or a transcription error; it cannot prove the underlying evidence is sound. A receipt-presence check proves coverage claims are backed by artefacts; it cannot prove those artefacts were produced correctly. A hash-equality check proves identity between two artefacts — here, between what was sealed and what was later validated — and is the strongest of the four because it does not depend on re-running anything. An absence probe proves a negative: it prevents a later reconstruction from being passed off as the original. Two instances show the classes in use. The E1 records show the sealed request's SHA-256 beginning `7adc6e2616f61bc7` matching `validation_v6.json`, with the fixture receipt matching on the same basis. The E9 records show 41 graded rows resolving to 40 unique competitions against a denominator of 75 in the streaming arm and exactly one valid grade in the merged-VLLM arm, which is the check that confirms the two serving arms were never pooled.

## B.7 The receipt model

A receipt in this campaign is a durable JSON artefact binding an outcome to the inputs that produced it, and the binding carries the evidential weight rather than the file's existence. The clearest statement of the principle is the receipt-bundle auditor's docstring, which records that the campaign and the receipt builders are kept deliberately separate from the auditor, and that the auditor "never imports a provider SDK, contacts a service, launches a job, or treats `status`/`online`/a URL/ID alone as provenance". The auditor reconstructs contract coverage, receipt predicates, canonical hash rules, budget caps and cross-artefact bindings, carrying its expectations as constants rather than reading them from what it checks — suite counts of 12 training, 14 primary-evaluation and 26 total, and budget constants of 18.00 USD maximum, 16.50 USD operational cap and a 1.50 USD safety reserve — so a receipt set that quietly loses a suite fails the audit rather than redefining it.

Receipts bind four dimensions of a run: protocol, runtime, source and event order. The frozen-runtime record applies that discipline to the runtime dimension alone, binding five artefacts — protocol document, screening manifest, launch manifest, launch fingerprint and source binding — by SHA-256 and failing closed on any mismatch. The judgment layer for subjectively scored questions follows the same pattern: one durable receipt per judgment binds the state bytes, the question text and the raw answer, and battery execution is gated on a health check rather than the service's availability, so a degraded answer cannot be laundered into a valid one. Where a lane could not be scored, its terminal state is recorded in the ledger of record with a named reopen condition, so the absence of a result is itself receipted.

## B.8 Evidence loss in the finish era

One loss of evidence occurred and is recorded rather than concealed. During post-review disk cleanup the untracked `.codex-run/` working tree was deleted. Four paths were confirmed absent by the absence probes described above: `finish_20260912/e1_recovery_v6/bounded_sdk.py`, holding the E1 wave10 repair code; `finish_20260912/e9_completion/restored/`, holding 1,613 hash-verified restored dependency sources from the OpenHands and Composio forks used to build the MLDevBench runtime; `finish_20260912/e6_continuation/checks_v1.py`, an offline receipt validator the reconciliation record describes as structure-only and of low impact; and `public_native_improve_v2/e1/metadata_wave08.py`, the driver for the E1 multilingual run that graded 35 of 300 tasks.

The loss does not invalidate any figure reported in this thesis, for three reasons that hold independently. First, none of the four paths is the source of any reported score: the E1 wave10 repair and the E9 runtime build are execution sources for lanes that remain unlaunched, whose status is `REBUILD_READY_LAUNCH_PENDING` and whose values appear in no results table, and the E1 multilingual figure of 35 of 300 graded is reported as an incomplete replacement-scope state rather than as a suite score. Second, the sealed requests, validation receipts, regression fixtures and the 10-of-10 targeted-test log from the lost E1 code all survive, so a faithful re-implementation is constrained by surviving fixtures rather than free to invent behaviour; that re-implementation has since landed under a tracked path and reproduces the lost suite's record exactly, four process-group regressions and six cleanup regressions. Third, the loss is confined to finish-era iteration code: the replacement-lane native runners survive in tracked locations, including a 515-line multilingual runner carrying no reference to the deleted tree. The policy adopted in response — that execution sources live in tracked repository paths and never in untracked scratch trees — is the durable outcome of the incident.

## B.9 The Semester 3 and Semester 4 provenance boundary

The boundary between inherited and individual work is a git tag. `capstone-final-2026-04-25` resolves to commit `21a99ef766543a1ca86bfbad188445d3596ea73c`, dated 23 April 2026, whose subject line records the final metric-separation rewrite that segregated training rewards, held-out capability and proxy diagnostics. Two hundred and fifty commits precede that tag. A reviewer can reproduce the state at the boundary by adding it as a worktree, `git worktree add ../tinker-rl-lab-sem3 capstone-final-2026-04-25`, which materialises the Semester 3 tree without disturbing the current checkout.

The Semester 3 group comprised six students — Arvind C R, Sandhya Jeyaraj, Madhu Kumara L, Mohammad Rafi, Dhruva N Murthy and Arumugam Chetty K — under guides Anwesh Reddy Paduri and Narayana Darapaneni. Their work is the multi-framework benchmark infrastructure and the initial GRPO baseline experiments. Semester 4 was carried out individually under Ramesh Prakash Guledgudd, and the declaration facing this report assigns to that contribution the research direction, the experimental expansion, the statistical analyses, the P1–P8 paper series and the E1–E14 campaign. The solo-authorship commit `282e6b4f0f158de11b3f5bb4656e2d871f68f5c0`, dated 7 July 2026, records that division at paper level. The provenance record states that 584 commits and 5,050 changed files separated the boundary from the tree as it then stood, and that the eight paper PDFs were compiled at commit `006e90a38e4dabe2d0b4778be01dc7b27f2d3462` on 9 July 2026, one recompiled the following day after a findings-ledger rename. Those counts carry an as-of date rather than describing the current tree, so a reviewer recomputing them will obtain a larger number.

One consequence of this history shapes the repository's structure: the implementation remains shared, with experiment drivers, result tables, figures, LaTeX sources and audit scripts referencing one another across the boundary, and the provenance record is explicit that this is why the code is not split into separate trees. What the boundary separates is authorship of the research contribution, not access to the machinery. Two reporting rules follow and are enforced throughout this report: original-contract and replacement-scope results are never pooled, with no cross-suite aggregate, which the ledger check independently verifies through the arm-separation and component-split checks; and no replacement benchmark's score is presented as the score of the benchmark it replaces, with every substitution named as such at its point of use.

## B.10 Residual non-determinism and the limits of reproduction

Not everything here is reproducible in the strict sense, and the tolerances exist because of it. Three sources of residual non-determinism survive even with fixed seeds and deterministic cuDNN settings: remote LoRA training is not bit-deterministic across scheduler decisions, with runs replayed months apart drifting by two to four percentage points on GSM8K last-10, documented through three reruns of the same seed-123 configuration; some matmul operations on H100 and A100 hardware introduce non-determinism through CUDA and cuBLAS; and sampling at temperature 0.8 with top-p 0.95 executes in a remote virtual machine, so a fixed seed does not fix the sampled trajectory. The record states the consequence itself: run-to-run variance on this setup is of the same order as the effect reported in the paper's PPO-versus-GRPO comparison, which is why that comparison is reported as statistically indistinguishable rather than as a difference.

Three limits remain. Lanes blocked on external access, cloud quota or authorisation cannot be reproduced from this repository, because the blocking resource is not in it. The evidence-loss paths cannot be reproduced in their original form, only re-implemented against surviving fixtures, and that distinction should be preserved in any future citation. And figures originating at the Semester 3 boundary describe the tree at that tag, so reproducing them requires the worktree checkout rather than the current HEAD.
