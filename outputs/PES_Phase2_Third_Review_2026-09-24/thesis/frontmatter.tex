\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

This report presents a multi-framework benchmark for group-relative
reinforcement-learning post-training of large language models, a study of that
post-training's behaviour across libraries, scales, group sizes and datasets,
and a held-out evaluation of a single trained actor against fourteen agentic
and reasoning benchmark suites.

The benchmark is multi-framework by construction: a shared task, reward and
decoding configuration is dispatched through thin adapters onto each backend's
own interface, so the same experiment is runnable on any of them without
re-implementing the grader. That design property is distinct from completed-run
parity, and this report does not conflate the two. Of the cross-launcher roster,
only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs in this release; the veRL and
OpenRLHF entries in the comparison artefact are dry-run placeholders that
resolve a launch plan without executing it, and are never pooled with real
results or read as framework-comparison evidence (Chapter 3 \S3.8; Chapter 5
\S5.1). Comparative claims in this report are therefore constrained to the
runs actually recorded, and the diagnostic portability results are stated at
the strength their own source asserts.

The central methodological commitment is \emph{attribution}: every comparative
claim is made with the entire training stack held fixed except the one factor
under test. Four de-confound pillars --- a matched same-stack PPO/GRPO
contrast, the Zero-Variance Fraction as a signal-starvation diagnostic, a
group-size sweep, and a length-bias and held-out-generalisation study --- are
reported under that commitment, alongside a cross-library cross-scale scaling
study, a minimum reporting standard for stack-conditioned results, a
machine-readable stack registry, an adaptive group-size controller derived from
the ZVF diagnostic, and an applied credit-card fraud case study.

The principal results are deliberately conservative. Across more than seventy
runs spanning roughly 2.4 orders of magnitude in model scale, \textbf{no
reliable scaling law is recovered from reward curves}. A matched same-stack
comparison of PPO and GRPO finds the two estimators \textbf{statistically
indistinguishable} (Welch $p = 0.7605$). A single-seed group-size sweep does
\textbf{not} establish an optimum, and trainability is non-monotone in group
size. Four of eleven GRPO runs peak before 65\% of training and then decay,
consistent with length bias rather than capability gain, while Dr.\ GRPO does
not exhibit the pattern.

The E1--E14 campaign evaluates one Tinker-trained actor
(\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B} with the
\texttt{pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal} adapter) using each suite's
own native evaluator at a pinned revision, with no per-lane tuning. Four suites
carry strictly complete graded scopes: LAB-Bench public split (1{,}967/1{,}967
questions, micro accuracy 0.2288), AgentDojo benign utility (97/97, utility
0.9072), VerilogEval (312/312, $129/312 = 41.35\%$ pass@1), and Omni-MATH (all
4{,}428 dispositions recorded, official accuracy $2{,}271/4{,}428 = 51.31\%$
reproduced). Two carry full verified scores under their original contracts:
SWE-bench Pro ($2/731 = 0.274\%$ pass@1, with all eighteen non-native outcomes
retained in the denominator) and VerilogEval. The remaining lanes are reported
as partial, quota-blocked, or externally blocked, each with an explicit
terminal state and a named reopen condition.

Original-contract and replacement-scope benchmark results are never pooled, no
cross-suite aggregate is computed, and no claim of improvement over a baseline
is made anywhere in this work. All structural claims were re-verified by
deterministic code checks on 19 September 2026 (11/11 PASS).
