\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

This report presents a multi-framework training harness for group-relative
reinforcement-learning post-training of large language models, studies of GRPO
signal starvation and of that post-training's behaviour across libraries,
scales, group sizes and datasets, and an evaluation of a single trained actor
against fourteen agentic and reasoning benchmark suites that were not part of
its training data.

The harness is multi-framework by construction: a shared task, reward and
decoding configuration is dispatched through thin adapters onto each backend's
own interface, so the same experiment is runnable on any of them without
re-implementing the grader. That design property is distinct from a completed
multi-framework benchmark, which this report does not claim. Of the
cross-launcher roster, only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs in this
release, and those two used different base checkpoints (Qwen3-8B-Base on
Tinker, the instruction-tuned Qwen3-8B on TRL), so no framework comparison is
drawn from them; the veRL and
OpenRLHF entries in the comparison artefact are dry-run placeholders that
resolve a launch plan without executing it, and are never pooled with real
results or read as framework-comparison evidence (Chapter 3 \S3.8; Chapter 5
\S5.1). Comparative claims in this report are therefore constrained to the
runs actually recorded, and the diagnostic portability results are stated at
the strength their own source asserts.

The central methodological commitment is \emph{attribution}: every comparative
claim is made with the entire training stack held fixed except the one factor
under test. Four de-confound pillars --- a matched same-stack PPO/GRPO
contrast, the Zero-Variance Fraction as a signal-starvation diagnostic, a
group-size sweep, and a length-bias and held-out-generalisation study --- are
reported under that commitment, alongside a cross-library cross-scale scaling
study, a minimum reporting standard for stack-conditioned results, a
machine-readable stack registry, an adaptive group-size controller derived from
the ZVF diagnostic, and an applied credit-card fraud case study.

The principal results are deliberately conservative. Across the twelve fitted anchors of a
70+-run roster, spanning roughly 2.4 orders of magnitude in model scale,
\textbf{no reliable scaling law is recovered from reward curves}. On a saturated
Qwen2.5-0.5B two-digit addition task, a same-stack comparison of PPO and GRPO
over five paired seeds finds \textbf{no detectable difference} (evaluation
accuracy 0.992 against 0.990, paired $\Delta = -0.002$, $p = 0.374$, on fresh
draws from the training generator rather than a disjoint split). At that
ceiling the null cannot separate no effect from no headroom, and it does not
establish equivalence (TOST at $\pm 0.005$, $p = 0.10$). The
Zero-Variance Fraction's association with final held-out accuracy is not
distinguishable from zero ($\rho = 0.27$, 95\% CI $[-0.37, 0.88]$, $n = 23$),
so it is reported as a descriptive diagnostic, not a predictor. A three-seed group-size sweep
($G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$) on the same saturated task (evaluation accuracy
0.978--0.990) finds its arms statistically indistinguishable and does
\textbf{not} establish an optimum. A roster-level flag marks four of
eleven GRPO runs as peaking before 65\% of training and then decaying, but its
thresholds are post hoc and it is not specific to length, so it is
\textbf{not} evidence of length bias; in the controlled sixteen-run comparison
the same rule fires on both GRPO and Dr.\ GRPO (five of six GSM8K-CoT runs),
no run shows length growing, and completion length drifts shorter rather than
longer.

The E1--E14 campaign evaluates one Tinker-trained actor
(\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B} with the
\texttt{pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal} adapter) using each suite's
own native evaluator at a pinned revision, with no per-lane tuning. The actor
received 40 steps of critic-free policy-gradient training (group-normalised
REINFORCE, no KL, no clipping) on API-Bank and SWE-Gym, so the campaign is
reported as an evaluation-governance exercise, not as evidence about GRPO.
Three
scopes are strictly complete: the replacement scopes LAB-Bench public split
(1{,}967/1{,}967 questions, micro accuracy 0.2288), AgentDojo benign utility
(97/97 episodes, utility $88/97 = 0.9072$), and the original-contract suite
VerilogEval (312/312, $129/312 = 41.35\%$ pass@1, Wilson 95\% CI
$[36.0, 46.9]\%$). Omni-MATH is terminal-complete under its native protocol (all
4{,}428 dispositions recorded, 4{,}426 accepted; accuracy
$2{,}271/4{,}428 = 51.29\%$ with the two unjudged rows counted as failures, against the official
scorer's $51.31\%$ over the 4{,}426 judged rows). Original-contract full-coverage scores exist for SWE-bench Pro
($2/731 = 0.274\%$ pass@1, with all eighteen non-native outcomes retained in the
denominator) and, as above, VerilogEval. The LAB-Bench and VerilogEval figures
are end-to-end harness scores: 1{,}259 of 1{,}967 and 150 of 312 responses
yielded no gradable answer. The remaining lanes are reported
as partial, quota-blocked, or externally blocked, each with an explicit
terminal state and a named reopen condition.

Original-contract and replacement-scope benchmark results are never pooled, no
cross-suite aggregate is computed, and no claim of improvement over a baseline
is made anywhere in this work: the one paired trained-versus-base comparison,
a small-scale rerun of all fourteen lanes on 5--151 identical items each, finds
no significant difference in any lane, which at those sizes is inconclusive. Eleven named deterministic ledger checks
(exact divisions, receipt presence, hash equality and lost-source probes)
passed on 19 September 2026; they cover the arithmetic of the headline lanes,
not every figure in the report.
