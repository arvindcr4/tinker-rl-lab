\subsection{Frontier-Model Cross-Examination: The ZVF Diagnostic}
\label{sec:frontier-zvf}

To stress-test the ZVF diagnostic beyond our own measurements, we
submitted the Pillar~2 construction to an external cross-examination of
two frontier reasoning systems (ChatGPT Pro Extended and Gemini Deep
Think). The exchange is a source of \emph{arguments and proposed
diagnostics}, not additional experiments; we report the sharpest
contributions and, where they bear on our reported results---the weak
outcome correlation ($\rho = 0.27$, Section~\ref{sec:zvf-cross-experiment})
and the temporal stickiness / upward drift
(Section~\ref{sec:zvf-dynamics})---we say so explicitly.

\paragraph{Why $\rho = 0.27$ is structural: mastery--incapacity aliasing.}
In an external cross-examination, both models argued that ZVF is a weak
\emph{outcome} predictor by construction because it is an \emph{unsigned}
saturation statistic. Writing $K_x=\sum_{i=1}^{G} R_i$ for the within-group
success count, $\mathrm{ZVF}=\Pr(K_x=0)+\Pr(K_x=G)$ assigns the same value
to an all-wrong group ($K_x=0$, incapacity) and an all-correct group
($K_x=G$, mastery)---aliasing the two ends of the difficulty axis that
have opposite outcome implications. A modest $\rho\approx0.27$ over $80$
runs is thus the \emph{expected} behavior of a sign-blind scalar, not a
defect of the underlying quantity (frontier synthesis). This rationalizes
our own reading: ZVF is a mechanistic \emph{signal-availability}
diagnostic, not a standalone performance predictor.

\paragraph{A proposed ZVF Inelasticity Theorem (frontier synthesis).}
Gemini Deep Think proposed formalizing the aliasing as a
\emph{Combinatorial Inelasticity Theorem}. Under binary rewards, latent
per-prompt success probability $p_x\in(0,1)$, and conditionally i.i.d.\
rollouts (so $K_x\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(G,p_x)$), the expected
zero-variance fraction is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ZVF}(p_x,G) \;=\; p_x^{\,G} + (1-p_x)^{\,G},
  \label{eq:zvf-inelasticity}
\end{equation}
which is symmetric, $\mathrm{ZVF}(p_x,G)=\mathrm{ZVF}(1-p_x,G)$: a
U-shaped ``topological trap'' that cannot separate zero-shot failure
($p_x\to0$) from reward saturation ($p_x\to1$). Its payoff is the
\emph{elasticity of gradient yield to compute} $G$: at the max-entropy
point $p_x=\tfrac12$, $\mathrm{ZVF}=2^{1-G}$ and
$\partial_G\ln\mathrm{ZVF}=-\ln 2$, so extra rollouts kill dead groups
exponentially fast, whereas in the tails ($\epsilon\ll 1/G$) a Taylor
expansion gives $\mathrm{ZVF}\approx 1-G\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)$ and
$\partial_G\mathrm{ZVF}\approx-\epsilon\to 0$---group-size scaling is
crushed into a linear crawl. This is the frontier-side explanation for
our temporal-stickiness result: as training drives $p_x\to1$ on mastered
prompts, Eq.~\eqref{eq:zvf-inelasticity} pushes ZVF toward $1$
\emph{and} makes it inelastic to $G$, so ZVF drifts up and does not
return---matching the monotonic phase-1-to-phase-3 drift and high lag-1
autocorrelation ($\rho_1\approx0.94$) we measure.

\paragraph{Replacement diagnostics: PCD and a sign-resolving quality score.}
Because ZVF measures only the \emph{existence} of contrast, the frontier
models proposed replacements that measure its \emph{magnitude} and
\emph{sign}. Gemini's Pairwise Contrast Density (PCD) is the expected
within-group reward variance, equivalently the mean squared pairwise
contrast that GRPO's baseline projection actually consumes:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PCD}
  \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x}\!\big[\mathrm{Var}(r\mid x)\big]
  \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x}\!\Big[\tfrac{1}{G^2}\!\sum_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2\Big]
  \;=\; \tfrac{G-1}{G}\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\big[p_x(1-p_x)\big],
  \label{eq:pcd}
\end{equation}
a sharp parabola peaking at $p_x=\tfrac12$ rather than the flat plateau
that $1-\mathrm{ZVF}$ forms over intermediate $p_x$. The models offered a
concrete \emph{micro-jitter falsification}: injecting noise
$\epsilon\sim U(0,10^{-4})$ into rewards (e.g.\ from a length penalty)
makes ZVF flatline to $0$ globally---falsely reporting a fully healthy
batch---whereas PCD is essentially invariant. ChatGPT proposed instead a
sign-resolving scalar, Learning-Adjusted Rollout Quality,
$\mathrm{LARQ}_\beta=\mathbb{E}_x[\hat p_x+\beta\cdot4\hat p_x(1-\hat p_x)]$
with $\hat p_x=K_x/G$, whose first term distinguishes all-correct from
all-wrong (curing the aliasing) while the second rewards frontier mass at
$\hat p_x\approx\tfrac12$. Both were offered with a head-to-head protocol
against ZVF on our $80$ anchors, with a stated target of
$\rho\gtrsim0.45$ (frontier synthesis)---a falsifiable bar, not a claimed
result.

\paragraph{Active gradient bandwidth and group size.}
The frontier synthesis recasts the group-size effect as a law about
gradient utilization $\mathrm{GU}_G=1-\mathrm{ZVF}_G$. From
Eq.~\eqref{eq:zvf-inelasticity}, the marginal group nonzero-yield gained
per extra rollout is
\begin{equation}
  h_G(p)-h_{G+1}(p) \;=\; p^{\,G}(1-p) + (1-p)^{\,G}\,p,
  \qquad h_G(p)=p^{\,G}+(1-p)^{\,G},
  \label{eq:gu-marginal}
\end{equation}
which is maximized near $p_x\approx 1/G$ or $1-1/G$, \emph{not} at
$p_x\approx\tfrac12$ (where ZVF is already tiny) nor at $p_x\approx 0,1$
(where even large $G$ fails). This motivated the models' ``Iso-Yield''
dynamic-grouping idea---route mastered/frontier prompts to small $G$ and
spend the freed budget on stubborn tails---and a matching
\emph{contrastive-yield effective compute} axis
$C_{\mathrm{eff}} = T\cdot G\cdot \mathbb{E}_x[\,1-p_x^{\,G}-(1-p_x)^{\,G}\,]\cdot\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert_{\mathrm{KL}}$,
in which extra samples count only insofar as they create mixed-reward
groups. Equation~\eqref{eq:gu-marginal} is consistent with the
$0.845\to0.631$ ZVF drop we observe as $G:2\to16$: the reduction is
concentrated in the interior-$p_x$ prompts and saturates in the tails.

\paragraph{Two caveats that sharpen our own claims.}
First, on our anti-herding falsification: the models cautioned that the
measured residual
$\Delta_G=\mathrm{ZVF}^{\mathrm{obs}}_G-\mathbb{E}_x[p_x^{\,G}+(1-p_x)^{\,G}]\in[-0.23,-0.13]$
should be read as \emph{edge-thinning / excess contrastive yield}
($13$--$23$ percentage points fewer zero-advantage groups than the
i.i.d.\ Bernoulli null), \emph{not} as negative pairwise rollout
correlation. ZVF is an extreme-event statistic, whereas correlation is a
second-moment property, and one model produced an explicit counterexample
($G=4$, $p=\tfrac12$) with $\Delta_G<0$ yet pairwise $\rho>0$. This
reinforces the conservative stance already taken in
Section~\ref{sec:zvf-dynamics}. Second, and as a genuine \emph{challenge}
to the ``zero gradient'' framing in Section~\ref{sec:zvf-gradient}: under
a KL-penalized reward $r_i=r^{\mathrm{task}}_i-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}_i$, when
$\mathrm{Var}(r^{\mathrm{task}})=0$ the advantage normalization cancels
$\beta$ and yields
$A_i=-(\mathrm{KL}_i-\overline{\mathrm{KL}})/\sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}$, so a
zero-variance group emits not a null update but a covert, full-magnitude
distillation pull toward the reference policy. The frontier models framed
this as the single falsifying experiment for Pillar~2: if masking these
updates destabilizes training, ZVF states are load-bearing regularization
anchors rather than pure dead compute---a caveat we flag but do not
resolve here.
