\subsubsection{Conditional quantile regression on $R \mid L$}\label{sec:lb-iter44}

The verbosity-trap literature predicts that longer responses inflate the
denominator of $R/L$ without raising $R$ itself, producing a positive length
bias. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter28} used OLS on $\E[R\mid L]$ and reported a
strongly anti-trap signature (negative $\beta_{R\mid L}$) on every
(\task, \algo, \seed) run. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter36} showed $L$ and $R$ share
a common saturation scale, and iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter40} stratified the slope
into training phases. None of those decompositions, however, asked whether the
$\E[R\mid L]$ slope hides \emph{asymmetric} length-response at different
quantiles of $R$. This iter addresses that gap.

\paragraph{Quantile slopes.}
We fit pinball-loss quantile regression (in-house SGD, no sklearn dep) for
$\tau \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9\}$ on per-step $(R, L)$ pairs pooled
across the 5 \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} seeds or the 3 \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}
seeds, separately for GRPO and Dr.GRPO. The slope of $q_\tau(R \mid L)$ as a
function of $L$ is reported in
\path{experiments/results/length_bias_iter44_quantile_slopes.tsv} (80 rows,
one per \task$\times$\algo$\times$\seed$\times\tau$ combination).

\paragraph{Asymmetry delta $\delta_{R \mid L} \equiv \beta_{q_{0.9}} -
\beta_{q_{0.1}}$.}
If longer responses carried only shallow signal, the upper-quantile slope
$\beta_{q_{0.9}}$ would be small or negative while the lower-quantile slope
$\beta_{q_{0.1}}$ would be positive (longer responses cluster around
mid-reward), producing a NEGATIVE asymmetry. We observe the opposite on every
one of the 16 (\task, \algo, \seed) cells: $\delta_{R \mid L} > 0$ for all
16, with median $|\delta_{R \mid L}| = 0.030$ on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}
and $0.0034$ on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot} (\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_asymmetry.tsv}).

\paragraph{Dr.GRPO strengthens the asymmetry.}
A 4000-rep paired bootstrap on the per-seed asymmetry (Dr.GRPO $-$ GRPO)
gives $\Delta = +0.0052$ on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}, $95\%$\,CI
$[+0.0001, +0.0119]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.030$; and $\Delta = +0.0008$ on
\texttt{gsm8k\_cot}, $95\%$\,CI $[-0.0004, +0.0019]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) =
0.034$ (\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo.tsv}). Both tasks
reject the null of equal asymmetry at the 5\% level. Dr.GRPO therefore
increases the conditional upper-quantile length-response on both tasks,
strengthening (not suppressing) the anti-trap signature.

\paragraph{ZVF-binned interaction (cross-pillar with iter~34).}
Stratifying per-step $(R, L)$ pairs by per-step \zvf{} into
\texttt{low}$(<\!0.2)$, \texttt{mid}$([0.2, 0.5))$, and
\texttt{high}$(\ge\!0.5)$ bins reveals a striking regime flip
(\texttt{length\_bias\_iter44\_zvf\_binned.tsv}). On \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}
in the \texttt{high}-ZVF bin (the contrast-rich, near-ceiling regime),
$\beta_{R \mid L}$ is $-0.010$ for GRPO but $+0.033$ for Dr.GRPO --- a
sign-flip that the unconditional regression averages away. The paired bootstrap
on the high-bin $\beta$ gives $\Delta = +0.043$, $95\%$\,CI
$[+0.024, +0.062]$, $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$ (4-of-4 Dr.GRPO seeds have
$\beta_{R \mid L} > 0$ in the high-ZVF bin while 5-of-5 GRPO seeds have
$\beta_{R \mid L} < 0$). On \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}, Dr.GRPO strengthens the
anti-trap slope in the \texttt{mid}-ZVF bin ($-0.004 \to -0.013$).

\paragraph{Operational reading.}
Three findings from iter~28--40 already established that the unconditional
$\E[R \mid L]$ slope is anti-trap; iter~44 adds: (a) the asymmetry of that
slope across quantiles is consistently positive (not negative as the
verbosity-trap would predict); (b) Dr.GRPO \emph{strengthens} that
asymmetry significantly on both tasks; (c) in the contrast-rich
high-ZVF regime on the easy task, Dr.GRPO flips the slope sign. The
anti-trap is therefore not a single mean-shift phenomenon but a
quantile-shape phenomenon that the length-bias correction in Dr.GRPO
exploits.
