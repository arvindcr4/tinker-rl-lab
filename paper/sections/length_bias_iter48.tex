\subsubsection{Plateau-anchored length-drift decomposition}\label{sec:lb-iter48}

Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter28} regressed $\E[R\mid L]$, iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter36}
jointly saturated $R$ and $L$ on a shared scale, iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40}
stratified by training phase, and iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter44} decomposed the
conditional $R\mid L$ into quantiles. All four decompose the within-step
coupling between reward and length. None of them anchor the decomposition at
the moment reward stops improving, which is the moment the Dr.GRPO length
trap is mechanistically most active. Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter48} closes that gap.

\paragraph{Plateau definition.}
For every (\task, \algo, \seed) trajectory we compute
$R_{\max} = \max_t R_t$, smooth $R_t$ with a 3-step centered moving average,
and let $\tplat$ be the first step at which the smoothed $R_t$ crosses
$0.90 \cdot R_{\max}$. The trajectory is then split into a \emph{rising}
phase $[0, \tplat)$ and a \emph{plateau} phase $[\tplat, T)$. We fit OLS
$\frac{dL}{dt}$ on each phase; the \emph{signature} is the marginal length
tax incurred \emph{after} reward saturates,
$\signature = (\tfrac{dL}{dt})_{\text{plat}} - (\tfrac{dL}{dt})_{\text{rise}}$.

\paragraph{Post-plateau drift on \texttt{arithmetic\_easy}.}
The cleanest Dr.GRPO signature appears here. GRPO's plateau-phase
$\tfrac{dL}{dt}$ is $-0.0013$ (length still slowly \emph{compressing}); Dr.GRPO's
is $+0.0015$ (length \emph{expanding}). The paired difference is
$-0.0028$, $95\%$ bootstrap CI $[-0.0045, -0.0014]$, entirely below zero.
\emph{Dr.GRPO is the only algorithm whose length keeps drifting up after
reward has saturated on arithmetic\_easy.} Figure~\ref{fig:lb-iter48-plat}
displays this with a side-by-side bar.

\paragraph{Pre-plateau compression on \texttt{gsm8k\_cot}.}
GSM8K CoT trajectories are longer (mean completion length $\approx 195$ tokens
vs $\approx 5$ tokens on arithmetic), so the relevant budget is the
\emph{pre}-plateau compression rate. GRPO compresses at $\tfrac{dL}{dt} = -0.451$
tokens/step, Dr.GRPO at only $-0.189$. Paired difference $-0.263$, $95\%$ CI
$[-0.554, -0.093]$. By the time GSM8K trajectories hit their (lower) reward
ceiling, Dr.GRPO has spent $60{+} \approx 60$ extra tokens per prompt on
average -- the same "stay-longer" trap, surfaced through the rising phase
because GSM8K's plateau is too short (only $\sim 1$ step) for a
post-plateau drift to manifest.

\paragraph{Two-task synthesis.}
The two findings are not contradictions but two views of the same Dr.GRPO
mechanism, surfaced at different time-horizons. On the short, dense
arithmetic trajectory the trap fires \emph{after} the policy has solved the
task (no further reward gradient, but length still drifts up). On the long
GSM8K CoT trajectory the trap fires \emph{before} the policy has solved the
task: Dr.GRPO's flattened group baseline blunts the length-compression signal
that GRPO receives from correct rollouts. In both cases the cross-algorithm
gap is significant with bootstrap CIs that exclude zero.

\paragraph{Cross-pillar with the ZVF proxy.}
Stratifying by the ZVF bin at the plateau step (Table
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter48\_zvf\_anchored.tsv}) shows that the arithmetic
plateau sits in the high-ZVF regime (0.5+) for \emph{every} Dr.GRPO seed and
in mid/high for GRPO -- consistent with iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter40}'s stage
stratification and consistent with the cross-pillar claim that Dr.GRPO's
length bias only activates once within-group contrast has collapsed.
