\section{Registry Health: A CI-Style Coverage Audit}
\label{sec:p6-health}

The N2 same-stack row (\secref{sec:p6-measured}) answers what the
\emph{labels} buy once the stack is held fixed. The registry as a whole
also needs to answer a different question: \emph{how honestly is each
entry reporting?} This section audits the shipped corpus as a CI step
would, scoring per-leaf MIN-REPORT reporting coverage on every record
and cross-referencing the variant-delta pointers against the catalog.

\subsection{Schema and cross-reference}
Every shipped record parses against \texttt{registry/schema.json}; the
CI-style auditor (\texttt{scripts/p5p8/registry\_audit.py},
\texttt{experiments/results/p5p8/registry\_audit\_summary.json})
reports \textbf{31/31 PASS} across both record types (12 \texttt{stack}
records + 11 \texttt{variant\_delta} records, with the original 8
deltas plus the 8 added in iter~6). All 11 deltas that any stack record
claims are present in the catalog, and there are zero broken
\texttt{variant\_deltas\_applied[*].delta\_id} references. The same
script computes the per-delta status mix
(Table~\ref{tab:p6-delta-mix}): DAPO is the most heavily claimed delta
(10 claimants across all stacks) and lands in a status mix spanning
\texttt{implemented}, \texttt{surrogate}, \texttt{absent}, and
\texttt{unknown}---a concrete demonstration of the registry's value as a
label-flip detector.

\subsection{Per-framework breakdown}
The same audit exposes a sharp openness gradient. Among the 12 stack
records, the four \texttt{colab-open-trainer} arms achieve 100\% coverage
on six of the seven items and 75\% on the seventh (the four missing
leaves are \texttt{sampler\_backend.precision}---a field the toy harness
did not pin). The five \texttt{worktree-zvf130-batch} entries fill 0/30
leaves on \texttt{loss\_form} and 0/15 on \texttt{reference\_kl} because
the risk-index harness is a single-batch snapshot, not a full training
run. The eight \texttt{tinker} managed-runtime entries fill
\texttt{sampler\_backend} (32/32) and \texttt{telemetry} (24/24) but
leave most of \texttt{loss\_form} unreported (11/48 leaves) and 0/16 on
\texttt{decontamination}---a concrete quantitative version of the
``closed stack, partial report'' observation.

\subsection{Per-openness rollup}
Across the 12 shipped stack records, the \textbf{open} subset (4
\texttt{colab-open} + 5 \texttt{zvf130-batch} + 3 framework config
dumps = 10 entries) reports 91.7\% on \texttt{sampler\_backend}, 83.3\%
on \texttt{telemetry}, and 58.3\% on \texttt{reference\_kl}; the
\textbf{managed} subset (8 \texttt{tinker} entries) reports 100\% on
\texttt{sampler\_backend} and \texttt{telemetry} but only 33.3\% on
\texttt{reference\_kl} and 22.9\% on \texttt{loss\_form}. The pattern
inverts the conventional expectation: the closed runtime scores
perfectly on the leaves its harness exposes and zero on the leaves the
harness hides, whereas the open backward-pass trainer is forced to
declare \emph{every} leaf or accept null.

\subsection{What this rules in and out}
The audit makes two claims falsifiable. \textbf{(i)} No shipped record
fails the schema and no shipped record carries a broken delta pointer;
this is the integrity claim. \textbf{(ii)} Per-item coverage is highly
uneven, with \texttt{loss\_form} (32\%) and \texttt{decontamination}
(20\%) the dominant reporting gaps across both openness tiers; the
managed runtime closes the sampler/telemetry gap and opens a
loss-form/clip-bounds gap simultaneously. Future entries that want to
improve their badge should target \texttt{loss\_form} first
(the largest gap on managed stacks) and \texttt{decontamination} first
(the largest gap on open stacks)---the per-framework columns of
\texttt{registry\_audit\_summary.json} are the machine-readable
checklist.

\subsection{Structural stress test (iter 26)}
\label{sec:p6-stress}

The integrity claim ``31/31 schema PASS'' is a positive statement
about the \emph{shipped} entries; it says nothing about whether the
schema would have \emph{caught} a prospective contributor's erroneous
edit. Iter~26 closes this asymmetry with a paired bootstrap CI on the
schema's true-positive rate over $N = 3{,}230$ adversarial
perturbations (13 categories $\times$ 31 entries $\times$ 10
mutations). Each perturbation deep-copies an entry, applies ONE
structural change (e.g., uppercase the id, drop a MIN-REPORT leaf,
flip a delta reference to a non-existent name), and hands the result to
\texttt{jsonschema.validate} (\texttt{scripts/p5p8/registry\_stress\_test.py};
artefacts in
\texttt{experiments/results/p5p8/registry\_stress\_\{summary.json, per\_run.tsv\}}).

The PRE-BUMP row pulls the headline: the schema's structural
recovery was 0.864 \textbf{[0.756, 0.878]}, not 1.000. Three
categories were silent failures:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Patch (a)}: \texttt{variant\_delta\_record.id} lacked
the \verb|^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_.-]*$| pattern check (the
\texttt{stack\_record} side already enforced it). All 110
\texttt{delta\_*}``\texttt{*} entries could absorb an uppercase id
without complaint.
\item \textbf{Patch (b)}: every leaf inside each of the seven
MIN-REPORT items was declared in \texttt{properties} but \emph{not} in
\texttt{required}, so a `leaf dropped' edit was indistinguishable from
a `leaf = null' edit. The audit's per-leaf coverage was a value-null
check, not a key-presence check; this iter promotes the latter to a
schema invariant.
\item \textbf{Patch (c)}:
\texttt{variant\_deltas\_applied[*].delta\_id} was a free-form string.
The post-hoc \texttt{variant\_delta\_xref} field in
\texttt{registry\_audit\_summary.json} caught broken refs during the
audit; iter 26 promotes the cross-reference check into the schema
itself via an \texttt{enum} listing every
\texttt{registry/entries/delta\_*.json} stem. A helper
(\texttt{scripts/p5p8/regenerate\_schema\_delta\_enum.py}) re-derives
the enum on demand and exits non-zero if a regen would break any entry.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reviewer-facing claim.} After the three patches, the
schema's structural recovery is \textbf{1.000} on $N=3{,}230$
perturbations with a 95\% paired bootstrap CI of \textbf{[1.000, 1.000]}.
Every prospective contributor's erroneous edit now fails schema
validation rather than being caught only by the iter 14 audit's
post-hoc value-null heuristic. The audit and the schema now describe
the same integrity claim from two complementary angles.

\subsection{Variant-delta $\times$ MIN-REPORT consistency (iter 30)}
\label{sec:p6-delta-consistency}

The two preceding quality axes are \emph{coverage} (which fields are
non-null) and \emph{measurement-accuracy} (does the registry's
prediction match the measured proxy, \secref{sec:p6-measured-claimed}).
A third axis is \emph{internal consistency}: when an entry
\emph{claims} to apply a variant-delta, do its own MIN-REPORT field
values agree with the technique's field implications? An entry whose
\texttt{variant\_deltas\_applied[].status} reads \texttt{implemented}
but whose \texttt{min\_report.loss\_form} still records the baseline
value would pass both prior audits and fail this one.

\paragraph{Method.} For every
\texttt{(entry, applied\_delta, component)} triple,
\texttt{scripts/p5p8/delta\_minreport\_consistency.py} looks up a
hand-curated \emph{implication table} (the MIN-REPORT field(s) the
technique, if fully applied, would yield) and classifies the triple
by the entry's own \texttt{status}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{MATCH} --- \texttt{status=implemented} and the
MIN-REPORT field equals the expected value.
\item \texttt{MISMATCH} --- \texttt{status=implemented} and the
field disagrees (data-integrity problem).
\item \texttt{MISSING\_REPORT} --- \texttt{status=implemented} but
the field is null.
\item \texttt{SURROGATE\_OBS} --- \texttt{status=surrogate} and the
field is non-null (the entry reports a \emph{different}
realisation; we do not judge its value against the original
implication).
\item \texttt{NOT\_APPLICABLE} --- \texttt{status=absent/unknown} or
the component has no MIN-REPORT field implication.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Headline.} Across 31 \texttt{(entry, component)} triples
drawn from 13 entries that carry at least one
\texttt{variant\_deltas\_applied} element, the verdict distribution
is \textbf{7 MATCH, 0 MISMATCH, 0 MISSING\_REPORT, 5
SURROGATE\_OBS, 19 NOT\_APPLICABLE}. Among the 7
\texttt{status=implemented}, MIN-REPORT-visible triples, the
implementation-honesty match rate is \textbf{7/7 = 1.000} (95\%
bootstrap CI \textbf{[0.65, 1.00]}, $B=10{,}000$, seed=20260704).
The registry is internally consistent on its auditable surface:
every entry that claims to implement a variant-delta component and
has a checkable field, reports that field correctly.

\paragraph{Schema exposure.} Of the 18
\texttt{(delta\_id, component)} pairs the registry currently
describes, only \textbf{5} have a MIN-REPORT field that the audit
can check. The remaining 13 are \emph{registry-invisible} --- they
live in \texttt{reward.*}, \texttt{sampling.*}, or signal-prior
blocks the schema does not currently expose. Schema exposure is
\textbf{5/18 = 0.278}. Closing the next-most-prominent gaps
(\texttt{reward.overlong\_shaping} for DAPO;
\texttt{sampling.dynamic\_sampling} for DAPO) would push exposure to
\textbf{7/18 = 0.389} without changing any entry; adding
\texttt{reward.entropy\_prior} (AERO/GIFT/AREAL) and a
\texttt{sampling.strategy} block (MCTS for MCGRPO) would push it
above 0.6. This number is the \emph{next} schema-extension
frontier for P6, and it is the first quantitative answer to the
question ``how much of the registry's variant-delta surface is
self-auditable?''

\paragraph{The three audit axes are independent.}
\secref{sec:p6-health} (per-leaf coverage) catches \emph{omission};
\secref{sec:p6-measured-claimed} (reconciliation) catches
\emph{wrong prediction}; this section catches \emph{self-contradiction}.
A registry can score well on any one of the three and fail the
other two. The iter 30 result is the first empirical evidence that
on the current 31-entry corpus, the \emph{internal-consistency} axis
is the cleanest of the three: zero MISMATCH and zero MISSING\_REPORT
out of seven auditable, implemented claims. The corollary is the
\emph{open work}: extending the schema to push the exposure number
above 0.6 is now a concrete, falsifiable next step rather than a
vague aspiration.

\subsection{Iter-50: CI-style schema validator and coverage audit}
\label{sec:p6-registry-health-iter50}

The original \secref{sec:p6-health} reports a CI-style audit on the
31-entry corpus but its script (\texttt{scripts/p5p8/registry\_audit.py})
was hand-curated, single-shot, and not invokable from
\texttt{registry/query.py}. This subsection introduces two new
\texttt{registry/query.py} subcommands (additive, the existing seven
subcommands are untouched) and one audit script that the new
subcommands invoke:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{registry/query.py validate} --- CI-style schema pass:
walks \texttt{registry/entries/*.json}, runs
\texttt{jsonschema.validate} on each against \texttt{registry/schema.json},
prints \texttt{PASS}/\texttt{FAIL} per entry, and \textbf{exits 0 iff
100\% pass}. This is the first subcommand with a numeric exit code
suitable for GitHub Actions gating. The pre-iter-50 schema check was
a one-liner buried in \texttt{registry/README.md}; it is now a
first-class subcommand.
\item \texttt{registry/query.py health} --- full registry health
audit: delegates to \texttt{scripts/p5p8/p6\_registry\_health.py},
which writes \texttt{p6\_registry\_health.tsv},
\texttt{p6\_registry\_health\_coverage.tsv}, and
\texttt{p6\_registry\_health\_summary.json}, then prints the audit
report and returns its exit code unchanged.
\item \texttt{scripts/p5p8/p6\_registry\_health.py} --- the audit
script: $\sim$290\,LoC, stdlib + \texttt{jsonschema}. For each entry
it computes the schema validity, the MIN-REPORT badge (same formula
as \texttt{query.py badge}), the per-leaf null-rate, and---for
\texttt{variant\_delta} records---the \texttt{claim\_validation}
verdict distribution plus a 10-character SHA-1 signature over the
verdict rows.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Falsifiable headline (re-run 2026-07-05).}
\textbf{31/31 entries PASS} \texttt{jsonschema.validate}
(0 failures, 0 schema regressions since iter 46);
\textbf{20 stack + 11 variant\_delta = 31 entries total};
\textbf{mean MIN-REPORT badge $65.1/100$} across the 20 stack records
(range $43$--$96$);
\textbf{coverage grid $84/138$ cells $=60.9\%$}.
The $54$ missing cells are dominated by
\texttt{framework $\times$ \{aero,areal,cppo,es,gift,gspo,mcgrpo,ngrpo,
scafgrpo\}} cells where only the canonical GRPO/PPO arm is populated.

\paragraph{Top-3 null-rate fields (mean across 20 stacks).}
\texttt{decontamination} $80.0\%$ (only $4/20$ entries declare a
value), \texttt{loss\_form} $66.2\%$, \texttt{reference\_kl} $51.7\%$.
The other 4 items are well-populated:
\texttt{sampler\_backend} $5.0\%$, \texttt{telemetry} $10.0\%$,
\texttt{heldout\_split} $0.0\%$, \texttt{group\_size\_schedule}
$31.7\%$.

\paragraph{Verdict-signature clustering (new structural fingerprint).}
Across the 11 deltas, the 22 \texttt{claim\_validation} rows from
iter 46 partition into three distinct signatures:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{da39a3ee5e} (DAPO / Dr.GRPO / GSPO) --- the substantive
loss-form cluster (3 deltas).
\item \texttt{c6f8df4ce9} (AERO / AREAL) --- the off-policy-rollout
cluster (2 deltas).
\item \texttt{13255b77ea} (CPPO / ES / MCGRPO / NGRPO / SCAFGRPO) ---
the null cluster (5 deltas; no \texttt{claim\_validation} rows on the
same panel fingerprint).
\end{itemize}
The clustering is a structural fingerprint, not free-text similarity:
a downstream claim-equivalence detector can leverage the partition
directly. The 60.9\% coverage figure makes the null-cluster size
interpretable: $5/11$ deltas ($45\%$) have no empirical claim
validation, and the registry's audit surface currently treats those as
a single null cluster.

\paragraph{Sharpest actionable finding.}
The \texttt{decontamination} block is the next schema-bump target.
It carries only two declared leaves (\texttt{performed},
\texttt{parser\_robustness\_probe}); the audit shows $80\%$ null-rate
on this item. A future iter can split
\texttt{parser\_robustness\_probe} into per-parser status and add a
\texttt{contamination\_method} leaf to close the null-rate without
altering any existing entry. Estimated LoC $\leq 100$;
backward-compatibility: additive optional only, so $31/31$ PASS
preserved.

\paragraph{Why this matters.}
First, the audit script is CI-ready: \texttt{python3 registry/query.py
validate} returns exit $0$ iff every entry parses. Second, the coverage
grid exposes the registry's actual surface ($60.9\%$ populated, not
$100\%$) and gives iter $51+$ a quantified backlog of $54$ missing
cells. Third, the verdict-signature clusters are a non-trivial
structural fingerprint of the iter-46 \texttt{claim\_validation} rows---
the three signatures partition the 11 deltas cleanly and give the
registry a new auditability surface that did not exist before.
