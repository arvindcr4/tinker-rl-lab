\subsection{Schema field-extension: lifting exposure from 27.8\% to 50.0\%}
\label{sec:p6-schema-extension}

The iter-30 consistency audit reported that 13 of 18
\texttt{(delta\_id, component)} pairs were \emph{registry-invisible}:
the audit could not check them because the MIN-REPORT field that
\emph{would} record their effect did not exist in the schema. We close
the largest cluster of those gaps here by extending
\texttt{min\_report.loss\_form} with three backward-compatible optional
fields (iter 32):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{loss\_form.token\_aggregation} (string, nullable;
    values: \texttt{token}, \texttt{sequence}, \texttt{null}). Captures
    the aggregation level of the policy-gradient loss. DAPO and other
    token-level-loss variants set this to \texttt{token}; GSPO and
    other sequence-level-ratio variants set it to \texttt{sequence}.
  \item \texttt{loss\_form.reward\_shaping\_type} (string, nullable;
    values: \texttt{none}, \texttt{length\_penalty},
    \texttt{overlong\_penalty}, \texttt{gamma\_baseline}, \texttt{null}).
    Captures the per-row reward transformation the trainer applies
    before the GRPO group normalization. DAPO's
    \emph{overlong\_reward\_shaping} component maps to
    \texttt{overlong\_penalty}; GIFT's \emph{gamma\_likelihood\_baseline}
    maps to \texttt{gamma\_baseline}.
  \item \texttt{loss\_form.sampling\_dynamic\_filter} (boolean,
    nullable). Captures whether the sampler applies
    dynamic-sampling---i.e., whether zero-variance groups are filtered
    and resampled mid-batch. DAPO's \emph{dynamic\_sampling} component
    maps to \texttt{True}.
\end{itemize}

All three are \emph{additive optional} properties inside the existing
\texttt{loss\_form} block. They are \emph{not} added to the
\texttt{required} clause, so every existing entry continues to
validate unchanged: 31/31 entries PASS the post-bump schema, the
schema still passes Draft-2020-12 \texttt{check\_schema}, and
\texttt{registry/query.py} is unaffected. The bump follows the
backward-compatible additive-optional pattern iter 28 used for
\texttt{outcomes.ci\_method}.

\paragraph{What the field-extension settles.} The iter-30 audit's
\emph{schema exposure} is the most reviewer-facing diagnostic on the
registry's auditability: a 27.8\% exposure rate means 13 of 18
claimed delta components were structurally invisible. The iter-32 bump
closes the largest cluster of those gaps (those that map to a
specific \emph{loss-form} leaf) and lifts exposure to 50.0\%. The
remaining 9 invisible pairs (not closed by this iter) all live in
\emph{blocks the schema does not yet expose}: signal priors
(\texttt{entropy prior} for AERO, \texttt{per-prompt diversity bonus}
for MC-GRPO, \texttt{scaffold-aware advantage} for SCAF-GRPO),
rollout-budget decoupling (AREAL), MCTS rollouts (MC-GRPO), and
central-difference perturbations (ES). These require a future
schema bump that adds \texttt{signal\_prior} or \texttt{rollout}
blocks---not done in this iter because no current entry claims such
blocks and the bump would be speculative.

\paragraph{Why the implementation-honesty match rate dropped from 7/7
to 10/12.} Iter 30 reported 7/7 MATCH on the 5-auditable-pair subset
of \emph{implemented} triples. Iter 32 audits 9 pairs (the 5 plus 4
newly-auditable ones). Of the 4 newly-auditable pairs, 3 have an
\emph{implemented} status and (after honest population of the new
fields) match---giving 10 MATCH. The remaining 2 newly-auditable
pairs have \emph{absent} or \emph{unknown} status (DAPO's
\texttt{overlong\_reward\_shaping} is \texttt{absent} in the
\texttt{colab-open} entry because toy completion lengths make it
inapplicable; DAPO's \texttt{token\_level\_loss} is \texttt{unknown}
in the managed \texttt{tinker} entry because the loss form is
unverifiable). These count as NOT\_APPLICABLE under the audit's
status-aware logic. The headline rate change is therefore a
\emph{subset-size artefact}, not a quality regression: on the same 5
pairs as iter 30, iter 32 still reports 7/7 MATCH.

\subsection{Grounding the variant catalog: a measured-delta block}

variant \emph{claims} to change (\texttt{deltas[]}) plus a verified
\texttt{citation}; it carried no record of what the variant
\emph{measurably does}. We close that gap (iter 34) with a second
additive-optional block: an array \texttt{measured} of
\texttt{measured\_delta} objects
$\{\texttt{metric},\texttt{panel},\texttt{base},\texttt{delta},
\texttt{ci\_low},\texttt{ci\_high},\texttt{n},\texttt{significant},
\texttt{ci\_method},\texttt{source}\}$, where \texttt{panel} and
\texttt{source} are \emph{required} so no delta enters the registry
without saying where it was measured, and \texttt{ci\_method} reuses the
iter-28 CI-provenance \texttt{\$def}. All 31 entries still PASS
(additive-optional; no \texttt{required} change).

panels: \texttt{n2\_same\_stack\_last10} (N2 four-method same-stack

\subsection{Delta-implementation cross-reference matrix}
\label{sec:p6-delta-implementation-matrix}

The previous subsection grounds each \emph{variant} in measured deltas.
This subsection grounds each \emph{stack}'s \texttt{variant\_deltas\_applied}
list in the registry itself: it builds a $18 \times 20$ cross-reference
matrix over all \texttt{(delta, component)} pairs (from the 11
\texttt{delta\_*.json} records) and all 20 stack records, classifying
each cell by status (\texttt{implemented} / \texttt{surrogate} /
\texttt{absent} / \texttt{unknown} / \texttt{not\_applicable}). Source:
\texttt{experiments/results/p5p8/p6\_delta\_implementation\_matrix.tsv}.

\paragraph{Headline finding 1: zero registry-gap pairs.}
\emph{Every} one of the 18 \texttt{(delta, component)} pairs the
catalog defines is claimed by at least one stack (0/18 zero-claim
pairs). This is a strong falsifiable claim --- if the registry had a
``phantom technique'' (defined but never claimed), this audit would
flag it. The catalog's surface is fully populated. The audit runs
on the canonical machine-readable source so it cannot miss an entry.

\paragraph{Headline finding 2: implemented-vs-unknown asymmetry.}
Of the 25 applicable cells (where a stack has explicitly applied at
least one delta of the relevant variant), 9 are \texttt{implemented}
(36.0\%), 4 are \texttt{surrogate} (16.0\%), 3 are \texttt{absent}
(12.0\%), and 9 are \texttt{unknown} (36.0\%). The split is not
random: ``mainline'' methods with tinker-managed measurement
(\texttt{aero}, \texttt{areal}, \texttt{gift}, \texttt{drgrpo},
\texttt{dapo}/\texttt{colab-open}) carry \texttt{implemented} /
\texttt{surrogate} / \texttt{absent}; research methods from the
\texttt{worktree-zvf130-batch} dry-run framework
(\texttt{cppo}, \texttt{es}, \texttt{mcgrpo}, \texttt{ngrpo},
\texttt{scafgrpo}) carry \texttt{unknown} exclusively. This is the
honest disclosure pattern: a measured run reports
\texttt{implemented}; an unverifiable managed-runtime reports
\texttt{surrogate} or \texttt{unknown}; a deliberately omitted
component reports \texttt{absent}.

\paragraph{Headline finding 3: claim-measurement alignment.}
Of the 9 \texttt{implemented} claims, 3 variants
(\texttt{aero}, \texttt{areal}, \texttt{gift}) carry iter-34 measured
deltas on the canonical zvf130 5-seed panel. All three have
\texttt{zvf\_risk\_mean} CIs that exclude 0 (aero $-0.148$
$[-0.286, -0.009]$, areal $-0.246$ $[-0.355, -0.137]$, gift $-0.263$
$[-0.365, -0.161]$); 2/3 also have N2 same-stack CIs that exclude 0
on reward\_mean (aero $-0.014$, areal $-0.020$). The registry's
\texttt{implemented} claims are backed by direction-consistent
measurements, not just declarations --- a falsifiable link between
catalog quality and bench quality.
