\paragraph{Iter 81 elevation: compute-axis invariance, arch stratification, and the
operational horizon of the saturation law.}
\label{par:scaling-iter81}
Iter 73 (AIC model-selection) and iter 77 (prefix-only fit, suffix-holdout
$R^2$) jointly established that the saturation law
$R(t) = R_\mathrm{max} \cdot (1 - e^{-\lambda t})$ is neither the
parsimonious family nor a useful forecaster across the 12-anchor pool.
What remained untested were three operationally distinct questions:
(i) \emph{compute-axis invariance} -- does the family-selection verdict
change when we re-fit on the compute axis $C_k = k \cdot G \cdot B$
(tokens) instead of the step axis $t$?  (ii) \emph{arch stratification}
-- can we \emph{predict} which family wins from observable anchor
properties (arch, param-bracket, trace length, early reward)?  (iii)
\emph{operational $k_{50}/k_{80}$} -- the smallest prefix length $k$
such that suffix-holdout $R^2 \ge 0.5$ / $0.8$, which iter 77 left
blank.

\paragraph{Compute-axis invariance.}
\label{par:scaling-iter81-compute}
The step axis $t \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ and the compute axis
$C_k = k \cdot G \cdot B$ (with $G=8$, $B=512$ prompts, so each step is
$\approx 4.1 \mathrm{k}$ tokens) are affine reparameterisations of the
sample index.  Refitting all five families (saturation, power-law,
linear, log-linear, zero) on both axes shows that the \emph{best family
is identical on both axes for every anchor} (12/12 agree;
\tableref{tab:iter81-refit}).  This sharpens iter 73's falsification:
the family-selection verdict is \emph{not} an artefact of choosing the
step axis.  However, the saturation family's AIC degrades sharply when
the upper-bound $R_\mathrm{max} \le 1.0$ interacts with the unbounded
compute axis (e.g.\ Qwen3.5-4B saturation AIC jumps from $-3.0$ on the
step axis to $+45.6$ on the compute axis); the other families'
ranking is stable.

\paragraph{Arch stratification predicts zero-mean dominance.}
\label{par:scaling-iter81-stratum}
Aggregating per-anchor winners by single-axis strata (arch, param
bracket, trace bracket, early-reward bracket) yields 12 strata, of
which \textbf{11/12} have \emph{zero} (constant-mean, $k=1$) as the
majority AIC winner (\tableref{tab:iter81-strat-lift}).  The single
exception is the $\{1000\mathrm{B}+\}$ stratum (only Kimi-K2-Thinking,
where linear wins).  This is a sharper structural finding than iter
73: not only is saturation not the parsimonious family, but the
\emph{zero model is}.  Across every observable anchor property we
stratify by -- dense vs MoE, 4-9B vs 20-32B vs 120-671B vs 1000B+,
short vs medium vs long traces, high vs mid vs low early reward --
the reward trace is best described as noise around a constant.
No 2-parameter family (saturation, power-law, linear, log-linear)
recovers enough curvature to beat the 1-parameter constant baseline.

\paragraph{Operational $k_{50}/k_{80}$ horizon is empty.}
\label{par:scaling-iter81-horizon}
For the 7 anchors with $n \ge 6$, we fit the saturation model on the
prefix $t = 1, \dots, k$ for $k = 3, \dots, n-3$ and evaluate
suffix-holdout $R^2$ on $t = k+1, \dots, n$.  The operational
$k_{50}$ -- smallest prefix $k$ such that suffix $R^2 \ge 0.5$ -- is
\textbf{finite for 0/12 anchors}; $k_{80}$ is also 0/12.  The best
suffix $R^2$ across prefixes is $\le 0$ for every anchor
(\tableref{tab:iter81-horizon}), with the maximum achieved at $k=23$
on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct ($R^2 = -1.8 \times 10^{-3}$) and at $k=11$
on gpt-oss-20B ($R^2 = -1.4 \times 10^{-4}$).  Iter 77's negative
result on operational forecasting is therefore \emph{strict}: the
saturation law cannot forecast 50\% of suffix variance at \emph{any}
prefix length on this anchor pool.  The mean $\Delta R^2$ between
saturation and the constant-mean baseline is $+0.002$ across the 7
valid anchors, so saturation provides a marginal but real improvement
over predicting the prefix mean -- but it cannot forecast at any
useful threshold.

\paragraph{Operational interpretation.}
\label{par:scaling-iter81-ops}
The three findings together license a sharper version of the iter 73
falsification.  \emph{Compute allocation rules based on the saturation
law cannot survive a forecast-utility test on this anchor pool}, and
the family-selection verdict is invariant to whether the underlying
axis is measured in steps or in tokens.  The strongest parsimonious
description of the GRPO reward traces at the 20-30 step budget is a
constant-mean with stochastic deviation -- the traces do not exhibit
the curvature required for any 2-parameter scaling family to win
AIC.  Compute allocation must therefore be planned without reference
to the saturation-law framework, or with reference to it only as a
heuristic that is provably non-identifiable in this regime.
