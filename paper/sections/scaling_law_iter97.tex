% Pillar 1 iter97 -- 8-family head-to-head battery (3 new families) +
% variance-scaling regression.  Closes Q4 (any new functional form
% beats constant on out-of-sample criteria?) and Q5 (does the GRPO
% reward noise floor depend on model scale?).  Both questions yield

\paragraph{Iter 97 elevation: three new functional families (Monod,
Gompertz, piecewise-2-segment-OLS) do not dethrone constant-mean on
out-of-sample criteria, and the GRPO reward noise floor is
approximately scale-invariant ($\hat\beta = -0.0074$, bootstrap 95\% CI
$[-0.023, +0.008]$, $R^2 = 0.04$).}
\label{par:scaling-iter97}

iter 93 (Pillar 1) closed Q1--Q3 by showing that \textbf{constant-mean}
wins all three parsimony criteria (AIC/BIC/LOOCV) while \textbf{AR(1)}
wins the forecast criterion -- a parsimony-vs-forecast inversion that
turns out to be reproducible on the same 12-anchor pool.
iter 97 asks the next two questions in the same falsifiable register:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Q4 (functional-form coverage).}  We add three new
families to the head-to-head battery: \textbf{Monod / Michaelis-Menten}
$R(t) = R_\mathrm{max}\, t / (K + t)$ ($k=2$, hyperbolic ceiling),
\textbf{Gompertz} $R(t) = a \exp(-b e^{-c t})$ ($k=3$, asymmetric
S-curve from growth modelling), and \textbf{piecewise-2-segment OLS}
($k=5$, optimal changepoint + two independent OLS lines).  Each new
family has at least one parameter not present in iter 93's battery; the
question is whether ANY of them beats constant-mean on
out-of-sample criteria.
\item \textbf{Q5 (variance scaling).}  Does the GRPO reward noise
floor $\sigma^2_i$ of anchor $i$ scale with $\log_{10} \mathrm{params}_i$?
We regress $\sigma^2_i$ (variance of the constant-fit residual) on
$\log_{10} \mathrm{params}_i$ and test whether the slope $\beta$ is
significantly different from zero under bootstrap resampling.
\end{enumerate}

Both questions yield negative results
(\tableref{tab:scaling-iter97-winners} and
\tableref{tab:scaling-iter97-variance}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Q4.}  On the strongest parsimony criterion (BIC),
constant-mean wins 9 of 12 anchors; the remaining 3 are split among
1-segment OLS ($n=3$).  On AIC, constant-mean wins 6 of 12 and
piecewise-2-segment wins 4 (the AIC penalty does not yet bite at
$k=5$ for short traces); on LOOCV-RMSE, constant-mean wins 8 of 12.
\textbf{No new family wins BIC, LOOCV, AIC, in-sample RMSE, or $R^2$
as a single dominant universal family} -- the same outcome as iter 93
extended to 8 families.  Monod wins 0 anchors on every criterion;
Gompertz wins 0.  Pw2seg's 7 in-sample RMSE wins evaporate under
LOOCV ($n=0$), the classic over-parameterization failure.
\item \textbf{Q5.}  The OLS regression
$\sigma^2 = \alpha + \beta \log_{10}(\mathrm{params}_B)$ yields
$\hat\alpha = 0.044$, $\hat\beta = -0.007$, $R^2 = 0.040$.  Bootstrap
95\% CI on $\beta$ is $[-0.023, +0.008]$, which covers zero.  The
GRPO reward noise floor is \emph{approximately scale-invariant}: the
homoscedastic assumption that iter 81 / iter 85 / iter 89 / iter 93
inherit from the constant-mean baseline is \emph{upheld by data}, not
just an analytic convenience.
\end{itemize}

The Iter 97 finding is therefore the \emph{uniform negative result}:
extending the functional-form battery from 5 to 8 families does not
falsify the constant-mean parsimony winner, and the noise floor is
homogeneous across model scales.  Combined with iter 93's
parsimony-vs-forecast inversion, the operational recommendation is
\textbf{use constant-mean for parsimony reporting and AR(1) for
deployment forecasting}, and \textbf{do not} attempt to learn a
scale-dependent variance correction -- the data do not support one.
