\section{ZVF as a Collapse Predictor on the Five-Anchor Scaling-Law Corpus}
\label{sec:zvf-scaling}

This appendix closes the cross-pillar loop by asking whether the
\texttt{is\_collapse} flag attached to the five-anchor scaling-law
table (\texttt{experiments/results/scaling\_law\_three\_phase.tsv})
is predictable from per-step heldout-reward statistics that are
ZVF-adjacent: the fraction of optimizer steps with heldout
reward $<0.1$ (\texttt{frac\_below\_0p1}) and the per-step
zero-reward fraction (\texttt{zero\_fraction}). Both are direct
surrogates for the within-group all-wrong regime that drives
$\mathrm{ZVF} \to 1$ in the Pillar~2 framework.

\paragraph{Reading the table.}
The two ZVF-proxy columns (\texttt{frac\_below\_0p1} and \texttt{zero\_fraction}) take their maximum value (0.55) on the Nemotron-120B row and are 0 (or 0.07) on every non-collapse row. $\rho_{\mathrm{Spearman}}(\text{frac\_below\_0p1}, \text{is\_collapse}) = +1.0$ by construction ($n=5$, one collapse), and the same holds for $\rho(\text{zero\_fraction}, \text{is\_collapse})$. The diagnostic value is not the magnitude of the correlation but the \emph{separation}: on this corpus, every model with $\text{frac\_below\_0p1} \geq 0.1$ is a collapse, and every model with $\text{frac\_below\_0p1} < 0.1$ is not.

\paragraph{What this is evidence for.}
The collapse rule used in \texttt{scaling\_law\_three\_phase.tsv} is $p > 0.7 \wedge \ell < 0.35$, a peak-vs-last10 rule. The ZVF-proxy rule $\text{frac\_below\_0p1} \geq 0.1$ picks the same row. The two rules are not identical ($p$ is the maximum heldout reward, $\text{frac\_below\_0p1}$ is the time-aggregated mass at zero), but on this five-anchor corpus they agree perfectly. This is the first cross-pillar evidence we have that Pillar 2's ZVF diagnostic picks out the same failure mode as Pillar 1's collapse label on a corpus that Pillar 1 owns.
