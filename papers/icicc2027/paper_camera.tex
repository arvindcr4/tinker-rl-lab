\begin{abstract}
Benchmark scores can conceal incomplete coverage, replacement scopes and missing
execution evidence. We examine a receipt-bound reporting protocol through one
language-model actor across fourteen agentic and reasoning lanes. The study is
an evaluation-governance case study, not a test of the actor's reported
40-step group-relative post-training. Original contracts and replacements have
separate identities, denominators and dated states. The historical strictly
complete scopes comprise two replacements, LAB-Bench public (450/1,967) and
AgentDojo benign utility (88/97), and one original contract, VerilogEval
(129/312). Omni-MATH is terminal-complete: its native accepted-row score is
2,271/4,426 (51.31\%), versus 2,271/4,428 (51.29\%) over all dispositions.
SWE-bench Pro resolves 2/731 public tasks, with substantial patch-format failures.
Six later replacement executions are reported separately with protocol
deviations. Extraction failures and serving differences prevent interpreting
these scores as intrinsic capability. Eleven historical checks cover selected
arithmetic and bindings, not independent reproduction. A separate small
same-engine trained/base pilot is inconclusive for its seventeen primary
outcome contrasts. The contribution is explicit scope and evidence accounting;
content-level training overlap, full-scale matched baselines and some execution
records remain unresolved. No training benefit, cross-suite aggregate or measured
improvement in governance effectiveness is claimed.
\keywords{Evaluation governance \and Agentic benchmarks \and Reproducibility \and Large language models}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A benchmark score is interpretable only with its task population, actor,
serving stack, grader and missing-outcome policy. Agentic evaluations can
prepare hundreds of tasks while grading few, replace a gated benchmark with a
public proxy, or retain a number after losing its execution source. Inadequate
holdouts and reproducibility problems are established concerns in agent
research~\cite{kapoor2024agentsmatter}. We study a concrete reporting response,
using a heterogeneous fourteen-lane campaign with incomplete provenance.

The actor has reported group-relative policy-gradient training lineage.
GRPO uses rewards relative to sampled groups~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath}, but
this campaign neither verifies the exact historical objective nor identifies
its causal effect. We therefore frame the study around evaluation governance.
Training-suite separation is not proven item-level decontamination, and no
blanket held-out claim is made.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{receipt-bound reporting structure} joining scope, actor, serving
configuration, grader, outcomes and evidence availability
\item A \textbf{coverage taxonomy} distinguishing preparation, attempts,
native grading and original versus replacement contracts
\item A \textbf{dated case study} retaining errors, incomplete scopes and
protocol changes rather than pooling unlike results
\item A \textbf{failure-attribution analysis} separating observable formatting
failures from unverified explanations and training-effect claims
\end{itemize}
This is not a new optimizer or benchmark. No controlled study establishes
that the reporting structure reduces errors, costs or replication time.

\section{Related Work and Benchmark Identity}
Reinforcement-learning reproducibility studies~\cite{henderson2018deep} and the
NeurIPS reproducibility program~\cite{pineau2020improving} motivate explicit
execution records. Model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards} and
datasheets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} organize disclosures at model and dataset
level; our unit is a run within a named benchmark scope. Hash equality proves
byte identity, not grader correctness or the truth of a receipt.

Selection was purposive: software, tools, web tasks, biology, ML engineering,
safety, hardware, application deployment, games and mathematics expose
different interfaces. It is neither representative nor exhaustive capability
sampling. Access-driven replacement introduces selection bias.
SWE-bench Pro~\cite{deng2025swebenchpro}, CORE-Bench~\cite{siegel2024corebench}
and MLE-bench~\cite{chan2025mlebench} measure different engineering outcomes.
WebArena~\cite{zhou2024webarena}, LAB-Bench~\cite{laurent2024labbench} and
BALROG~\cite{paglieri2025balrog} use web success, biology answers and game
progress. AgentDojo benign utility~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} is distinct
from AgentHarm harmful-task evaluation~\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm}.
VerilogEval has two framings of shared designs~\cite{liu2023verilogeval,pinckney2025revisiting};
Omni-MATH measures Olympiad mathematics~\cite{gao2024omnimath}.
The campaign's Tau3 banking replacement is the $\tau^3$ banking-knowledge component of Sierra's
$\tau$-Knowledge release~\cite{shi2026tauknowledge}, not the original $\tau$-bench. All original identities appear in Table~\ref{tab:lanes}; verified
benchmark links and unresolved identity details accompany the source package.

Comparability requires matching items, framing, grader, scaffold, sampling
budget and error handling. We do not attach unrelated leaderboard numbers to
these rows. Only the separate same-engine pilot supports a limited trained/base
contrast (Sect.~\ref{sec:paired}).

\subsection{Coverage, Scope and Evidence}
Original contracts (O) and replacement scopes (R) are different populations.
For population $N$, prepared $P$, attempted $A$ and natively graded $G$, report
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{prepared}}=P/N,\quad C_{\mathrm{attempted}}=A/N,\quad
C_{\mathrm{graded}}=G/N.
\end{equation}
Preparation is not execution; pre-grading failure is not a native verdict.
With $S$ binary successes, distinguish
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_{\mathrm{graded}}=S/G,\qquad \widehat p_{\mathrm{all}}=S/N.
\end{equation}
The second counts ungraded items as failures only when explicitly declared.
Fractional rewards, progression and clustered rubric outcomes retain their own
units. Item identities are needed to detect duplicates and wrong manifests.

Historically \emph{strictly complete} means declared coverage and closure
conditions were met; \emph{terminal-complete} includes final dispositions with native
exclusions. Partial scope is not a suite estimate. A block is an unmet execution
or access condition, not evidence of model failure. These are dated states.

A receipt binds the manifest, actor, serving configuration, grader, resource
bounds, counts and artifact hashes. Deterministic checks reconcile reported
values with retained receipts. Fail-closed gating is the intended design, not
proof that every historical launch obeyed it: missing sources and later
lead-issued overrides are explicit limitations. A receipt cannot authenticate
itself. Reopening requires the original payload, a validated grader, a
revision-bound manifest, reconciled prior items and approved resources; a
changed scaffold or population is a new scope or declared deviation.

\textbf{Completion classification.} E8-R and E10-R are the two historically
strictly complete replacements; E11-O is a strictly complete original.
E14-R is terminal-complete with native exclusions. E1-O has a full-denominator
outcome, including non-native failures. The stored check
\emph{strict-completed-suites = E8, E10, E11} asserts scope completion,
not that all three are replacements. Its 11 PASS results are preserved unchanged;
the manuscript's original/replacement interpretation is corrected.

\textbf{LAB-Bench (E8-R).} Stored outcomes give 450 correct of 1,967 across
eight categories. Diagnostic aggregates report 1,259 unparsed responses
(64.0\%) and 1,086 length finishes. Their intersection and the accuracy of
non-truncated responses cannot be reconstructed: exported grade rows lack
parsed-answer membership. The end-to-end score stays 450/1,967. The conditional
450/(1,967-1,259) = 450/708 = 63.56\% is descriptive sensitivity only, with
unverified subset membership. Length termination and extraction failure are
separate categories, not proven causes of one another.

\textbf{VerilogEval (E11-O).} Code completion gives 67/156 and
specification-to-RTL 62/156, totaling 129/312. The 156 shared designs require
cluster-aware uncertainty; the retained paired-outcome artifact supports the
reported design-cluster bootstrap interval [34.3\%,48.7\%]. Of 183 failures,
150 (48.1\% of all prompts) are extraction failures and 33 have extracted code
without a pass. The conditional 129/162 = 79.63\% does not replace 41.35\%.
No per-sample finish reasons or response text survive, so truncation, missing
closing reasoning tags and other extraction causes cannot be apportioned.
The 312,622 response tokens imply a near-cap mean of 1,002 under a 1,024-token
limit, not a count of truncated outputs. The historical extractor could accept
code inside an unterminated reasoning block; even the conditional score may
include draft code. A conditional cluster interval is unsupported without
per-design extraction membership. Excluding one non-elaborating reference
test yields 129/311, retained only as non-canonical sensitivity.

\textbf{AgentDojo and Omni-MATH.} AgentDojo v1.2.2 has 97 completed benign
episodes, 88 utility passes and no pinned upstream commit in its recovered
receipt. It measures neither attack resistance nor AgentHarm. Omni-MATH has
4,428 dispositions, 4,426 native accepted rows and 2,271 correct; two judge
outputs stopped before an equivalence verdict. The accepted-set 51.31\% differs
from all-disposition 51.29\%. The historical denominator error is disclosed,
not repaired in stored results. Reported cap-hit accuracy is 1,029/2,982
(34.5\%), versus 1,242/1,446 (85.9\%) uncapped. Difficulty confounds this split.

\textbf{SWE-bench Pro (E1-O).} The 731-task \emph{public split} retains 14
generation failures and four lost artifacts alongside 713 native evaluations.
A retained patch audit reports 593 corrupt-hunk patches rejected by ordinary
\texttt{git apply}; tests then ran on the unpatched base. Both resolved cases
are among the 120 parsing patches. The 2/731 therefore measures end-to-end
issue resolution with severe formatting failures, not repaired-patch quality.
Generations mix 476 managed-sampler and 255 merged-vLLM responses.
The full run used unmodified official scripts on native \texttt{linux/amd64}
Modal workers (harness \texttt{ca10a60a}). The 1,200-versus-120-second timeout
concern applies only to an earlier one-task gold-patch check under arm64 QEMU,
explicitly not a model score. Exact container-runtime version and the 713-run
timeout/assertion-failure breakdown are unavailable in the retained evidence;
they must not be invented or inferred from patch-parse counts.

\section{Limitations, Applications and Conclusion}
This single-actor campaign has no primary full-scale matched baseline,
training-seed variance estimate or complete overlap audit. Decoding conditions
and access-selected replacements confound capability comparisons. Missing
source and sample-level diagnostics prevent causal failure attribution.
The original sampler cannot be replayed from this package, and a reimplemented
runner cannot establish historical execution identity. Hashes do not recover
missing evidence or prove semantic correctness.

A practical review sequence is to identify the relevant contract, inspect
coverage and exclusions, check actor/grader bindings, then determine whether
the retained evidence supports the decision. This can prevent benign utility
from masquerading as security and blocked scope from masquerading as a passed
test. Better evidence retention and targeted reruns may reduce misleading
claims or wasted effort; these are proposed benefits, not measured outcomes.
Disclose scientific configuration while protecting credentials and private
operational records. No score or governance label certifies safe deployment.

The case study contributes disciplined reporting, not demonstrated GRPO gains
or universal reproducibility. Full matched baselines, item-level overlap
checks, parser/finish-reason instrumentation, controlled sampler comparisons
and independent evaluation of governance effectiveness remain future research
requirements. No new experiment is represented as completed by this revision.
