\begin{abstract}
Benchmark scores can conceal incomplete coverage, replacement scopes and missing
execution evidence. We report an experience case study of receipt-bound
reporting for one language-model actor across fourteen agentic and reasoning
lanes: 23 tracked original/replacement scopes, three historically strictly
complete. It does not test the actor's reported 40-step group-relative
post-training: with 35/40 zero-loss steps and a null pilot, lane scores are
base-model-plus-serving measurements at pilot resolution. The strictly
complete scopes are LAB-Bench public (450/1,967), AgentDojo benign utility with
no attacks run (88/97) and the original VerilogEval contract (129/312).
Omni-MATH is terminal-complete: 2,271/4,426 (51.31\%) accepted rows, versus
2,271/4,428 (51.29\%) over all dispositions. SWE-bench Pro resolves 2/731
public tasks, a two-stack generation mixture with substantial patch-format
failures. Six later replacement executions are reported separately with
deviations and attempt counts. Extraction failures and serving differences
prevent reading scores as intrinsic capability. Receipts are unsigned
operator-written JSON; eleven historical checks cover selected arithmetic and
bindings, not reproduction. A small same-engine trained/base pilot is
inconclusive for its seventeen primary outcome contrasts. Content-level
training overlap, full matched baselines and some execution records remain
unresolved. No training benefit, cross-suite aggregate or measured improvement
in reporting or governance effectiveness is claimed.
\keywords{Receipt-bound reporting \and Agentic benchmarks \and Reproducibility \and Large language models}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A benchmark score is interpretable only with its task population, actor,
serving stack, grader and missing-outcome policy. Agentic evaluations can
prepare hundreds of tasks while grading few, replace a gated benchmark with a
public proxy, or keep a number after losing its execution source. Holdout and
reproducibility problems are established concerns in language-model and agent
evaluation~\cite{liang2023helm,biderman2024lessons,kapoor2024agentsmatter};
harnesses standardize tasks and prompts, whereas our unit is a run's scope,
coverage stage and evidence state.

The actor has a reported group-relative policy-gradient (GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath})
training lineage, but this campaign neither verifies the historical objective
nor identifies its causal effect. The reporting framing was adopted after the
results were known, so its lessons are post hoc. ``Receipt-bound'' means that
each printed number carries its scope, counts and evidence tier; it asserts no
enforcement regime. Training-suite separation is not item-level
decontamination, and no held-out claim is made.

Contributions are (i) a \textbf{receipt-bound reporting structure} joining
scope, actor, serving configuration, grader, outcomes and evidence
availability; (ii) a \textbf{coverage taxonomy} separating preparation,
attempts, native grading and original versus replacement contracts; (iii) a
\textbf{dated case study} retaining errors, incomplete scopes and protocol
changes rather than pooling unlike results; and (iv) a \textbf{failure
description} separating observed formatting failures from unverified
explanations, stating where causes cannot be apportioned. This is not a new
optimizer, benchmark or validated method; no controlled study shows that the
structure reduces errors, costs or replication time.

\section{Related Work and Benchmark Identity}
Reproducibility studies~\cite{henderson2018deep,pineau2020improving} motivate
explicit execution records; model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards} and
datasheets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} organize model- and dataset-level
disclosures. The LM Evaluation Harness~\cite{biderman2024lessons} standardizes
tasks and the Agentic Benchmark Checklist~\cite{zhu2025abc} audits task and
outcome validity; this paper is an experience report applying them. Receipts
add four per-run fields, each against a failure checklists can miss: prepared/attempted/graded counts (prepared items reported as graded),
original/replacement identity with dated state (a public proxy replacing a
gated original), attempt counts with the retry rule (censored restarts) and an
evidence tier (a number outliving its execution source). Hash equality proves
byte identity, not grader correctness or the truth of a receipt.

Lane selection was purposive, not representative; access-driven replacement
adds selection bias. SWE-bench
Pro~\cite{deng2025swebenchpro}, a successor to SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench},
CORE-Bench~\cite{siegel2024corebench} and MLE-bench~\cite{chan2025mlebench}
measure different engineering outcomes; WebArena~\cite{zhou2024webarena},
LAB-Bench~\cite{laurent2024labbench} and BALROG~\cite{paglieri2025balrog}
measure web success, biology answers and game progress. AgentDojo benign
utility~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} is distinct from AgentHarm harmful-task
evaluation~\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm}. VerilogEval has two framings of
shared designs~\cite{liu2023verilogeval,pinckney2025revisiting}; Omni-MATH
measures Olympiad mathematics~\cite{gao2024omnimath}. The Tau3 banking
replacement is the $\tau^3$ banking-knowledge component of Sierra's
$\tau$-Knowledge release~\cite{shi2026tauknowledge}, not the original
$\tau$-bench. The E2, E3, E6, E7, E12 and E13 originals are identified only by
mutable websites or repositories, not archival papers; verified links and
unresolved identity details accompany the source package. Comparability
requires matching items, framing, grader, scaffold, sampling budget and error
handling, so no leaderboard numbers are attached; only the same-engine pilot
supports a limited trained/base contrast (Sect.~\ref{sec:paired}).

\textbf{Related texts.} The same ledger (actor, lanes, headline cells, check
record, pilot) appears in a companion PES--IEEE manuscript by the same authors
on training-signal diagnostics and in the authors' public thesis revision of 11
October 2026, the narrative source for most primary scores. This paper's delta
is the coverage taxonomy, dated O/R accounting and evidence tiers; shared cells
are one evidence base, not independent corroboration.

\subsection{Coverage, Scope and Evidence}
Original contracts (O) and replacement scopes (R) are different populations.
For population $N$, prepared $P$, attempted $A$ and natively graded $G$, report
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{prepared}}=P/N,\quad C_{\mathrm{attempted}}=A/N,\quad
C_{\mathrm{graded}}=G/N.
\end{equation}
Preparation is not execution; pre-grading failure is not a native verdict.
With $S$ binary successes, distinguish
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_{\mathrm{graded}}=S/G,\qquad \widehat p_{\mathrm{all}}=S/N.
\end{equation}
The second counts ungraded items as failures only when declared. Fractional
rewards, progression and clustered rubric outcomes keep their own units; item
identities detect duplicates and wrong manifests.

Historically \emph{strictly complete} means declared coverage and closure
conditions were met; \emph{terminal-complete} includes final dispositions with
native exclusions. Partial scope is not a suite estimate, and a block (an unmet
execution or access condition) is not model failure. These are dated states.

A receipt binds the manifest, actor, serving configuration, grader, resource
bounds, counts and artifact hashes. Fail-closed gating is the intended design,
not proof that every historical launch obeyed it; missing sources and
lead-issued overrides are explicit limitations. Receipts are unsigned
operator-written JSON without external timestamping and cannot authenticate
themselves; the fields above are a proposed schema, not a formal one, and the
checks in Sect.~\ref{sec:paired} re-derive the receipts' own arithmetic. This
is reporting, not demonstrated governance. Reopening requires the original
payload, a validated grader, a revision-bound manifest, reconciled prior items
and approved resources; a changed scaffold or population is a new scope.

\textbf{LAB-Bench (E8-R).} Stored outcomes give 450 correct of 1,967 across
eight categories. Diagnostic aggregates report 1,259 unparsed responses
(64.0\%) and 1,086 length finishes; their intersection cannot be reconstructed
because exported grade rows lack parsed-answer membership, and neither is a
proven cause of the other. \emph{Non-citable sensitivity, not a score:} the
conditional 450/(1,967-1,259) = 450/708 = 63.56\% has unverified membership.

\textbf{VerilogEval (E11-O).} Code completion gives 67/156 and
specification-to-RTL 62/156, totaling 129/312. The 156 shared designs require

design-cluster bootstrap interval [34.3\%,48.7\%]. Of 183 failures, 150 (48.1\%

pass. No per-sample finish reasons or response text survive, so truncation,
missing closing reasoning tags and other causes cannot be apportioned; the
mean of 1,002 response tokens under a 1,024 cap is not a truncation count. The
historical extractor could accept code inside an unterminated reasoning block.
\emph{Non-citable sensitivities, not scores:} the conditional 129/162 = 79.63\%
(which may include draft code and has no cluster interval) and 129/311
(41.48\%) after excluding one non-elaborating reference test.

\texttt{ca10a60a}); a 1,200-versus-120-second timeout concern applies only to an
earlier one-task arm64 QEMU gold-patch check, not a model score. Container
runtime version and a timeout/assertion breakdown are unavailable.

\section{Limitations, Applications and Conclusion}
This single-actor campaign has no primary full-scale matched baseline,
training-seed variance estimate or complete overlap audit. Decoding conditions
and access-selected replacements confound capability comparisons; the original
sampler cannot be replayed, and hashes recover neither missing evidence nor
semantic correctness. Before accepting retained evidence, review the contract,
coverage, exclusions and actor/grader binding: benign utility is not a security
result and a blocked scope is not a passed test. Better retention and targeted
reruns are proposed, not measured, benefits; no score or receipt label
certifies safe deployment. The contribution is disciplined reporting, not a
GRPO gain or universal reproducibility. Matched baselines, item-level overlap
checks, finish-reason instrumentation and an independent test of reporting
effectiveness remain future work.
