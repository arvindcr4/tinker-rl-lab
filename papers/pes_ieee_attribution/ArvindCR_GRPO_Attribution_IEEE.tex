\begin{abstract}
This is a measurement and audit report. It proposes no estimator and claims
no capability or training effect; its contribution is a provenance and
ledger method for GRPO-family runs, demonstrated on archived evidence. The
Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) counts prompt groups whose uniform rewards zero
the centred advantage; we treat it as bookkeeping over logged rewards, not
as a validated predictor of learning. Tensor-backed and log-only material
come from uncrossed populations and are reported separately, and no effect
is allocated to stack versus algorithm. Same-stack contrasts are
inconclusive: PPO versus GRPO over five seeds at a 0.98--1.00 ceiling gives
$\Delta=-0.002$ ($t_4=-1.0$, $p=0.374$), and four variants versus GRPO over
eight paired seeds stay below their minimum detectable effects
(0.010--0.015). The fourteen-lane ledger (E1--E14) is a governance case
study on one 40-step LoRA actor whose training rows are unrecovered and
whose small paired rerun found no difference from its base model. Three
lane scopes are strictly complete and two are qualified: Omni-MATH is
terminal-complete (2,271/4,426 judged rows, 51.31\%) and SWE-bench Pro is
full-suite with a denominator note (2/731); VerilogEval records 129/312
(41.35\%). No training benefit, aggregate score, algorithm equivalence or
stack attribution is established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) replaces the learned
reward model of RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training} with a programmatic checker.
PPO~\cite{schulman2017proximal} needs a value network;
GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1} removes it by sampling a
group of $G$ completions per prompt and standardising each reward against
the group. For group $g$ with rewards $r_1,\dots,r_G$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
A_i = \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon}, \;
\mu_g = \tfrac{1}{G}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{G} r_j, \;
\sigma_g^2 = \tfrac{1}{G}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{G} (r_j-\mu_g)^2 .
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
Implementations differ in this normalisation ($1/G$ versus $1/(G-1)$, group
versus batch standard deviation, or none).

Two consequences are invisible in standard logs. \emph{Mechanism:} if every
completion in a group receives the same reward, $r_i-\mu_g=0$ for all $i$,
so the centred reward-driven advantage term is zero although the group was
sampled and scored at full cost. This is scoped to that term: KL, entropy,
clipping or length terms, batch-level normalisation and some advantage
shapings change what a uniform group contributes (Table~\ref{tab:est}).
\emph{Provenance:} ``GRPO'' is implemented in
Tinker~\cite{thinkingmachines2024tinker}, TRL~\cite{vonwerra2022trl},
veRL~\cite{sheng2024verl}, OpenRLHF~\cite{hu2024openrlhf} and many forks,
each with its own parser, temperature, KL coefficient, clip range, LoRA
targets~\cite{hu2022lora} and evaluator, so two runs labelled GRPO can
differ in many ways that plausibly change the outcome.

This paper is a measurement/audit report from an individual M.Tech
project. Its contributions, scoped to the delivered evidence, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item an audit method: source-bound recomputation of ZVF bookkeeping from
stored reward tensors, with logged-only material kept descriptive and never
pooled with tensor-backed material, and every number bound to a repository
path (Appendix~A); the dispatch harness is infrastructure only (completed
harness runs come from Tinker and TRL; veRL/OpenRLHF entries are unexecuted
launch plans);
\item ZVF as a logging standard with its elementary tie-probability
baseline, a per-estimator scoping of what ZVF means, and an offline
arithmetic replay of an adaptive group-size trigger (no controller benefit
and no predictive validity are claimed);
\item inconclusive same-stack contrasts reported with seed-level data and
minimum detectable effects, label-collision exhibits, and an eight-item
minimum reportable stack;
\item a fourteen-lane evaluation ledger as a governance case study on one
null, partly undisclosed actor (not a verified held-out experiment), with
every lane at its terminal state under one completeness taxonomy.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

GRPO was introduced with DeepSeekMath~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} and
popularised by DeepSeek-R1~\cite{guo2025deepseekr1}. DAPO~\cite{yu2025dapo}
adds decoupled clipping and dynamic sampling that discards zero-variance
groups, GSPO~\cite{qwen2025gspo} moves the ratio to the sequence level, and
Dr.\ GRPO~\cite{liu2025understanding} removes length and standard-deviation
normalisation. RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024backtobasics} uses a leave-one-out
baseline that also vanishes on uniform binary groups. GRESO~\cite{zheng2025greso}
skips predicted zero-variance prompts before rollout, AERO~\cite{zhang2026aero}
adapts rollout allocation, and RL-ZVP~\cite{le2025rlzvp} shapes advantages on
zero-variance prompts. The tie probability $p^G+(1-p)^G$ is elementary; we use
it as a measurement baseline. Deep RL results are
sensitive to seeds and code-level
details~\cite{henderson2018deep,engstrom2020implementation};
checklists~\cite{pineau2020improving}, model cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards},
datasheets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} and evaluation
frameworks~\cite{liang2022helm,biderman2024lmeval} set reporting norms that the
ledger method extends to post-training runs.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Provenance-bound design}
A matched comparison holds model, prompts, verifier, decoding, step budget
and evaluation fixed while changing one factor. No task in this corpus is
replicated across two stacks with tensors intact (Table~\ref{tab:confound}),
so cross-bundle entries are manifest-bound case studies and no stack effect
is identified. A shared task, reward and decoding specification is
dispatched through thin adapters; only Tinker and TRL completed harness runs,
and dry-run plans are never pooled with results. Two experiments
(Section~IV-B, IV-D) instead use a self-contained minimal trainer
(Transformers on Modal). Every ZVF statement is scoped to the estimator and
runner that produced it (Table~\ref{tab:est}).

\subsection{Telemetry and the Zero-Variance Fraction}
The training loop emits per-step ZVF, entropy, completion length and KL;
tensor-instrumented runners persist per-group rewards. For a batch
$\mathcal{B}$ of prompt groups,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ZVF} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{B}}
\mathbf{1}\left[\sigma_g = 0\right],
\label{eq:zvf}
\end{equation}
recomputed exactly where tensors are stored. $\mathrm{GU}=1-\mathrm{ZVF}$ is
only a plotting axis. ZVF is unsigned and needs mean reward for
interpretation; it cannot separate saturated mastery, impossible prompts,
reward coarseness, truncation or checker failure.

\subsection{A tie-probability baseline for group size}
If a prompt with success probability $p$ yields $G$ conditionally i.i.d.\
binary rewards, its expected ZVF is $h_G(p)=p^G+(1-p)^G$: (i) U-shaped in
$p$ with minimum $2^{1-G}$ at $p=0.5$, and (ii) strictly decreasing in $G$
for $p\in(0,1)$. These are restatements of the algebra; agreement of ZVF
with $h_G(\hat p)$ computed from the same rewards is bookkeeping
consistency, not out-of-sample prediction. Prompt-difficulty mixtures raise
batch ZVF above $h_G(\bar p)$, and within-group correlation (shared prefix,
temperature, checker determinism) has the same effect; we do not estimate
either, so ZVF cannot distinguish ``too easy/hard'' from ``too correlated.''
No gradient-magnitude or learning-outcome prediction is made or tested; the
predictive validity of ZVF is unestablished.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
The seed is the unit of replication. Matched contrasts use two-sided paired
$t$-tests on per-seed differences ($\alpha=0.05$, df reported); MDEs are
for 80\% power from the noncentral $t$; the four variant contrasts of
Section~IV-D form one Benjamini--Hochberg family. Training steps are not
independent observations. Single-seed panels, logged summaries and offline
replays are descriptive only. Inconclusive tests do not establish
equivalence.

\subsection{Evaluation campaign (E1--E14): a governance case study}
The campaign demonstrates ledger mechanics on a null, partly undisclosed
actor: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with a Tinker-trained rank-32 LoRA adapter (all
linear layers, bfloat16). Its recovered record reports 40 steps at $G=4$
with 2 prompts per step (80 prompt draws) from a 512-row API-Bank/SWE-Gym
tool-use pool, learning rate $10^{-5}$, temperature 0.7 and seed 809;
reward was zero on 22 of 40 steps. The executed training code (hence its
loss form), the 80 drawn prompts and the consumed-row inventory are not
recovered, so item-level training separation is not established and the
campaign is not described as held-out. The pool composition and a
decontamination check against the lanes cannot be published from recovered
records \authortodo{supply the pool manifest and overlap check, or retain
this non-claim}. The primary training-effect statement is the small paired
trained-versus-base rerun (Section~V), which found no difference. Each lane
names one original contract; several have a declared replacement scope
(Table~\ref{tab:lanes}). Scopes are never pooled, and replacements do not
close original contracts. Timeline: original contracts and terminal states
frozen in the 19~September 2026 ledger; paired rerun 26~September; six
exploratory replacement executions under a protocol override 27~September.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Zero-variance frequency in the Phase-1 configuration}
Phase-1 is Qwen3.5-4B on GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021training} on Tinker, $G=8$, 16
prompts per step, 40 steps, one seed, with four runner labels in a
mean-centred runner without $\sigma_g$ division. The labels are internal
dispatch flags (\url{variance_mitigation_integration.py}), not the
external systems of similar names: GRPO is the baseline; AERO sets an
adaptive-group-size flag (double/halve $G$ on rolling-ZVF thresholds; not a
verified reimplementation of~\cite{zhang2026aero}); GIFT blends the centred
advantage with a group-mean ``implicit reward''; AReaL sets an
asynchronous/stale-buffer flag \authortodo{rename the internal flags or
supply archival definitions}.

The four tensor files are byte-distinct (SHA-256 in Appendix~A), use the
same prompt indices, and are four distinct runs: GRPO and AERO rewards
differ in 225 of 640 groups, their per-step uniform-group counts agree on
only 12 of 40 steps, and total rewards differ (4,271 versus 4,237). Their
\emph{aggregate} summaries nevertheless coincide exactly (mean step ZVF
0.7203; 461 uniform groups, 427 all-correct, 34 all-wrong), a coincidence
of totals, not a duplicated run. Because $G$ stayed 8 in all 40 AERO steps,
the adaptive flag never acted, so the AERO arm is a second realisation of
the baseline configuration rather than a distinct method; we therefore
draw no cross-method conclusion from this panel. Across labels, mean step
ZVF is 0.706--0.770 and all-correct groups are 0.647--0.711 of all groups,
so most uniform groups are prompts solved in the observed group (not proof
of $p=1$). Under the GIFT flag, all 455 all-correct uniform groups (of 493) carry a
constant advantage of $+0.5$ (Table~\ref{tab:est}), so ZVF is not a
zero-advantage count for that arm. A separate sampling-only cohort
(Qwen3-8B, 200 GSM8K test problems per seed, $G=8$, temperature 1.0, three
seeds, no training) sits at interior accuracy (0.67--0.71) with
stored-tensor ZVF 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155; it is a different population and
is not compared with Phase-1.

\subsection{Group size: a mechanism, not a universal optimum}
In a 12-run sweep (minimal Modal trainer, Qwen2.5-0.5B, synthetic
arithmetic, $G\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, three seeds, 40 steps) mean ZVF falls from
0.838 at $G=2$ to 0.631 at $G=16$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gu}). Post-training
accuracy is at ceiling (0.97--0.995 per seed) on problems freshly sampled
from the training distribution after training (not a disjoint split), so
the sweep establishes no accuracy optimum, and larger $G$ costs
proportionally more rollouts.

The logged \texttt{zvf-audit} project (368 W\&B runs; nine model labels;
$G\in\{4,6,8,16\}$; 2--12 steps) is a heterogeneous summary, not a cohort.
All 350 finished runs carry final reward and mean ZVF; the 18
crashed/failed runs carry neither and are excluded. In fixed 0.1-wide
final-reward bins (chosen after the fact), mean run ZVF is 0.959 for the
207 runs below 0.1 and 0.881 for the 52 runs at or above 0.9; the 10 runs
in $[0.1,0.6)$ (bin $n=2,3,1,1,3$) have bin means 0.118--0.375, and the 81
runs in $[0.6,0.9)$ (bin $n=11,26,44$) have 0.822, 0.773 and 0.812. This
is the $h_G$ bookkeeping shape (final reward near 0 or 1 mechanically
implies mostly uniform groups), not a test of (i). Monotonicity in $G$
appears for Qwen3-32B (0.33, 0.18, 0.08 at $G=4/8/16$; 2, 2, 1 runs) but
not for Qwen3-4B-Instruct (0.89, 0.84, 0.92; 51, 52, 3 runs), and model,
task, $G$ and horizon vary together. An earlier gradient-norm association
was logged without its number of observations and is withdrawn; no
correlation between ZVF and gradient magnitude is reported.

\subsection{An offline arithmetic replay of the adaptive trigger}
An earlier four-arm fixed-versus-adaptive comparison has no retained per-arm
records and is not reported. Replaying a symmetric trigger (escalate on any
uniform group) over the stored Phase-1 tensors (4 runs $\times$ 640
prompt-steps $=2{,}560$ distinct observations) fires 1,867 times; 1,723
(92.3\%) are all-correct groups and 144 all-incorrect. No controller ran and
no fresh rollouts were drawn, and plugging $\hat p\in\{0,1\}$ into $h_G$
forces zero predicted rescue by construction. The replay is arithmetic
only: no trigger is recommended or rejected without a live, compute-matched
A/B that includes a larger-$G$ baseline at equal rollout budget.

\emph{PPO versus GRPO (at ceiling).} In the minimal Modal trainer
(Qwen2.5-0.5B, arithmetic, 40 steps, five seeds) the per-seed accuracy
difference GRPO$-$PPO is $-0.002$ (95\% CI $[-0.008,+0.004]$, $t_4=-1.0$,
$p=0.374$) at a 0.98--1.00 ceiling. The stored runs drew evaluation items
from the shared RNG after training, so the two arms of a seed were scored on
different items that can overlap their training draws (the trainer's
documentation estimates about 40\% for PPO and 7\% for GRPO); they are paired
by seed only. The design could not detect a plausible algorithm effect and
is retained only to demonstrate the protocol; it is not evidence of
equivalence. An earlier Qwen3-8B PPO/GRPO comparison mixed back-ends and
treated autocorrelated steps as $n=10$; it is not used.

\emph{Four variants versus GRPO.} With TRL (Qwen3-8B, GSM8K, $G=4$, 30
steps, $\beta=0$, eight paired seeds, 500 evaluation items each) the means
are 0.633 (GRPO), 0.634 (DAPO), 0.638 (GSPO), 0.631 (Dr.\ GRPO) and 0.632
(AERO). All four contrasts are inconclusive with MDEs (0.010--0.015) at or
above the 0.010 design margin (Table~\ref{tab:variants}).

\emph{Label collisions.} Two runs labelled ``DAPO'' on different stacks
showed mean ZVF of 0.00 and 0.58; the managed run's closed internals
prevent verifying why. Nominally matched Tinker and TRL GRPO runs on
Qwen3-8B GSM8K reached last-10 training rewards of 0.856 and 0.050, but the
base checkpoint also changed (Qwen3-8B-Base versus Qwen3-8B), so this is an
under-specification exhibit, not a back-end effect. On one managed platform
four labels landed within 0.710--0.744 training reward (three seeds, 15
steps, no evaluation metric); this is not equivalence. These exhibits
motivate an eight-item minimum reportable stack: (1) loss form, (2)
reference policy and KL, (3) sampler and back-end, (4) telemetry, (5)
group-size schedule, (6) evaluation split, (7) decontamination, and (8)
pass@$k$ under a stated evaluator; a registry diff flags the ``DAPO'' pair
from manifests alone.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}

The paper's training-effect statement is the paired rerun (26 September):
the trained actor and the base model were scored on identical items under
one vLLM configuration, 5--151 items per lane, using public subsets or
declared substitutes for blocked lanes (so it does not rerun the original
contracts). No lane showed a difference (smallest McNemar $p=0.25$), and no
full-suite base comparator exists, so every E1--E14 score is a ledger entry
for a null actor at one revision. The training run had zero reward on 22 of
40 steps, and Tinker's GRPO internals are closed. Several scores carry
harness limits (150 of 312 VerilogEval responses failed answer extraction
under a 1,024-token cap with thinking enabled; per-sample causes were not
recorded), and the VerilogEval managed sampler path no longer exists, so a
rerun would change the sampler as well as the run. \emph{Length bias} is
not established: a post-hoc reward-based roster flag is not a length test,
and the only controlled comparison (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GSM8K, three
seeds) was right-censored at a 200-token cap with starting lengths near the
cap (mean length 195.0$\to$180.3 GRPO, 193.9$\to$186.8 Dr.\ GRPO; gain
difference $+0.012$, $t_2=1.61$, $p=0.25$), so it cannot test unconstrained
length inflation. Shared evidence across sibling manuscripts is one
evidence base, not independent corroboration (Appendix~A).

\section{Conclusion}
The contribution is provenance and ledger discipline, not attribution: the
uncrossed corpus cannot allocate effects to stack versus algorithm,
same-stack contrasts were inconclusive at their detectable effect sizes,
ZVF is retained as bookkeeping with unestablished predictive validity, and
the fourteen-lane ledger is a governance case study on a null actor. No
controller benefit or capability gain is established. Evidence-bound
practice rules: (1) at the dispersions observed here, eight paired seeds
leave MDEs of 0.010--0.015, so sub-point variant differences at $\le$8
seeds are inconclusive, never equivalence; (2) log per-group reward tensors
(or at least ZVF with mean reward, estimator form and $G$) so bookkeeping
can be recomputed; (3) treat larger $G$ as buying variance-bearing groups
at proportional rollout cost (ZVF 0.838$\to$0.631 for $G=2\to16$ here),
not as free accuracy; (4) hash-compare arms before reading a method panel,
since coinciding summaries can come from distinct runs and inactive flags;
(5) freeze a lane ledger with original/replacement scopes and terminal
states before scoring.
