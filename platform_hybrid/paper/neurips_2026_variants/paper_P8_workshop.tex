\section{Setup (Compact)}
\label{sec:setup}
% =============================================================================

We instrument GRPO-style training with step-level group reward statistics
across 79 runs spanning 7 RL libraries (TRL, Stable Baselines3, CleanRL,
Tianshou, PufferLib, rl\_games, d3rlpy), the closed Tinker API, and the open
Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Llama-3.x, DeepSeek-V3.1, and Nemotron-120B model families at
total-parameter scales of 0.6B--671B (active 0.6B--37B for the largest MoE
checkpoints). Headline results use Qwen3-8B with LoRA rank 32, Adam
($\beta_1{=}0.9$, $\beta_2{=}0.95$), learning rate $10^{-4}$, and 5 seeds in
$\{42,123,456,789,1024\}$. Frontier-scale and Tinker-API runs are single-seed
and reported as case studies. The full task suite, library taxonomy,
hyperparameter mapping, and per-run manifest are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{sec:run-manifest} and surrounding sections so the main text fits
the workshop page budget.

\section{Headline Result: ZVF as a Descriptive GRPO Diagnostic}
\label{sec:zvf-analysis}
% =============================================================================

\textbf{Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)} at step $t$ is the fraction of prompts
for which all $G$ completions receive identical rewards:
\[
  \mathrm{ZVF}_t = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_t}
  \mathbf{1}\bigl[\operatorname{Var}_{c \sim \mathcal{C}(p)}\,r(c) = 0\bigr].
\]
When $\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 1$, every prompt contributes zero gradient.
\textbf{Gradient Utilization} is $\mathrm{GU}_t = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}_t$.

Across $N{=}15$ experiments spanning 5 model families and 0.6B--671B total
parameters (0.6B--37B active for the largest mixture-of-experts checkpoints),
mean ZVF \emph{co-occurs} with final training performance: Pearson
$r{=}-0.769$, $p{=}0.0008$ (Spearman $\rho{=}-0.784$, $p{=}0.0005$).
\textbf{The pooled correlation is confounded by task type.} Tool-use
experiments reach $\mathrm{ZVF}=100\%$ at $\sim$0\% reward, while GSM8K
experiments average $\mathrm{ZVF}=8.5\%$ at much higher reward. Stratifying
by task collapses the apparent relationship within each subgroup; we
therefore read the pooled $r=-0.769$ as ``tool-use failed and GSM8K did not''
rather than as a clean diagnostic--performance link.
Stratifying within GSM8K ($N{=}19$ Tinker GSM8K runs), the apparent monotone
relationship does not survive: Pearson $r{=}+0.40$ ($p{=}0.09$); restricted to
the seven model-varying GSM8K runs, $r{=}+0.13$ ($p{=}0.79$). The pooled
correlation reflects task identity, not within-task ZVF dynamics.
We treat ZVF as a cheap descriptive logging quantity, not as a standalone
causal predictor.

\paragraph{Held-out negative control (workshop headline).}
On a separate five-seed Qwen3-8B GSM8K base-vs-GRPO comparison, the post-GRPO
mean is $83.3\%$ versus $82.0\%$ for the base model under the same greedy
protocol. The $+1.3$pp delta is not statistically significant
($t{=}1.32$, $p{=}0.26$). This is the workshop's clearest negative result:
strong online training reward does not by itself imply held-out gain.

\paragraph{Group size: brief.}
We ran a single-seed exploratory $G$-sweep on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K whose results
are non-monotonic across $G$; see
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:group-size-reconcile} for the token-budget
reanalysis. \textbf{We do not headline a winning $G$.} A 3-seed
token-budget reanalysis at $T{\ge}16$M tokens places a fitted apex near
$G{\approx}32$ for one stack and objective, but the apex shifts with $T$ and
does not conflict with smaller-$G$ optima under different cost objectives; we
deliberately avoid promoting a group-size law. A subsequent two-seed matched-budget
panel (2{,}560 rollouts per arm; $G{=}2\times160$ vs.\ $G{=}16\times20$
steps) adds one trajectory-level datum: the $G{=}2$ arms drive train
reward to $\approx0.9$--$1.0$ on the sampled pool, then end in a sustained
all-\emph{correct} zero-variance regime ($\mathrm{ZVF}\approx0.75$--$1.0$),
while $G{=}16$ ends mid-learning ($\approx0.3$--$0.5$) with
$\mathrm{ZVF}\le0.25$ --- small $G$ trades early
step-count for endgame signal exhaustion.

\section{Reproducibility and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% =============================================================================

We release pinned Docker images, deterministic seed management, and
\texttt{rliable}-based analysis scripts. Anonymous code, logs, and
checkpoints are at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/agentic-grpo-bench-anon-2C6B}.
The release distinguishes three artifact tiers explicitly:
(i)~\textbf{precomputed ZVF / GU traces}, released for every reported run;
(ii)~\textbf{step-level aggregate logs} (mean reward, peak reward,
last-10), released for every reported run;
(iii)~\textbf{raw per-group rollout JSONL} (per-prompt, per-completion
reward arrays), released for a documented subset of stacks, with the full
subset enumerated in the run manifest (Appendix~\ref{sec:run-manifest})
and the parser contract released with the anonymous artifact.

The release has known gaps that the workshop framing makes explicit. (i)~Most
Tinker experiments are single-seed and depend on a closed-source backend; we
report them descriptively. (ii)~Empirical evidence concentrates on
short-horizon GSM8K with binary verifiable rewards; tool-use, code, and
non-math coverage are sparser and yield negative or descriptive evidence
only. (iii)~Train-set reward in the Tinker case studies is \emph{descriptive
instrumentation}, not an inferential signal: our primary inferential claims
are limited to the mechanical ZVF~$\to$~zero-gradient theorem
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:zvf-formalization}) and the held-out 5-seed Qwen3-8B
GSM8K test-set control ($83.3\%$ vs.\ $82.0\%$, $t{=}1.32$, $p{=}0.26$).
Tinker-derived stratified correlations (e.g., the within-GSM8K ZVF
re-analysis) are reported as artifact analyses with closed-backend caveats,
not as benchmark statistics. (iv)~Cross-library comparisons are confounded by
checkpoint, hardware, and default-config differences; we report them as
stack-level observations, not as algorithm-level statements. (v)~Direct KL
tracking was blocked by a PyTorch gradient-graph error; the corrected
implementation is in the artifact but is not validated against ZVF in the
current paper. (vi)~Verifiable rewards reduce reward-model subjectivity but
do not eliminate reward hacking, format gaming, or train-set reward
inflation; we do not claim they do.
