\section{Artifact Design}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Tasks and reward functions.}
Completed manifest cells cover (i) GSM8K math reasoning with binary
verifiable reward (most cells), (ii) synthetic arithmetic (the Qwen2.5-0.5B
TRL cell and the non-LM PPO floors), and (iii) two single-seed
function-calling tool-use cells with shaped schema-validity reward. HumanEval
appears only as a failed Modal cell with no completed training step, and no
MATH-500 training cell is in the manifest. Verifiable
rewards reduce reward-model subjectivity but do \emph{not} eliminate
reward hacking, format gaming, or train-set reward inflation; we discuss
residual failure modes in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. The full task
table, per-task data splits, and data preprocessing architecture are listed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:run-manifest} and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:architecture}.

\paragraph{Cross-stack coverage.}
LM post-training cells come from two stacks: TRL (on Modal) and the
closed-source Tinker API. CleanRL, Stable~Baselines3 and Tianshou appear only
as non-LM PPO sanity floors (a non-LLM policy network on synthetic math).
PufferLib and \texttt{rl\_games} have default hyperparameter mappings
(Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}) but no runs, and veRL/OpenRLHF appear only as
parser field mappings (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:zvf-pipeline}); none of
these has a manifest cell. The per-(stack, model, task)
tier classification lives in Appendix~\ref{sec:run-manifest} and is the
single source of truth referenced throughout this paper.

\paragraph{Models and training protocol.}
Manifest cells cover 23 base LMs from six families (Qwen2.5/3/3.5,
Llama-3.x, DeepSeek-V3.1, Nemotron-3-Super, GPT-OSS, Kimi-K2) spanning 0.5B
(Qwen2.5-0.5B) to ${\approx}$1T total parameters (Kimi-K2-Thinking and
Kimi-K2.5), with 3B--37B active parameters for the MoE checkpoints. Tier A
holds the 5-seed TRL Qwen2.5-0.5B cell (Modal L4) and the 4-seed Tinker
Llama-3.3-70B-Inst cell; the 15 classic-RL PPO runs (5 seeds each) are
non-LM sanity floors outside the LM tiers. The Tinker campaign runs use LoRA
rank 32, $G{=}8$ unless swept, learning rate $10^{-5}$, 30 steps and
512-token completions; the Qwen3-8B Wave-6 sensitivity ablation is 1 seed
per cell across 12 cells (Tier B). Tier-B rows (single-seed Modal
H100 Llama-3.2-\{1,3\}B; single-seed Tinker Qwen3-8B GSM8K headline; the
single-seed frontier-model case-study runs across model scale on Tinker)
are reported descriptively. Frontier-scale Tinker runs are case studies,
not inferential evidence.

\subsection{Zero-Variance Fraction: cross-stack diagnostic}
\label{sec:zvf-analysis}

We introduce three descriptive quantities that summarise when GRPO groups
provide little within-group learning signal. \textbf{Zero-Variance
Fraction (ZVF)} at step $t$ is the fraction of prompts for which all $G$
completions receive identical rewards:
\[
  \mathrm{ZVF}_t = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_t}
  \mathbf{1}\bigl[\operatorname{Var}_{c \sim \mathcal{C}(p)}\,r(c) = 0\bigr].
\]
\textbf{Gradient Utilization} $\mathrm{GU}_t = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}_t$ is the
mixed-reward-group fraction. Under conditionally independent binary
rollouts at prompt success probability $p$, the expected homogeneous fraction
is $p^G+(1-p)^G$, not an identity for an observed finite batch. Average this
quantity over prompt-specific probabilities when prompts are heterogeneous.
Mixed coefficients do not guarantee a nonzero aggregate parameter gradient.
See Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:zvf-formalization}.

\paragraph{Pooled cross-task descriptive correlation.}
Across the historical pooled $N = 15$ cells (inclusion list not
released), mean ZVF is negatively associated with final training
performance: $r_{\text{Pearson}} = -0.769$ ($p = 0.0008$),
$\rho_{\text{Spearman}} = -0.784$ ($p = 0.0005$).
We treat this association as descriptive (B-grade), not causal: ZVF is
mechanically coupled to reward sparsity, group size, and baseline
accuracy, and the pooled $N{=}15$ sample mixes tasks.

\paragraph{Same-cohort descriptive analysis.}
The $N=19$ GSM8K cohort is a subset of the extended $N=29$ pool, not the
historical $N=15$ pool. In the extended pool, $r=-0.443$ becomes $-0.026$
with task fixed effects; the GSM8K subset has $r=+0.401$. These estimates
motivate caution about task confounding without establishing a predictive law.
A change between the historical and extended pools also changes inclusion,
so it cannot be attributed entirely to conditioning. See the stratified appendix
for the reported summaries; immutable inclusion IDs remain required for reuse.

\paragraph{Group-size sweep (B-grade case study).}
The single-seed group-size sweep on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K
($G \in \{2,4,8,16,32\}$) is non-monotone in both peak and last-10
reward; neither the smallest nor the largest $G$ is uniformly best. We
report this as a case study against the heuristic that ``larger $G$ is
always better'' rather than as a prescription. The
token-budget-normalised reconstruction in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:group-size-reconcile} uses hand-entered,
reconstructed accuracy cells, not measured runs; its per-token efficiency
proxy $\widehat{\mathrm{GE}}_{\text{tok}}$ decreases monotonically in $G$
at every budget, and the rightward shift of the reconstructed accuracy argmax
is only a hypothesis for a measured sweep; per-row
training-reward numbers are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:full-results}.
A two-seed matched-budget follow-up (2{,}560 rollouts per arm;
$G{=}2\times160$ vs.\ $G{=}16\times20$ steps, 512-token completions,
LoRA rank 4, lr $3{\times}10^{-5}$, 8 prompts/step)
upgrades one facet of the case study: the $G{=}2$ arms drive train reward
to $\approx0.9$--$1.0$ on the sampled pool and terminate in a sustained
all-correct zero-variance regime ($\mathrm{ZVF}\approx0.75$--$1.0$),
while $G{=}16$ ends mid-learning ($\approx0.3$--$0.5$) with per-step
$\mathrm{ZVF}\le0.5$ (max $0.5$ and $0.375$ for the two seeds). At
$G{=}2$ a high ZVF is largely mechanical, since $p^2+(1-p)^2\ge0.82$ for
$p\ge0.9$. Artifacts:
\texttt{tinker-runs/results/er2b\_*.json}.

The pooled $N{=}15$ scatter is not plotted because its inclusion list is not
released; Figure~\ref{fig:zvf-closed-form} (appendix) shows the per-problem
closed-form comparison and the measured small-scale ZVF-vs-$G$ sweep.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The repository provides analysis code and historical summaries. We do not
certify complete raw per-group rollout JSONL, checkpoints, pinned containers,
or step traces for every manifest row. Aggregate traces do not substitute
for raw groups. Auditability checkmarks below are historical availability
reports pending a file-level hash inventory, not verified release guarantees.
Design schematics of the data-processing, licensing, contamination and
reproduction pipelines, with the release status of each, are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:architecture}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The current release has documented gaps; full discussion (dual-use,
compute accounting, carbon estimate, data provenance, closed-backend
acknowledgement, and known methodological limits) is in
Appendix~\ref{sec:impact_standalone}. In summary: (i) all B-grade rows are
single-seed and depend on a closed-source Tinker backend; we report them
descriptively. (ii) Most historical empirical evidence is concentrated on
short-horizon GSM8K with binary verifiable rewards; tool-use,
code-generation, and non-math coverage are sparser. (iii) Inferential
eligibility is unresolved for the historical cohort. The theoretical
expectation $\mathbb E[\widehat{\mathrm{ZVF}}] = p^G + (1{-}p)^G$
assumes conditionally i.i.d.\ binary rewards at fixed $p$; it is not
an empirical inferential result. Later, separate five-seed
Qwen3-8B GSM8K evidence is future-paper evidence ($+1.3$pp; one-sample seed-level $p{=}0.26$ against
a fixed baseline, not a paired-seed or reviewed-record test). (iv) Many
cross-library comparisons are confounded by
checkpoint, hardware, or default-config differences and should be read
as stack-level rather than algorithm-level statements.

\paragraph{Scope relative to companion ZVF vehicles.}
Any ZVF identity or stratification result reported here is \emph{bench
instrumentation} cross-cited for auditability. Readers seeking the
descriptive diagnostic contribution or calibration theory should consult the
companion pillar papers; this vehicle does not re-open those claims as
independent main-track science.
