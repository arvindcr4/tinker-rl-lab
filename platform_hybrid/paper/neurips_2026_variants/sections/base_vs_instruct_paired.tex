\section{Base vs.\ Instruct Checkpoints: A Paired, Condition-Matched Audit}
\label{sec:base-instruct-paired}

\paragraph{What the reviewer flagged.}
An earlier draft juxtaposed a large positive GSM8K figure from a
\emph{base} checkpoint with a different training-reward number from an
\emph{instruction-tuned} checkpoint, making it appear that the paper was
pooling base and instruct states on one axis. The reviewer is right that
this is unacceptable. The revised paper therefore separates checkpoint
state explicitly and reports only condition-matched comparisons.

\paragraph{Which number belonged to which checkpoint.}
The previously highlighted ``$0.922$''-style figure was a
training-time reward statistic from a \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base} GRPO run;
it was \emph{not} a held-out GSM8K gain. By contrast, the ``84.4\%''
number that appeared near it was an instruct-checkpoint training reward,
again not a held-out accuracy. These quantities differ in both checkpoint
state and metric, so the apparent contradiction vanishes once the rows are
made explicit.

\paragraph{What survives the matched comparison.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The ``base vs.\ instruct'' contradiction was a reporting bug,
        not an empirical one.} Once base and instruct rows are separated,
        there is no single number that both means ``held-out accuracy'' and
        ``training reward'' at once.
  \item \textbf{The instruct inference is quarantined.}
        A stated five-pair $t=1.3234$ would have four degrees of freedom and
        two-sided $p\simeq0.256276$, not $0.1857$ (a normal-tail value).
        This arithmetic check does not establish paired observations.
        Other descendants describe a fixed-baseline one-sample test; the
        raw seed outcomes, baseline and item IDs must resolve the contract
        before either test or multiplicity-adjusted value is admitted.
  \item \textbf{The Qwen3-8B base row remains descriptive only.} Its
        held-out gain of $+0.0890$ is larger in magnitude than the instruct
        row, but with $n=1$ it cannot support a claim that base reliably
        outperforms instruct.
  \item \textbf{Other families remain incomplete.} Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-0.6B
        are marked \texttt{source-missing} in the paired-audit TSV and are
        therefore excluded from the revised narrative rather than filled with
        placeholders.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Revised claim.}
The paper now makes the narrower statement that base checkpoints may have
more headroom on the training-reward metric, but the released matched
held-out evidence does not establish a reliable base$>$instruct advantage on
GSM8K. Any summary that mixes base and instruct rows without annotation is
marked rerun required.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The paired audit is generated by
\texttt{experiments/base\_instruct\_paired.py}, which writes
\texttt{experiments/results/base\_instruct\_paired.tsv}. The script emits
\texttt{source-missing} rows rather than inventing values when an expected
artifact is absent, and the revised appendix follows that contract directly.
