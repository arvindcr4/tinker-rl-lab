\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reinforcement learning post-training now underpins much of modern
language-model reasoning and alignment~\citep{ouyang2022training,%
guo2025deepseekr1nature}, but empirical claims in this area are hard to
compare across stacks. PPO~\citep{schulman2017proximal},
GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath}, and DPO~\citep{rafailov2024direct} are
typically evaluated on different model families, with different framework
defaults, different reward functions, and different notions of success. The
field needs auditable, tier-classified artifacts more than it needs another
leaderboard.

\paragraph{Artifact-first contribution.}
\ours{} is a run collection with a manifest
(Appendix~\ref{sec:run-manifest}). Manifest rows include PPO pipeline
checks, GRPO runs, partial runs and unstarted plans; they cannot be summed
into a count of comparable GRPO experiments. Every (backend, model, task)
cell is classified into one of three evidence tiers, used consistently in
the abstract, Table~\ref{tab:claims-tier}, Table~\ref{tab:artifact-card},
the manifest, and the conclusion:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{(A)} completed LM cells with at least three seeds at a fixed
        configuration; this is a completion classification, not automatic
        inferential eligibility.
  \item \textbf{(B)} completed single- or two-run case studies (mostly on
        the closed-backend Tinker API), reported descriptively only.
  \item \textbf{(C)} failed, partial, quarantined, or never-started cells,
        enumerated in the manifest for honesty.
\end{itemize}
Mathematical statements (the ZVF expectation), the separate five-seed
held-out evaluation, and the classic-RL PPO runs on a non-LM policy (listed
as sanity floors) are kept outside the LM evidence tiers.

\paragraph{What the artifact lets reviewers do.}
A reviewer can (i) recompute every training-reward number in
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} from the named run JSON with a released script;
(ii) read off a per-bucket auditability matrix
(Table~\ref{tab:artifact-card}) that states, for each cell, whether a
reward trace, a step-level ZVF trace, and held-out evaluation exist; and
(iii) consult the claims table (Table~\ref{tab:claims-tier}) to see which
artifact each claim depends on. End-to-end re-execution is not offered as a
one-command target (Section~\ref{sec:repro-onecmd}).

\paragraph{What the evidence does and does not show.}
The ZVF expectation $\mathbb E[\mathrm{ZVF}_t]=p^{G}+(1{-}p)^{G}$ under
conditionally i.i.d.\ binary sampling characterises when binary rewards
produce zero centered reward contrast in a group-standardized runner; we use
ZVF as a descriptive diagnostic, not as a predictor of downstream
performance. The separate five-seed Qwen3-8B GSM8K held-out evaluation gives
$+1.3$pp over a single base-model run ($t(4){=}1.32$, $p{=}0.26$), which
does not establish a gain. Strong online reward curves on a closed backend
do not by themselves establish generalization. An audit of earlier drafts
found several analyses with no traceable input (pooled and stratified
ZVF--reward correlations, a token-budget group-size grid, compute and carbon
tables, and some table rows); these are withdrawn and listed in the
relevant appendices so that readers of earlier versions can see what
changed.
