\begin{abstract}
We audit whether twelve single-seed group-relative policy optimization
(GRPO)~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} post-training traces on GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k},
spanning 4B to 1T nominal total parameters, support a model-size scaling law.
They do not. Horizons are 3--30 logged steps, 5 of 12 anchors start at reward
$1.0$, and model size is perfectly aliased with the training recipe: every
anchor of at least 120B parameters uses a different learning rate, group size,
LoRA rank and horizon from every anchor of at most 32B. Saturation-curve fits are
weakly identified: with the asymptote $R_\infty$ restricted to the reward range
$[0,1]$, 8 of 12 fitted asymptotes lie on a bound. The fitted slope of $R_\infty$
per decade of $N$ has a 95\% interval of $[-0.50,+0.27]$, so the panel cannot
exclude a change of about $+0.65$ in asymptotic reward across its 2.4 decades.
This is an evidence limitation of this panel, not a demonstration of zero slope.
On a separate 40-step, five-seed GRPO audit of one small model, the same
saturation fit was identifiable on 5 of 5 traces, so horizon and ceiling, not
GRPO as such, plausibly drive the failure. Earlier permutation,
model-selection, architecture-gap and leave-one-anchor claims are not retained
as inferential conclusions; their recomputed values are reported for
traceability only.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is (and is not) claimed}

\paragraph{Claim.} The twelve documented short-horizon GSM8K anchors are
insufficient to identify a positive GRPO size trend or a universal saturation
rate. Because size and recipe are aliased (Table~\ref{tab:panel}), no
difference between the $\le$32B and $\ge$120B groups can be assigned to size.

\paragraph{Non-claims.} No multi-seed powered cross-scale significance test; no
causal architecture ranking; no claim that larger models cannot improve under
other stacks, horizons, or tasks. All anchors are single-seed (seed 42) and
descriptive by design. Predictions recorded in an internal iteration ledger are
\emph{not} an immutable external preregistration.

\paragraph{Positioning.} Pre-training scaling laws fit loss as a function of
parameters and data~\cite{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022chinchilla}. Recent RL
post-training work fits saturating curves of reward against compute or training
steps and reports predictable trajectories for stable
recipes~\cite{khatri2025artscaling,nimmaturi2025predictive}; large-scale RL with
verifiable rewards is also reported to improve reasoning~\cite{deepseek2025r1}.
This note tests a narrower question: can a saturation fit of the form used in
that line of work, applied to these short traces, identify a trend in
\emph{model size} $N$? It cannot, and this note does not contest the cited
compute- or step-axis results.

These rows do not constitute a controlled 4B--1T experiment. Remote logs confirm
only part of the panel. Weights \& Biases histories reproduce the fitted traces
exactly for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, gpt-oss-20B and Kimi-K2-Thinking. For

transcribed Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.5-27B or Qwen3-30B-A3B traces. Tinker training-run
records with matching base model and LoRA rank exist for the seven non-partial
anchors. A row-level mapping of checkpoint revisions, prompt IDs, backend
versions, decoding and stopping rules is not available. Ceiling-saturated
instruct models and 3--5-step probes are not comparable completed optimization
trajectories, so panel-wide inferential conclusions are withdrawn. The source
repository is \url{https://github.com/arvindcr4/tinker-rl-lab}; this note does
not provide a frozen archival release.

\section{Recomputed checks and withdrawn analyses}

The saturation model is $R(t)=R_0+(R_\infty-R_0)(1-e^{-\lambda t})$, fitted by
bounded least squares per trace. The historical fit
(\nolinkurl{platform_modal/scripts/scaling_law_iter109.py}) used
$R_0\in[-1.5,1.5]$, $R_\infty\in[0,1.5]$, $\lambda\in[10^{-4},50]$. Those bounds
admit asymptotes above the reward cap of 1, and three fits (Qwen3-8B,
Qwen3-30B-A3B, Nemotron) returned $\hat R_\infty=1.5$. We refit with
$R_0,R_\infty\in[0,1]$. Slopes are ordinary least squares on $\log_{10}N$.
Two-sided permutation $p$-values use $10^4$ permutations. The minimum detectable
slope (MDS) is the slope detectable with 80\% power at $\alpha=0.05$,
$(t_{0.975}+t_{0.80})\,\mathrm{SE}$. Table~\ref{tab:neg} reports the results.

\section{Interpretation}

Plateau, drift, and collapse are descriptive trajectory labels, not an
established stack-conditioned taxonomy. Recipe, horizon, prompt difficulty,
checkpoint capability and size co-vary. This audit therefore supports neither
a universal positive scaling law nor its universal negation. Here
\emph{identifiability} means that the per-trace parameters $(R_\infty,\lambda)$
cannot be estimated away from their bounds from the available data. For
practitioners, a size-trend claim needs, at minimum, a shared recipe across
sizes, horizons long enough for interior fits (40 steps sufficed in the
small-model audit above), anchors with headroom below the reward ceiling, and
repeated seeds. No standalone scientific novelty for these generic
identifiability limitations is claimed.

\section{Document scope}
This correction note withdraws overstatements in the historical scaling audit.
It does not certify the longer draft or replace missing panel provenance.
