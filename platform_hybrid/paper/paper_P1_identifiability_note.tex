\begin{abstract}
We audit whether a smooth size-scaling law is identifiable for group-relative
policy optimization (GRPO) post-training on a multi-family GSM8K anchor panel
($0.6$B--$1$T, managed Tinker runs, mostly single-seed). The answer is negative
and recomputable offline: the cross-scale slope of mean training reward is
consistent with zero under permutation tests; saturation-rate $\lambda$ is
pinned at the fit boundary on four of five frontier traces; a constant model
wins AICc on every anchor family tested in the elevation suite; and the sole
dramatic outlier (Nemotron-120B) is a collapse phase (zero-reward step fraction
$0.55$, $5$ of $20$ steps above reward $0.25$), not a point on a smooth curve.
An earlier MoE-vs-dense positive gap ($+0.338$, one-sided $p{=}0.023$) is
\textbf{withdrawn}: Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B is LatentMoE, so the 6-vs-6 split
mislabelled the collapse anchor. We report a stack-conditioned taxonomy of
ceilings, not a Chinchilla-style law in $N$.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is (and is not) claimed}

\paragraph{Claim.} In this anchor set, model size alone does not identify a
positive GRPO reward trend or a universal saturation exponent.

\paragraph{Non-claims.} No multi-seed powered cross-scale significance test; no
causal architecture ranking; no claim that larger models cannot improve under
other stacks, horizons, or tasks. Frontier anchors are single-seed and
descriptive by design. Predictions recorded in an internal iteration ledger are
\emph{not} an immutable external preregistration.

\section{Panel}

Managed/open GRPO post-training traces on GSM8K spanning dense and MoE families
up to Kimi-K2 ($1$T) and DeepSeek-V3.1 ($685$B class), plus Nemotron-120B
(LatentMoE: $120$B total / $12$B active). Source traces live under
\texttt{platform\_hybrid/experiments/} (scaling-law TSV suite and
\texttt{tinker-runs/results/frontier\_gsm8k\_*.json}).

\section{Reading}

The defensible object is a \emph{local, stack-conditioned taxonomy}: plateau /
drift / collapse endpoints co-move with recipe, horizon, and architecture, so
cross-scale differences cannot be assigned to size. That is an identifiability
failure in \emph{these} anchors, not a universal ban on post-training scaling
laws. Related public scaling-law work occupies the broad positive claim space;
this note's contribution is the negative audit with withdrawn false positives.
