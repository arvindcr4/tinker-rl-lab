\subsection{Frontier-Model Cross-Examination: Group Size and \texorpdfstring{GRPO$\leftrightarrow$DPO}{GRPO <-> DPO}}
\label{sec:frontier-group-size}

To stress-test the group-size analysis beyond our own measurements, we
submitted the Pillar~3 construction to an external cross-examination of
two frontier reasoning systems (ChatGPT Pro Extended and Gemini Deep
Think). The exchange is a source of \emph{proposed theorems and
falsifiable tests}, not additional experiments; we report the sharpest
contributions and, where they bear on our evidence---the measured
$100.3\%$ retention of $G{=}2$ vs $G{=}16$ on the near-ceiling
arithmetic task, the reconstructed $0.24$ absolute $G{=}4\!\to\!G{=}32$ swing at
canonical scale, and the sub-$\sqrt{G}$ SNR
(Table~\ref{tab:groupsize-iter27-synthesis})---we say so explicitly.

\paragraph{A proposed contrast-projection theorem: GRPO as an endogenous all-pairs DPO (frontier synthesis).}
Writing $s_i = \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_i \mid x)$ for the
per-rollout score and $r_i$ for the reward, the frontier synthesis
derives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i}(r_i - \bar r)\, s_i \;=\; \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i,j}(r_i - r_j)\, s_i,
  \label{eq:fs-contrast}
\end{equation}
so the mean-centred GRPO reward-contrast term has an all-pairs preference
(DPO) form in which every ordered pair contributes its reward margin
$r_i-r_j$ (folding over unordered pairs,
$\sum_{i,j}(r_i-r_j)s_i=\sum_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)(s_i-s_j)$). Two consequences follow. First, any group with zero reward
variance ($r_i \equiv r_j$) is projected out of that centered term; this
does not assert a zero total gradient. This is the algebraic origin of the zero-variance floor
$\mathrm{ZVF}(p,G)=p^G+(1-p)^G$ used throughout
Section~\ref{sec:group-size}. Second, unlike DPO, GRPO supplies its own
margins: it self-induces a preference signal \emph{only} on
mixed-reward groups. For binary rewards with $K$ successes
($W$, $|W|{=}K$) and $G-K$ failures ($L$), define the
winner--loser pair sum
$S_\mathrm{pair}(x)=\sum_{i\in W,\,j\in L}(s_i-s_j)$ over the
$K(G-K)$ pairs, with mean pair vector
$\bar S_\mathrm{pair}=S_\mathrm{pair}/[K(G-K)]$. The unwhitened
centred term is then exactly
$\sum_i(r_i-\bar r)s_i=\tfrac1G S_\mathrm{pair}
=\tfrac{K(G-K)}{G}\,\bar S_\mathrm{pair}$, i.e.\ with the usual
outer $1/G$ loss normalisation the per-prompt gradient is
$\tfrac{K(G-K)}{G^2}\,\bar S_\mathrm{pair}$. Under population-std
whitening ($\sigma_r=\sqrt{K(G-K)}/G$ for binary rewards),
$\tfrac1G\sum_i\hat A_i s_i
=S_\mathrm{pair}/[G\sqrt{K(G-K)}]
=\tfrac{\sqrt{K(G-K)}}{G}\,\bar S_\mathrm{pair}$: whitening
replaces the pair-count coefficient $K(G-K)/G^2$ by its square
root $\sqrt{K(G-K)}/G$. In an external
cross-examination the models argued this is precisely why our measured
$G{=}2$ run retains $100.3\%$ of $G{=}16$ on the near-ceiling task: at
$p\!\to\!1$ almost every non-degenerate group already yields one usable
win--loss pair, so extra rollouts buy only redundant pairs. We note that these
identities are standard U-statistic and leave-one-out algebra
\citep{ahmadian2024backtobasics}; this section is a synthesis of
known results via the frontier exchange, not a claim of new
theorems.

\paragraph{Whitening turns the baseline into an adaptive-margin auto-curriculum.}
Gemini Deep Think separates GRPO's centering from its $1/\sigma$
whitening. Centering alone is a scaled leave-one-out (LOO) control
variate, $r_i - \bar r = \tfrac{G-1}{G}\,(r_i - b_{-i})$ with
$b_{-i}=\tfrac{1}{G-1}\sum_{j\ne i} r_j$, which is unbiased but injects
an $O(1/G)$ baseline-estimation penalty
$\mathrm{Var}(b_{-i}) = p(x)(1-p(x))/(G-1)$, with $p(x)$ the
prompt's success probability. Dividing by the reward
standard deviation then \emph{breaks} control-variate neutrality
(cf.\ the whitening-bias analyses of \citep{tong2025drgrpo,vojnovic2025grpo}):
for a group with $K$ successes, and the \emph{population} standard
deviation $\sigma_r^2=\tfrac1G\sum_i(r_i-\bar r)^2$ (a
Bessel-corrected sample standard deviation would rescale every
whitened advantage below by $\sqrt{(G-1)/G}$), the whitened
advantages collapse to the deterministic DPO-style scalars
$A_+ = \sqrt{(G-K)/K}$ and $A_- = -\sqrt{K/(G-K)}$, with adaptive
margin
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_\text{margin} = A_+ - A_- = \frac{G}{\sqrt{K(G-K)}}.
  \label{eq:fs-margin}
\end{equation}
The frontier reading is that GRPO is a self-scheduling curriculum:
balanced groups ($K{=}G/2 \Rightarrow \Delta{=}2$) are gently throttled
while rare breakthroughs ($K{=}1 \Rightarrow \Delta \approx \sqrt{G}$)
receive amplified gradient. This rationalizes our measured
sub-$\sqrt{G}$ SNR ($1.46 \to 2.16$ from $G{=}2$ to $G{=}16$, only
$52\%$ of the $\sqrt{G}$ law, Table~\ref{tab:groupsize-iter27-synthesis}): the
$O(1/G)$ LOO penalty and the whitening non-linearity together cap
variance reduction well below the i.i.d.\ $\sqrt{G}$ ideal (frontier
synthesis).

\paragraph{Signal yield $Y_G(p)$ and why ``bigger $G$'' is not monotone.}
The frontier models formalize the useful-signal rate
\begin{equation}
  Y_G(p) = 1 - p^G - (1-p)^G,
  \label{eq:fs-yield}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{identical} to our gradient-utilization closed form
$\mathrm{GU}(p,G) = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}(p,G)$: their ``contrastive yield''
and our ``gradient utilization'' are the same object reached from the
preference and the variance side respectively. Per rollout,
however, the yield $Y_G(p)/G$ has no interior optimum:
$Y_2/2=Y_3/3=p(1-p)$, and $Y_G/G$ strictly decreases for $G\ge3$
(by strict concavity of $Y_G$ in $G$), so
$\arg\max_{G\ge2}Y_G(p)/G=\{2,3\}$ for \emph{every} $p$.
Per-rollout yield alone therefore always picks the minimal group
size---mirroring the monotone per-token gradient efficiency
$\mathrm{GE}_{\mathrm{tok}}$---and cannot by itself produce a
budget-dependent interior optimum. Such an optimum requires an
explicit trade-off: per-step signal-to-noise (improving, but
sub-$\sqrt{G}$, in $G$) against the number of optimizer steps a
fixed token budget $T$ affords,
$n_\mathrm{step}(G)=T/(G\bar L)$ with mean completion length
$\bar L$, or a hard constraint $\mathrm{ZVF}(p,G)\le\epsilon$.
Under the latter, with $p_{\min}=\min(p,1-p)$, the bound
$G\gtrsim\log\epsilon/\log(1-p_{\min})$ (e.g.\
$p_{\min}{=}0.1\Rightarrow G\approx29$ for $\mathrm{ZVF}<0.05$)
gives the smallest $G$ that unlocks near-degenerate prompts.
Whether that unlocking, rather than variance reduction, drives
the measured $G{=}4\!\to\!G{=}32$ swing at canonical scale
(Table~\ref{tab:groupsize-g4-g32}) is exactly what the
$2\times2$ test below is designed to decide.

\paragraph{A decisive test separating variance-reduction from ZVF-reduction.}
Our data show a large held-out swing with $G$ but cannot, on their own,
say \emph{why}. The frontier synthesis proposes a $2{\times}2$
super-group design that separates the two candidate mechanisms.
\emph{Sampling protocol (a proposal; not run):} every arm first
draws a super-group of $G_\mathrm{large}$ rollouts per prompt, and
token cost is counted on \emph{all generated} rollouts, including
ones an arm discards---we state this accounting choice explicitly,
since counting only used rollouts would flatter the subsampling
arms. Arm~A uses only the first $G_\mathrm{small}$ rollouts of
each super-group (natural small $G$). Arm~B (variance-only)
restricts to prompts already unlocked at $G_\mathrm{small}$
(mixed-reward within the first $G_\mathrm{small}$ rollouts) but
uses all $K(G_\mathrm{large}{-}K)$ pairs in the full super-group.
Arm~C (ZVF-only) uses every prompt unlocked at
$G_\mathrm{large}$ but only one random win--loss pair per prompt
(a subsampled gradient), with the optimizer-step count matched
to the other arms by prompt replication. Arm~D uses the full
super-group (natural large $G$). With a target swing of
$\Delta_{32-2}\approx 0.020$, the preregistered rule is
\emph{ZVF-dominant} if Arm~C recovers $\ge 75\%$ of the gain while
Arm~B recovers $\le 25\%$, and variance-dominant if the pattern
reverses. A complementary single-batch \emph{residual-isomorphism}
check subtracts a synthetic margin-scaled DPO gradient
$V_\text{mDPO}=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)(s_i-s_j)$ from the
empirical GRPO gradient and tests whether the residual is pure
KL-penalty. For the check to be informative, $V_\text{mDPO}$ must
be built with the \emph{same} per-prompt $1/\sigma_r(x)$ whitening
and the same length normalisers as $V_\text{GRPO}$; otherwise the
residual is trivially nonzero from rescaling alone, and the test
measures bookkeeping rather than structure. With that matching in
place, $\cos(V_\text{GRPO}-V_\text{mDPO},\,V_\text{KL})\approx1$
would be consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:fs-contrast} exhausting
the non-KL update, whereas a value well below $1$ would suggest
the group baseline carries structure beyond pairwise preference.
On the mechanism, the frontier models lean toward ZVF-reduction:
Gemini conjectured that the $G{=}4\!\to\!G{=}32$ retention gap is
concentrated in high-accuracy bins, quoting $+0.253$ at
$p\ge0.75$ versus $+0.010$ at $p<0.5$; we stress that these two
numbers are an \emph{unmeasured external-model hypothesis}---no
run of ours produced them---offered as the predicted signature of
escaping the ZVF floor rather than of classical $\sqrt{G}$
variance reduction, to be tested rather than cited as evidence. This
\emph{challenges} the variance-reduction reading we offer for the
$G{=}32$ reconstruction in Section~\ref{sec:group-size} and is a concrete,
falsifiable next experiment.

\paragraph{The Baseline-Equivalence-Index (BEI).}
Finally, the frontier synthesis proposes a Baseline-Equivalence-Index
to pin \emph{when} the GRPO group mean, a PPO value head, and a DPO
margin are interchangeable. Rather than compare baseline mean-squared
errors it compares the resulting actor \emph{updates} $g_G$ (group
baseline) and $g_V$ (value head),
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BEI}_G =
  \frac{E_x\langle g_V, g_G\rangle}
       {\sqrt{E_x\|g_V\|^2\; E_x\|g_G\|^2}}
  \cdot
  \min\!\Big(\tfrac{E_x\|g_G\|}{E_x\|g_V\|},\,\tfrac{E_x\|g_V\|}{E_x\|g_G\|}\Big),
  \label{eq:fs-bei}
\end{equation}
so that $\mathrm{BEI}\approx 1$ requires the two updates to match in
\emph{both} direction and scale---a value head can match direction
while silently rescaling the effective learning rate, which a cosine
alone would miss. The proposed boundary is exchangeable above
$\approx 0.95$, distinct estimators below $\approx 0.8$ or on a
projection sign flip; equivalently, the group mean overtakes a trained
critic once its $O(1/G)$ Monte-Carlo variance drops below the critic's
mean-squared error. Because our measured SNR rises only sublinearly
($52\%$ of $\sqrt{G}$), the frontier prediction is that BEI should climb
sublinearly and \emph{saturate before} critic-free GRPO becomes fully
value-head-equivalent at the group sizes we test---i.e.\ $G{=}16$ is not
yet in the PPO-equivalent regime. At the opposite end the same account
recovers the GRPO$\leftrightarrow$DPO bridge up to a stated scalar:
at $G{=}2$ a mixed group is a single win--loss pair, on which the
\emph{unwhitened} centred update assigns $\pm\tfrac12$,
population-std whitening assigns $\pm1$, and Bessel-corrected
whitening $\pm1/\sqrt2$---a one-pair DPO step under any of these
conventions, with the step size set by the convention chosen.

\paragraph{Bottom line (frontier synthesis).}
Taken together, the external analysis recasts the group-size knob as a
preference-learning dial: $G$ controls how many endogenous DPO pairs a
prompt contributes (Eq.~\ref{eq:fs-contrast}), the whitened margin turns
that count into an auto-curriculum (Eq.~\ref{eq:fs-margin}), and the
useful fraction $Y_G(p)=\mathrm{GU}(p,G)$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fs-yield})
shows per-rollout yield alone favours minimal $G$, so any interior
compute optimum must come from trading per-step SNR against step
count at a fixed token budget. This is consistent
with our measured near-ceiling equivalence at small $G$ and with the
failure of that equivalence at canonical scale, while sharpening the one
question our data leave open---whether the large-$G$ win is
variance-reduction or ZVF-escape---into a preregistered, falsifiable
test. We stress again that these are external reasoning contributions,
not experiments this paper ran.
