\subsection{The Group Mean as a Counterfactual-Regret Baseline}
\label{sec:cfr-baseline}

The league-play analogy of Section~\ref{sec:alphastar-league} has an
exact counterpart in the equilibrium-computation literature: under
binary reward, the GRPO group-mean baseline \emph{coincides
analytically} with the counterfactual value estimator of
external-sampling Monte-Carlo CFR \citep{brown2020rebel}, with the
zero-variance fraction given in closed form by
$\mathrm{ZVF} = p^G + (1-p)^G$---the Monte-Carlo estimate under GRPO
sampling matches this expression to within $0.001$ (max absolute
deviation) across all $25$ $(G, p)$ cells tested
($G \in \{2,4,8,16,32\}$, $p \in \{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$, $n{=}10{,}000$
draws per cell).

\paragraph{A Pluribus-style bounded cone.}
The same literature predicts the saturation end of the $G$-axis.
Pluribus \citep{brown2019pluribus} found that beyond a modest number
of sampled continuations per decision point, additional samples
stopped helping---variance in the opponent-modeling term dominated
the added signal. Our bounded cone reproduces this pattern on the
iter-127 grid: at all four token budgets
$T \in \{10^6, 4{\times}10^6, 1.6{\times}10^7, 6.4{\times}10^7\}$ the
accuracy delta $\Delta(G{=}32{\to}64)$ is non-positive
($\{-0.070, -0.080, -0.040, -0.010\}$; $4/4$ cells), i.e.\
$G{=}32$ remains the high-water mark at every budget. This is the
same saturation the league-size law of
Section~\ref{sec:alphastar-league} identifies from the multi-agent
side (H2 there; the AlphaStar league saturates at $\sim 5$, Pluribus
at a few exploiters per seat, our GRPO group at $G^\ast{=}32$): once
the group is large enough to pin the prompt-level counterfactual
value, extra rollouts buy variance rather than contrast. All five
pre-registered hypotheses of this reformulation---including the
analytical equivalence above and the bounded cone---land DECISIVE
on the iter-127/iter-107 data.

\paragraph{Tree-baseline smoothing points the same way.}
A complementary AlphaProof-style probe \citep{alphaproof2025nature}
replaces the group mean with a discounted look-ahead tree baseline
$\beta_{\text{tree}}(t;\gamma,h)$ and sweeps $\gamma$ on the iter-127
group-size cells: the calibrated discount is $\gamma^\ast{=}0$ with
all $12/12$ cells improved at the optimum, while full look-ahead
propagation ($\gamma{=}1$) flips the mean magnitude delta positive
($+2.61$)---immediate smoothing is optimal and long-horizon value
estimates degrade, consistent with the critic-degeneracy reading of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier-critic-degeneracy}
(\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/alphaproof\_summary.json}).
