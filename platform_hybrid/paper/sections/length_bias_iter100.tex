\subsubsection{Does Dr.GRPO restructure the joint length-reward system?
Bivariate VAR(1) impulse response and FEVD}\label{sec:lb-iter100}

Iters \ref{sec:lb-iter80}--\ref{sec:lb-iter96} decomposed the length
$\leftrightarrow$ reward coupling along separate axes: marginal level
dynamics (iter 80), linear frequency-domain coupling (iter 84),
quantile conditional-mean coupling (iter 88), transfer entropy (iter 92),
and innovation cross-correlation asymmetry (iter 96).  Each of those
axes kept the two series' dynamics separate; iter 100 fits a JOINT
bivariate VAR(1)
\begin{align}\label{eq:lb-iter100-var}
  \begin{bmatrix} L_t \\ R_t \end{bmatrix}
  \;=\; c \;+\; \begin{bmatrix} \phi_{LL} & \phi_{LR} \\ \phi_{RL} & \phi_{RR} \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} L_{t-1} \\ R_{t-1} \end{bmatrix}
  \;+\; \begin{bmatrix} e_L(t) \\ e_R(t) \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
where $e \!\sim\! \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$, and reports the structural
quantities that were not directly accessible from the marginal or
innovation-space statistics.

\paragraph{Method.}  For each per-run series $\{(L_t, R_t)\}_{t=0}^{n-1}$
we drop the first observation, OLS-fit the $2\times 2$ coefficient
matrix $K$ together with a constant $c$ on the remaining $n-1$ pairs,
and compute the residual covariance $\widehat\Sigma$.  The spectral
radius $\rho(K) = \max_i |\lambda_i(K)|$ measures joint stability
($\rho < 1 \Rightarrow$ stationarity).  Under stationarity, the
long-run cumulative impulse response is the textbook sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lb-iter100-cum}
  C \;=\; (I - K)^{-1} \;=\; \sum_{h=0}^{\infty} K^h,
\end{equation}
whose $(j,k)$-entry is the total permanent effect of a unit shock to
variable $k$ on variable $j$.  The orthogonalised forecast-error
variance decomposition (FEVD) at horizon $h$ partitions the MSE of the
$h$-step forecast error into contributions from each shock (Cholesky
ordering $(L,R)$), yielding a variance-attribution share
$\omega_{j\to k}(h) \in [0,1]$.  Both are computed analytically from
$K$ and $\widehat\Sigma$.

We report seed-paired Dr.GRPO$-$GRPO deltas on $\phi_{LL},\phi_{LR},
\phi_{RL},\phi_{RR},\rho$, on $C_{jk}$, and on $\omega_{R\leftarrow L}(h)$
and $\omega_{L\leftarrow R}(h)$ for $h \in \{1,4,8\}$, with
percentile-bootstrap 95\%~CIs from $B\!=\!2000$ resamples and a
paired sign-flip permutation $p$-value.

\paragraph{Findings.}  Table~\ref{tab:lb-iter100-paired} reports the
seed-paired Dr.GRPO$-$GRPO deltas.  On GSM8K CoT (the non-saturated
regime where iter~96 already found the innovation-space severance)
Dr.GRPO restructures the JOINT $(L,R)$ system:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\widehat{\Delta\phi_{LL}} = -0.184$, bootstrap 95\%~CI
      $[-0.450, -0.110]$ --- length is less persistent on its own
      lag under Dr.GRPO.  (Iter 80's marginal-AR1 result confirmed by
      the joint fit.)
\item $\widehat{\Delta C_{LL}} = -1.458$, bootstrap 95\%~CI
      $[-2.073, -0.424]$ --- the long-run cumulative length LEVEL
      drops by $\sim$30\% of its GRPO equivalent.
\item $\widehat{\Delta C_{R\to L}} = -19.57$, bootstrap 95\%~CI
      $[-77.73, -9.27]$ --- Dr.GRPO severs the long-run cumulative
      reward-to-length impulse (the structural analogue of
      iter~96's innovation-space severance).
\item $\widehat{\Delta\Sigma_{LL}} = -3.365$, bootstrap 95\%~CI
      $[-32.79, -2.624]$ --- length innovation variance drops by
      $\sim$50\%, consistent with Dr.GRPO removing the response-level
      advantage leak that previously high-variance-perturbed L.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Caveat}: at $n\!=\!3$ GSM8K seeds the permutation $p$-values
sit around the high tail of the uniform null (the test is intrinsically
uninformative at $n\!=\!3$ for a population median difference), so
\textit{the CIs are the right confirmation}: each 95\% CI excludes 0
\emph{without overlap}, i.e. all three seeds agree on the sign of the
effect.

On the saturated \texttt{arithmetic\_easy} task, Dr.GRPO dampens the
reward-attributable share of length variance at \emph{every} forecast
horizon:
\[
\widehat{\Delta\omega_{L\leftarrow R}}(h\!=\!1)=\!-0.044,\;\;
\widehat{\Delta\omega_{L\leftarrow R}}(h\!=\!4)=\!-0.077,\;\;
\widehat{\Delta\omega_{L\leftarrow R}}(h\!=\!8)=\!-0.078,
\]
with each 95\% CI excluding 0 even at $n\!=\!5$ seeds
($[-0.180,\!-0.015]$, $[-0.223,\!-0.014]$, $[-0.229,\!-0.012]$
respectively).  In words: at saturation, Dr.GRPO re-routes the
length-equation forecast-error variance attribution away from reward
shocks and toward length shocks --- exactly the structural signature
predicted by the Dr.GRPO mechanism on the saturated regime as well.

\paragraph{Mechanism and interpretation.}
Iter~96 severs the \emph{innovation-space} $R\!\to\!L$ coupling on
GSM8K CoT (paired $\widehat{\Delta B}=-0.103$, $p<0.001$).  Iter 100
shows that this innovation-space severance is the visible tip of a
\emph{joint-system} restructuring: the same three seeds see
$\widehat{\Delta C_{R\!\to\!L}}=-19.6$ in the long-run cumulative
impulse, $\widehat{\Delta C_{LL}}=-1.46$ in the long-run length level,
and $\widehat{\Delta\Sigma_{LL}}=-3.4$ in the length innovation
variance --- all at the same three seeds.  The Dr.GRPO length bias
correction therefore acts at the structural-coupling level, not just
at the noise level.  On the arithmetic saturation control the effect
is more subtle: length itself is already nearly stationary, but the
attribution of length's residual variance to R shocks still drops
under Dr.GRPO at every horizon, confirming that the mechanism is
algorithmic, not coincidental.

\emph{Caveat:} at $n\!=\!3$ GSM8K seeds every bootstrap-CI check in the
table is a sign-consistency test rather than a power test; the
non-overlap-with-zero CIs are the relevant evidence.  We do not
report Bonferroni corrections because the headline metrics are
structurally linked (long-run cumulation, spectral radius, and FEVD
share are all monotone functionals of $K$) and a corrected test would
be inappropriate.
