\subsection{Severship-reward coupling test}
\label{sec:length-bias-iter112}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Iter~\ref{sec:length-bias-iter108} established that on GSM8K CoT,
Dr.GRPO's severship of the backward innovation coupling
$|CCF(e_L,e_R;k<0)|$ grows monotonically across training progress
(Spearman $\rho(\Delta,w)=-0.80$, late-window
$\Delta=-0.44$).  Operationally, however, a *mechanism* is not a
*guarantee*: an aggressive severship could in principle be wasted if
the severed channel carried little reward information, or amplified
into runaway reward-hacking if it carried too much.  Iter~112 tests
the *operational link* between the per-window severship magnitude and
the per-window mean-reward delta on the same 8 paired-runs used by
iter108 (5 arithmetic\_easy seeds, 3 GSM8K CoT seeds).

\paragraph{Method.}
For each run we (i) slice the 40-step $R_t$ series into $n_w=4$
equal-length progress windows and compute
$R_w = \overline{R_t}$ over each window, mirroring iter108's window
definition; (ii) read the iter108 per-run progress TSV to obtain
$\mathrm{CCF}_{bwd}$ per (task, algo, seed, window); (iii) compute the
paired Dr.GR$-$GR delta in *both* variables per (task, window, seed):
$\delta\mathrm{bwd} = \Delta\mathrm{CCF}_{bwd}$,
$\delta R = \overline{R_w}^{\,\mathrm{Dr}}-\overline{R_w}^{\,\mathrm{GR}}$.
We define the *severship intensity* as $-\delta\mathrm{bwd}$ (positive
when Dr.GRPO severs more than GRPO) and the *reward delta* as
$\delta R$ (positive when Dr.GRPO improves more than GRPO).

We then ask three questions.  \textbf{(Q1, pooled Spearman).}  For
each task, pool (seed, window) points and report Spearman(sever,
$\delta R$) with the two-sided $p$-value from a
$10^5$-permutation null on $\rho$.  \textbf{(Q2, cumulative).}
For $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ take the per-seed mean severship across
windows $\{0,\ldots,k\!-\!1\}$ and its accumulated reward delta,
and report Spearman(sever$_{(0:k)}$, $\delta R_{(0:k)}$); the question
is whether the severship *compounds* into reward as windows
accumulate.  \textbf{(Q3, per-window)}: report the per-window
Spearman on its own to make the temporal evolution explicit.

\paragraph{Headline findings.}
The operational link between severship and reward is *weak and
negative* at the per-window level on both tasks:
$\rho_{\mathrm{arith}}=-0.37$ ($p_{\mathrm{param}}{=}0.11$,
$p_{\mathrm{perm}}{=}0.11$, $n{=}20$),
$\rho_{\mathrm{gsm8k}}=-0.16$ ($p_{\mathrm{param}}{=}0.62$,
$p_{\mathrm{perm}}{=}0.62$, $n{=}12$).  Consensus across both tasks
($n{=}32$) is $\rho=-0.21,\;p=0.24$, with bootstrap 95\% CI
$[-0.51,+0.10]$ on arithmetic\_easy and $[-0.78,+0.60]$ on GSM8K
(Table~\ref{tab:length-bias-iter112-pooled}).  The naive hypothesis
(severship magnitude is monotonically converted to per-window reward
gain) is therefore \emph{falsified} at the per-window resolution on
both tasks.  Per-window Spearman is volatile
(Fig.~\ref{fig:length-bias-iter112-sever}C): arithmetic\_easy
$w{=}1$ has $\rho=-0.98$ ($p{=}0.005$, $n{=}5$) but other windows
straddle zero; GSM8K $w{=}0$ and $w{=}3$ show $\rho=\pm 1.00$ on
$n{=}3$ seeds, a statistical artefact of zero residual ranks.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Three observations sharpen the operational reading:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Severship is a structural reallocator, not a per-window
reward amplifier.}  iter108 showed Dr.GRPO redirects the backward
coupling away from both the high-$R$ and the high-$L$ regimes; iter112
shows this redirection does not produce a per-window reward boost on
either task.  The right operational model is therefore: \emph{Dr.GRPO
gradually severs a misleading channel; the severed channel is being
used by GRPO as a low-quality reward proxy, so removing it grants
neither an immediate reward gain \emph{nor} an immediate loss but
reduces the variance / herding pressure on the reward signal across
training}; the longer-horizon heldout accuracy gain comes from the
\emph{cumulative} effect of this de-herding, not from a per-window
boost.

\item \emph{The signal is asymmetric across tasks.}
On GSM8K CoT the cumulative-window regression (Fig.~D) has
$\rho(\mathrm{sever}_{\le 3},\,\delta R_{\le 3})=+1.00$ ($n{=}3$
seeds, $p_{\mathrm{param}}{=}0$ -- $n{=}3$ rank-resolution
artefact), but the regression at $k{=}4$ drops to $\rho=+0.50$
($p{=}0.67$).  On arithmetic\_easy the cumulative regressor hovers
near zero at every $k$.  This asymmetry is consistent with iter108's
finding that the severship grows on the harder GSM8K task but not on
the easier one: the severship mechanism only fires when there is a
substantial spurious channel to remove.

\item \emph{The severship does \emph{not} over-react into runaway reward
hacking.}  A remaining concern with a mechanism that severs a major
feedback channel is that it might rewire the policy onto a degenerate
outcome.  The weak negative $\rho$ ($-0.21$, $95\%$ CI
$[-0.51,+0.10]$) rules out \emph{strong positive} coupling
(severship $\to$ reward gain per window) on the data; equally, the
weak sign fails to reject the null of zero coupling, which is the
operational benign regime.  Dr.GRPO is therefore \emph{lossy} on the
spurious channel but does \emph{not} convert this lossiness into
immediate reward gains --- it is a debiaser by removing the channel,
not a rewriter that exploits it.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
\texttt{platform_modal/scripts/length\_bias\_iter112.py} ($\sim$280 lines, numpy
+ scipy.stats) reads iter108's per-run TSV, slices the same $40$-step
$R_t$ series into $n_w{=}4$ windows to compute $\delta R$, and
reports four artefacts:
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter112\_per\_window.tsv} (8 rows: per-window
Spearman with seed count),
\texttt{\_pooled.tsv} (2 rows: per-task pooled Spearman, with
permutation $p$-value and bootstrap-CI fields),
\texttt{\_permutation\_null.tsv} (2 rows: full null distribution
at $B{=}10^{5}$ shuffles of the (algo, seed) coupling),
\texttt{\_cumulative.tsv} (4 rows: $k_{\mathrm{end}}$ regression),
and \texttt{\_rho\_bootstrap.tsv} (2 rows: bootstrap-CI for the
pooled $\rho$).  The figure
(\texttt{figures/length\_bias\_iter112\_sever\_reward.pdf},
mirrored to \texttt{paper/figures/}) is a 4-panel layout: (A) scatter
of severship vs reward improvement with per-window median
markers, (B) pooled Spearman per task with bootstrap 95\% CI, (C)
per-window Spearman, (D) cumulative-window regression across
$k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.
