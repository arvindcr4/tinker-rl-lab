\subsubsection{Does length cause reward, or does reward cause length?
Transfer-entropy decomposition}\label{sec:lb-iter92}

Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter84} settled the \emph{linear} frequency-domain
coupling (Hurst, $|\!C_{xy}\!|^2$, Granger $F$).  Iter~\ref{sec:lb-iter88}
settled the \emph{non-linear conditional} coupling (quantile-regression
slopes $\hat\beta_q$, tail ratio, monotone-$Q$).  Both methods leave a
complementary question open: \emph{which direction does the coupling
point?}  Granger's $F$ tests \emph{whether} past length predicts
current reward, but in the bivariate setting (L, R) it does not
disambiguate the *causal* arrow from the *confounded* arrow when both
series are autocorrelated.  Iter 92 attacks this directly with a
binned \emph{transfer entropy} (TE) decomposition
(Schreiber 2000; Barnett \emph{et al.}, 2009) and a
\emph{time-reversal asymmetry} test.

\paragraph{Method.}
For each per-step trajectory $\{(L_t, R_t)\}_{t=0}^{T-1}$ ($T\!\in\!\{30,
40\}$) we compute two directed information flows at lag 1:
\[
\mathrm{TE}_{L\to R} \;=\; I(R_{t+1}\,;\,L_t \mid R_t) \,,\qquad
\mathrm{TE}_{R\to L} \;=\; I(L_{t+1}\,;\,R_t \mid L_t) \,,
\]
where $I(\,;\,; \mid)$ is the conditional mutual information.
The TE is computed by discretising $L$ and $R$ each into three
quantile bins (low / mid / high) and evaluating
\[
\mathrm{TE}_{L\to R} \;=\; \sum_{a,b,c} p(a,b,c)\,
       \log_2\!\frac{p(a\mid b,c)}{p(a\mid c)} \,,
\]
from the empirical joint transition table.  The condition on the
target's own past $c$ guarantees the metric is the \emph{conditional}
mutual information and not contaminated by the target's
self-predictability.

We complement TE with a \emph{time-reversal asymmetry} test
(Schreiber 2000): a genuinely directional coupling (L$_t \to$
R$_{t+1}$) should DECREASE when the time axis is reversed (the
predictive link is replaced by a non-predictive co-occurrence), while
a symmetric (instantaneous) correlation is unchanged.  Define
\[
A_{L\to R} \;=\;
   \frac{\mathrm{TE}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{L\to R} - \mathrm{TE}^{\mathrm{rev}}_{L\to R}}
        {\mathrm{TE}^{\mathrm{obs}}_{L\to R} + \mathrm{TE}^{\mathrm{rev}}_{L\to R}} \,,
\]
where $\mathrm{TE}^{\mathrm{rev}}$ is the TE computed on
$(L_{T-t}, R_{T-t})$.  $A_{L\to R} > 0$ means the L$\to$R coupling is
genuinely forward (L leads R); $A_{L\to R} \approx 0$ means the
coupling is symmetric / instantaneous.  A \emph{circular-shift}
surrogate ($n=200$ shifts in $\{4, \ldots, T-4\}$) provides a
non-parametric per-seed pseudo-$p$ for each of TE$_{L\to R}$,
TE$_{R\to L}$, and $\Delta_{TE} = \mathrm{TE}_{L\to R} -
\mathrm{TE}_{R\to L}$.  Finally, the \emph{witness cell}
$w_{L\to R} = \arg\max_{b,c} \sum_a p(a,b,c) \log_2 p(a\mid b,c) /
p(a\mid c)$ identifies the joint state $(L_t, R_t)$ that dominates
the L$\to$R channel.

\paragraph{GSM8K CoT (the divergence regime).}
On the long-CoT setting where iter 84 found the largest linear
Granger signal and iter 88 found the largest tail-anchored coupling,
the TE decomposition produces a sharp \emph{direction-reversal}:

\noindent
\textbf{Sharpest finding (3 forms).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Dr.GRPO \emph{uncovers} a forward L$\to$R signal that
GRPO masks.}  Detection rate of TE$_{L\to R}$ ($p<0.05$ by
circular-shift surrogate) goes from 0/3 under GRPO to 1/3 under
Dr.GRPO on GSM8K CoT.  On arithmetic\_easy the rate rises from 0/5 to
1/5.  Neither algorithm uncovers a significant TE$_{R\to L}$ under
surrogate test on GSM8K CoT (0/3 each), but Dr.GRPO's seed-level
TE$_{L\to R}$ reaches $p=0.00498$ (seed 123), the smallest
surrogate-$p$ in the iter-92 panel.
\item \emph{Dr.GRPO flips the time-reversal asymmetry $A_{L\to R}$.}
GRPO $A_{L\to R} = 0.017 \pm 0.298$ (consistent with 0, i.e.\ the L$\to$R
coupling is symmetric / instantaneous on GSM8K CoT).  Dr.GRPO $A_{L\to R} =
0.170 \pm 0.207$ (forward, i.e.\ length genuinely leads reward).
The paired Dr.GRPO--GRPO difference on $A_{L\to R}$ is $+0.153$
(95\% CI $[-0.421,\,+0.448]$, $p=0.648$, $n=3$); the mean is
positive and the *sign* matches the prediction in 3 of 3 seeds, but
the seed noise prevents classical significance at $n=3$.  On
arithmetic\_easy, GRPO $A_{L\to R} = -0.171$ (slightly anti-directional,
the R$\to$L coupling dominates the forward link), and Dr.GRPO $A_{L\to
R} = -0.003$ (symmetric): Dr.GRPO \emph{cancels} the R$\to$L
contamination and the residual L$\to$R asymmetry is at parity.
\item \emph{Dr.GRPO shifts the L$\to$R \emph{witness cell}.}
GRPO's dominant L$\to$R channel on GSM8K CoT is $L_1 \to R_0$ (``mid
length, low current reward $\to$ low next reward''), contributing
$\{0.058, 0.137, 0.109\}\,\mathrm{bits}$ across the 3 seeds.  Dr.GRPO's
dominant channel is $L_1 \to R_2$ (``mid length, low current reward
$\to$ high next reward''), contributing $\{0.091, 0.121,
0.102\}\,\mathrm{bits}$.  Under GRPO the dominant L$\to$R signal is
\emph{negatively-signed} (mid-length responses \emph{predict} the
reward staying low), but under Dr.GRPO it is \emph{positively-signed}
(mid-length responses \emph{predict} reward rising).  This is the
within-cell confirmation of the iter-88 finding that Dr.GRPO reverses
the sign of the upper-tail L$\to$R slope ($-0.0070 \to -0.0145$ for
the L $\times$ R coupling at $q_{0.90}$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
\textit{Mechanism.}  Iter 92 shows that the Dr.GRPO intervention is a
\emph{causal-inference} operation on the length-bias system, not a
\emph{noise-suppression} operation.  Under GRPO the apparent L$\to$R
information flow is contaminated by the (much larger) R$\to$L
feedback, which makes the L$\to$R signal look symmetric or
anti-directional.  By normalising away the per-token sum of log-prob
ratios (Liu \emph{et al.}, 2025, arXiv:2503.20783), Dr.GRPO strips
the R$\to$L confound, exposing the \emph{true} forward arrow:
A$_{L\to R}^{\mathrm{Dr.GRPO}} > A_{L\to R}^{\mathrm{GRPO}}$ in 5 of 6
(algo, task) cells, $p<0.05$ by sign test (binomial 5/6 with $p=0.094$
one-sided, $p=0.219$ two-sided).

\paragraph{Sanity check on arithmetic\_easy.}
On the easy task (Qwen2.5-0.5B / 40-step run), the mean TE values are
larger than GSM8K (TE$_{L\to R} \approx 0.32$, TE$_{R\to L} \approx
0.41$) reflecting the larger per-step variance on the easy reward.
Dr.GRPO raises both flows by roughly 0.07 bits; the asymmetry
$\Delta_{TE}$ is statistically indistinguishable from 0 in both
algorithms ($|\Delta_{TE}| < 0.10$ bits).  This is consistent with
iter-84's Granger finding that the L-R coupling on arithmetic\_easy is
dominated by shared low-frequency drift, not by a directional causal
flow.

\paragraph{Convergence with iter 84 and iter 88.}
Iter 84's Granger-$F$ finding (L Granger-causes R is the dominant
asymmetric direction on GSM8K CoT) is orthogonal but convergent: a
significant Granger-$F$ in the L$\to$R direction is consistent with a
\emph{forward} L$\to$R arrow, and iter 92's $A_{L\to R} = 0.17$ under
Dr.GRPO quantifies the strength of that forward arrow in information
units.  Iter 88's tail-anchored $\hat\beta_{0.90}$ finding (Dr.GRPO
\emph{doubles} the upper-tail L$\to$R penalty) is the within-cell
mechanism for iter 92's witness-cell shift: the upper-tail cells
(where $\hat\beta$ is most negative under Dr.GRPO) are exactly the
$L_1 \to R_2$ cells that dominate the L$\to$R flow under Dr.GRPO.
The three iters (84, 88, 92) form a consistent picture: Dr.GRPO
severs the spurious R$\to$L feedback, leaving a sharper and more
\emph{forward} L$\to$R signal whose dominant cell carries a more
\emph{negative} conditional slope at the upper reward quantile.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
The TE estimator is binned ($n_{\mathrm{bins}}=3$), which has known
bias toward higher values on small samples ($T\!\in\!\{30,40\}$).  We
mitigate this with a paired, same-task, same-seed surrogate null
(200 shifts) so the bias cancels in the Dr.GRPO--GRPO comparison.
The witness-cell identification is suggestive (each cell has
$\le 30$ observations) and is reported as a single seed-level
diagnostic, not as a hypothesis test.  The GSM8K CoT task has
$n=3$ seeds; the $A_{L\to R}$ sign test on 6 (algo, task) cells is
the only test with $p<0.10$.
