% Pillar-3 (group size) standalone abstract
\begin{abstract}
The group size $G$ is GRPO's most distinctive hyperparameter: it sets how many
completions are sampled per prompt and, through the group-relative baseline, how
much preference signal each prompt contributes. We study $G$ as one pillar of
\ours{}, a benchmark of 70+ RL runs across seven libraries and seven model
families ($0.5$B--$1$T), using only measured runs. On a saturated task
(Qwen2.5-0.5B, two-operand addition, $G\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, three seeds) held-out
accuracy is flat: $G{=}2$ reaches a retention ratio of ${\approx}1.003$ ($100.3\%$)
of $G{=}16$, a ratio of accuracies rather than an accuracy above $100\%$, and
seed-level Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) at $\epsilon{=}0.02$ support equivalence on
training reward for $G{=}4$ and $G{=}8$ but only borderline for $G{=}16$. On an
unsaturated task at \emph{equal compute} (two-digit multiplication, $128$
rollouts per step for every $G\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$, five seeds, base accuracy
$0.20$) every arm learns to $0.58$--$0.61$ held-out, with no gain from larger
groups ($G{=}32$: $0.580$ vs.\ $G{=}4$: $0.612$) and no measurable effect of
GRPO's $1/\sigma_r$ whitening. Four Qwen3-8B adapters trained on GSM8K at
$G\in\{2,4,16,32\}$ land within base-model evaluation noise of each other on
pass@$k$. Across settings, larger $G$ steadily lowers the zero-variance fraction
(ZVF, the share of groups whose rewards are all equal and carry no group-relative
signal)---from $0.83$ to $0.35$ on the unsaturated task---and the measured ZVF
sits well above the homogeneous closed form $p^G+(1-p)^G$, as prompt-difficulty
heterogeneity predicts; but lower ZVF did not translate into held-out accuracy.
We also make explicit the sense in which the group-centred update is an
all-pairs preference (DPO-like) contrast with zero-variance groups projected out,
and report a separate 505-task analytical audit in which a declared
$(1-\mathrm{ZVF})/\sqrt{G}$ proxy selects $G{=}4$
(Section~\ref{sec:p3-utility-audit}). An earlier version's token-budget grid,
which suggested an optimum near $G{=}32$, had no training runs behind it and has
been removed.
\end{abstract}
\paragraph{Evidence boundary.} All group-size numbers come from released runs.
The measured sweeps are small (0.5B models, 40--60 steps; one 8B training seed
per $G$) and bound large effects rather than certify equivalence.
