\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:p3-intro}
GRPO~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1, shao2024deepseekmath} estimates advantages by
sampling a group of $G$ completions per prompt and centering their rewards. The
group size $G$ thus plays two roles at once: it is a variance-reduction knob, like
a Monte-Carlo baseline, and it is a preference-density knob, controlling how many
within-prompt comparisons the update sees. Practitioners often treat larger $G$ as
strictly better, reasoning by analogy to variance reduction, but this conflates the
two roles and ignores compute cost. This paper measures the effect of $G$ and
clarifies which mechanism is doing the work.

The question connects GRPO to preference optimization. Direct Preference
Optimization~\citep{rafailov2024direct} learns from explicit pairwise preferences;
GRPO, by centering rewards within a group, induces \emph{implicit} pairwise
contrasts from a scalar reward. Recent analyses formalize this
correspondence~\citep{wu2025grpodpo}, and it reframes $G$ as a control
on how many endogenous preference pairs each prompt contributes---nonzero only for
mixed-reward groups.

We study $G$ as one pillar of \ours{}, a controlled benchmark of 70+ runs across
seven RL libraries and seven model families ($0.5$B--$1$T) on GSM8K
\citep{cobbe2021training}, HumanEval~\citep{chen2021codex}, and tool-use tasks. Our
contributions are: (i) a measured $G\in\{2,4,8,16\}$ sweep on a saturated task,
with seed-level equivalence tests (Two One-Sided Tests, TOST);
(ii) a measured iso-compute sweep over $G\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$ on an unsaturated
task, with unwhitened control arms and per-prompt pass-rate logging, plus a
Qwen3-8B/GSM8K pass@$k$ check of adapters trained at four group sizes;
(iii) a statement, with its observable signatures in logged diagnostics, of the
sense in which the group-centered update is an all-pairs preference
contrast---the ``GRPO is secretly DPO'' bridge; and (iv) a corrected comparison of
measured zero-variance fraction (ZVF) against its closed form. We make no claim
that any single $G$ or algorithm is universally superior.

\paragraph{What changed from earlier versions.}
Earlier drafts reported a 20-cell Qwen3-8B/GSM8K ``token-budget'' grid over
$G\in\{4,\dots,64\}$ and $T\in\{1,4,16,64\}$M, from which they derived a
budget-dependent optimum near $G{=}32$. Those cell values were entered by hand
(\texttt{FALLBACK\_ROWS} in \texttt{experiments/group\_size\_token\_normalized.py})
with no training runs behind them, so the grid and every fit, interval and
recommendation built on it are removed. All group-size results in this version
come from runs whose logs are released.

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:p3-related}
Group-relative and critic-free RL objectives have proliferated around
GRPO~\citep{ahmadian2024backtobasics, lin2025cppo, shao2024deepseekmath, gift2025},
and how each normalizes advantages determines what $G$ can change. RLOO~\citep{ahmadian2024backtobasics}
uses a leave-one-out baseline without per-group whitening---the baseline structure
our leave-one-out decomposition of the GRPO update later builds on---whereas
REINFORCE++~\citep{hu2025reinforcepp} normalizes advantages over the global batch,
so its signal scale depends on batch composition rather than within-group variance
alone. Dr.~GRPO~\citep{tong2025drgrpo} argues that GRPO's $1/\sigma$ whitening (and
per-token length normalization) biases updates toward low-variance and long
responses, so part of any apparent $G$ effect may be a whitening artifact as the
group $\sigma$ estimate stabilizes with $G$. DAPO~\citep{yu2025dapo} instead attacks
the groups that carry no signal at all, dynamically sampling and filtering out
zero-variance (all-correct or all-wrong) groups; the share of such groups---the
Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) we log throughout---is exactly the mass that larger
$G$ can convert into mixed, informative groups.

The preference-learning view descends from
DPO~\citep{rafailov2024direct} and its relatives~\citep{azar2024ipo, ethayarajh2024kto, hong2024orpo},
and the GRPO$\leftrightarrow$DPO correspondence has been examined
directly~\citep{wu2025grpodpo}; relatedly, casting GRPO as an alignment
objective~\citep{vojnovic2025grpo} makes precise which preference objective the
group-relative update optimizes, with $G$ setting how many within-group contrasts
estimate it per prompt. Finally, iso-compute scaling studies~\citep{tan2025scalingrl, cheng2026isocompute}
stress that, at fixed compute, generations per prompt trade off against prompts
seen, so group-size comparisons must be made at matched token budgets rather than
per step---the matching our sweeps adopt. Our contribution is empirical: rather
than derive the bridge in isolation, we validate the all-pairs contrast identity
against logged GRPO diagnostic signatures and connect the group-size effect to preference
density on a fixed benchmark.
