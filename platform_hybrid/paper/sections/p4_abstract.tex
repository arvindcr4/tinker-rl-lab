\begin{abstract}
GRPO's response-length and reward-standard-deviation normalizations can affect
its update; Dr.\,GRPO removes those normalizations. We audit mean-length and
reward traces from Qwen2.5-0.5B arithmetic training (five seeds per algorithm,
40 steps) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT training (three seeds per
algorithm, 30 steps, 200-token generation cap). Both algorithms show decreasing
mean completion length over these short horizons. This is a bounded
trajectory observation, not proof of absent length bias or algorithmic
equivalence. A generation cap censors the regime in which length inflation
could appear. A review rerun that logs every rollout's length by correctness
finds incorrect rollouts longer than correct ones but no widening gap over
30 steps, including at a 512-token cap that only 6\% of rollouts reach. On a fixed 200-item GSM8K held-out slice both
algorithms improve over the pre-RL checkpoint (seed-level mean gains
$+6.2$ and $+5.0$ points, $n{=}3$ seeds each); the between-algorithm
difference is not resolved at this seed budget and is not evidence of
equivalence. We do not infer a causal verbosity mechanism or
held-out superiority. Earlier exploratory damping, efficiency, and
window-level hypothesis-test claims are withdrawn pending estimator and
provenance reconciliation. The useful result is a precise limit on what
these capped, short-horizon traces can establish.
\end{abstract}
\paragraph{August 2026 evidence boundary.} This bounded null is not evidence
of a general deployment or capacity claim outside its named length-capped setup.
