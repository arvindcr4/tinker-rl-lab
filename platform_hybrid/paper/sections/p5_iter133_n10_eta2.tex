\subsection{N10 5-seed GRPO panel: per-axis $\eta^2$ and chained-R portability audit (iter 133)}
\label{sec:p5-iter133-n10-eta2}

Iter 125 chained the mega-98 stack-axis $\eta^2_{\mathrm{stack}}$ with the
N2 algorithm-axis $\eta^2_{\mathrm{algo}}$ and reported dominance ratios
$R = \eta^2_{\mathrm{stack}} / \eta^2_{\mathrm{algo}} \in [6.4, 12.6]$ on
the four dominant (axis, metric) pairings. The remaining open question is
\emph{cross-corpus portability}: does that ratio survive on a panel with
\emph{no} stack variation at all?

The \texttt{n10\_seed\_expansion/} corpus (Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, GRPO G=8,
$lr=10^{-5}$, $max\_tokens=256$, 5 of 8 seeds completed as of iter 133,
s$\in\{42,179,316,453,590\}$, 15 steps per seed, $N=75$) is the
single-stack / single-algorithm panel that closes this leg. Iter 133
re-runs the same per-channel $\eta^2$ decomposition on axes
$\{\mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{step\_band}\}$ with paired-seed cluster-bootstrap
($B=2000$, $seed=20260705$) and reports $R = \eta^2_{\mathrm{step\_band}} / \eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}$.

The per-axis $\eta^2$ split (Table~\ref{tab:p5-iter133-eta2}) reveals that
on this single-stack / single-algo panel, the 4 channels fall into two
regimes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{band-dominant} (reward, mean\_len): $\eta^2_{\mathrm{band}}$
  exceeds $\eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}$; the curriculum-like trajectory explains
  $14\%$--$15\%$ of variance versus $5\%$ explained by seed.
\item \textbf{seed-dominant} (zvf, loss): $\eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}$ exceeds
  $\eta^2_{\mathrm{band}}$; zvf at $\eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}=0.10$ is the
  largest seed-axis variance share on the panel.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Headline hypotheses (H1--H4)}
\label{sec:p5-iter133-headlines}
\textbf{H1 (PASS)} --- $\eta^2(\mathrm{step\_band})$ exceeds $\eta^2(\mathrm{seed})$
on $\geq 2$ of 4 channels (curriculum-trajectory dominance on the channels
that exhibit it). On N10 the 2/4 partition is exactly $\{$
reward, mean\_len$\}$.

\textbf{H2 (PASS)} --- the ratio $R = \eta^2_{\mathrm{band}} / \eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}$
exceeds 1 with CI-lo $>1$ on $\geq 2/4$ channels. Reward $R=2.97$
$[2.25, 28.95]$, mean\_len $R=3.17$ $[1.88, 881.98]$ --- both
$P(R>1)=1.0$; zvf and loss have $R<1$ with $P(R>1)\approx 0.34$.

\begin{sloppypar}
\textbf{H3 (REFUTED)} --- the iter-125 cross-corpus portability claim
$R \geq 4$ on the zvf channel does NOT transfer from mega-98 to N10.
On mega-98 multi-stack panel, $R_{\mathrm{zvf}}^{\mathrm{stack/algo}}=10.32$;
on N10 single-stack panel, $R_{\mathrm{zvf}}^{\mathrm{band/seed}}=0.34$.
\textbf{The 10.32-vs-0.34 inversion is the first measured refusal of
cross-corpus portability on the contrast-signal channel.} reward and
mean\_len carry the cross-corpus invariant (both regimes show band-curriculum
dominance); the zvf/stack behaviour is corpus-shape-dependent.
\end{sloppypar}

\textbf{H4 (INSUFFICIENT)} --- $\eta^2(\mathrm{step\_band}, \mathrm{zvf}) = 0.035$
does NOT meet the operational gate $\geq 0.10$ that the iter-119 / iter-131
controllers implicitly assume. P7 controllers should adopt
$\eta^2(\mathrm{step\_band}, \mathrm{zvf}) \geq 0.10$ as a per-corpus
eligibility check; the N10 panel fails it.

\subsubsection{Cross-paper coupling and operational recommendation}
\label{sec:p5-iter133-coupling}

\textbf{Coupling to iter-7 row-13:} iter 7 reported steady-state zvf predicts
held-out accuracy $r=0.607$ $[0.408, 0.779]$ across N10 seeds --- \emph{seed
is the principal axis of zvf variation that predicts the external outcome}.
Iter 133 confirms that finding at the per-channel level: on N10, zvf is
seed-dominated ($\eta^2_{\mathrm{seed}}=0.10$), not step-band-dominated.

\textbf{Coupling to iter-119 / iter-131 controllers:} both controllers treat
step-level zvf as a per-step control signal. On a corpus where zvf is
seed-dominated (N10), step-level decisions are noisy proxies for
seed-level decisions. The H4 gate
$\eta^2_{\mathrm{step\_band}, \mathrm{zvf}} \geq 0.10$ would have flagged
N10 as controller-ineligible.

\textbf{Coupling to iter-125 chained-R:} iter-125's ``$R \geq 4$ on zvf''
finds corpus-shape-invariant interpretation at the multi-stack level.
iter-133's $R=0.34$ on N10 is the corresponding measurement on a
single-stack panel with seed as the noise axis. \emph{The two corpora
are two regimes, not one}: mega-98 multi-stack responds to systematic
variance sources; N10 single-stack responds to seed-noise variance.

\textbf{Operational recommendation:} (a) cross-corpus P5 claims must
specify which axes are swept in each corpus; (b) cross-corpus portability
of dominance ratios is \emph{not} automatic --- report both absolute
$\eta^2$ per axis per channel (corpus-comparable) AND relative $R$
(corpus-shape-relative); (c) P7 controllers should require
$\eta^2_{\mathrm{step\_band}, \mathrm{zvf}} \geq 0.10$ as a per-corpus
eligibility check before applying step-level zvf-based firing rules.
