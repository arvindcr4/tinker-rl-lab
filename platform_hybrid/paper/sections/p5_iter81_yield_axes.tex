\subsection{Multi-axis yield-residual v2.2 (Items 14--17)}
\label{sec:p5-iter81-yield-axes}

The iter-80 row 95 Item~13 (\texttt{zvf\_yield\_residual}) was the \emph{first}
MIN-REPORT item to independently strengthen fingerprint-vs-outcome coupling.
The iter-80 primary recommendation asked whether MIN-REPORT is structurally
under-recorded by a single item, or whether \emph{multiple} yield-residual
axes each carry independent signal. We close that recommendation here by
testing four additional yield-residual candidates per cell, all derived
from the live \texttt{group\_tensors/} data already in the repository.

\subsubsection{Four candidates (Items 14--17)}
Each candidate is a per-cell scalar fully determined by the
$\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{vec}} \in \{0,\dots,G\}^{\,n_{\mathrm{groups}}}$ vector of
success-counts across prompts and the per-cell $p_c = \mathrm{mean\_reward}/100$:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Item14}(c) &\;=\; \mathrm{Var}(K) - G \cdot p \cdot (1-p) \tag{K-variance residual}\\
  \mathrm{Item15}(c) &\;=\; |\{\,k : k \in K\,\}| \tag{distinct K-count, $\le G{+}1$}\\
  \mathrm{Item16}(c) &\;=\; \max_k\,\bigl(\#\{x : K_x = k\}\bigr) / n_{\mathrm{groups}} \tag{max K-share}\\
  \mathrm{Item17}(c) &\;=\; \mathrm{Var}\!\bigl(K/G\bigr) \tag{prompt-$\hat p$ spread}\\
  \label{eq:items14-17}
\end{align}
All four are populated on $\mathbf{98/98}$ cells with zero additional harvest.

\subsubsection{Information budget uplift (H1)}
Table~\ref{tab:p5-v22-uplift} shows the cumulative fingerprint budget.
Items~14--17 collectively add $+15.86$ bits to v2.1, $\mathbf{3.4\times}$
as much as Item~13 alone. The total v2.2 budget is $38.78$ bits
($+69.2\%$ over v2.1; $+147.6\%$ over v1). Per-item cardinality on the
98-cell corpus: Item 14 $n_{\mathrm{unique}} = 49$, Item 15 $= 5$ (bounded
by $G+1$), Item 16 $= 20$, Item 17 $= 49$.

\subsubsection{Single-item Spearman (H2)}
\textbf{Each individual item 14--17 alone} has higher fingerprint-vs-$|\Delta z|$
Spearman $\rho$ than the \emph{full seven-field v1 fingerprint}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Item 14 (K-variance residual): $\rho = 0.558$
  \item Item 15 (K-unique count): $\rho = 0.588$
  \item Item 16 (max K-share): $\rho = 0.589$
  \item Item 17 (prompt-$\hat p$ spread): $\rho = 0.556$
\end{itemize}
The v1 fingerprint's $\rho = 0.436$ is exceeded by $\sim 30\%$ even at
single-axis granularity -- evidence that the structural \emph{shape} of
the success-count distribution carries more anti-herding signal than
any string-valued fingerprint axis.

\subsubsection{Paired bootstrap coupling (H3)}
Paired bootstrap ($B=2000$ with $N_{\mathrm{pairs}}=2000$ cell-pairs):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta\rho(\mathrm{v2.2}_{\mathrm{all}} - \mathrm{v2.1}) = \mathbf{+0.0748}$, 95\% CI $[\mathbf{+0.066,\,+0.084}]$ -- excludes zero.
  \item $\Delta\rho(\mathrm{v2.2}_{13+15} - \mathrm{v2.1}) = +0.0443$ (CI $[+0.039,\,+0.049]$) -- the strongest \emph{single} candidate.
  \item Items 14, 16, 17 each add $+0.031$ to $+0.039$ with CIs excluding zero.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Every} (Item 13 + one new item) combination independently strengthens
fingerprint-vs-outcome coupling.

\subsubsection{Binomial null control (H4)}
To separate signal from binomial noise we simulated per-cell
$K_x \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(G_c,\, p_c)$ for $n=200$ replications and
recomputed each item's $H_{\mathrm{bits}}$. Excess
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{bits}} = H_{\mathrm{obs}} - \bar H_{\mathrm{null}}$:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Item14:}\;&\ \Delta H = +0.186\quad(\!\approx\!+2.8\sigma), &
  \mathrm{Item15:}\;&\ \Delta H = +0.255\quad(\!\approx\!+6.2\sigma), \\
  \mathrm{Item16:}\;&\ \Delta H = -0.123\quad(\!\approx\!-1.9\sigma), &
  \mathrm{Item17:}\;&\ \Delta H = +0.257\quad(\!\approx\!+3.6\sigma)
  \label{eq:null_excess}
\end{align}
\textbf{Items 14, 15, 17} encode information \emph{beyond} $\mathrm{Binomial}(G,p)$
they are signal-bearing. \textbf{Item 16 is REJECTED}: its empirical
max-K-share is \emph{higher} than binomial expectation (more concentrated),
i.e. it is anti-herding-by-construction but does not add new empirical
information beyond what the binomial model's K-distribution already encodes.

\subsubsection{Verdict and operational recommendation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Adopt Items 14, 15, 17} into MIN-REPORT v2.2.
  \item \textbf{Reject Item 16} (max K-share) -- negative binomial-null excess.
  \item \textbf{Displace} one v1 placebo item (e.g.\ \texttt{sampler\_backend\_precision}, $n_{\mathrm{unique}}=1$ on the live corpus).
  \item Recommended \textbf{v2.2 schema = 14 items}, $H_{\mathrm{bits}}$ budget $= 35.20$, paired $\Delta\rho$ CI excludes zero.
\end{itemize}
The frontier synthesis (FRONTIER\_INSIGHTS.md Round 1) frame
Y$(p,G) = 1 - \mathrm{ZVF}$ as \emph{Contrastive Yield}; iter-80 Item~13 is
its first per-cell realisation, and iter-81 items 14, 15, 17 add
\textbf{three} further realisations of contrast-yield information at the
$(K$-distribution) granularity. The four v2.2 items together give a
\textbf{4-vector of yield-residual signal} per cell, sufficient to
reconstruct the underlying GRPO contrast-preservation regime.
