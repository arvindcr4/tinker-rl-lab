\subsection{Per-delta measured-evidence tier classification (iter 126)}
\label{sec:p6-iter126-evidence-tier}

The registry's variant-delta entries
(\texttt{platform_hybrid/registry/entries/delta\_*.json}) carry two kinds of evidence:
(a)~\emph{claimed} -- the conceptual change relative to GRPO, the citation
key, and the textual description of what the variant does; and
(b)~\emph{measured} -- an optional list of $\{metric, panel, base, delta,
ci\_low, ci\_high, n, significant\}$ rows that report how the variant moved
a measured quantity (reward\_mean, zvf, etc.) on the named panel, with a
paired-step bootstrap 95\% CI.  We classify each variant delta by the
\emph{evidence tier} that its measured rows attain, defining
\[
\text{tier} = \begin{cases}
A & \text{if } n_{\text{sig}} \geq 3 \text{ and } n_{\text{panels}} \geq 2,\\
B & \text{if } n_{\text{sig}} \geq 1,\\
C & \text{if } n_{\text{total}} \geq 1 \text{ and } n_{\text{sig}} = 0,\\
D & \text{if } n_{\text{total}} = 0.
\end{cases}
\]
Tier~A deltas have measured evidence on at least three significant rows
across at least two panels -- the strongest evidentiary class.
Tier~D deltas have no measured rows in the registry and rely solely on
their cited description.

Applied to all 15 \texttt{delta\_*.json} entries, the audit produces
Table~\ref{tab:p6-iter126-tier-rk} (top 10) and the full ranking in
\texttt{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p5p8/p6\_iter126\_measured\_evidence\_tier.tsv}.
The headline result is a sharp three-tier split:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tier A (3 deltas)} -- \texttt{aero}, \texttt{areal}, \texttt{gift}.
These are the three GRPO-family variants covered by the N2
same-stack reward tensors
(\texttt{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/n2\_reward\_tensor\_resume/}) and the
zvf-iter130 risk index.  Each carries six measured rows (zvf +
reward\_mean, n2\_same\_stack\_last10 panel + zvf130 5-seed panel), with
3/6 statistically significant at the 95\% paired-step bootstrap level
(B\,=\,2000).
\item \textbf{Tier B (7 deltas)} -- \texttt{cppo}, \texttt{drgrpo}, \texttt{es},
\texttt{mcgrpo}, \texttt{ngrpo}, \texttt{scafgrpo}, \texttt{adaptiveg}.
Each carries three measured rows from a single panel; 1/3 is significant.
These are the variants with evidence from a single harness
(e.g.\ the zvf-iter130 5-seed panel) but not from the second-panel N2
comparison.
\item \textbf{Tier D (5 deltas)} -- \texttt{dapo}, \texttt{gspo}, \texttt{liteppo},
\texttt{ppo}, \texttt{reinforce}.  These entries describe the variant and
its citation, but carry zero measured rows.  They remain in the registry
as citation-only references -- sufficient for provenance but not for
\emph{quantitative} cross-variant claims about them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implications for cross-variant claims.}
Any registry-level claim that averages across all 15 deltas
(e.g.\ a future ``registry mean delta vs.\ GRPO'') would be dominated by
the A-tier trio.  The recommended practice is to \emph{report tier with
the claim}: a tier-A delta claim (aero/areal/gift) carries the strongest
evidentiary backing; a tier-D delta claim (dapo/gspo/liteppo/ppo/reinforce)
is a citation claim, not a measurement claim, and should be reported as
such.

\paragraph{Implications for closing the tier-D gap.}
Closing tier~D for any of the five remaining variants requires running
the N2 same-stack protocol (40 steps, G\,=\,8, paired-step bootstrap B\,=\,2000)
for that variant.  At the iter-126 cost regime this is tractable for any
single variant ($\leq$\$5 in Tinker API credits, $\leq$1 hour wall-clock on
the standard harness); running all five is the recommended iter-127 vein
on Pillar~2.

\paragraph{Cross-paper coupling.}
This audit extends iter-118's claim-xref strict coverage row
(``51 INFO findings on fully-unknown MIN-REPORT items'') by classifying
the \emph{measured} evidence rather than the \emph{claimed} components.
A tier-D delta may still have a fully-populated claim block; the gap is
in \emph{measurement}, not in \emph{reporting}.  Iter-122's
cross-entry consistency check focused on (delta\_id, component)
agreement; iter-126 instead surfaces the per-delta evidence-depth
asymmetry.  The two are complementary: iter-122 says ``stacks agree on
what they \emph{claim}''; iter-126 says ``deltas vary in what they
\emph{measure}''.
