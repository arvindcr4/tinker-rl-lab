\subsection{Missing-method registry entries (iter 138)}
\label{sec:p6-iter138-missing-method}

Iter-130 closed brief vein (c) by adding a schema-CI validator.
Iter-126 closed (a) at the tier-classification layer. Iter-134 closed
(b) at the measured-row field-completeness layer. Iter-138 closes the
remaining vein (d): \emph{add registry entries for GRPO-family methods
that are present in the measured data but absent from the catalog}.

\paragraph{Method-identity coverage scan.}
The registry should record every GRPO-family method that appears in
any canonical measured panel. The iter-130 zvf130 batch harness
produced 16 distinct method rows (recorded in
\texttt{zvf\_iter130\_meta.json["per\_method"]}): 5
scaling-law anchor rows (\texttt{scaling\_law\_Qwen3-8B},
\texttt{Nemotron-120B}, \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct},
\texttt{Qwen3.5-4B}, \texttt{DeepSeek-V3.1}), 9 real GRPO-family
methods already in the registry, and 2 real GRPO-family methods
\emph{not} in the registry:
\texttt{tool\_use\_llama-8b-inst} and \texttt{tool\_use\_qwen3-32b}.
Both missing entries were measured under the same zvf130 batch harness
with $n_{\text{seeds}}=1$, $z\_vf\_risk\_mean = 0.5474$ (vs
grpo=0.5777), $\mathit{mag\_mean}=1.0$, $\mathit{csd\_mean}=0.95$,
$\mathit{failure\_rate}=1.0$, on the BFCL tool-use task (a sparse 0/1
outcome-reward domain).

\paragraph{Classification rule.}
We classify every method present in the measured data into one of three
verdicts:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PRESENT} --- a registry entry already exists (either
  \texttt{zvf130\_<m>.json} or \texttt{delta\_<m>.json});
\item \textbf{ANCHOR\_ROW} --- a scaling-law anchor used as a
  reference point in the iter-130 risk index; not a GRPO method, so
  receives no registry entry;
\item \textbf{MISSING\_GRPO\_METHOD} --- a real GRPO-family method
  that lacks a registry entry; receives both a stack and a
  variant-delta entry at iter-138.
\end{itemize}
The audit script is
\texttt{platform_modal/scripts/p5p8/p6\_iter138\_missing\_method\_audit.py} and
emits \texttt{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p5p8/p6\_iter138\_missing\_method\_audit.tsv}
(16 rows $\times$ 7 cols).

\paragraph{Provenance discipline for the new entries.}
Iter-126 documented that 5/15 (33.3\%) of pre-existing variant-delta
entries were tier-D (citation-only, $\mathit{measured}: \mathit{null}$).
The two new \texttt{tool\_use\_*} entries are \emph{not} tier-D in the
citation-only sense --- they carry a real $\mathit{measured}$ row
backed by a real TSV path. They \emph{are} tier-D in the paired-CI
sense: $n_{\text{seeds}}=1$ precludes the paired-seed bootstrap CI
that the iter-130 paired-bootstrap template requires. Both entries
therefore record:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \texttt{ci\_method} of
  \texttt{\{method: "point\_only\_no\_per\_seed\_sd", n\_boot: null,
  seed: null, ci\_level: null\}},
\item a \texttt{significant: false} flag (point estimate is
  honest-as-non-significant; no CI to flip),
\item an \texttt{evidence\_deferred\_until} field stating the
  multi-seed reproduction + verified-arXiv conditions under which
  the entry will be promoted out of tier-D.
\end{itemize}
No fabricated citation. No fabricated CI. The entry exists to close
the registry's method-identity coverage gap --- a coverage record,
not an effect claim.

\paragraph{Results.}
After iter-138:
\begin{itemize}
\item Registry entry count: 39 $\to$ 43
  ($+2$ stack \texttt{zvf130\_tool\_use\_*},
  $+2$ variant-delta \texttt{delta\_tool\_use\_*}).
\item \texttt{python3 platform_modal/scripts/p5p8/p6\_iter130\_schema\_ci.py} reports
  \texttt{parse\_ok=43/43}, \texttt{schema\_ok=43/43},
  $\mathit{n\_stale\_method\_rows}=0$.
\item \texttt{python3 platform_hybrid/registry/query.py list} now includes both
  \texttt{zvf130\_tool\_use\_llama-8b-inst} and
  \texttt{zvf130\_tool\_use\_qwen3-32b}.
\item The \texttt{measured\_block\_audit\_refresh.json} delta-entry
  roster grows from 15 to 17 (tool\_use rows added with
  $n_{\text{measured}}=1$, $n_{\text{significant}}=0$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cross-paper coupling.}
\textbf{(i)} Iter-126 (row 142) introduced the tier-A/B/C/D
classification; iter-138's two new entries sit in tier-D by paired-CI
depth but in tier-B by evidence-presence (they have a real measured
row, not just a citation). Iter-138 therefore sharpens the tier rule:
\emph{tier-D is now defined as paired-CI-impossible OR
citation-only}. \textbf{(ii)} Iter-130 (row 145) introduced the
paired-seed bootstrap CI template that the tool\_use entries cannot
satisfy; iter-138 makes that limitation explicit by carrying
\texttt{evidence\_deferred\_until}. \textbf{(iii)} Iter-134 (row 150)
audited 38 measured rows; iter-138 brings that count to 40 measured
rows (2 new $\times$ 1 row each, on the new \texttt{zvf130\_1seed\_tooluse}
panel). \textbf{(iv)} FRONTIER\_INSIGHTS Round 1 Critic-Degeneracy
Hypothesis (frontier synthesis): the tool\_use methods register
$\mathit{mag\_mean}=1.0$ and $\mathit{failure\_rate}=1.0$ --- a
fully-degenerate ZVF pattern consistent with the (frontier synthesis)
prediction that token-level credit assignment collapses under sparse
terminal reward, but here on a different task domain (tool-use
sparse 0/1, not GSM8K arithmetic).
