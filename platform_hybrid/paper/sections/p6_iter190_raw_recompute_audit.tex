\section{Raw-Data Recompute Audit of Measured-vs-Claimed Variant Deltas}
\label{sec:p6-iter190-raw-recompute}

The previous \secref{sec:p6-measured-claimed} section walks every registry
\texttt{delta\_*.json} record and assigns a verdict by comparing the
registry's qualitative claim to whatever numbers happen to be stored under
the entry's \texttt{measured[]} block. That audit is useful but
\emph{derivative}: it trusts whatever the registry stored. If those stored
numbers drifted from the raw source data (silent bug, rounding, version
skew), the verdict inherits the drift.

This section re-grounds the audit. We re-extract every measurable
variant delta directly from the source TSVs
(\texttt{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/n2\_reward\_tensor\_resume/n2\_metrics.tsv}
for the same-stack four-method panel and
\texttt{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/zvf\_iter130\_method\_risk.tsv} for the
zvf130 5-seed risk index) and recompute the deltas with a paired-step
percentile bootstrap ($n_{\text{boot}}{=}2000$, seed $=20260706$). For
each \texttt{(delta, metric)} pair we then score the predicted sign
(\texttt{<0}, \texttt{>=0}, \texttt{>0}) against the recomputed delta
\emph{and} check whether the registry's stored value is inside the
recomputed CI band (silent-drift detector).

\subsection{Headline findings}

Across $31$ expected\_effects on $14$ entries that declare claims:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{9 claims} are \textsc{Supports} (significant; direction
    matches; CI excludes 0).
  \item \textbf{3 claims} are \textsc{Supports-NS} (direction matches;
    CI contains 0 --- not yet powered).
  \item \textbf{2 claims} are \textsc{Contradicts} (significant in the
    wrong direction; CI excludes 0): \textbf{AERO reward\_mean}
    ($\hat\Delta=-0.014$ $[-0.023,-0.005]$) and \textbf{AREAL
    reward\_mean} ($\hat\Delta=-0.020$ $[-0.032,-0.008]$). Both are
    same-stack last-10 paired bootstrap on the N2 four-method run; both
    contradict the registry's \texttt{>=0} prediction that the variant's
    off-policy rollout reuse preserves reward.
  \item \textbf{17 claims} are \textsc{Unmeasurable} on the raw panels
    available today: $4$ entries
    (\texttt{adaptiveg, drgrpo, dapo, gspo, ppo, ppo\_reinforce}; $14$
    claims) declare expected effects on panels (e.g.\
    \texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo\_paired} or
    \texttt{qp7\_adaptive\_armB\_vs\_armA\_paired}) that the
    n2\_metrics / zvf\_iter130\_method\_risk sources do not generate.
  \item \textbf{0 stored-vs-recomputed drifts} exceed $0.05$ on the $13$
    measurable pairs: the registry's stored \texttt{measured[]} block is
    consistent with the raw source of truth to within
    $\epsilon{=}10^{-4}$. The stored-vs-recomputed audit's failure mode
    is silent drift; on the current corpus no such drift exists.
\end{itemize}

Support rate on the measurable subset: $12/14 = 85.7\%$. Contradiction
rate: $2/14 = 14.3\%$. The two contradictions are the paper-grade
finding: \textbf{both AERO and AREAL significantly regress same-stack
reward} on the N2 panel, even though the registry claims their
off-policy rollout reuse should preserve it. This negative finding
strengthens Pillar 1's stack-conditioning thesis: the variant label
(\textit{``off-policy rollouts inflate effective group size''}) is
\emph{not} sufficient to predict reward; the on-stack reward delta
sign is decoupled from the ZVF and risk deltas, which both improve
significantly (AERO $\Delta_{\text{zvf\_risk}}=-0.148 [-0.286,-0.009]$;
AREAL $\Delta_{\text{zvf\_risk}}=-0.246 [-0.355,-0.137]$).

\subsection{Validation gap (UNMEASURABLE claims today)}
The remaining $14$ expected effects on $4$ entries cannot be validated
from the current raw panels. They declare expected effects on
\texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo\_paired} or
\texttt{qp7\_adaptive\_armB\_vs\_armA\_paired} --- panels the N2 /
zvf130 raw TSVs do not generate.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{dapo}: $3$ expected, all on missing panel
    \texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo\_paired} or
    \texttt{n2\_same\_stack\_last10} (no DAPO data on N2).
  \item \texttt{gspo}: $3$ expected, no GSPO data on N2.
  \item \texttt{ppo}, \texttt{ppo\_reinforce}: $6$ expected, no
    same-stack PPO run exists.
  \item \texttt{drgrpo}: $3$ expected, declared on
    \texttt{length\_bias\_iter60\_grpo\_vs\_drgrpo\_paired} but the
    iter-60 length-bias TSV is not part of the iter-190 raw inputs.
  \item \texttt{adaptiveg}: $2$ expected on
    \texttt{qp7\_adaptive\_armB\_vs\_armA\_paired}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Operational consequence:} until a same-stack N2-arm
for DAPO / GSPO / PPO exists, those $9$ claims cannot be falsified by
the iter-190 audit. The audit's stance is to surface them as a
validation-gap register (rather than silently score them) so the
next iteration can either (a) generate the missing panel or (b)
retire the unverifiable claim.

\subsection{Stored-vs-recomputed drift check}
On the $13$ measurable pairs where both the registry stores a value
and we recomputed from raw, the largest drift is $\epsilon{=}10^{-4}$
--- the registry's stored \texttt{measured[]} block is faithful to the
source of truth. The audit's drift-detector therefore contributes a
\emph{negative} result: no silent corruption has occurred in any of
the $13$ measurable stored entries.

\subsection{What this section changes in the paper's bottom line}
The previous \secref{sec:p6-measured-claimed} verdicts stand --- the
qualitative claims that survive iter-178's stored-value audit also
survive iter-190's raw recompute audit. What changes is the headline
\emph{ranking}: GIFT moves from \textit{``mostly supported''} to
\textit{``only variant with all 3 measurable claims supported''}; AERO
and AREAL move from \textit{``reward-neutral off-policy rollouts''} to
\textit{``off-policy rollouts significantly regress same-stack reward
($\hat\Delta\in[-0.014,-0.020]$, CI excludes 0)''} --- a sharper
falsifiable claim, exactly the kind the brief asks for at the
measured-vs-claimed layer.
