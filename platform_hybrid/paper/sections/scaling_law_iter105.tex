% scaling-law falsification.  Two fresh angles not covered by iter81
% (compute invariance), iter85 (three-phase), iter97 (8-family head-to-head),
% iter101 (cross-anchor transfer + AIC stack):
%   (A) Six-mode failure-mode classifier over the 12-anchor GSM8K panel
%       (converged, plateau, drift, oscillation, collapse, divergence)
%       using observable trace statistics -- not a parametric model.
%   (B) R_max*(N) Chinchilla-analogue scaling-law fits (loglinear,
%       inverse, chinchilla-power).  Asks whether the asymptotic
%       reward ceiling of GRPO post-training is predictable from the
%       model size alone, the way pretraining loss is.
%   (C) Leave-one-family-out (LOFO) check on the Chinchilla-power fit
%       -- a family-invariance score that quantifies how much of the

% All three converge on the same falsifiable statement: \emph{the

\paragraph{Iter 105 elevation: the Chinchilla-analogue $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ scaling law fails on the 12-anchor GSM8K panel --- best fit is loglinear with $R^2 = 0.054$ (AIC $=-23.37$) and the leave-one-family-out residual for Qwen3.5-27B is $-0.79$, meaning a 27B dense model is predicted at $0.79$ above its actual late-reward of $0.00$; the same fit drops $R^2$ to $0.001$ under the inverse form, indicating no monotone or asymptotic ceiling exists in $\log_{10}N$.}
\label{par:scaling-iter105}

Iter 97 closed Q4 (no single functional form beats constant-mean on
parsimony) and Q5 (the GRPO reward noise floor is approximately
scale-invariant).  Iter 101 closed Q6 (the per-anchor
$(R_\mathrm{max},\lambda)$ fits degrade 19.4\% when transferred across
anchors) and Q7 (AIC-stacked ensemble does not beat the best single
family on held-out forecast MAE).  Iter 105 takes the next two
operational questions in the same falsifiable register, asking whether
\emph{the size of the model} alone is enough to predict the asymptotic
GRPO post-training reward ceiling, and whether the failure mode of the
fit (when it does fail) is family-asymmetric:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Q8 (failure-mode taxonomy).}  For each anchor in the
iter81/iter97/iter101 12-anchor pool, we compute five observable
trace statistics --- early-mean / late-mean / peak / mean / zero-fraction /
residual variance after linear detrend --- and run them through a
hand-crafted six-mode classifier
(\emph{converged}, \emph{plateau}, \emph{drift}, \emph{oscillation},
\emph{collapse}, \emph{divergence}).  This is a
\emph{description-first} taxonomy: the modes are not derived from a
parametric model (no three-phase fit, no saturation fit), only from
what the trace \emph{looks like}.  Output: a hard label and a soft
posterior $P(\mathrm{mode} \mid \mathrm{trace})$ for each anchor
(Table~\ref{tab:iter105-modes}).

\item \textbf{Q9 (Chinchilla-analogue $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$).}
Define $R_\mathrm{max}^\star$ as the empirical asymptotic reward ceiling
for each anchor: the mean of the last third of the trace for
non-collapse anchors, and the peak for collapse / divergence anchors
(to reflect the model's best attainable performance, not the
post-collapse floor).  Fit three parametric forms on
$(N_i, R_{\mathrm{max},i}^\star)$ for $i = 1, \ldots, 12$:
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{loglinear:} \quad & R_\mathrm{max}^\star = a + b \cdot \log_{10} N, \\
  \mathrm{inverse:} \quad & R_\mathrm{max}^\star = a - b / N, \\
  \mathrm{chinchilla:} \quad & R_\mathrm{max}^\star = a - b / N^{c}.
\end{align*}
The chinchilla form is the most permissive (3 parameters; grid-search
over $c \in [0.05, 1.5]$, 25 log-spaced points) and is the closest
analogue to the Hoffmann et al.\ (2022) scaling law for
post-training.

\item \textbf{Q10 (leave-one-family-out, LOFO).}  Refit the
chinchilla-power form on each family in turn ($\mathrm{dense}$ vs
$\mathrm{MoE}$), predict $R_\mathrm{max}^\star$ on the held-out family,
and record the residual.  The \emph{family-invariance score} is
$1 - \overline{\sigma_\mathrm{per-family}} / \sigma_\mathrm{all}$
(1.0 means the fit residual is invariant to which family you drop,
0.0 means dropping each family produces wildly different residual
distributions).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Findings}

\paragraph{F1. Failure modes split 6 plateau / 3 drift / 2 converged / 1 collapse.}
Of the 12 GSM8K anchors, only Nemotron-120B is classified as
\emph{collapse} (zero-fraction 0.55, peak 0.875, late-mean 0.25 -- the
canonical ``transient success then divergence'' signature already
flagged in iter 85).  Three anchors -- Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.5-27B, and
Kimi-K2-Thinking -- are classified as \emph{drift} (late-mean drops
$\geq 0.10$ vs.\ early-mean).  Two anchors -- Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst and
Qwen3-235B-MoE -- are \emph{converged} (peak $\geq 0.95$, mean
$\geq 0.95$, residual variance $< 0.005$ -- the trace is locked at the
ceiling).  The remaining six are \emph{plateau}: stable around a fixed
non-extreme level with no significant trend.  No anchor is classified
as \emph{oscillation} or \emph{divergence} on this panel.  The
distribution is dominated by a stable plateau regime; the headline
``Nemotron collapse'' failure mode is a single outlier (Table
\ref{tab:iter105-modes}).

\paragraph{F2. The Chinchilla-analogue $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ scaling law fails.}
All three parametric forms produce fits with $R^2 \in [0.001, 0.054]$
--- essentially noise.  The best-by-AIC is the loglinear form with
$a = 0.487$, $b = 0.104$, $R^2 = 0.054$, $\mathrm{AIC} = -23.37$.  The
chinchilla-power grid picks $c = 0.05$ (the model collapses back to
near-log-linear because the data has no curvature to fit) with
$R^2 = 0.049$ and $\mathrm{AIC} = -21.31$.  The inverse form, which is
the most restrictive, has $R^2 = 0.001$.  The 12-anchor panel --
which spans 4B to 1000B parameters and includes both dense and MoE
families -- does \emph{not} support a monotone or asymptotic ceiling
law in $\log_{10} N$.  This is a sharper falsification than iter 101's
``transfer degrades 19.4\%'': here the law itself is not transferable
because there is no law to transfer.

The visual evidence is in
\path{experiments/results/scaling_law_iter105_scaling_law.tsv}: the
two 8B dense anchors \emph{bracket} the panel (Qwen3-8B
$R_\mathrm{max}^\star = 0.34$, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
$R_\mathrm{max}^\star = 0.84$); a 27B dense model sits at $0.00$
while a 20B MoE sits at $0.88$; a 685B-class MoE is at $0.81$ and a 1000B
MoE is at $0.75$.  Size alone does not predict ceiling.

\paragraph{F3. Family-asymmetric residuals: the MoE-only fit predicts all dense anchors badly.}
LOFO on the chinchilla-power form yields a family-invariance score of
$0.376$ (well below 1.0).  The per-family residual standard deviation
is $\sigma_\mathrm{dense} = 0.386$ and $\sigma_\mathrm{moe} = 0.193$
--- the MoE family is internally consistent ($\sigma$ half the
overall), but the dense family is internally divergent ($\sigma$
roughly $2\times$ overall).  The worst single offender is
\textbf{Qwen3.5-27B}, held out from the MoE fit, predicted at
$R_\mathrm{max}^\star = 0.79$ vs.\ observed $0.00$, a residual of
$-0.79$.  Three other dense anchors (Qwen3-32B $-0.67$, Qwen3-8B
$-0.39$, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct $+0.11$) also miss by more than
$0.10$.  The MoE family alone cannot reproduce the dense family's
$R_\mathrm{max}^\star$ distribution; the inverse also fails
(Nemotron-120B, the collapse outlier (MoE / LatentMoE, previously mislabelled dense), is held out from one family fit and
over-predicted by $+0.05$, while gpt-oss-20B is under-predicted by
$+0.48$ when held out from dense).

\paragraph{F4. Sharpest falsifiable statement.}
$R_\mathrm{max}^\star$ is \emph{not} a function of $\log_{10} N$
alone on the 12-anchor GSM8K panel.  Any single-parametric-form fit
gives $R^2 < 0.06$; any family-stratified fit produces residuals of
$0.3$--$0.8$ on individual anchors.  The implication is that
\emph{operational scaling-law reporting on GRPO post-training must
condition on architecture family (dense vs MoE), on
post-training-only vs instruct-pretrained initialization, and on
collapse / drift status}, not on size alone.  The 4B Qwen3.5-4B and
the 1000B Kimi-K2-Thinking have nearly identical $R_\mathrm{max}^\star$
($0.85$ vs.\ $0.75$) but vastly different failure modes (plateau vs.\
drift); a single scaling curve passing through both is meaningless.
