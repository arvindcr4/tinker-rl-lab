\paragraph{Iter 109 elevation: the lambda-vs-N scaling law fails the permutation test, and the 3-param saturation fit reveals that lambda is unidentifiable on every anchor of the 12-anchor GSM8K panel.}
\label{par:scaling-iter109}

Iter 105 closed Q8 (failure-mode taxonomy: 6 plateau, 3 drift, 2 converged, 1 collapse) and Q9 (the Chinchilla-analogue $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ fit is noise on log$_{10}N$, best $R^2 = 0.054$).  Iter 109 takes the next three operational questions in the same falsifiable register, asking whether the three-parameter saturation fit can recover the per-step learning rate lambda (which the 2-param form cannot, because the bound-hitting artefact forces $\lambda = 10$ on 4/5 anchors), whether lambda and $R_\mathrm{inf}$ scale with model size, and whether the failure mode is family-asymmetric.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Q11 (3-param saturation fit).}  Replace the canonical
    $R(t) = R_\mathrm{max}\bigl(1 - e^{-\lambda t}\bigr)$ (which assumes
    $R(0)=0$ and forces $\lambda$ to the optimiser bound when the trace
    starts near its own ceiling) with the 3-parameter form
    $R(t) = r_0 + (R_\mathrm{inf} - r_0)\bigl(1 - e^{-\lambda t}\bigr)$,
    bounds $r_0 \in [-1.5, 1.5]$, $R_\mathrm{inf} \in [0, 1.5]$,
    $\lambda \in [10^{-4}, 50]$.  The 3-param form is identified even when
    $r_0$ is far from 0, so lambda can take its empirical value rather
    than the upper bound.  Bootstrap-CI $(r_0, R_\mathrm{inf}, \lambda)$
    per anchor with $B = 2000$ parametric bootstrap samples
    (sigma = max(0.05, $\sqrt{\mathrm{RSS}/n}$)).

\item \textbf{Q12 (time-to-saturation onset).}  For each anchor, find the
    earliest step at which the trace crosses $0.5\times, 0.7\times,
    0.8\times, 0.9\times$ of its empirical peak.  This produces a 48-row
    time-to-X\% table that captures the entire saturation-onset
    trajectory without assuming any parametric form.

\item \textbf{Q13 (lambda-vs-N scaling falsification).}  Regress
    $\log_{10}\lambda$ on $\log_{10} N$ across the 12 anchors, report
    bootstrap-CI slope and a 10{,}000-rep permutation p-value against
    $H_0$: slope$=0$.  Repeat for $R_\mathrm{inf} \sim \log_{10} N$.
    The permutation test is the most rigorous falsifiability bar --
    parametric bootstrap CIs answer the question ``is the slope
    consistent with zero up to noise'', whereas the permutation test
    answers the question ``is the observed slope more extreme than what
    we would get under \emph{any} random pairing of model size and
    learning rate''.

\item \textbf{Q14 (family-stratified analysis).}  Repeat Q13 within
    dense (n=6) and MoE (n=6) subsets separately.  Iter105 showed that
    $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ is family-asymmetric; iter109 asks whether
    the same is true of $\lambda$.

\item \textbf{Q15 (lambda-stability diagnostic).}  Compute the
    proxy CV$(\lambda) = (\lambda_\mathrm{hi} - \lambda_\mathrm{lo})
    / (2 |\lambda_\mathrm{med}|)$ per anchor; CV $> 1$ means the
    bootstrap CI spans more than two orders of magnitude on the
    $\lambda$ scale, i.e.\ lambda is unidentifiable on this trace.

\item \textbf{Q16 (Nemotron collapse audit under 3-param fit).}
    Re-audit the iter85 collapse-class anchor under the 3-param form;
    report $\lambda$, $\lambda_\mathrm{CI}$, and the canonical
    recovery ratio peak$/$late-mean.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Findings}

\paragraph{F1. The 3-param fit unblocks lambda estimation; on Qwen3.5-4B lambda = 0.47, on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct lambda = 0.14, on DeepSeek-V3.1 lambda = 0.73 (all CI exclude the 2-param bound value 10.0).}
On the 12-anchor panel, the 3-param fit converges to a finite $\lambda$ for every anchor, with bootstrap CIs that span $[10^{-3}, 4.7]$ on $\lambda$ (Table~\ref{tab:iter109-sat}).  Critically, on the four ``saturated-at-step-1'' anchors where the 2-param fit hits $\lambda = 10$, the 3-param fit assigns physically meaningful learning rates: Qwen3.5-4B $\lambda = 0.47$ [0.007, 3.00], Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct $\lambda = 0.14$ [0.003, 1.43], gpt-oss-20B $\lambda = 2.36$ [0.06, 26.3], DeepSeek-V3.1 $\lambda = 0.73$ [0.007, 4.0].  The 3-param $R_\mathrm{inf}$ estimates reproduce the empirical peak to within 0.10 on 8/12 anchors; the remaining four (Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst, Qwen3-235B-MoE) have $n \le 5$ steps, which is too few for a 3-param fit to be identified.

\paragraph{F2. The permutation test fails to reject $H_0$: $\lambda$ does not scale with $N$.}
\label{par:f2-lambda-perm}
Across the 12 anchors, $\log_{10}\lambda \sim \log_{10} N$ gives
slope $= -0.138$ per decade, 10{,}000-permutation null distribution
mean $= -0.003$, sd $= 0.387$, two-sided $p = 0.738$ ($z = -0.35$).
The observed slope is \emph{not more extreme than what we would get
under a random pairing} of model size and learning rate.  Bootstrap-CI
slope for the filtered subset (RMSE $\le$ panel median) is
$[-0.89, +0.88]$, which also includes 0.  For the $R_\mathrm{inf}$
regression the permutation test gives $p = 0.506$ (slope $= -0.159$,
null sd $= 0.224$).  Neither the ceiling nor the learning rate scales
with model size; this is the empirical negative result that closes
Q13.  Table~\ref{tab:iter109-perm}.

\paragraph{F3. The lambda-vs-N failure is not family-asymmetric.}
Within-family regressions give dense: slope $= -0.78$, bootstrap CI
$[-3.70, +2.90]$, permutation $p = 0.424$; MoE: slope $= -0.40$,
bootstrap CI $[-1.43, +0.66]$, permutation $p = 0.441$.  Both families
fail the scaling test independently.  This is stronger than iter105's
finding that $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ is family-asymmetric -- here,
the failure of the lambda-vs-N scaling law is family-\emph{invariant},
so the null result is not driven by a single architecture type.
Table~\ref{tab:iter109-family}.

\paragraph{F4. Lambda is unidentifiable on every anchor (CV > 1.0 for all 12).}
The bootstrap-CI span $(\lambda_\mathrm{hi} - \lambda_\mathrm{lo}) / (2 |\lambda_\mathrm{med}|)$ exceeds 1.0 on all 12 anchors; the worst offender is Nemotron-120B (CV $\approx 437$, $\lambda_\mathrm{CI} = [0.0007, 1.662]$) and the best is still 2.71 (DeepSeek-V3.1).  This means that on the 12-anchor GSM8K panel, $\lambda$ is not identifiable at the trace level: the data is consistent with any learning rate spanning two-plus orders of magnitude.  This is a structural limitation of GRPO reward traces of length 3--30 steps -- they are too short and too noisy to pin down a continuous learning-rate parameter under a saturation model.  Combined with F2 this means: there is no identifiable per-anchor $\lambda$ AND there is no $\lambda$-vs-$N$ scaling law.  Table~\ref{tab:iter109-stability}.

\paragraph{F5. Time-to-saturation onset is bimodal: anchors either saturate at step 1 or never reach the 90\% threshold within the trace.}
The 48-row $t_X$ table (4 fractions $\times$ 12 anchors) shows that 7/12 anchors reach $t_{50}$ at step 1 (the trace \emph{starts} above 50\% of its peak), 5/12 reach $t_{90}$ within 3 steps, and 0/12 anchors take more than 5 steps to reach $t_{90}$.  The traces are \emph{either already saturated} (peaked within the first 1--3 steps and then either holding or drifting) or \emph{never saturate} (Kimi-K2-Thinking never reaches $t_{90}$ at all in 20 steps, $R_\mathrm{max} = 1.0$ but only at the final step).  This is consistent with iter85's three-phase partition, in which the slow-start phase is often skipped entirely because the policy starts near its eventual ceiling -- which is exactly why the 2-param fit hits $\lambda = 10$ on 4/5 anchors.

\paragraph{F6. Nemotron-120B collapse audit under 3-param fit: $\lambda = 0.002$ [$0.001, 1.662$], recovery ratio 4.20$\times$.}
Under the 3-param fit Nemotron-120B has $\lambda = 0.0019$ with bootstrap CI $[0.0007, 1.662]$ -- essentially the slowest possible learning rate.  The peak reward of $0.875$ (achieved at step 3) is not retained: late-window mean is $0.208$, giving a peak/late-mean ratio of $4.20\times$.  Zero-reward fraction is $0.55$ (11/20 steps).  The 3-param fit assigns $R_\mathrm{inf} = 1.5$ (the upper bound), which is uninformative -- the data is consistent with the model never escaping its own initial transient spike.  This is a sharper version of iter85's collapse classification: not only does the trace fail to converge to a stable ceiling, but the saturation-fit ceiling is itself unidentifiable.

\paragraph{F7. Sharpest falsifiable statement.}
$\lambda$ does not scale with $N$ on the 12-anchor GSM8K panel (permutation $p = 0.738$, $n = 12$, all 95\% bootstrap CIs include 0, family-stratified $p \ge 0.42$ on both dense and MoE subsets).  Combined with iter105's failure of $R_\mathrm{max}^\star(N)$ ($R^2 \le 0.054$, LOFO family-invariance score $= 0.376$) and the present finding that $\lambda$ is unidentifiable on every anchor (CV $> 1$ for all 12), the operational scaling-law question for GRPO post-training is closed: \emph{model size alone predicts neither the asymptotic ceiling $R_\mathrm{max}^\star$ nor the per-step learning rate $\lambda$}.  Any future reporting of a GRPO post-training scaling coefficient must condition on architecture family, on instruct-vs-base initialization, and on collapse/drift status -- not on size alone.
