% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Three-Phase Hypothesis (Nimmaturi et al.): Summary-Reconstructed Falsification Battery}
\label{sec:scaling-law-iter33}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Nimmaturi et al.~\citep{nimmaturi2025predictive}
(\emph{Predictive Scaling Laws for Efficient GRPO Training of Large
Reasoning Models}) propose that GRPO training proceeds in three
\emph{consistent} phases: \emph{slow start}, \emph{rapid improvement},
\emph{plateau}. Iter~17 observed that the five-anchor frontier set
splits into four phase labels (plateau, saturation, drift, collapse)
under a heuristic rule, but the falsification battery was implicit and
the Nemotron-120B collapse was not mechanically tested across all
twelve anchors. Iter~33 evaluates an exploratory 12-anchor battery on
the summary-reconstructed frontier table (complementing the raw
per-step BIC and monotonicity tests on the $n=5$ and $n=7$ long-horizon
anchors in \secref{par:scaling-iter117} and \secref{par:scaling-iter133}).

\paragraph{Internally recorded predictions.}
The three-phase hypothesis yields five falsifiable predictions on the
twelve-anchor frontier set:
\begin{description}
\item[P1.] Rapid improvement happens $>$10\% into the trace:
  $\mathrm{peak\_step}/n_{\mathrm{steps}} > 0.10$.
\item[P2.] Improvement is positive (not a drift):
  $\mathrm{late\_mean} - \mathrm{early\_mean} > 0$.
\item[P3.] Plateau holds: $\lvert \mathrm{late\_mean} - \mathrm{peak} \rvert
  \le \sqrt{\mathrm{var}} + 0.05$.
\item[P4.] A trace-level \emph{phase score}
  $(\mathrm{slope_{early}} - \mathrm{slope_{late}}) /
  (\lvert\mathrm{slope_{early}}\rvert + \lvert\mathrm{slope_{late}}\rvert
  + \varepsilon)$ predicts mean reward (Pearson $\rho$).
\item[P5.] Nemotron-120B is the unique trace with the joint
  extreme-collapse signature
  $\mathrm{zero\_frac} > 0.50 \wedge \mathrm{mean} < 0.20$.
\end{description}
P1--P3 are the three-phase-hypothesis predictions proper; P4 is a
sanity check that the phase score carries information about
performance; P5 isolates the summary signature of the Nemotron-120B
collapse observed in iter~17.

\paragraph{Method.}
Because five of the twelve anchors in
\texttt{experiments/results/scaling\_law\_extended\_frontier.tsv} are
short probes ($T \in \{3, 4, 5\}$) stored as 6-statistic summaries
($\mathrm{mean}, \mathrm{var}, \mathrm{peak\_step}, \mathrm{early\_mean},
\mathrm{late\_mean}, \mathrm{zero\_frac}$), the driver
\texttt{scripts/scaling\_law\_iter33.py} constructs a piecewise
synthetic reward trace from each anchor's summary tuple and applies the
iter~17 four-class rule (plateau / saturation / drift / collapse). We
emphasise that this 6-statistic reconstruction is a coarse summary
approximation: P1, P2, P3, and P5 depend only on the exact summary
columns in \texttt{scaling\_law\_extended\_frontier.tsv}, whereas P4
and post-peak slope estimates depend on the synthetic interpolation and
are superseded by the raw per-step analyses in
\secref{par:scaling-nemotron-autopsy} and \secref{par:scaling-iter117}.
Each trace is classified along the phase-score axis (1.0 = pure plateau,
0.0 = pure linear, $< 0$ = drift or collapse), and the five predictions
are tested with explicit $p$-values: binomial exact for P1--P3 and P5,
Pearson for P4. Phase stability is quantified by leave-one-out
bootstrap agreement ($B=200$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The three-phase hypothesis of \citet{nimmaturi2025predictive} is \textbf{partially
falsified} on this benchmark: P1 (``rapid improvement happens
$>$10\% in'') and P2 (``late $>$ early'') both fail. P2 fails because
3/12 traces are drifts (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B,
Kimi-K2-Thinking) and 1/12 is a collapse (Nemotron-120B); together
these four traces have $\mathrm{late\_mean} < \mathrm{early\_mean}$.
The three-phase hypothesis implicitly assumes the post-peak plateau
holds in mean, which is violated on these four traces. P3 (the literal
``plateau holds'') is borderline ($p=0.39$); P4 (phase score predicts
mean reward) is decisively falsified ($\rho = 0.046$, $p = 0.92$),
meaning the trace-level shape does \emph{not} predict the final reward
in this dataset---what predicts final reward is the
\emph{pre-RL capability regime}, not the training-curve shape.

\textbf{P5 is the sharpest sustained finding.} Nemotron-120B is the
only trace in the twelve-anchor set with the joint
$\mathrm{zero\_frac} > 0.50 \wedge \mathrm{mean} < 0.20$ signature.
This is a clean \emph{single-attribute} uniqueness claim: every other
frontier trace has either $\mathrm{zero\_frac} \le 0.34$ (the next
worst is Qwen3.5-27B at 0.33) or $\mathrm{mean} \ge 0.25$ (the next
lowest is Qwen3-8B at 0.29). The Nemotron-120B collapse is therefore
\emph{extreme} and \emph{unique} in the data; it is not a generic
large-model failure mode but a specific mechanism that the other
traces do not exhibit.

\paragraph{Nemotron-120B collapse mechanism and synthetic-vs-raw slope reconciliation.}
Iter~17 applied three raw-trace collapse criteria ($\hat R_{\max} \ge 0.40$,
$\bar R_{\text{late}} < 0.4 \hat R_{\max}$, and $\mathrm{zero\_frac} \ge 0.30$;
\tableref{tab:scaling-nemotron-autopsy}), which uniquely flag
Nemotron-120B on the raw 20-step trace (where steps 1--2 are $0.000$,
step~3 spikes to $0.875$, and steps 4--20 decay with OLS slope
$-0.0036$/step across steps 4--20, or $-0.015$/step including step~3,
to a post-peak BIC segment mean of $0.154$ and last-5-step mean of
$0.208$). By contrast, when \texttt{scaling\_law\_iter33.py}
reconstructs a 20-step trace from the 6 summary statistics alone, it
places the $55\%$ zero-reward block immediately after step~3 and
assigns $\mathrm{late\_mean} = 0.208$ to the final window, creating an
artificial positive slope ($+0.0096$/step) between the mid-trace zeros
and the tail window:
\begin{itemize}
\item Under the synthetic 6-statistic reconstruction (\texttt{zero\_frac} $> 0.3$,
  synthetic post-peak slope $< 0$, peak $< 0.95$), Nemotron-120B meets
  2/3 criteria because the summary-reconstructed post-peak segment
  rises from $0.000$ to $\bar R_{\text{late}} = 0.208$.
\item 3/12 synthetic traces meet $\ge 2$ of the three criteria:
  \texttt{Qwen3-32B}, \texttt{Qwen3.5-27B}, and \texttt{Nemotron-120B}
  (with \texttt{Qwen3.5-27B} meeting all three on its 4-step probe).
\end{itemize}
This comparison highlights why the raw per-step trace in
\tableref{tab:scaling-nemotron-autopsy} and \tableref{tab:scaling-iter117-bic}
is required for post-peak slope estimation, whereas the model-free
summary threshold ($\mathrm{zero\_frac} > 0.50 \wedge \mathrm{mean} < 0.20$)
robustly isolates Nemotron-120B even from aggregated statistics.

\paragraph{Phase stability.}
Bootstrap ($B=200$) leave-one-out agreement of the four-class
classifier is low: only 3/12 traces have agreement $\ge 0.95$
(Qwen3-8B, gpt-oss-20B, Qwen3-30B-MoE). The remaining 9/12 traces
flip phase class on the majority of bootstrap resamples. This is not
a bug in the classifier: it is a property of the twelve-anchor set,
where summary statistics from $n \le 30$ step traces are not
sufficient to fix the phase label under resampling. The classification
is therefore \emph{useful as a partition of the frontier set} but
\emph{not} a stable estimator on individual traces---a caveat the
three-phase hypothesis does not address.

\paragraph{Cross-architecture phase distribution.}
Under the corrected architecture classification (\tableref{tab:scaling-moe-vs-dense}),
5 of the 12 anchors are dense and 7 are MoE (whereas the legacy
\texttt{scaling\_law\_iter33.py} script grouped \texttt{Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B}
with dense models in a 6-vs-6 split):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Corrected 5 dense vs.\ 7 MoE:} dense ($n=5$) has 2 plateau,
  1 saturation, 2 drift, 0 collapse; MoE ($n=7$) has 2 plateau,
  3 saturation, 1 drift, 1 collapse (\texttt{Nemotron-120B}).
\item \textbf{Legacy 6-vs-6 script split:} dense ($n=6$) had 2 plateau,
  1 saturation, 2 drift, 1 collapse; MoE ($n=6$) had 2 plateau,
  3 saturation, 1 drift, 0 collapse (Mann-Whitney on phase score
  $p{=}0.48$; $\chi^2$ contingency $p{=}0.31$).
\end{itemize}
Under both splits, architecture does not significantly predict phase
class ($p > 0.30$), and under the corrected LatentMoE assignment the
sole collapse anchor belongs to the MoE class.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1) The phase classifier in \texttt{scaling\_law\_iter33.py} operates
on \emph{synthetic} traces reconstructed from the 6 summary statistics
in \texttt{scaling\_law\_extended\_frontier.tsv}, not raw per-step
reward logs (see \secref{par:scaling-iter117} and \secref{par:scaling-iter133}
for raw per-step tests).
(2) The five internally recorded predictions are not independent
(P1, P2, P3 share information); a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of
$\alpha/5 = 0.01$ would still falsify P2, P4 and still sustain P5.
(3) $n=12$ is too small for definitive arch-level contingency inference; the
Mann-Whitney result should be treated as descriptive.
