% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Functional-Form Identifiability: Is GRPO Reward Growth Exponential?}
\label{sec:scaling-law-iter37}
\label{sec:iter37}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Iter~17--33 fit $R(t) = R_{\max}\,(1 - e^{-\lambda t})$ to the twelve-anchor
frontier set as the default saturation form, and showed that the
three-phase hypothesis (slow start, rapid improvement, plateau) is
\emph{partially falsified} on that set (P2 fails: 4/12 anchors are drift
or collapse; P3 borderline; P4 decisively falsified; only P5 -- Nemotron
uniqueness -- sustained, see \S\ref{sec:scaling-law-iter33}). None of
the prior iters, however, tested whether the concave exponential
\emph{itself} is the right functional form when training curves do
exhibit a non-trivial climb.

\paragraph{Method.}
Iter~37 fits five candidate reward-curve forms to each anchor via
nonlinear least squares and ranks them by AIC and BIC with Akaike
weights:
\begin{description}
\item[(A)] Exponential saturation: $R_{\max}\,(1 - e^{-\lambda t})$
\item[(B)] Michaelis-Menten: $R_{\max}\,t/(t_{1/2} + t)$
\item[(C)] Hill ($n=2$): $R_{\max}\,t^2/(K^2 + t^2)$
\item[(D)] Power-law approach: $R_{\max}\,(1 - (1 + t)^{-\alpha})$
\item[(E)] Linear (null/baseline): $a + b\,t$
\end{description}
We run the battery three ways to separate summary-reconstruction
artefacts from genuine per-step functional-form signal:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Summary-reconstructed (iter37 main, 12 anchors):} the
  piecewise-linear per-step trace reconstruction from the 6 summary
  statistics in \texttt{scaling\_law\_extended\_frontier.tsv} used in
  iter~33, providing a direct check on whether summary-reconstructed
  traces can support nonlinear curve fitting.
\item \textbf{Dynamic-only summary-reconstructed (iter37b, 10 anchors):}
  filter to $\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{reward}) > 0.005$, removing the two
  flat short-horizon anchors (\texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst},
  \texttt{Qwen3-235B-MoE}) that have zero curvature signal by
  construction.
\item \textbf{Raw per-step cross-check (iter37c, 10 runs):} fit the
  same five forms directly to the \emph{raw} 40-step per-step reward
  trajectories from the same-stack PPO/GRPO runs in
  \texttt{experiments/results/samestack\_ppo\_grpo.json}
  (5 GRPO + 5 PPO seeds, \texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B}).
\end{itemize}
We further run a parametric bootstrap ($B=500$, $\sigma=0.10$ added
reward noise) to estimate the win-rate of each form under sampling
variability, and a 2000-replicate column-permutation test of the
hypothesis ``$w_{\mathrm{sat}} = \text{uniform}(1/5)$''.

\paragraph{Results: summary-reconstructed 12-anchor set (iter37) and dynamic 10-anchor subset (iter37b).}
On the 12-anchor frontier set reconstructed from 6 summary statistics,
the linear baseline wins AIC on 9/12 anchors and exponential
saturation (A) wins on only 1/12 (\texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE}), with mean
Akaike weight $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{sat}} = 0.044$ (bootstrap win-share
$0.096$ vs.\ $0.732$ for linear). Removing the two zero-variance probes
(\texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst}, \texttt{Qwen3-235B-MoE}) in \texttt{iter37b}
leaves 10 dynamic summary traces where linear still wins 7/10 and
saturation wins 1/10 ($\bar{w}_{\mathrm{linear}}=0.66$,
$\bar{w}_{\mathrm{hill}}=0.13$, $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{MM}}=0.10$,
$\bar{w}_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.05$, $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{power}}=0.06$;
column-permutation $p=1.0$). This confirms an important methodological
lesson: \emph{6-statistic summary reconstructions linearly interpolate
between early, peak, and late means, erasing real step-level curvature
and biasing model selection toward the linear baseline}.

\paragraph{Results: raw per-step cross-check (iter37c).}
When we instead fit the same five candidate forms to the \emph{raw}
40-step per-step reward trajectories from
\texttt{samestack\_ppo\_grpo.json} (\texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B}, 5 seeds each
of GRPO and PPO), the linear baseline loses completely: on GRPO, linear
wins 0/5, the sigmoidal Hill ($n=2$) form wins 3/5, and exponential
saturation wins 2/5 (mean Akaike weights $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{hill}}=0.62$,
$\bar{w}_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.38$, all other forms $\le 0.001$). On PPO,
Hill ($n=2$) wins 2/5 (and achieves the highest mean Akaike weight
$\bar{w}_{\mathrm{hill}}=0.40$, followed by $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{MM}}=0.25$,
$\bar{w}_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.21$, and $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{linear}}=0.14$).
Notably, this resolves the apparent GRPO-vs-PPO divergence in
\secref{sec:scaling-law-iter29}: when the candidate set was restricted
to $\{\text{constant}, \text{linear}, \text{exponential saturation}\}$,
PPO's initial critic warm-up lag prevented the concave exponential from
fitting steps 1--10 and forced AICc to pick the linear model; once the
sigmoidal Hill ($n=2$) form is included, both GRPO and PPO prefer
saturating nonlinear kinetics, with Hill ($n=2$) accommodating PPO's
slower initial inflection.

\paragraph{Interpretation: summary traces cannot identify curvature, whereas raw 40-step trajectories favour Hill ($n=2$) and exponential saturation.}
The contrast between \texttt{iter37}/\texttt{iter37b} (linear wins on
summary-reconstructed traces) and \texttt{iter37c} (Hill $n=2$ and
exponential saturation win on raw 40-step traces) has two causes:
(1)~the 12-anchor frontier traces are either already pre-saturated at
step~1 or only 3--5 steps long, and reconstructing per-step trajectories
from 6 summary statistics destroys curvature; (2)~on genuine 40-step
learning trajectories that climb from $\approx 0.25$ to $0.99$,
nonlinear saturation is strongly supported and the sigmoidal Hill
($n=2$) curve slightly outperforms the concave exponential.

\paragraph{Extrapolation comparison (iter37d).}
We re-run the iter~21 two-anchor log-log extrapolation battery under
both forms. On the 6-anchor holdout (\texttt{Qwen3-32B},
\texttt{Qwen3.5-27B}, \texttt{DeepSeek-V3.1}, \texttt{Qwen3-235B-MoE},
\texttt{Nemotron-120B}, \texttt{Kimi-K2-Thinking}),
the mean absolute error on $R_{\max}$ is essentially identical:
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.907$, $\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{hill}}=0.908$.
The $t_{80}$ predictions diverge wildly: $\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{sat}}=584$,
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{hill}}=2.4 \times 10^{8}$. The reason is that
on the frontier traces the saturation fit hits the upper
bound $\lambda \le 5$ and the Hill fit hits the lower bound $K \ge
0.001$, making $t_{80}$ unidentifiable from a 3--30 step trace.
This reinforces \secref{par:scaling-iter25}: $t_{80}$ is not
identifiable on pre-saturated frontier traces under either functional
form.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
For frontier-trace analysis on $n_{\mathrm{steps}} \le 30$ records,
report the saturation form as the literature default alongside the
Hill ($n=2$) fit and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}$; on $n_{\mathrm{steps}} \ge 30$
raw per-step learning curves (such as the 40-step PPO/GRPO trajectories),
include the sigmoidal Hill ($n=2$) family so that critic warm-up lag is
not misclassified as linear drift.

\paragraph{Sharpest claim.}
\textit{``The literature's $R(t) = R_{\max}(1 - e^{-\lambda t})$
parameterisation is not uniquely identified on the 12-anchor frontier
set (where step-1 pre-saturation and short horizons favour constant or
linear baselines) or on the 10-run raw 40-step benchmark (where the
sigmoidal Hill $n=2$ form wins or ties across both GRPO and PPO once
initial warm-up curvature is modelled).''}
