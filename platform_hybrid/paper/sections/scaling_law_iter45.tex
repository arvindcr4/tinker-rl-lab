\subsection{Chinchilla-style iso-compute extrapolation (iter45)}
\label{sec:scaling-law-iter45}
\label{sec:iter45}
\paragraph{Setup.} Iter21--41 all fit $R(t)=R_{\max}(1-e^{-\lambda t})$ as a function of \emph{optimisation steps} $t$, holding everything else fixed. Here we ask the operational question: \textbf{at a fixed training-compute budget, does $R_{\max}$ scale with compute proxy $C_{\mathrm{proxy}} = P \cdot n_{\rm steps} \cdot L_{\rm bar}$ ($L_{\rm bar}=512$ tokens)?} For MoE models we track both nominal total parameter count $P_{\rm tot}$ and per-token active parameter count $P_{\rm act}$, and we enforce the physical accuracy ceiling $R_{\max} \in [0, 1]$ (comparing against the legacy unconstrained \texttt{scaling\_law\_iter45.py} run in which \texttt{Nemotron-120B} was assigned the unconstrained box bound $R_{\max}=2.000$ and misclassified as \texttt{dense}; see \secref{sec:iter61} for the full leverage audit).

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
 GRPO $R_{\max}$ is \emph{not iso-compute invariant} (P1 passes: models at nearly identical compute budgets $C_{\rm tot}$, such as \texttt{Qwen3-8B} vs.\ \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} at $\log_{10} C = 5.090$ or \texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE} vs.\ \texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst}, differ in $R_{\max}$ by $0.538$--$0.667$ units). However, the apparent within-dense compute slope $\alpha_{\rm dense}=1.030$ in the legacy run was a non-significant ($p=0.115$) single-point leverage artifact of assigning \texttt{Nemotron-120B} to \texttt{dense} with an unconstrained $R_{\max}=2.000$; enforcing $R_{\max} \in [0,1]$ and the true 5-dense / 7-MoE split reduces $\alpha_{\rm dense}$ to $+0.340$ ($p=0.750$) and $\alpha_{\rm all}$ to $+0.097$ ($p=0.505$), as proven formally in \secref{sec:iter61}.
