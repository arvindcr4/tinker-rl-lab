\subsection{Two-parameter iso-FLOP joint fit and LOO predictive decomposition (iter49)}
\label{sec:scaling-law-iter49}
\label{sec:iter49}
\paragraph{Setup.}
Iter~45 fixed a single axis --- $\log_{10}C$ where $C_{\rm proxy}=P\cdot n_{\rm steps}\cdot L_{\rm bar}$ --- and tested 1D compute slopes. Iter~49 promotes the joint fit to two parameters, $R_{\max}=a\,\log_{10}P + b\,\log_{10}C + c$, over the 12 frontier anchors, and asks (i) whether the joint fit is predictive under leave-one-out (LOO), (ii) which phase class carries the largest LOO residual, and (iii) given a target compute budget $C^{\star}$, which anchor $(P^{\star}, C^{\star})$ the closed-form predictor selects. To maintain full provenance with the downstream diagnostics in \secref{sec:iter53} and \secref{sec:iter61}, Table~\ref{tab:iter49-ols} reports both the corrected $[0,1]$-bounded 5-dense / 7-MoE fit and the legacy unconstrained 6-vs-6 fit stored in \texttt{scaling\_law\_iter49\_joint\_fit.tsv}.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
On the joint anchor pool ($n=12$) the linear combination carries almost no signal ($R^2=0.087$ on the bounded table; $R^2=0.184$ on the legacy unconstrained table). Moreover, comparing the top and bottom panels of Table~\ref{tab:iter49-ols} previews the central finding of \secref{sec:iter61}: the legacy within-dense $R^2=0.779$ ($\alpha_{\log C}=0.980$) was an artifact of including \texttt{Nemotron-120B} with its unconstrained $R_{\max}=2.000$ in the dense subset; under the bounded $[0,1]$ fit and true 5-dense / 7-MoE split, $\alpha_{\log C}$ drops to $+0.119$ on dense models.

In both the legacy unconstrained fit and the bounded $[0,1]$ fit, \texttt{Nemotron-120B} produces the largest single leave-one-out error ($|r|=1.202$ in the legacy fit where $R_{\max}=2.000$ vs.\ predicted $0.798$; $|r|=0.659$ in the bounded fit where $R_{\max}=0.182$ vs.\ predicted $0.841$), confirming that the two-parameter $(\log_{10} P, \log_{10} C)$ linear surface cannot predict the \texttt{Nemotron-120B} collapse.

\paragraph{Iso-FLOP optimal anchor picker.}
Applying $\hat{R}_{\max}=a\,\log_{10}P + b\,\log_{10}C + c$ at $\log_{10}C^{\star}\in[4.5,7.1]$, \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} wins every budget in the low-to-mid range because $\alpha_{\log P} < 0$ in the pooled fit: at fixed compute $C^{\star}$, a smaller $P$ allows proportionally more training steps $n_{\rm steps}$.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The two-parameter iso-FLOP joint fit fails out-of-sample prediction on both the bounded ($R^2=0.087$, LOO RMSE $=0.346$) and legacy ($R^2=0.184$, LOO RMSE $=0.504$) tables, and its largest residual consistently isolates \texttt{Nemotron-120B}.
