\paragraph{Iter 65 elevation: phase-conformity geometry of the three-phase hypothesis.}
\label{par:scaling-iter65}
\label{sec:scaling-law-iter65}
\label{sec:iter65}
The iter 33 battery tested the canonical three-phase hypothesis
(\emph{slow start, rapid improvement, plateau}) of
\citet{nimmaturi2025predictive} with five summary statistics
and reported $4/5$ falsified.  Iter 37 sharpened the test with
hold-out anchors; iter 41 re-ran on the canonical 12-anchor pool;
iter 45 reframed it as iso-compute extrapolation.  What remained
as a gap is a \emph{geometric} test that characterises the
per-phase OLS slopes (slow, fast, plateau) and quantifies how
well each trace's three slopes match the canonical template.

This iteration formalises the test with a phase-conformity index
(PCI) and bootstrap-resampled phase boundaries.

\paragraph{Per-trace three-piece decomposition.}
We fit three OLS segments per trace by choosing two changepoints $(i, j)$ that minimise the
sum of within-segment squared errors.  Because 5 of the 12 anchors are short capability probes with $n_{\text{steps}} \in \{3, 4, 5\} < 6$ (\texttt{Qwen3-32B}, \texttt{Qwen3.5-27B}, \texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE}, \texttt{Qwen3-30B-MoE-Inst}, \texttt{Qwen3-235B-MoE}), \texttt{scripts/scaling\_law\_iter65.py} evaluates the 12-anchor pool on the 6-statistic synthetic step-trace reconstructions (padded to $\ge 6$ grid points), whereas restricting 3-segment OLS strictly to the $n=7$ long-horizon anchors with $n_{\text{steps}} \ge 20$ marks those 5 short probes as \texttt{insufficient\_data} and leaves only $1/7$ long-horizon traces (\texttt{DeepSeek-V3.1}) with the \texttt{three-phase} class label.  The three canonical slope
targets are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    |m_1| &< m_{\text{slow}}^{\max} = 0.020,\\
    m_2 &\geq m_{\text{fast}}^{\min} = 0.040,\\
    |m_3| &< m_{\text{plat}}^{\max} = 0.015,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:iter65-pci}
\end{equation}
in reward units per step.  The PCI is then
\[
  \mathrm{PCI} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \mathbb{1}[\text{constraint}_k] \cdot \left(1 + 0.5 \cdot w_k\right),
\]
with $w_k \in [0, 1]$ the proximity-to-bound weight (or $\mathrm{PCI}_{\text{base}} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \mathbb{1}[\text{constraint}_k] \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ before proximity weighting, with the weighted score clipped to $[0, 3.0]$ in \texttt{scaling\_law\_iter65.py} and reaching up to $4.5$ unclipped when $w_k = 1$).  A trace
failing all three constraints has $\mathrm{PCI} = 0.0$.

\paragraph{Architecture composition.}
Across both the corrected 5-dense / 7-MoE architecture split (where \texttt{Nemotron-120B}, $\mathrm{PCI}=2.00$, is classified as \texttt{moe}) and the legacy 6-vs-6 split, mean PCI is slightly \emph{lower} for MoE than for dense models ($1.64$ vs.\ $1.70$ under 5-dense / 7-MoE; $1.58$ vs.\ $1.75$ under 6-vs-6; \tableref{tab:iter65-arch}), and neither architecture has a
majority of three-phase traces.  This is consistent with the
iter 41 cross-scale result that the saturation law is
\emph{taxonomic} (partitioning the frontier into saturated vs
unsaturated) rather than \emph{predictive}.

\paragraph{What iter 65 proves.}
The three-phase hypothesis is geometrically falsified: only
\textbf{2/12}
anchors (\textbf{1/7} long-horizon traces) receive the \texttt{three-phase} label, and \textbf{6/12} (\textbf{5/7} long-horizon traces) fall into the non-monotone \emph{valley} class (\tableref{tab:iter65-class-dist}).
Wait-and-recover or peak-then-decay trajectories violate the monotone slow-start $\to$ fast-rise $\to$ flat-plateau template of \citet{nimmaturi2025predictive}: on the summary-reconstructed trace, \texttt{Nemotron-120B} is grouped into \texttt{valley} ($m_1 > 0, m_2 < 0, m_3 = 0$), whereas on the raw 20-step trace (\tableref{tab:scaling-iter117-bic}) its 3-segment BIC decomposition isolates its post-peak collapse ($\{0.000, 0.875, 0.154\}$).  Iter 65 sharpens iter 33 by
showing that the falsification is not driven by a single
metric but by the joint $(m_1, m_2, m_3)$ slope signature across
all three phases.  The PCI provides a single-number summary
that can be tracked across training budgets and group sizes;
the iter 33 battery's $4/5$ falsification rate is consolidated
into a single geometric statistic.
