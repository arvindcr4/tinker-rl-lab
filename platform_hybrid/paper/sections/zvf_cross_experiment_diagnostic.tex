\section{ZVF as a Cross-Experiment Failure Diagnostic}
\label{sec:zvf-cross-experiment}

Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:zvf-formalization} formalizes ZVF and
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:zvf-pipeline} specifies the per-framework
reward-field mapping. This section closes the
diagnostic loop by aggregating every ZVF stream in our measurement
matrix into a single per-experiment summary, attaching a deterministic
failure taxonomy, and reporting the correlation between mean-ZVF and
the heldout-accuracy outcome. The artifact is
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_summary.tsv} and is regenerated from
\texttt{scripts/zvf\_diagnostic.py}.

\subsection{Failure Taxonomy}

We classify every experiment cell into one of four mutually exclusive
buckets with an ordered rule cascade on the heldout-accuracy trajectory
(\texttt{classify} in \texttt{platform\_modal/scripts/zvf\_diagnostic.py});
the first rule that fires assigns the label:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{collapse} &\;\Leftarrow\; \ell \le 0.001 \;\vee\; (p > 0.7 \,\wedge\, \ell < 0.35) \label{eq:collapse}\\
\text{else}\quad\mathrm{drift} &\;\Leftarrow\; \ell < 0.85\,p \label{eq:drift}\\
\text{else}\quad\mathrm{plateau} &\;\Leftarrow\; p < 0.5 \label{eq:plateau}\\
\text{else}\quad\mathrm{converged} & \label{eq:converged}
\end{align}
where $p$ is the peak heldout accuracy observed in the run and
$\ell$ is the average over the last $10$ optimizer steps. The
thresholds are not tuned to our data: $\ell \le 0.001$ catches runs that end
with no correct answers at all (including runs that never had any),
$p > 0.7$ requires that the run \emph{reached} a useful operating point,
and $\ell < 0.35$ requires that the run \emph{lost} it by the end. The
\texttt{collapse} bucket is the answer to the
\textsc{collapse-or-recovery} question posed by reviewer~W3.

\subsection{Signature Failure Cells}

Three cells isolate ZVF as a diagnostic on real, non-synthetic data.

\paragraph{Tool-use collapse (ZVF\,=\,1.0 everywhere).}
The two cross-tool trajectories (\texttt{cross\_tool\_qwen3-32b},
\texttt{cross\_tool\_llama-8b-inst}; source
\texttt{experiments/results/tool\_code\_reward\_diagnostics.tsv})
emit reward $= 0.0$ on every rollout of every step: $\mathrm{ZVF}_t
= 1.0$ for all $t$ and heldout $= 0.0$ for the entire
30-step window. They fall in \texttt{collapse} through the first clause
of Eq.~\ref{eq:collapse} ($\ell = 0$); they never reached an operating point,
so ``collapse'' here means zero signal throughout rather than a lost peak.

\paragraph{Nemotron-120B collapse (peak $0.875 \to$ late $0.154$).}
The five-model three-phase scaling-law table
(\texttt{experiments/results/scaling\_law\_three\_phase.tsv})
contains one row whose peak segment-mean accuracy is $p = 0.875$ and
whose late segment-mean accuracy is $\ell = 0.1544$; under
Eq.~\ref{eq:collapse} this is \texttt{collapse}. It is the \emph{only}
one of the five scaling-law rows that collapses: the other four recover
to their peak (three \texttt{converged}, one \texttt{plateau}) on their
segment-mean accuracies. Per-step ZVF was not recorded for this run, so
the row carries \texttt{NA} in the ZVF columns but is preserved in the
taxonomy for cross-reference.

\subsection{Correlation With Heldout Outcome (withdrawn)}
\label{sec:zvf-cross-experiment:corr}

An earlier revision reported a Spearman correlation of $\rho=0.27$
(95\% bootstrap CI $[-0.37,0.88]$) between per-cell mean ZVF and last-10
outcome over ``23 cells''. \textbf{We withdraw it as a finding.} Only 17
of the 23 cells carried a finite ZVF, and only 8 of those came from measured
training runs; 2 are $G{=}1$ tool-use cells whose ZVF is $1$ by construction; and
the outcome column mixes held-out accuracy, training reward, and synthetic-task
accuracy across different tasks and models. Restricted to the 8 measured cells
the coefficient is $-0.37$ ($p=0.36$), i.e.\ the sign is not even stable. No ZVF--outcome
association is claimed, and no association with the \texttt{collapse} label
either: the only collapse cells with logged ZVF are the two $G{=}1$ tool-use
cells.

\subsection{Relation to AERO and RL-ZVP}
\label{sec:zvf-cross-experiment:aero}

Zhang \etal~\cite{zhang2026aero} introduce AERO, while
Le \etal~\cite{le2025rlzvp} introduce RL-ZVP. Both target zero-variance prompts,
by adaptive rollout allocation or by advantage shaping on those prompts. ZVF is
the prevalence metric these methods act on. Our matched comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:zvf-cross-library}) includes AERO but not RL-ZVP: AERO's mean ZVF
($0.70$) and held-out accuracy ($0.632$) match GRPO's on that 30-step horizon,
at $44\%$ of the generated tokens.

\subsection{Practical Diagnostic Recipe}

We recommend the following two-line test to decide whether ZVF is
a useful diagnostic for a given RL post-training run:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute ZVF per optimizer step using
      \texttt{scripts/zvf\_compute\_cross\_framework.py} on the live
      training log; keep a $30$-step trailing-window mean.
\item If the trailing-window mean exceeds $0.6$ before the
      heldout-accuracy curve has plateaued near its peak, the run
      is collapsing into all-correct or all-wrong regimes and
      grouping will yield approximately zero advantage signal. The
      recipe is conservative: it flags only the regime where most
      prompts are no longer informative.
\end{enumerate}
Raw ZVF alone cannot apply this rule: the 30 converged rows in our matrix have
median ZVF $0.77$, above the trigger, because near-ceiling runs saturate into
all-correct groups. The qualifier ``before the heldout curve has plateaued near
its peak'' is therefore essential, and the signed split of
Section~\ref{sec:zvf-signed} separates the two cases directly (all-correct
saturation versus all-wrong starvation). The collapse rows with recorded per-step
ZVF (the two tool-use cells) reach ZVF $=1.0$ with zero reward, i.e.\ entirely
all-wrong groups.

\paragraph{Why this is evidence and not a circular claim.}
The collapse label is computed only from peak vs.\ last-10, never
from ZVF. The correlation between ZVF and the collapse label is
therefore not a fit. With so few collapse cells (two of them $G{=}1$
tool-use cells whose ZVF is $1$ by construction) and converged rows that also
carry high ZVF (median $0.77$), raw ZVF does not separate collapse from
convergence in this matrix; only its sign does.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We deliberately do \emph{not} report partial correlations
``controlling for advantage variance'' or ``controlling for
entropy'': under binary $\{0,1\}$ rewards, within-group advantage
variance is a deterministic transform of ZVF (zero iff ZVF) and
partialling it out is circular (already documented in
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_partial\_correlations.tsv}). Step-
level p-values are not reported either: per-step ZVF rows are
autocorrelated (typical lag-1 $\rho \approx 0.9$ on our rollout
trajectories), and any t-statistic inferred from the $480$
measurements would be overconfident by roughly an order of
magnitude.

\subsection{Group-Level ZVF Versus the Binomial Prediction (corrected)}
\label{sec:zvf-cross-source-falsification}

An earlier revision reported a ``cross-source anti-herd falsification'':
a per-problem divergence
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}}=\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}-\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{obs}}$
of $+0.1224$ (95\% CI $[+0.112,+0.133]$) on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K and $-0.067$ on
Qwen2.5-0.5B synthetic arithmetic, read as anti-herding versus herding, and
derived an ``empirical iso-$G$'' sizing table from it. \textbf{Both the
reading and the iso-$G$ table are withdrawn.}

\paragraph{The GSM8K value is sign-guaranteed.} There, $\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}$
was evaluated at the plug-in $\hat p_x=k_x/G$ from the \emph{same} group:
$\delta_x=h_G(k_x/G)-\mathbf{1}\{k_x\in\{0,G\}\}$ with
$h_G(p)=p^G+(1-p)^G$. If $k_x\in\{0,G\}$ both terms are $1$; otherwise the
indicator is $0$ and $h_G>0$. So $\delta_x\ge0$ for every group, and under
\emph{exactly} i.i.d.\ Bernoulli rollouts at the observed $\bar p=0.694$ its
expectation is already $+0.105$. The value says nothing about rollout
correlation.

\paragraph{The arithmetic sign has competing sources.} There, $p$ was the
\emph{estimated} step-mean reward $\hat{\bar p}_t$ and $\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{obs}}$
the step's batch ZVF. With heterogeneous prompts and i.i.d.\ rollouts,
$\E[\mathrm{ZVF}_t]=\E_i[h_G(p_i)]\ge h_G(\bar p_t)$ because $h_G$ is convex, which
pushes $\delta_t$ negative without any herding; but the plug-in
$h_G(\hat{\bar p}_t)$ carries a positive finite-sample bias
$\E[h_G(\hat{\bar p}_t)]-h_G(\bar p_t)\ge0$, so the sign of $\delta_t$ is not
fixed by convexity alone. The logs store only step-mean reward and step ZVF
($16$ prompts per step), not per-group counts, so the two terms cannot be
separated here.

\paragraph{Correct reading.} Batch ZVF is a convex functional of the
prompt-difficulty distribution, $\E[\mathrm{ZVF}]=\E_i[h_G(p_i)]$; its excess
over $h_G(\bar p)$ measures the dispersion of $p_i$. On the 600 GSM8K groups
the observed ZVF is $0.158$ and $h_8(\bar p)=0.054$. The $k$-histogram is
strongly over-dispersed relative to a single Binomial (likelihood-ratio deviance
$341.3$ on $7$ df, above all $20{,}000$ parametric-bootstrap replicates; the
unpooled Pearson statistic is not used because two expected cells are below $1$).
A two-parameter beta-binomial fitted to the same groups (marginal intra-class
correlation $0.160$) gives ZVF $0.170$, but only by offsetting errors: it expects
$4.2$ all-fail and $98.1$ all-success groups against $19$ and $76$ observed, and
it is rejected (Pearson $\chi^2=82.2$, $6$ df). The three seeds are independent
shuffles of the test split (503 distinct problems, 103 repeated-problem pairs
whose success counts correlate at $r=0.65$), and single-pass groups cannot
separate prompt dispersion from within-prompt rollout dependence. Recomputation:
\texttt{analysis\_publication\_fixes/p2\_delta\_artifact.py} (output in
\texttt{experiments/results/publication\_fixes/zvf\_confound/}); the goodness-of-fit,
bootstrap and repeated-problem statistics by
\texttt{tools/review\_ledger/P2\_zvf\_falsification\_note\_audit.py}. This is the
same quantity as the Advantage Collapse Rate~\cite{he2026advantage}; see
Section~\ref{sec:zvf-cross-experiment:related}.

\paragraph{Prospective prediction test (not run).} The convex-functional
reading makes a testable prediction: with $M\ge G$ independent calibration
rollouts and $m_i$ successes on prompt $i$, the unbiased estimator
$[\binom{m_i}{G}+\binom{M-m_i}{G}]/\binom{M}{G}$ of $h_G(p_i)$ (the plug-in
$h_G(m_i/M)$ is biased upward), averaged over prompts, should predict a fresh
batch's ZVF at any $G$. We have not run this test.

\subsection{Relation to Advantage-Collapse and Difficulty-Filtering Work}
\label{sec:zvf-cross-experiment:related}
ZVF is not a new diagnostic. It is equivalent, up to aggregation
granularity, to the Advantage Collapse Rate~\cite{he2026advantage}, which
quantifies the same advantage collapse under homogeneous group rewards and is
reported to predict stagnation and final performance. The corrected reading
above---true degeneracy exceeds the i.i.d.\ Bernoulli prediction by Jensen's
inequality---was proved first by~\cite{nie2026gradstarvation}. DARA~\cite{zheng2026dara}
uses the complementary active-group density to reweight rewards in
multi-reward RL. DAPO's dynamic sampling~\cite{yu2025dapo}, GRESO's
pre-rollout skipping~\cite{zhang2026greso}, VCRL's variance-based
curriculum~\cite{jiang2025vcrl}, and online difficulty
filtering~\cite{bae2025online} act on it. Our contribution here is limited
to the measurement artefacts and the corrections above.
