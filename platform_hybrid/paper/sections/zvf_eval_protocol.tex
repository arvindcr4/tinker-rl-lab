\subsection{How Many Seeds Does the Headline Need? Eval-Protocol Hardening}
\label{sec:zvf-eval-protocol}

An earlier revision applied the cost-efficiency lens of
\citet{yehudai2025survey} and the ablation discipline of
$\tau^2$-Bench~\citep{barres2025tau2} to the per-seed scores of the iter130 risk
index (9 variance-mitigation methods $\times$ 5 seeds) and concluded that one
seed suffices for the top-1 method claim. Those seeds are draws from the
simulated variance-mitigation projection, whose per-method trajectories differ
across seeds only by injected noise, and the risk index itself is withdrawn
(Section~\ref{sec:zvf-risk-index}); seed stability on them says nothing about
how many training seeds a real comparison needs, so that conclusion is withdrawn
(\texttt{experiments/results/berkeley/eval\_protocol\_summary.json} is retained
for the audit trail).

The earlier fused-risk AUROC and threshold analysis is withdrawn. It used
synthetic/imputed anchors and cannot validate calibrated monitoring, a safe
threshold, compute savings, or a stopping policy. The reviewed early-triage
set has only two collapsed cases, both synthetic tool use and both with early
reward zero; it provides no incremental diagnostic evidence over early reward.
Future evaluation must prospectively declare the positive class, estimand,
threshold policy, and held-out decision outcome.
