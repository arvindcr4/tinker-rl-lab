\subsection{ZVF Predictive Calibration vs AERO/RL-ZVP}
\label{sec:zvf-calibration}

Iter~94 closed the cross-library diagnostic dashboard; iter~98 materialised
the over-dispersion ratio $\overline{\rho} = \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{emp}}/
\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}$. Iter~102 pushes the same diagnostic one
\emph{predictive} step further: rather than asking ``is empirical ZVF bigger
than the i.i.d.\ null?'' (a ratio), we ask ``by how much is empirical ZVF
bigger than what difficulty forces?'' (a residual). The two views are
complementary: $\rho$ is dimensionless and tracks herding pressure, while
the \emph{calibration gap}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
\Delta \;=\; \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{emp}} \;-\; \bigl(p^{G}+(1-p)^{G}\bigr)
\end{equation}
is a probability-mass residual that maps directly onto the i.i.d.\ null of
the frontier-synthesis formalisation (Round~2 of \texttt{FRONTIER\_INSIGHTS.md}).
$\Delta>0$ means the sampler herds beyond what difficulty alone would
produce; $\Delta=0$ is the i.i.d.\ null; $\Delta<0$ is anti-herding. By
contrast, $\rho$ blows up as $p\to0$ or $p\to1$ even when the i.i.d.\ null
is correctly tracked, which makes it a noisy proxy for herding when $p$ is
extreme.

\paragraph{AERO-vs-GRPO paired test (5 seeds, G=8).}
The direct empirical test of AERO's claim~\cite{le2025rlzvp} that it
\emph{detects zero-variance prompts and reshapes advantages} is to compare
AERO's $\Delta$ to GRPO's $\Delta$ on the same seeds.  We compute, for
each of the 5 seeds in \texttt{variance\_mitigation.tsv},
$\Delta_{\mathrm{method}} = \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{mean}} - (p^{8}+(1-p)^{8})$
and pair AERO against GRPO:
\[
\overline{\Delta}_{\mathrm{aero}} = 0.173, \qquad
\overline{\Delta}_{\mathrm{grpo}} = 0.417, \qquad
\overline{\Delta}_{\mathrm{aero}-\mathrm{grpo}} = -0.244.
\]
AERO runs at \emph{strictly lower} $\Delta$ on every one of the 5 paired
seeds (paired difference $-0.244$, all 5 seeds negative).  This is the
cleanest possible falsification of the null ``AERO and GRPO run at the
same herding pressure'':  the paired estimate is $-0.244$ and the rank
sign is unanimous ($5/5$).  Source:
\texttt{zvf\_iter102\_aero\_paired.tsv}, paired row in
\texttt{zvf\_iter102\_aero\_test.tsv}.

\paragraph{Calibration gap vs training failure.}
The natural follow-up diagnostic question is whether $\Delta$ predicts
the binary \texttt{is\_collapse} label better than raw $\mathrm{ZVF}$.
Across the 14 rows we compute Pearson, Spearman, and rank-AUC for both
predictors (bootstrap CIs, $B=2000$;
\texttt{zvf\_iter102\_failure\_corr.tsv}):

\begin{table}[ht]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
predictor & Pearson $\rho$ [95\% CI] & Spearman $\rho$ \\
\midrule
$\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{emp}}$          & $+0.753\;[+0.523,\,+0.970]$ & $+0.376$ \\
$\Delta$ (calibration gap) & $-0.274\;[-0.662,\,-0.043]$ & $-0.864$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Pearson/Spearman of (raw ZVF, calibration gap) against the
\texttt{is\_collapse} indicator across 14 (library, experiment) rows.
Raw $\mathrm{ZVF}$ wins on Pearson because collapsed runs hit ZVF$=1.0$
\emph{by definition} (all rewards identical). $\Delta$ wins on Spearman
because collapsed runs hit $\Delta=0$ (i.i.d.\ maximum when $p=0$) while
drift runs hit $\Delta=+0.4$ to $+0.5$ (herding beyond difficulty).}
\label{tab:zvf-calib-fail}
\end{table}

\noindent
\emph{Rank-based AUC} of $\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{emp}}$ vs \texttt{is\_collapse}
is $1.000$ (perfect separation); rank-AUC of $\Delta$ vs
\texttt{is\_collapse} is $0.167$ (anti-predictive on rank). The right
operational reading is therefore: \emph{raw ZVF is the collapse indicator}
(high ZVF $\Rightarrow$ collapse); \emph{calibration gap is the
\emph{anti}-collapse indicator} for the failure-of-difficulty sort (low
$\Delta$ $\Rightarrow$ collapse, because collapse is the i.i.d.\ maximum).
This is the cleanest separation of concerns in the iter-94/98/102 series:
ZVF answers ``is the sampler converging to zero-variance?'' while $\Delta$
answers ``by how much is it \emph{herding beyond difficulty}?''.

\paragraph{Headline summary.}
Across the 14-row cross-library aggregator, GRPO is the worst herder
($\Delta=+0.46$), AERO runs 2.3$\times$ lower ($\Delta=+0.20$), and the
5-seed paired AERO-vs-GRPO difference is $\overline{\Delta}_{\mathrm{aero}-\mathrm{grpo}}=-0.244$
with all 5 paired seeds showing the negative sign. The calibration gap
ranks libraries \emph{inversely} to training failure (Spearman $-0.864$)
because collapsed trajectories sit exactly at the i.i.d.\ maximum
$\Delta=0$, while drift trajectories are the most herded. AERO's
documented objective (anti-herding via advantage reshaping) is directly
falsified-as-claimed by the paired test: it does run at lower $\Delta$
than GRPO on the same prompts and the same group size.
