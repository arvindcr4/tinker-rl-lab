\subsection{Iso-Yield ZVF, Per-Seed AERO Traceability, and Operating-Point Sweep}
\label{sec:zvf-iso-yield-iter122}

The iter 118 section turned ZVF into a \emph{first-class}
cross-library diagnostic: pooled AUROC of mean-ZVF against
\texttt{is\_collapse} reaches $1.000$ with $95\%$ CI $[1.000,\,1.000]$,
AERO halves ZVF on the variance-mitigation suite ($-0.260$
absolute, paired bootstrap CI $[-0.262,\,-0.259]$), and the
predictive-calibration curve exposes the bimodal failure mode
(drift at low ZVF, collapse at high ZVF). Iter 122 extends the
diagnostic into an \emph{operational} layer by asking three new
questions: (a) for a fixed task, how much group-size investment
does it take to push ZVF below a target contrast level
(\emph{iso-yield dynamic grouping})? (b) is the AERO advantage
deterministic across seeds or does it vary? and (c) when ZVF is
used as a binary alarm above a threshold, what precision/recall
trade-off does it deliver?

\paragraph{Iso-yield curve: G-vs-ZVF saturates at $0.63$ for arithmetic\_synthetic.}
The groupsize-zvf-sweep experiment measured
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}(G)$ at $G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ on the arithmetic-synthetic
task (Qwen2.5-0.5B, $n_{\rm seeds}{=}3$). Iter 122 linearly
interpolates $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ in $\log_2 G$ between measurement points
and computes the minimum $G$ that achieves each $\tau$ target;
see Table~\ref{tab:zvf-iter122-iso-yield}. The empirical ZVF curve
asymptotes at $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}(16) = 0.631$, so any target $\tau < 0.50$
is UNREACHABLE through a group-size intervention alone (slope at
$G{=}16$ is shallow enough that the linear extrapolation blows
past $G{=}128$). The implication is operationally sharp: \emph{ZVF
$\ge 0.5$ is the asymptotic floor of the rollout-only group-size
lever on this task}. Reaching $\meanZVF \le 0.30$ requires an
algorithm-level intervention (e.g.\ AERO; iter 118), not just a
bigger $G$. Source:
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_iter122\_iso\_yield.tsv}.

\paragraph{Per-seed AERO traceability: the $-0.260$ gap is essentially deterministic.}
Iter 118 reported the pooled AERO-vs-GRPO gap on mean-ZVF as
$-0.260$ with paired bootstrap CI $[-0.262,\,-0.259]$. Iter 122
opens the box on that pooled estimate by reporting each of the
five seed-level gaps. Across all five seeds the gap lies in the
narrow range $[0.2584,\,0.2629]$ with mean $0.2605$ and SD
$0.0015$ (Table~\ref{tab:zvf-iter122-aero}). The across-seed
SD is $\sim\!170\times$ smaller than the gap, so the AERO
advantage is essentially deterministic: the contrast-improvement
is \emph{structural} to the algorithm, not noise. Even with
$n_{\rm seeds}{=}5$ the per-seed gap is monotone and the
five-value CDF has no outliers.
The last-10 accuracy gap is correspondingly tight
(SD $0.0010$, mean $-0.0203$): AERO consistently improves
last-10 accuracy by $\sim\!0.02$. Source:
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_iter122\_aero\_quantile.tsv}.

\paragraph{Operating-point sweep: the ``high ZVF'' classifier is precision-bounded.}
The iter 118 single-AUROC summary of mean-ZVF against
\texttt{is\_collapse} reached $1.000$, but that scalar averaged a
bimodal failure mode into one number. Iter 122 re-runs the
operating-point sweep explicitly with $\tau$ in
$\{0.30,\,0.40,\,0.50,\,0.60,\,0.70,\,0.80\}$ and the prediction
rule $\widehat{\text{collapse}} = [\meanZVF > \tau]$. For
\texttt{is\_collapse} (positive class = pure collapse),
recall is \emph{anchored} at $2/7 = 0.286$ across all $\tau \le
0.80$: only the two cross-tool tool-use collapses (ZVF $\equiv 1$)
are catchable by the high-ZVF rule, and the five scaling-law
collapse cells carry $\meanZVF{=}\texttt{NA}$ and are excluded
from the count entirely. Precision climbs monotonically with
$\tau$ ($0.20$ at $\tau{=}0.30$ to $0.67$ at $\tau{=}0.80$) but
never reaches $1.0$: the false positives are the GRPO plateau
cells whose high ZVF coexists with eventual convergence (recall
the iter 118 bimodal story). For
\texttt{is\_collapse\_or\_drift}, recall is anchored at $2/11 =
0.182$ for the same reason; the drift cells live at LOW ZVF and
the high-ZVF classifier systematically misses them. The
take-away: any single-$\tau$ alarm that uses ZVF alone will
either over-fire on plateau cells or miss drift cells, and the
two failure modes require \emph{two complementary} thresholds,
not one. Source:
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_iter122\_op\_sweep.tsv}.

\paragraph{Take-away for the paper.}
Iter 122 turns the first-class ZVF diagnostic into the three
operational artefacts the brief calls for:
(1) the iso-yield curve shows that group size $G$ has a hard
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}} \ge 0.5$ floor on this evidence base -- beyond-$G$
interventions must cede to algorithm-level contrast manipulation;
(2) the per-seed AERO traceability shows that the $-0.260$
contrast-reduction gap is essentiallydeterministic across the
$n_{\rm seeds}{=}5$ variance-mitigation measurements (SD
$0.0015$, $170\times$ smaller than the gap), so the headline
effect size inherits a confidence-itself effect: with such tight
per-seed behaviour, the bootstrap CI is essentially reporting
numerical-precision noise;
(3) the precision-recall operating-point sweep confirms that
ZVF alone is not a sufficient alarm and motivates \emph{two
complementary thresholds}: one above $\tau{=}0.7$ for the
tool-use collapse band, one below $\tau{=}0.15$ for the
variance-mitigation drift band. Frontier synthesis: ZVF is the
load-bearing cross-library diagnostic that the brief asked for
(it ives AERO-vs-GRPO a measurable, near-deterministic
contrast-gap), but it cannot collapse to a single alarm
because the failure mode is intrinsically bimodal.
