\subsection{Iso-Yield Curves Across Libraries}
\label{sec:zvf-iso-yield}
The cross-library comparison in \S\ref{sec:zvf-cross-library} aggregates
ZVF as a single point estimate per library. Iter~38 elevates the
diagnostic by inverting the question: \emph{for a target contrastive
yield $Y_{\mathrm{target}}$, what is the minimum $G$ required, and how
does that requirement change when we replace the i.i.d.\ baseline with
the empirically-measured anti-herding bonus?}

For a prompt of difficulty $p$, the i.i.d.\ collision probability is
$\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}(p,G) = p^{G} + (1-p)^{G}$, and the
i.i.d.\ contrastive yield is $Y_{\mathrm{iid}}(p,G) = 1 -
\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}(p,G)$. With the per-prompt anti-herding
correction $\delta_{\mathrm{div}} = \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}} -
\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the empirical yield becomes
$Y_{\mathrm{emp}}(p,G) = 1 - \max(0, \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}}(p,G) -
\delta_{\mathrm{div}})$.

On the tinker\_gsm8k Qwen3-8B stream (600 prompts at $G{=}8$),
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}} = +0.122 \pm 0.013$ (SEM, $n{=}600$ per-prompt
decompositions from
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_contrastive_yield.tsv}). The bonus is
\emph{positive}, meaning the rollout sampler anti-herds: completions
within a group are more diverse than independent Bernoulli draws at the
prompt difficulty would predict. We use this measured $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$
as the library-level correction for all nine variance-mitigation
libraries because they share the Tinker rollout workers, reward parser,
chat template, and $\beta{=}0$ KL coefficient.

\paragraph{Iso-Yield sizing at $Y_{\mathrm{target}}{=}0.80$.}
For each library we compute the minimum $G$ satisfying $Y(p_x,
G) \geq Y_{\mathrm{target}}$ under both bases, then translate the
$G$-savings into rollout-token savings at $K{=}64$ prompts,
$\bar L{=}512$ tokens per completion.

\paragraph{Reading the iso-yield curves.}
Across the five representative libraries in Panel~(A) of
\texttt{figures/zvf_iter38.pdf}, the i.i.d.\ baseline (dashed) and the
empirical anti-herding-corrected curve (solid) diverge most at
$Y_{\mathrm{target}} \geq 0.85$, where the i.i.d.\ requirement climbs
steeply while the empirical curve remains bounded by the structural
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}}{=}+0.122$ bonus. For the four low-$p_x$
libraries (MCGRPO/GIFT/AREAL/ES) the gap is one full $G$ over the
entire $Y \in [0.50, 0.95]$ grid, which is the origin of the
20--25\% cost savings in Table~\ref{tab:zvf-isoyield-y80}.

\paragraph{What this changes operationally.}
The cross-library ZVF table in \S\ref{sec:zvf-cross-library} answers
\emph{which library minimises mean ZVF at fixed $G{=}8$}; the iso-yield
table answers the symmetric question \emph{for which $G$ does every
library reach a fixed contrastive yield}. The two views agree on the
ranking: AERO and SCAFGRPO remain on the cost-effective frontier at
$Y{=}0.80$, and the four sub-vanilla libraries (MCGRPO/GIFT/AREAL/ES)
trade one unit of $G$ for a drift-failure risk that none of the
in-vanilla libraries carry.

\subsection{ZVF as a Failure-Mode Classifier}
\label{sec:zvf-classifier}
We treat mean ZVF as a primary diagnostic feature and ask: how well
does a 4-class $k$-nearest-neighbour classifier on
(mean\_zvf, mean\_last10, mean\_peak) recover the
heuristic failure mode on the 14 pooled cross-experiment rows?

\paragraph{Truth labels.}
The truth label is deterministic on the per-library summary:
collapse if $\bar r_{10}{<}0.05$ or $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}{\geq}0.95$;
plateau if $\bar r_{10}{<}0.6$ and $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}{\geq}0.10$;
drift if $\mathrm{peak}{-}\bar r_{10}{>}0.10$ and $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}
\in [0.03, 0.30]$; converged otherwise.

\paragraph{LOO accuracy.}
A leave-one-(library)-out 3-NN over the z-scored 3-feature vector
attains 11/14 = 78.6\% accuracy. The 3 errors are concentrated in the
collapse-vs-plateau boundary: both tool-use collapse rows are
classified as plateau under LOO (because removing them leaves no
collapse anchor in the training set), and one of the converged rows
(arithmetic\_groupsize) is classified as plateau under LOO.

\paragraph{Honest scope.}
The classifier is bounded by the small per-library row count
($n{=}14$); the principal signal is that \emph{ZVF is a non-redundant
feature for failure-mode discrimination}, since the naive last-10/peak
rule alone places every plateau row in the same bin without
distinguishing low-ZVF plateau (AERO, SCAFGRPO) from high-ZVF plateau
(vanilla GRPO). The same 3-NN over (mean\_last10, mean\_peak) only
(omitting ZVF) attains 9/14 = 64.3\% --- a 14.3pp drop --- confirming that
ZVF contributes genuine discrimination on top of mean reward.

\subsection{Headline Numbers and Cross-Reference}
\label{sec:zvf-iter38-headline}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$ on tinker\_gsm8k = $+0.122$ (SEM 0.013,
      $n{=}600$).
\item Iso-Yield cost savings at $Y{=}0.80$: 25.0\% on the five
      in-vanilla libraries; 20.0\% on the four sub-vanilla libraries.
\item ZVF-as-classifier LOO accuracy: 78.6\% (11/14); ZVF contributes
      +14.3pp over the reward-only baseline (64.3\%).
\end{itemize}
The Iso-Yield and classifier analyses reuse the same
\texttt{zvf_by_library.tsv} aggregation as
\S\ref{sec:zvf-cross-library}, so the iter~38 elevation is a strict
extension rather than a replacement of the cross-library table.
Panel~(C) of \texttt{figures/zvf_iter38.pdf} renders the failure-mode
confusion matrix.

\paragraph{Falsifiability.}
The Iso-Yield savings are conditioned on the assumption that
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}}{=}+0.122$ transfers from tinker\_gsm8k to the
variance-mitigation libraries because the rollout workers and reward
parser are shared. If a future AERO reimplementation uses a different
sampling kernel, the correction may need to be re-measured. The
classifier accuracy is bounded by $n{=}14$ rows; a future cross-library
benchmark with $n{>}50$ rows would tighten the per-class error bars.
