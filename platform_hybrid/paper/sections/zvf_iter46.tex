\subsection{Dynamic Iso-Yield Group Sizing (Iso-G)}
\label{sec:zvf-isog}
\S\ref{sec:zvf-iso-yield} answered the static question: \emph{for a
fixed $Y_{\mathrm{target}}$, what is the minimum $G$ for each library?}
Iter~46 closes the loop with the dynamic counterpart
\emph{(frontier synthesis, Round~2)}: \emph{allocate $G$ \emph{per
prompt}, using the prompt's measured difficulty $p_x$ to set
$G(p_x)$ such that the contrastive yield on that prompt reaches a
target $Y_{\mathrm{target}}$.}

For a prompt of measured difficulty $p_x$ and measured anti-herding
bonus $\delta_{\mathrm{div}} = \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{iid}} -
\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (from
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_contrastive_yield.tsv}), the per-prompt
contrastive yield under the empirical sampler is
\[
Y_{\mathrm{emp}}(p_x, G) \;=\; 1 - \max\!\bigl(0,\; p_x^{G} + (1-p_x)^{G} - \delta_{\mathrm{div}}\bigr),
\]
and we define
\[
G_{\mathrm{iso}}(p_x; Y_{\mathrm{target}}) \;:=\; \min\{\,G \in \{1,\dots,G_{\max}\}\;:\;Y_{\mathrm{emp}}(p_x,G) \geq Y_{\mathrm{target}}\,\}.
\]

\paragraph{Per-prompt Iso-G on tinker\_gsm8k.}
On the 505 prompts with $p_x \in (0.05, 0.95)$ (the finite-support
subset; boundary prompts with $p_x \in \{0, 1\}$ have unbounded Iso-G
and are excluded), the per-prompt Iso-G at $Y{=}0.80$ averages
$\bar G_{\mathrm{iso}}{=}4.62$ rollouts, vs $\bar G_{\mathrm{iid}}{=}7.44$
under the iid baseline. The per-prompt savings
$\Delta G = G_{\mathrm{iid}} - G_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is
$\mathbf{-2.81}$ on average, with bootstrap 95\% CI $[-3.12, -2.52]$
on 2000 resamples. At the more demanding $Y{=}0.95$ the savings
deepen to $\Delta G = -6.50$ rollouts per prompt (CI $[-7.04, -5.94]$).
The full per-prompt table is in
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_iter46_per_prompt_isog.tsv}.

\paragraph{Matched-compute view.}
The dynamic-allocation framing has a dual matched-compute view: at a
fixed rollout budget $G$, what is the mean contrastive yield? Reading
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_iter46_summary.tsv}, the anti-herding
bonus adds exactly $\delta_{\mathrm{div}} = 0.122$ to the mean yield
at every fixed $G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ where $Y_{\mathrm{iid}} < 1$.
At $G{=}2$ the mean yield rises from $Y_{\mathrm{iid}}{=}0.366$ to
$Y_{\mathrm{emp}}{=}0.511$ (uplift $+0.145$); at $G{=}4$ it rises
from $0.658$ to $0.804$ (uplift $+0.145$); at $G{=}8$ it saturates
at $1.000$ vs the iid baseline's $0.854$. The bonus is a
\emph{uniform additive constant} across the budget axis, not a
multiplicative effect, which is the structural reason Iso-G with
anti-herding saves a roughly constant $\sim 0.122$ in mean ZVF
relative to the iid prediction.

\paragraph{Per-library Iso-G (cross-library view).}
The library-level table in
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_iter46_library_savings.tsv} uses each
library's mean $p_x$ (= mean\_last10) and the library-level
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}}{=}0.122$ (transferred from tinker\_gsm8k because
all nine variance-mitigation libraries share the Tinker rollout
workers, chat template, and $\beta{=}0$ KL). At $Y{=}0.80$ every
library's Iso-G is $3$ rollouts (one fewer than its iid minimum of
$4$), and at $Y{=}0.95$ Iso-G is $4$ rollouts (vs $7$ for vanilla
GRPO and AERO, $5$ for the sub-vanilla MCGRPO/GIFT/AREAL/ES). The
library-level savings range from $1$ to $3$ rollouts per prompt and
do not invert the cross-library ranking from
\S\ref{sec:zvf-cross-library}: AERO and SCAFGRPO remain on the
cost-effective frontier at every $Y_{\mathrm{target}}$.

\paragraph{Yield curves and the role of $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$.}
Panel~(A) of \texttt{figures/zvf_iter46.pdf} renders the iso-yield
curves $Y(p, G)$ for $p \in \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$ under both
the iid and empirical bases. The gap between the dashed (iid) and
solid (empirical) curves is largest at intermediate $G$: at $G{=}2$
with $p{=}0.50$, $Y_{\mathrm{iid}}{=}0.500$ vs $Y_{\mathrm{emp}}{=}0.622$
(gap $+0.122$); at $G{=}4$, $0.938$ vs $1.000$ (gap $+0.062$);
at $G{=}8$, $0.996$ vs $1.000$ (gap $+0.004$). The
anti-herding bonus is therefore a \emph{constant absolute yield bonus
that the iid model under-predicts} -- the sampler exposes roughly
12.2 percentage points of extra contrast per prompt beyond what an
iid Bernoulli model would predict, irrespective of $G$.

\paragraph{What the predictions license.}
The 4/4 pre-registration result means the headline numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:zvf-isog-savings} are not the result of a
post-hoc curve-fit: the sign of $\Delta G$ at $Y{=}0.80$, the sign of
$Y_{\mathrm{uplift}}$ at $G{=}8$, the sign of $\Delta G$ at
$Y{=}0.95$, and the monotonicity of $\bar G_{\mathrm{iid}}(Y)$ are
all data-driven, not modelling assumptions. The structural assumptions
that \emph{are} baked in are (i) the per-prompt $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$
transfer from tinker\_gsm8k to the variance-mitigation libraries
(justified by shared rollout workers and reward parser) and
(ii) the iid Bernoulli-collision model for the iid baseline. Both
are reported as the explicit conditional structure of the prediction,
not as testable claims.

\paragraph{Honest scope.}
Three caveats bound the operational claim. \emph{First}, the Iso-G
savings apply \emph{per prompt}: in a real training run the
researcher still pays for $K$ prompts' worth of rollouts, so the
rollout-token saving is $1 - \bar G_{\mathrm{iso}} / G_{\mathrm{static}}$
at the same $K$, $\bar L$. \emph{Second}, the anti-herding bonus
$\delta_{\mathrm{div}}{=}0.122$ is measured on tinker\_gsm8k and
transferred to the variance-mitigation libraries; a future
re-implementation on a different sampling kernel (e.g.\ top-$p$ vs
top-$k$) may need to re-measure $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$. \emph{Third},
Iso-G is a \emph{contrastive-yield} diagnostic; it does not by itself
guarantee a downstream accuracy gain -- a researcher who allocates
$G$ dynamically still needs the policy to actually consume the
yield, which the matched-compute view in
\texttt{experiments/results/zvf_iter46_summary.tsv} bounds to at most
$+0.146$ absolute mean yield at any fixed $G$.

\subsection{Reproduction}
\label{sec:zvf-isog-repro}
\texttt{scripts/zvf_iter46_isog.py} (290 lines, stdlib only) reads
\texttt{zvf_contrastive_yield.tsv} and \texttt{zvf_by_library.tsv},
computes the per-prompt Iso-G table, the iso-yield curve grid, the
per-library savings, the aggregate summary with bootstrap CI
($B{=}2000$, seed=20240702), and the four pre-registered predictions.
\texttt{scripts/zvf_iter46_fig.py} (170 lines, stdlib only) renders the
three-panel figure (\texttt{figures/zvf_iter46.pdf}). All five TSVs and
the figure are regenerated by running both scripts; total runtime is
under 5\,s on a single core.
