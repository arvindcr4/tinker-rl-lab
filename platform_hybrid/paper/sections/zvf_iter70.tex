\subsection{Failure-Aware ZVF: Tail Density, Trajectory Direction, Severity}
\label{sec:zvf-failure-aware}

The cross-library diagnostic in \S\ref{sec:zvf-stratified} reads
ZVF as a function of heldout-accuracy quintile and shows that AERO
cuts ZVF in the middle quintiles. The single-scalar
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ in the by-library table is still a
\emph{pooled} statistic; it cannot distinguish a trajectory that
spiked into $\mathrm{ZVF}=1$ once from one that stayed at
$\mathrm{ZVF}=1$ throughout. Iter 70 lifts ZVF into a
\emph{failure-aware} diagnostic by adding four metric families the
iter26 baseline does not produce.

\paragraph{Tail density $\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{tail}}$.}
For every per-step ZVF trace, we report
$\Pr(\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 1)$ and $\Pr(\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 0)$ plus the
Gini coefficient of the per-step distribution. A trajectory with
$\Pr(\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 1) \to 1$ is \emph{stuck}; one with
$\Pr(\mathrm{ZVF}_t = 0) \to 1$ is \emph{dead}. Both extremes are
collapse signatures even when the pooled
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ looks benign: a short step trace that
spends 30\% of its time at $\mathrm{ZVF}=1$ has the same
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ as one that spends 30\% at $\mathrm{ZVF}=0.7$.

\paragraph{Trajectory direction $\Delta\mathrm{ZVF}$.}
The mean of the first quartile of the step trace minus the mean of
the last quartile measures whether ZVF is rising or falling
\emph{within} a run. A positive $\Delta$ means the run is becoming
\emph{more} zero-variance-dominated over time (drift toward
saturation); a negative $\Delta$ means the run is recovering
signal availability (a library that cures a stuck policy). Iter 70
records both endpoints and the difference.

\paragraph{Severity score $S \in \{0,1,2,3\}$.}
We promote the four-state failure taxonomy in
\S\ref{sec:zvf-stratified} to a numeric score: $S=3$ for
full-collapse runs (last-10 acc $\leq 0.001$, the
tool-use and Nemotron-120B final-trajectory regime), $S=2$ for
the standard collapse rule (peak $\!>\!0.7$, last $\!<\!0.35$),
$S=1$ for drift (last $\!<\!0.85 \cdot$ peak), and $S=0$ otherwise.
This is a strict refinement of the iter26 label set: it preserves
ordering the paper argues for, and it isolates the two
extreme-outcome cells (tool-use 0\% and Nemotron-120B
post-collapse) into a single severity-3 bin.

\paragraph{The headline cross-experiment correlation is non-significant.}
With $n=65$ pooled rows, the Pearson correlation of
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ against the severity score is
$+0.279$ (95\% CI $[-0.294, +0.545]$); Spearman is $-0.110$ (95\%
CI $[-0.462, +0.270]$). \emph{Neither bound excludes zero.} The
naive narrative -- "high ZVF predicts failure" -- is therefore
\emph{not} supported as a pooled-experiment rule. The signal
lives entirely in the tails: the two \texttt{tool\_use} buckets at
$($ZVF$=1.0, S=3)$ and the two \texttt{gsm8k\_real} buckets at
$($ZVF$\approx 0.16, S=0, p_{\mathrm{tail}=0}=0.84)$ are the only
separable clusters. The 9-method variance-mitigation pool, which
contributes 45 of the 65 rows, sits at
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}\in[0.11, 0.48]$ with $S\in\{0, 1\}$ -- a
narrow band that does not provide enough severity contrast to
support a correlation.

\paragraph{Within-variance-mitigation, the ZVF-failure relationship
\emph{reverses}.}
On the 9-method slice only, mean ZVF is \emph{anti}-correlated
with severity: the four methods with the lowest
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ (ES 0.114, GIFT 0.114, AREAL 0.121,
MCGRPO 0.146) are exactly the four methods whose worst seed hits
$S=1$ (drift). The five methods with
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}\ge 0.22$ (AERO, CPPO, NGRPO, SCAFGRPO, GRPO)
all stay at $S=0$. This is the iter26 finding re-read under the
severity lens: aggressive ZVF reduction (below $\sim$0.15) on the
math-verifiable-RL suite correlates with reward-stagnation, not
with collapse-resistance. AERO's $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}=0.22$ is
the lowest \emph{that does not pay a severity tax}.

\paragraph{Trajectory direction separates stuck from dead.}
The two \texttt{tool\_use} trajectories sit at $(\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{first}}=1.0, \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{last}}=1.0, \Delta = 0)$
on Panel~C of Figure~\ref{fig:zvf70-quad}: they are stuck from
the first quartile. The \texttt{groupsize\_zvf\_sweep} mean is
$(\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{first}}=0.75, \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{last}}=0.71, \Delta = -0.04)$:
ZVF is slightly falling as the policy converges (it is gaining
within-group contrast as the policy moves off the all-correct
plateau). The \texttt{variance\_mitigation} family of runs
collapses to the single point $(0.39, 0.36, \Delta = -0.03)$ on
the figure, confirming that all nine methods have a mild
\emph{recovering} trajectory direction on this benchmark -- the
ZVF tail is not locked in at the start.

\paragraph{Reproduction.}
\texttt{scripts/zvf\_iter70.py} (stdlib + matplotlib; reads the
same six JSON/TSV sources as \texttt{zvf\_diagnostic.py} plus
\texttt{variance\_mitigation.tsv}). Severity assignment uses
the same deterministic four-state rule as iter26 (so the
$S=0$ bucket contains both \emph{converged} and \emph{plateau},
which is the conservative choice); tail density and trajectory
direction are computed on the per-step ZVF trace; bootstrap CIs
are 2{,}000-rep resamples over the pooled severity rows. Runtime
under $4$\,s on a single core.
