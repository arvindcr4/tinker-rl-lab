\subsection{ZVF Hazard / Survival: the Alarm as a Necessary-But-Imprecise Collapse Predictor}
\label{sec:zvf-hazard}

The iter78 EWS protocol (\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_iter78\_anchors.tsv})
reports a single
\emph{lead-time} scalar per method: how many steps before heldout
accuracy drops below $0.10$ the alarm first fires. Iter~82 re-frames
this as a survival-analysis hazard. For each variance-mitigation
trace $i$ with $\mathrm{ZVF}_{i,t}$ and a per-step failure flag
$f_{i,t} = \mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{rolling}_{10}\,\mathrm{acc} < 0.10\}$
(carrying the iter78 definition verbatim), define the
\emph{alarm} $a_{i,t} = \mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{ZVF}_{i,t} > 0.5\}$ and
the \emph{per-step hazard}
%
\begin{equation}
  h(t \mid a) = \Pr(f_{i,t+1} = 1 \mid a_{i,t} = a,\, i\;\text{alive at}\; t),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{HR} = \frac{h(t\mid 1)}{h(t\mid 0)}.
  \label{eq:zvf-hazard}
\end{equation}
%
Pooled across the 25 variance-mitigation traces that ever fail, with
Laplace smoothing ($\varepsilon=0.5$) applied to the no-alarm
denominator, we obtain
%
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathrm{HR}} = 87.6\;\mathrm{median},\quad
  \big[64.9,\;74.0,\;85.1,\;74.0,\;143.0\big]_{[\mathrm{aero,cppo,grpo,mcgrpo,ngrpo}]},
  \label{eq:zvf-hr-by-method}
\end{equation}
%
i.e.\ a one to two order-of-magnitude amplification of the per-step
collapse probability whenever the alarm fires. The
\emph{alarm coverage} (recall)
$\mathrm{cov} = \Pr(f_{t+1} = 1,\, a_t = 1)/\Pr(f_{t+1} = 1)$ is
\textbf{1.000 for every one of the five methods with any failure}
(\texttt{experiments/results/zvf\_iter82\_hazard\_ratio.tsv}).
By contrast, the \emph{false-alarm rate} $\mathrm{FAR} = 1 - h(t \mid 1)$
sits at $0.90$ (median), with per-method FAR in
$[0.90,\,0.91]$ for the five failure-prone methods. The
$F_1$ of the ZVF alarm as a binary collapse predictor is therefore
$F_1 = 0.18$ (median).

\paragraph{Reading the table.}
The ZVF-alarm is a \emph{necessary but not sufficient} condition for
collapse: every observed failure is preceded by an alarm, but the
alarm fires $\sim10\times$ more often than failures actually occur.
This is a clean reformulation of the lead-time finding from
iter78 in a survival vocabulary, and the result sharpens
the EWS-protocol iter78 results: the alarm is \emph{reliable} (it never
misses a collapse in any library that experienced one) but
\emph{imprecise} (it is essentially a proxy for being deep in the
starvation regime, not a sharp failure predictor).

\paragraph{Survival by base-ZVF quartile.}
Pooling all 53 traces (variance-mitigation, groupsize sweep, Tinker
GSM8K, tool-use anchors) and binning by per-trace mean
$\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ into tertiles, the survival
$S(t) = \Pr(t_\mathrm{fail} > t)$ separates cleanly by ZVF
quartile (\texttt{figures/zvf\_iter82\_survival.pdf}): the
\emph{high}-ZVF tertile (mean $\mathrm{ZVF} \in [0.30,\,0.84]$, $n=18$)
sustains the lowest survival at every $t \in [0, 40]$, while the
\emph{low}-ZVF tertile ($\mathrm{ZVF} \in [0.10,\,0.13]$, $n=18$,
driven by \texttt{es} and \texttt{gift}) sustains survival
$\approx 1$ for the entire run. The middle tertile
($\mathrm{ZVF} \in [0.13,\,0.30]$, $n=17$, dominated by AERO, CPPO,
NGRPO, MCGRPO, AREAL, GIFT) sits between these two envelopes.

\paragraph{Take-aways.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{ZVF$ > 0.5$ is a perfect recall, low-precision collapse
  alarm} ($\mathrm{recall}=1.000$, $F_1=0.18$, $\mathrm{HR}\sim 10^2$).
  It is the first cheap binary alarm in this benchmark that
  has zero false negatives across all five failure-prone libraries.
\item \textbf{Survival $S(t)$ stratifies by base-ZVF tertile}, not
  by method. This means the EWS protocol of iter78 can be sharpened
  by re-basing on $\overline{\mathrm{ZVF}}$ tertile rather than on
  the method identity, which the iter70 finding already foreshadowed.
\item \textbf{Iter 82 closes the survival circle} of iter74 (Markov
  chain) and iter78 (lead time): with hazard framing, the
  ZVF-alarm's role as a *necessary* precursor to collapse is
  formally established at $p_{\text{coverage}}=1.000$ across
  $5/5$ failure-prone methods.
\end{enumerate}
