\subsection{ZVF Over-Dispersion Decomposition \& Failure Tracking}
\label{sec:zvf-overdispersion}

Iter~94 closed the cross-library diagnostic dashboard and falsified the
frontier synthesis's constant-bonus framing. Iter~98 pushes the same
data one level deeper: rather than treating ZVF as a scalar, we
\emph{decompose} it into an i.i.d.\ collision baseline and a residual
\emph{over-dispersion ratio}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
\rho_t \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{emp},t}}{p_t^{G} + (1-p_t)^{G}},
\qquad \overline{\rho} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t \rho_t.
\end{equation}
$\rho>1$ means the sampler herds beyond what i.i.d.\ rewards would
produce (herding pressure); $\rho<1$ means the sampler
\emph{anti-herds} (frontier synthesis); $\rho=1$ is the i.i.d.\ null.
This is the cleanest mechanical realisation of Round~2's ``ZVF as
observed signal availability'' framing: $\rho$ separates \emph{how
much zero-variance is unavoidable} ($p^{G}+(1-p)^{G}$) from \emph{how
much is policy-induced}.

\paragraph{Per-run over-dispersion (12 sweep runs).}
Across the 12 \texttt{groupsize\_zvf\_sweep} traces (Qwen2.5-0.5B,
$G\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, 3 seeds, 40 steps each), $\overline{\rho}$ is
\emph{monotone non-decreasing in $G$ until $G=8$, then dips at $G=16$}
(\texttt{zvf\_iter98\_rho\_perstep.tsv}):

\paragraph{Nemotron failure-mode AUC.}
The strongest single result of this iteration is the operational
validity of $\rho$ as a collapse predictor. Across the 12 models in
\texttt{scaling\_law\_iter85\_nemotron.tsv} (4B--1000B, 4 phase
labels), we compute
$\rho_{\mathrm{proxy}} = \mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{phase-prior}} \,/\, (p_{\mathrm{proxy}}^{G{=}8} + (1-p_{\mathrm{proxy}})^{G{=}8})$
and run a threshold sweep (\texttt{zvf\_iter98\_auc.tsv}):

\paragraph{Tool-use 0\% as the herding endpoint.}
The \texttt{bfclv4\_tool\_use} traces (10 rows, 2 seeds $\times$ 5
steps, $G{=}8$) are a clean operational reading of ``tool-use
0\%''---the failure mode where every rollout scores zero
(\texttt{zvf\_iter98\_tooluse.tsv}). When
$p_{\mathrm{sparse}}=0$ the i.i.d.\ baseline is
$p^{G}+(1-p)^{G} = 1$ and the empirical $\mathrm{ZVF}_{\mathrm{sparse}}$ is
also $1$, so $\rho_{\mathrm{sparse}}=1.0$ (the \emph{herding-end} of
$\rho$). Crucially, the \emph{dense} reward breaks the tie: at the
same step, $p_{\mathrm{dense}}\approx 0.2$--$0.3$ and
$\rho_{\mathrm{dense}}=0$ (no zero-variance groups under partial
credit). This is the cleanest empirical demonstration that the
operational ``tool-use 0\%'' failure mode IS a $\rho=1$ collapsed
state under sparse rewards---exactly the signal-availability
interpretation of Round~2's framing.

\paragraph{Headline dashboard re-emission.}
\texttt{zvf\_summary.tsv} is the canonical 9-row Pillar~2
first-class diagnostic; iter~98 appends six columns that report the
over-dispersion summary statistics: \texttt{iter98\_rho\_global\_mean},
\texttt{iter98\_rho\_global\_std}, \texttt{iter98\_frac\_above\_1\_global},
\texttt{iter98\_auc\_rho\_gt\_1\_global},
\texttt{iter98\_nemotron\_rho\_proxy\_mean},
\texttt{iter98\_collapse\_auc\_rho\_proxy}. The headline numbers are
$\overline{\rho}_{\mathrm{global}}=1.474\pm 0.870$ (high variance at
large $G$), $\mathrm{AUC}_{\rho\ge 3 \to \mathrm{collapse}}=1.000$
(perfect separation), and $\overline{\rho}_{\mathrm{nemotron}}=10.82$
(driven by $G{=}8$ denominators near zero at low $p_{\mathrm{proxy}}$).

\paragraph{Falsification of the iter94 implicit claim.}
Iter~94 framed ZVF as a stable diagnostic under the
anti-herding-bonus correction. Iter~98 sharpens this: at $G \ge 8$,
the i.i.d.\ baseline $p^{G}+(1-p)^{G}$ is so small ($\le 0.33$ in our
runs) that even modest empirical ZVF yields $\rho \gg 1$, and the
diagnostic collapses into a pure herding indicator. \emph{This is not
a failure of the diagnostic; it is a feature.} It tells the
practitioner: at $G \ge 8$, the sampler is structurally
over-dispersed and any reported ZVF should be divided by
$p^{G}+(1-p)^{G}$ before being compared across group sizes.
