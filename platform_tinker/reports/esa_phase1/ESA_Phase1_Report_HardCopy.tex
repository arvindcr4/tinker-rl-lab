\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), and in particular Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has become the dominant recipe for
post-training reasoning-capable language models. Yet reported gains are hard to
reproduce: results are spread across incompatible frameworks (TRL, veRL,
OpenRLHF, Tinker), are frequently single-seed, and rarely expose the internal
training signal that would explain \emph{why} a run learned or failed to learn.
This project builds a \textbf{unified, provenance-tracked benchmark and diagnostic
harness} for GRPO/RLVR post-training. The same task, reward function, and
decoding configuration are held fixed across back-ends so that observed
differences are attributable to the \emph{stack} rather than the experiment, and
every training step emits per-step telemetry (reward, zero-variance fraction,
gradient utilisation, entropy, length, KL). On top of this harness we run a
portfolio of eight focused studies (P1--P8). We deliberately frame the
contribution as a \emph{measurement / diagnostic benchmark and reproducibility
tooling}, \textbf{not} a ``GRPO wins'' leaderboard. The honest headline findings
are mixed and include strong negatives: roughly $72$--$77\%$ of gradient steps
are wasted on zero-variance prompt groups (P2); there is \emph{no} robust
group-size sweet spot once multiple seeds are used (P3); a curriculum that
mechanically eliminates the wasted gradient does \emph{not} beat a matched
baseline across three seeds ($+0.028$ vs.\ $-0.028$ held-out, within noise) and
costs ${\sim}5$--$6\times$ more sampling (curriculum campaign); the apparent
step-1 predictability of per-layer adaptation (P1: top-$k$ overlap $=1.0$ at 1.5B)
does \emph{not} survive a scaled 3B multi-seed re-test (overlap $0.11$, ${\approx}$chance);
the clearest surviving positive is that a telemetry-based integrity
auditor reaches AUROC $0.84$ versus $0.43$ for a reward-only baseline (P8). All
findings were independently re-checked by two frontier models (kimi, codex),
which flagged several early single-seed conclusions as underpowered---prompting
the powered multi-seed re-test that produced the honest nulls reported here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro}

Post-training with reinforcement learning has shifted from human-preference
reward models (RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}) toward \emph{verifiable}
rewards, where a programmatic checker (an exact-match grader for GSM8K, a unit
test for code) supplies a binary or scalar reward with no learned reward model in
the loop~\cite{lambert2024tulu3,guo2025r1}. Within this RLVR setting, Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} has become the
default algorithm: it drops PPO's value network and instead estimates the
advantage of each sampled completion \emph{relative to its own group} of
same-prompt samples.

This simplicity has a cost that is rarely reported. Because the advantage is a
within-group standardisation, any prompt for which every sampled completion
receives the \emph{same} reward contributes an advantage of exactly zero, and
therefore \emph{zero gradient}. On an easy dataset the model quickly solves most
prompts, so a large fraction of every batch collapses to zero advantage. We call
this the \textbf{Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)} and it is, empirically, the
single largest source of wasted compute in GRPO post-training.

Three problems motivate this project:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A reproducibility crisis.} GRPO/RLVR results are reported on
  different frameworks with different (often undocumented) reward parsers,
  decoding temperatures, KL coefficients, and clip ranges. A headline number
  cannot be attributed to the \emph{algorithm} versus the \emph{stack}.
  \item \textbf{Gradient waste is invisible.} Standard training logs report reward
  and loss, but not what fraction of the batch produced any learning signal at
  all. Practitioners pay for rollouts that move no weights.
  \item \textbf{No provenance standard.} There is no machine-readable record of
  \emph{what} was trained, on which verifier version, with which rollouts, so
  contamination and silent reward drift go undetected.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contribution.}
We contribute (i) a unified harness that runs the identical task, reward, and
decoding configuration across TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF, and Tinker; (ii) a per-step telemetry layer that
surfaces ZVF, gradient utilisation, entropy and KL; and (iii) a portfolio of
eight studies P1--P8 that use this harness to \emph{measure} GRPO rather than to
market it. The scientific posture throughout is \emph{verify $\rightarrow$
multi-seed $\rightarrow$ report the honest null}.

\section{Literature Survey}
\label{sec:lit}

\paragraph{From RLHF/PPO to group-relative methods.}
Policy-gradient post-training began with PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} and
generalised advantage estimation~\cite{schulman2015gae}, used at scale for
instruction tuning~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}. GRPO~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath}
removed the learned value function and replaced the per-token advantage with a
group-relative baseline, and was central to the DeepSeek-R1 reasoning
models~\cite{guo2025r1}. RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024rloo} is a closely related
leave-one-out estimator that baselines each sample against the mean of the
\emph{other} samples in the group---i.e.\ it already performs a form of
cross-prompt/within-group baselining, which is directly relevant to our P2
analysis.

\paragraph{Fixing GRPO's biases and collapse.}
A fast-growing line of work targets specific GRPO pathologies. Dr.\
GRPO~\cite{liu2025drgrpo} removes the length and difficulty normalisation biases
in the standard objective. DAPO~\cite{yu2025dapo} introduces \emph{dynamic
sampling}, explicitly resampling to avoid groups whose reward variance is zero,
plus decoupled clipping---this is the most direct prior art for the ZVF problem
we quantify in P2 and the curriculum lever we test. Group-variance methods such
as GVPO~\cite{gvpo2025} re-weight by within-group variance. A cluster of very
recent variants (AERO, NGRPO, $\lambda$-GRPO, MC-GRPO, AVSPO) attack advantage
collapse, length bias, and rollout accounting from different angles.\footnote{The
newest GRPO-variant citations in this survey (AERO, NGRPO, GVPO, $\lambda$-GRPO,
MC-GRPO, AVSPO) were verified against the arXiv API on 2026-07-06; all six
identifiers are confirmed (see \texttt{originality\_map\_2026-07-06.md}). Note on
labels: ``AERO'' (arXiv:2602.14338) and ``AVSPO'' (arXiv:2605.21125) are method
acronyms that do not appear verbatim in their papers' titles (the method names
occur in the body / project page); the arXiv IDs, titles, first authors, and dates
are otherwise fully confirmed.}

\paragraph{Verifiable rewards, frameworks, and reporting.}
RLVR was formalised for open post-training in TÜLU~3~\cite{lambert2024tulu3}. The
implementations we unify are TRL~\cite{vonwerra2020trl}, veRL /
HybridFlow~\cite{sheng2024hybridflow}, and OpenRLHF~\cite{hu2024openrlhf}, plus
Tinker as a managed training back-end. Our evaluation tasks are
GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} and a HumanEval~\cite{chen2021humaneval} subset. For
the reporting/provenance pillar (P5/P6) we build on Datasheets for
Datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} and Model Cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards},
but target the \emph{RL-run} object (verifier version, rollout provenance, reward
drift) that neither of those artefacts captures.

\paragraph{How we position the portfolio (without overclaiming).}
Table~\ref{tab:position} states, per pillar, the closest prior art and the
narrow axis we claim. Two pillars (P2 zero-variance and P4 length bias) are
crowded; there we \emph{narrow} claims to measurement rather than a new
algorithm. The widest open ground is the systems/reporting side (P5/P6/P8),
where no GRPO-specific machine-readable standard or infrastructure-level
integrity benchmark currently exists.

\section{Design Approach and Architecture}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{A unified harness across frameworks}
\input{tikz/fig1.tex}
The core design commitment is \emph{attribution}: hold the experiment fixed and
vary only the stack. A single configuration object specifies the model, dataset,
reward function, decoding parameters (temperature, top-$p$, max tokens), group
size $G$, learning rate, KL coefficient, and seed. A launcher
(\texttt{platform_local/unified/launcher.py}) maps that one config onto any of the supported
back-ends through thin per-framework adapters
(\texttt{trl\_integrations/}, \texttt{verl/}, \texttt{platform_modal/openrlhf/},
\texttt{platform_hybrid/skyrl/}, \texttt{platform_tinker/atropos/}), while \texttt{platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo.py} implements
the reference GRPO loop against the managed Tinker back-end. Because the reward
grader and decoding are shared code, a difference in outcome between two
back-ends is attributable to the framework, not to a divergent reward parser or
sampler.

\subsection{Per-step telemetry}
Every training step emits a telemetry record to JSONL and to Weights \& Biases.
The fields that distinguish this harness from a standard trainer are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{ZVF} --- the fraction of prompt-groups in the step whose
  within-group reward variance is zero (hence zero advantage, zero gradient);
  \item \textbf{gradient utilisation} --- the complementary fraction of the batch
  that actually contributed a non-zero update;
  \item \textbf{collapse decomposition} --- of the collapsed groups, how many are
  \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus \emph{all-wrong};
  \item plus reward, loss, entropy, completion length, and KL to the reference.
\end{itemize}
The raw group reward tensors are also saved, so any downstream diagnostic
(e.g.\ \texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py}) can \emph{recompute} a published number
from the stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary.

\subsection{The eight-study portfolio (P1--P8)}
\input{tikz/fig7.tex}
\textbf{P1 --- Scaling / layer dynamics.} A white-box study of \emph{where} a model
adapts under GRPO-style updates, recording per-layer LoRA gradient norms to test
whether the layers that will matter can be identified early (predictive
layer-freezing). Requires per-layer gradient access, so it runs on a local GPU
rather than the managed back-end.

\textbf{P2 --- Zero-variance / gradient waste.} Quantifies ZVF on real reward
tensors and asks whether any re-baselining scheme can recover gradient from
collapsed groups.

\textbf{P3 --- Group size.} Studies how collapse, reward, held-out gain, and token
cost vary with the group size $G$, and reframes $G$ as a compute budget rather
than an integer.

\textbf{P4 --- Length bias (designed).} Proposes semantic-density / surprise-weighted
token normalisation instead of uniform or static length penalties.

\textbf{P5 --- Reporting / provenance (flagship, systems).} A GRPO post-training
datasheet plus cryptographic rollout provenance, so one can prove which verifier
version and which rollouts produced a checkpoint.
\input{tikz/fig6.tex}

\textbf{P6 --- Registry.} A versioned, temporal registry of evolving run state
(verifier-logic updates, reward drift), intended to fold into the P5 standard.

\textbf{P7 --- Controller (designed).} Classical PID control of decoding temperature
and clip range, using live advantage-variance / KL-drift as sensor inputs.

\textbf{P8 --- Integrity / anomaly.} An auditor that uses the per-step telemetry
(not just reward) to detect anomalous / manipulated runs.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{The Tinker GRPO loop}
\input{tikz/fig2.tex}
\input{tikz/fig3.tex}
The reference loop in \texttt{platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo.py} follows the standard
sample~$\rightarrow$~advantage~$\rightarrow$~loss~$\rightarrow$~optimise cycle.
For a prompt $q$ we draw a group of $G$ completions $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^{G}$ from the
current policy and score each with the verifiable reward $r_i$. The
group-relative advantage is the within-group standardisation
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon},
\qquad
\mu_g = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j} r_j,\quad
\sigma_g = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_j (r_j-\mu_g)^2}.
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
The optimisation objective is the PPO-clipped surrogate with a KL penalty to a
frozen reference policy $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) &\;=\;
-\,\mathbb{E}_{i,t}\!\left[
\min\!\big(\rho_{i,t}A_i,\;
\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{i,t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_i\big)
\right]
\;+\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\big),
\label{eq:loss}\\[0.2em]
\text{where}\quad
\rho_{i,t} &=\frac{\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}.
\nonumber
\end{align}
The critical observation, made mechanical by Eq.~\eqref{eq:adv}, is that whenever
$\sigma_g = 0$ every $A_i = 0$, so the whole group contributes nothing to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}. The zero-variance fraction is exactly the batch-mean of the
indicator $\mathbb{1}[\sigma_g = 0]$.

\subsection{Parallel runner}
To sweep configurations (e.g.\ $G \in \{2,4,8,16\}$ or three seeds
$\times$ two arms) within a fixed wall-clock budget, \texttt{parallel\_sweep.py}
and \texttt{campaign.py} launch runs concurrently. An early threaded runner hit
a real bug: Weights \& Biases keeps a thread-unsafe global \texttt{run} object,
so with four threads calling \texttt{wandb.init(reinit=True)} the summary writes
for later runs failed (``Run is finished'') even though per-step logs survived.
The fix, used by the multi-seed \texttt{campaign.py}, is one \emph{process} per
run rather than one thread. We report this because it is exactly the kind of
silent logging failure the benchmark is meant to surface (see the P3 caveat in
Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{The Colab white-box path (P1)}
P1 needs per-layer gradient norms, which the managed Tinker back-end does not
expose. It therefore runs on a Colab L4 GPU
(\texttt{p1\_layer\_profile.py}): Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with LoRA adapters on the
$q/k/v/o$ projections, five GRPO-style advantage-weighted steps, recording the
per-layer LoRA gradient norm at each step. This white-box route complements the
black-box managed runs used for P2/P3.

\subsection{P2 --- Gradient waste is massive and easy-driven}
On real reward tensors for four methods (40 steps each, $16$ prompts
$\times\,8$ samples, binary verifiable reward on GSM8K), the mean zero-variance
fraction is $\zvf = 0.72$--$0.77$: roughly three-quarters of every batch
contributes no gradient. The collapse is \emph{easy-driven}, not chance: about
$65$--$71\%$ of prompts are \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus only
${\sim}5\%$ all-wrong. We then tested whether a re-baselining trick could recover
gradient from collapsed groups. A naive cross-prompt baseline (advantage against
the global mean) gives $|A|\approx0.21$, but that ``signal'' is just a proxy for
prompt easiness/difficulty---not a learning signal---which is precisely what
RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024rloo} already captures. A difficulty-isocline baseline
correctly recovers $|A|\approx0.012$--$0.02$, i.e.\ essentially zero, confirming
that \emph{no recoverable gradient exists} in these groups. \textbf{Honest
redirect:} re-baselining is the wrong lever; the data point instead to
difficulty-targeted sampling / curriculum, motivating the experiments below.

\subsection{P3 --- No robust group-size sweet spot}
\input{tikz/fig8.tex}
A single-seed sweep (Table~\ref{tab:p3}) is suggestive: $G{=}2$ sits below the
learning threshold ($50\%$ of steps collapse, $+0.000$ held-out), $G{=}4$ looks
best ($+0.125$ held-out), and $G\ge8$ shows diminishing returns as token cost
scales roughly linearly with $G$ while the easy subset gets mastered and groups
collapse to all-correct anyway. But the $G{=}4$ ``win'' is literally one extra
correct held-out example ($7/8 \to 8/8$, $n=8$, single seed). When the multi-seed

\subsection{Curriculum --- eliminating waste is not a free lunch}
A curriculum that trains only on mixed-variance (non-collapsed) groups does
exactly what it is designed to do: it drives the zero-loss fraction from $0.50$
to $0.00$. But it pays $4.81\times$ the sampling cost (oversampling to \emph{find}
non-collapsed groups) and delivers an \emph{identical} held-out gain to the
baseline ($+0.05$ vs $+0.05$, held-out $n=20$, one seed, both gains equal to one
extra correct answer). Eliminating the visible gradient waste does not, by
itself, buy accuracy: the sampling overhead offsets the gradient-density benefit.

\subsection{Campaign --- the powered re-test yields an honest null}
\input{tikz/fig5.tex}

\subsection{P1 --- step-1 predictability holds at toy scale but collapses at 3B}
\input{tikz/fig4.tex}
The white-box layer profile at toy scale (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 28 layers, 5 GRPO-style
steps, a hardcoded arithmetic set) initially looked like the clearest positive in
the portfolio: the top-$k$ highest-gradient layers after a \emph{single} step were
identical to the top-$k$ over all steps (\texttt{step1\_predicts\_final\_topk\_overlap}
$=1.0$), suggesting one could decide what to freeze after one update---the
``predictive'' premise that would differentiate predictive layer-freezing from
methods that select layers \emph{during} training. \textbf{This did not survive
scaling.} A powered re-test (Qwen2.5-3B, 36 layers, 10 steps, \emph{real} GSM8K,
seeds $\{0,1\}$) drops the step-1$\to$final overlap to $0.11$ on both seeds---i.e.\
${\approx}$chance, with only layer~0 persisting. The mid-late band that dominates by
end-of-training \emph{emerges} over steps and is invisible at step~1, so the apparent
predictability was an artifact of the toy setup. Honestly, adaptation is only
\emph{moderately} concentrated: the top $25\%$ of layers carry ${\sim}48\%$ of the
total gradient norm (\texttt{concentration\_top25pct\_share} $=0.476$), not the
$>90\%$ that a ``freeze almost everything'' story would need. Layer~0 dominates
(grad-norm $\approx 63$ vs $6$--$17$ elsewhere), with a secondary mid-late band
(layers 17--23) also adapting. With step-1 predictability gone at scale, the only remaining (weaker) lever is the
\emph{emerged} concentration---and it is only moderate (top-25\% share $0.48$ at 1.5B,
$0.39$ at 3B, so it \emph{does} hold across scales), meaning aggressive freezing would
discard real signal in any case. This is a small proof-of-concept
(1.5B model, 5 steps, a tiny arithmetic set) that establishes the mechanism; the
quantitative FLOP saving from freezing needs a scaled run.

\subsection{P8 --- telemetry beats reward-only for integrity}
Using the per-step telemetry rather than reward alone, the integrity auditor
reaches AUROC $0.84$, versus $0.43$ for a reward-only baseline. This supports the
premise that internal training signal (ZVF trajectory, entropy, KL drift) carries
information about run integrity that the reward curve does not.

\paragraph{P8 evaluation set (reproducible).} The surrogate-detection result is recomputed from the raw \texttt{n2\_reward\_tensor\_resume} tensors by \texttt{experiments/openings/p8\_detector.py} (output \texttt{detector\_reproduced.json}). Four reward-optimization algorithms (\textsc{grpo}, \textsc{aero}, \textsc{areal}, \textsc{gift}) each contribute $40$ per-step rows ($N=160$); the positive ``surrogate'' class is the three GRPO variants reported as GRPO (\textsc{aero}/\textsc{areal}/\textsc{gift}, $120$ rows) and the negative class is genuine \textsc{grpo} ($40$ rows), a $3{:}1$ imbalance. Features are derived entirely from the raw reward/length/advantage arrays (\texttt{zvf}, \texttt{reward\_mean}, \texttt{reward\_std}, \texttt{frac\_all\_zero}, \texttt{frac\_all\_one}, \texttt{mean\_len}, \texttt{cv\_len}, \texttt{len\_reward\_corr}, \texttt{adv\_abs\_mean}, \texttt{adv\_std}, \texttt{lag1\_autocorr}); the training-side \texttt{loss} scalar is absent from the tensors and is omitted. A logistic-regression detector (median-imputed, standardized, $5$-fold stratified CV) reaches $\mathrm{AUROC}=0.838\pm0.010$ across five CV seeds ($0.826$ at seed~0), versus $\mathrm{AUROC}=0.426\pm0.027$ for a reward-only auditor (below chance). This reproducible, multi-seed result supersedes an earlier prose-only estimate of $0.63/0.35$; the higher value reflects the fuller, documented feature set. Remaining caveat: the base model/task are not recorded in the tensor rows (only method/seed/step/group\_size), so provenance of the underlying runs is incomplete---exactly the gap the P5 protocol is designed to close.

\section{Rigor and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:rigor}

The methodology is deliberately adversarial toward its own results.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed with matched baselines.} Every headline learning claim
  is re-tested across $\ge 3$ seeds against a baseline that differs only in the
  lever under study (same model, data, decoding, budget). This is what converted
  the single-seed ``curriculum wins / $G{=}4$ is best'' story into the honest
  null of Section~\ref{sec:results}.
  \item \textbf{Held-out evaluation.} Gains are measured on held-out prompts, and
  we report the held-out $n$ ($8$--$20$ here) alongside every gain so the reader
  can see that magnitudes are noise-limited. We do not quote a gain without its
  denominator.
  \item \textbf{Recompute-from-tensors.} Published diagnostics are recomputable
  from stored raw tensors (e.g.\ \texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py} regenerates
  the ZVF/collapse JSON), not merely read back from a logged summary.
  \item \textbf{Logging and provenance.} All runs log to Weights \& Biases
  (project \texttt{rlvr-openings}), with per-step telemetry and saved group
  tensors; runs are pinned by config, seed, and framework.
  \item \textbf{Independent verification.} Findings were re-checked by two
  frontier models used as adversarial auditors (kimi and codex). They recomputed
  the raw numbers and returned a \textbf{SUSPECT} verdict on the early
  single-seed P2/P3/curriculum conclusions---correctly identifying them as
  underpowered (P3 held-out $n=8$; curriculum $n=20$, one seed) and flagging that
  one isocline analysis script was not yet in the repo and that the four P2
  methods shared a deterministic prompt schedule. Those flags directly triggered
  the powered campaign re-test. The recomputed mechanics
  (ZVF $0.72$--$0.77$, all-correct $0.65$--$0.71$, naive $|A|\approx0.21$,
  curriculum $0.50\to0.00$ at $4.81\times$) all reproduced exactly.
\end{itemize}

Known threats to validity we do \emph{not} hide: small held-out sets; a
partially-recorded P3 sweep artefact (G8/G16 summaries lost to the W\&B
thread-safety bug); a deterministic prompt schedule shared across the four P2
methods (so method-to-method ZVF differences reflect training dynamics, not
independent samples); and the invalidated \texttt{zero\_loss\_frac} in the first
campaign batch.

\section{Conclusion and Phase-2 Plan}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This phase delivered a working unified GRPO/RLVR benchmark, a per-step diagnostic
telemetry layer, and eight studies whose honest verdict is: the algorithmic
levers we could test on this setup (naive curriculum, integer group-size sweeps)
do \emph{not} produce reliable held-out gains, while the large and reproducible
effect is the \emph{gradient waste} itself ($\zvf \approx 0.72$--$0.77$). Reporting
those negatives---rather than the cherry-picked single-seed positives---is the
contribution: it is what makes the portfolio defensible and the benchmark
trustworthy.

\paragraph{Where the original value is.} The strongest \emph{original} bet is
\textbf{P5, a GRPO provenance / reporting standard}, which the literature scan
confirms as open white space (no GRPO-specific machine-readable datasheet or
rollout-provenance protocol exists). The earlier second bet---\textbf{P1 predictive
layer-freezing}---is now weakened: its step-1 predictability held at 1.5B but collapsed
to chance ($0.11$) on the scaled 3B multi-seed re-test, so any freeze schedule would
have to rest on the \emph{emerged} band rather than a step-1 predictor.

\paragraph{Phase-2 plan.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Token-budget-optimal curriculum.} Replace naive ``filter collapsed
  groups'' with an allocation that trades oversampling against training under an
  explicit token budget with staleness bounds, tested multi-seed from the start.
  The Phase-1 null tells us the lever must be \emph{budget-aware}, not just
  waste-eliminating.
  \item \textbf{Re-examine P1.} The scaled 3B / multi-seed re-test (now run) shows
  step-1 predictability does \emph{not} hold (overlap $0.11$); the open question is whether
  the \emph{emerged} mid-late band supports any FLOP-saving freeze schedule when read off
  late-training gradients rather than step~1.
  \item \textbf{The P5 protocol.} Specify and implement the GRPO post-training
  datasheet plus cryptographic rollout provenance, and dogfood it on the harness's
  own runs so every benchmark result ships with a verifiable provenance record.
\end{enumerate}
