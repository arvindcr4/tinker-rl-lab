### 10.1 ZVF Formalization and Cross-Framework Computation Pipeline

**Addresses reviewer concerns:** W1 (ZVF tautology), Q1 (formalization), W13 (pipeline underspecification)

**Paper sections added:** `paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex`, `paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex`
**Reproducibility:** `platform_modal/scripts/partial_correlation_zvf.py`, `platform_modal/scripts/zvf_compute_cross_framework.py`

#### Formal Definition

Let a GRPO batch B_t at optimizer step t consist of |B_t| prompt-groups, each group g containing K rollouts with scalar outcome rewards r_{g,1}, ..., r_{g,K}. The Zero-Variance Fraction is formally defined as

> ZVF_t = (1 / |B_t|) · Σ_{g ∈ B_t} 1[ Var̂(r_{g,1}, ..., r_{g,K}) ≤ ε ]

where Var̂ is the unbiased sample variance and ε = 10⁻⁶ for rewards normalized to [0,1]. Intuitively, ZVF_t is the fraction of groups at step t whose rollouts all collapsed to the same outcome; those groups contribute zero group-relative advantage and therefore zero gradient signal to GRPO's policy update. This extends and makes rigorous the informal treatment in §5.12 of this report.

A reviewer may reasonably suspect that under binary outcome rewards and group-relative advantages ZVF is a direct function of batch mean reward r̄_t. This holds only at the extremes r̄_t ∈ {0,1}. For K i.i.d. Bernoulli rollouts with prompt-specific success probability p_g, the expected ZVF under the null independence model is E[ZVF_t] = E_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1 − p_g)^K], which depends on the *shape* of the difficulty distribution, not only its mean. Concretely, two populations with the same mean reward r̄ = 0.5 can differ dramatically: a bimodal population with p_g ∈ {0, 1} yields ZVF = 1, whereas a uniform-hard population with all p_g = 0.5 and K = 8 yields ZVF ≈ 2·(0.5)⁸ ≈ 0.008. ZVF therefore carries non-trivial information about the higher moments of the per-prompt success distribution, and the claim that it is tautologically equivalent to mean reward is false.

#### Partial-Correlation Ablation (Withheld Pending Artifact)

A reviewer-recommended test of "is ZVF just a re-expression of mean reward?" is the partial correlation between ZVF at an early reference window and final held-out accuracy, controlling for the obvious diagnostics. The conceptual design is:

- ZVF at an early reference window t* ∈ [25, 40] versus final held-out GSM8K-500 accuracy R_final.
- Controls: batch mean reward r̄_t, policy entropy H_π(t), within-group advantage variance Var_A(t), and KL drift to reference KL(π_t ‖ π_ref).
- Tier-A matched-protocol subset (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen2.5-0.5B on GSM8K; 5-seed).
- Script: `platform_modal/scripts/partial_correlation_zvf.py`; output: `experiments/results/zvf_partial_correlations.tsv`.

**Status (measured).** For the Qwen3-8B/frontier GSM8K corpus we still do not ship per-group step-level rollout logs, so no causal partial-correlation is claimed there. To answer the partial-correlation question directly we ran a fully instrumented, ungated validation (`experiments/modal/modal_groupsize_zvf_sweep.py` on Qwen2.5-0.5B arithmetic, 12 runs × 40 steps = 480 logged steps, G ∈ {2,4,8,16} × 3 seeds) that logs per-step ZVF, batch-mean reward, policy entropy, and advantage variance. `experiments/results/zvf_partial_correlations.tsv` now holds the **measured** result, but only the defensible part of it: ZVF↔reward **raw** `r = +0.74` (more all-correct groups → more zero-variance groups). We explicitly do **not** report a partial correlation "controlling for advantage variance": under binary rewards the within-group advantage variance is a *deterministic transform* of ZVF (zero iff zero-variance), so partialling it out is **circular** (regressing ZVF on a function of itself); and the 480 step-rows are 12 runs × 40 *autocorrelated* steps (lag-1 ≈ 0.9), so step-level p-values are invalid. The closed form `ZVF(p,G)=p^G+(1−p)^G` reproduces the qualitative *shape* across scales, but a per-problem Pearson `r ≈ 0.93` is mechanically inflated (observed per-problem ZVF = `1[p∈{0,1}]`, theory is a function of the same `p`; rank `ρ ≈ 0.65`), and the i.i.d. null mis-estimates the *level* (over-predicts at 8B, under-predicts at 0.5B). This is a small-scale consistency check on the diagnostic's shape, **not** a precise validation or a causal claim. The earlier 6-row table (r_partial −0.71→−0.31), the synthetic positive-sign stub, and the entropy/adv-var-"controlled" partials (−0.12/−0.13) are all **retracted**.

**Schema bridge.** A future run that emits per-step `entropy`, `advantage_variance`, `kl_drift`, `final_reward` (and `group_size`, `baseline_accuracy` for completeness) will populate the 4 confounder rows automatically. The canonical per-step emitter is `SaturationTracker.record_step_with_diagnostics(step, group_rewards, entropy=…, advantage_variance=…, kl_drift=…, final_reward=…)` in `experiments/10x_structural_ceiling/group_saturation_diagnostic.py`; field names match the aliases accepted by `platform_modal/scripts/partial_correlation_zvf.py::_extract_zvf_row`. ZVF is emitted as `zero_variance_frac` (also accepted as `zvf` or `zero_variance_fraction`). The schema is verified end-to-end: a synthetic 16-step run with all confounders populated produced r_partial values for every row including the joint-control row. See `platform_modal/scripts/partial_correlation_zvf.py` header docstring for the field conventions.

**The non-tautology argument does not depend on the partial-correlation table.** The conceptual defense against the tautology concern is the closed-form E[ZVF] = E_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1 − p_g)^K] under an i.i.d. Bernoulli null: ZVF is sensitive to the higher moments of the per-prompt success distribution, not only its mean (see "Why ZVF Is Not a Tautological Re-expression of Mean Reward" above). A reviewer who still finds that argument unsatisfying should weight the partial-correlation evidence as **future work**, not as a current empirical claim.

**Early-warning threshold τ_zvf also withdrawn.** The operational claim that "ZVF_{t*} > τ_zvf ≈ 0.85 cleanly partitions soon-to-collapse runs from healthy ones" was calibrated on the same partial-corr analysis and the same Tier-A subset, and inherits the same withdrawal. We do not recommend operationalizing ZVF as an early-warning signal at this time; the narrower claim — that high ZVF coincides with reward plateaus and regressions on the runs we have measured — is the one directly supported by the released evidence.

#### Cross-Framework Pipeline

The reference implementation normalizes each framework's rollout log into a [|B_t|, K] reward matrix and applies the canonical rule `(rewards_2d.var(axis=-1, ddof=1) ≤ ε).mean()`. The per-framework log-field mapping used by `platform_modal/scripts/zvf_compute_cross_framework.py` is:

| Framework | Reward key | Group boundary | Mask field |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRL (`GRPOTrainer`) | `rewards` (list per batch) | `batch_size / group_size` | `completion_mask` |
| TINKER (managed) | `rollout.reward` | `rollout.group_id` | `rollout.eos_mask` |
| OpenRLHF | `reward_score` | `prompt_index` | `attention_mask` |
| veRL | `data_source/reward` | `uid` | `response_mask` |

Group boundaries are recovered from an explicit group-id, a prompt-hash, or `batch_size / group_size` partitioning. Masks are only required for the advantage-variance secondary diagnostic, not for ZVF itself.

**Status of cross-framework portability.** The implementation-level claim is that when a framework exposes per-rollout rewards and group boundaries through the fields above, the same normalizer produces the canonical `[|B_t|, K]` reward matrix and the same ZVF definition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:zvf} applies without framework-specific reinterpretation. The narrower, implementation-level claim is the one directly supported by the released evidence (the parser, the field-mapping table, the cross-framework summary in `experiments/results/framework_comparison.json`).

We previously reported a per-step discrepancy of max 0.019 / mean 0.006 between matched TRL and OpenRLHF runs (Qwen3-8B / GSM8K / G=8 / 100 steps) as a quantitative portability check. **That figure is withdrawn.** The matched-run raw logs are not bundled, the per-step ZVF time series are not shipped, and the closest on-disk artifact (`framework_comparison.json`) is a 30-step / seed-42 four-way gap summary with the OpenRLHF and veRL entries explicitly tagged `mode: 'dryrun'` (deterministic fallbacks because the corresponding frameworks were not installed in the sandbox). A reviewer who wants the matched-run comparison should re-run the four frameworks on the same prompt set and tokenizer, save `zvf_{trl,tinker,openrlhf,verl}.jsonl` per step, and compute the max/mean per-step `|Δ|` between frameworks. Until that artifact is in the repository, we do not assert quantitative cross-framework agreement.

The tolerance is fixed at ε = 10⁻⁶, and the choice is justified for the binary-outcome-reward regime in scope (see "Scope and Boundary Conditions" below). We previously reported a "sensitivity sweep over ε ∈ {10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻²} shifts cross-framework ZVF estimates by at most 0.4% absolute" as a robustness check; that figure is withdrawn. The four-framework raw log fixtures required to reproduce it (`experiments/results/rollouts/{trl,tinker,openrlhf,verl}/*.jsonl`) are not bundled in the released artifact, and the cross-framework self-test exercises only single-step fixtures unrelated to that claim. We fix ε = 10⁻⁶ by the following argument: for binary outcome rewards (the only regime in scope, see "Scope and Boundary Conditions" below), the minimum non-zero within-group variance on K ≥ 4 rollouts is 1/K ≥ 1/4 = 0.25, and the maximum fp32 round-off error on a sum of K ≤ 32 products is well below 10⁻⁶. The threshold therefore separates "all rollouts returned the same bit" from "at least one rollout returned the opposite bit" exactly, with a margin of seven orders of magnitude. A reader who disagrees with this choice can re-derive ZVF at a different ε from the same per-group rewards without re-running training.

#### Scope and Boundary Conditions

ZVF is not universally informative. The following regimes are explicitly out of scope. The first four items are the boundary conditions carried by the original formalization; items (5)–(10) are additions in response to the W7 / Q6 / W1 rebuttal cycle and to the empirical limitations of the released artifact.

- **(1) Dense or continuous rewards** (e.g., process-reward models, graded code-execution partial credit): Var̂ is almost surely non-zero, so ZVF → 0 and must be replaced by a per-step-reward-variance diagnostic.
- **(2) K = 1**: group variance is undefined and ZVF is trivially 1.
- **(3) SFT-saturated baselines**: when the policy already solves the task (p̄_g → 1 ∀g), ZVF → 1 and discriminative power vanishes; this is a feature, not a bug — RL no longer provides useful signal in that regime.
- **(4) Non-outcome-reward RL (DPO, DAR)**: there are no group rollouts, so ZVF is ill-defined.
- **(5) Small group size K ∈ {2, 3}**: for binary {0, 1} rewards, Var̂ is a discrete function of the K outcomes (K = 2 yields Var̂ ∈ {0, 0.5} exactly), so the ≤ ε rule becomes a coin-flip detector sensitive to single-rollout sampling noise. The partial-correlation and τ_zvf ≈ 0.85 thresholds were estimated at K = 8 and are not portable to K ≤ 4 without re-calibration.
- **(6) Modified group-relative advantage formulations**: The ZVF count itself is framework- and advantage-formulation-independent: it is defined purely on the within-group outcome-reward variance and does not depend on the advantage formulation. The *interpretation* of ZVF > 0 as "zero gradient signal" is the claim that requires canonical A = (r − r̄_g) / σ_g. Under the Dr. GRPO formulation \cite{tong2025drgrpo}, which drops the σ_g divisor entirely (A = R(o) − mean(R), with no σ at all), an all-equal-outcome group still yields zero advantage signal at that step (since R − mean(R) = 0 identically), so the gradient-vanishes framing survives; but Dr. GRPO's σ-free advantage removes the σ_g → 0 degeneracy that the canonical A would otherwise suffer. Under DAPO's dynamic resampling \cite{yu2025dapo}, a ZVF-positive group is filtered out and replaced by a resampled group at the step boundary, so the diagnostic is computed *before* the resample and acts on the original (filtered) batch. Recommend a "policy-side" companion diagnostic (‖g_t‖) before backprop in these regimes.
- **(7) Mixed / composite outcome rewards** (e.g. r = 0.7·correctness + 0.3·format): the additive decomposition may include a less-informative sub-component (e.g. format) that retains within-group variance even when the more informative sub-component (e.g. correctness) has collapsed. In that regime, ZVF on the *total* reward can be ≈ 0 (the total reward still has variance from the noisy sub-term) while the informative sub-term has collapsed — i.e. ZVF misses the sub-term collapse. The failure is masked in the *opposite* direction from what one might first guess: a noisy sub-term masks an informative-sub-term collapse, not the reverse. Compute ZVF on each sub-term independently, or apply the diagnostic to the residual after projecting out the noise sub-term.
- **(8) Asynchronous or off-policy rollouts** (TINKER managed, veRL with stale-rollout replay): the |B_t| reward snapshot at optimizer step t may have been generated by π_{t−τ} for τ ≥ 1. ZVF_t then diagnoses the rollout policy's outcome distribution, not the current policy's. Restrict the early-warning interpretation to synchronous, on-policy rollouts (the regime the Tier-A TRL GSM8K runs use).
- **(9) Non-stationary prompt distributions** (curriculum learning, easy-prompt oversampling, difficulty-band reweighting): the t* ∈ [25, 40] early-warning window was calibrated on a fixed GSM8K-500 sampling distribution; under curriculum shift the null E[ZVF_t | P_t] changes with P_t, and the same τ_zvf corresponds to a different point on the collapse-vs-healthy partition. Re-derive τ_zvf on the specific curriculum schedule, or report ZVF alongside a prompt-distribution shift index (e.g. KL(P_t ‖ P_ref)) and only flag ZVF_{t*} > τ as an early warning when the prompt distribution is within ε of P_ref.
- **(10) Policy entropy / sampling temperature below effective-K threshold**: the i.i.d. Bernoulli null model in "Why ZVF Is Not a Tautological Re-expression of Mean Reward" assumes K independent draws from a non-degenerate policy. Under low-entropy policies or low sampling temperature, the K rollouts are strongly correlated, Var̂ ≈ 0 regardless of p_g, and the closed-form E[ZVF] = E[p^K + (1−p)^K] is no longer a useful approximation. The diagnostic correctly reports ZVF = 1 in this regime (the rollout distribution has collapsed); the *interpretive* cost is that ZVF = 1 cannot distinguish "converged to the correct answer" from "converged to a wrong mode." Pair ZVF with the batch mean reward r̄_t: only ZVF = 1 with r̄_t ≪ 1 indicates a pathological collapse rather than successful convergence.

**Prior art.** The quantity ZVF counts — "fraction of GRPO groups with all-equal rollout rewards" — has been independently named in the recent variance-mitigation literature. Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025 \cite{tong2025drgrpo}) names the "degenerate group fraction" and mitigates via the GRPO-Done-Right technique: removing the σ_g divisor from the advantage (so A = R(o) − mean(R), with no σ at all) and dropping the per-rollout 1/|o_i| length normalization. DAPO (Yu et al., 2025 \cite{yu2025dapo}) acts on the same event via Dual-Clip and Dynamic Sampling, which explicitly re-samples groups whose rewards are all-equal at the step boundary; the public `metrics/zero_std_ratio` runtime metric name is from the downstream verl/Zero-Plus-One reproduction rather than from the DAPO paper itself. EDGE-GRPO (Zhang et al., 2025 \cite{edgegrpo2025}) targets the same event via entropy-driven advantage reshaping, and is the cleanest cite for the suppression-style variance-mitigation class.

ZVF in this paper is not a new metric, nor a rename. ZVF counts the same event the prior art counts, with three genuinely new contributions layered on top: (i) a formal ε-rule on the unbiased sample variance, the K=8 Bernoulli-null E[ZVF] = E_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1−p)^K] sensitivity argument, and the seven-orders-of-magnitude margin justification for ε = 10⁻⁶; (ii) a cross-framework field-mapping table unifying the parser contract across TRL, TINKER, OpenRLHF, and veRL; and (iii) a partial-correlation ablation design that targets the W1 / Q1 reviewer concern (ZVF is not a re-expression of mean reward) but is **withdrawn** pending the per-step confounder log artifact, as discussed in the "Partial-Correlation Ablation (Withheld Pending Artifact)" section above. The third item is a *design*, not a current result; the partial-correlation table and the τ_zvf ≈ 0.85 operational threshold that previously anchored it are explicitly retired in that section.

Prior variance-aware baselines that pre-date the all-equal-group framing — VinePPO (Khalifa et al., 2024 \cite{vineppo2024}) and SRPO \cite{srpo2025} — quantify the same variance-vs-K tradeoff from a different angle. GPG (Chu et al., 2025 \cite{gpg2025}) is the GRPO-without-biases variant cited by Dr. GRPO and DAPO follow-ups as a baseline; it does not separately introduce an all-equal-outcome metric.

Within its scope — outcome-reward GRPO on verifiable tasks with K ≥ 4, canonical A = (r − r̄_g)/σ_g, synchronous on-policy rollouts, fixed prompt distribution, non-degenerate policy entropy — ZVF is a cheap, model-agnostic diagnostic. Outside that scope we recommend the surrogates documented in the paper's extended related-work appendix (`addendum/08-related-work-integration.md`) and the per-term / per-step variants above.
