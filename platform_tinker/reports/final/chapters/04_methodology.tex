\section{Design Commitment: Attribution}
\label{sec:harness}
\input{tikz/fig1.tex}
The core design commitment is \emph{attribution}: hold the experiment fixed and vary
only the stack. A single configuration object specifies the model, dataset, reward
function, decoding parameters (temperature, top-$p$, max tokens), group size $G$,
learning rate, KL coefficient, and seed. A launcher (\texttt{platform\_local/unified/launcher.py})
maps that one config onto any of the supported back-ends through thin per-framework
adapters (\texttt{trl\_integrations/}, \texttt{verl/}, \texttt{platform\_modal/openrlhf/},
\texttt{platform\_hybrid/skyrl/}, \texttt{platform\_tinker/atropos/}), while \texttt{platform\_tinker/tinker\_rl/grpo.py} implements the
reference GRPO loop against the managed Tinker back-end. Because the reward grader
and decoding are shared code, a difference in outcome between two back-ends is
attributable to the framework, not to a divergent reward parser or sampler.

\paragraph{Scope of what Phase-1 exercises.} The adapter layer for TRL, veRL, and
OpenRLHF is \emph{implemented}, but the empirical results in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}
are produced on the Tinker managed back-end (and a Colab GPU for the white-box P1
path). A head-to-head cross-framework divergence table --- identical config run on all
four back-ends --- is deferred to Phase-2; Phase-1 delivers the harness and the
attribution \emph{design}, and we are careful not to claim cross-framework results we
have not yet run.

\section{Per-Step Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry}
Every training step emits a telemetry record to JSONL and to Weights \& Biases. The
fields that distinguish this harness from a standard trainer are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{ZVF} --- the fraction of prompt-groups in the step whose within-group
  reward variance is zero (hence zero reward-relative advantage; the KL term may
  still act);
  \item \textbf{gradient utilisation} --- the complementary fraction of groups
  with non-zero reward-relative advantage signal, excluding KL-only updates;
  \item \textbf{collapse decomposition} --- of the collapsed groups, how many are
  \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus \emph{all-wrong};
  \item plus reward, loss, entropy, completion length, and KL to the reference.
\end{itemize}
The raw group reward tensors are also saved, so any downstream diagnostic (e.g.\
\texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py}) can \emph{recompute} a published number from the
stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary.

\section{Formalising the Zero-Variance Fraction}
For a prompt $q$ we draw a group of $G$ completions $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^{G}$ from the
current policy and score each with a verifiable reward $r_i$. The group-relative
advantage is the within-group standardisation
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon},
\qquad
\mu_g = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j} r_j,\quad
\sigma_g = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_j (r_j-\mu_g)^2}.
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
(We write the population standard deviation; the sample-variance form $\tfrac{1}{G-1}$
differs only by a constant factor and does not change the zero-variance condition
$\sigma_g=0$ that this project measures.) Whenever $\sigma_g = 0$ --- every completion in the group receives the same reward ---
every $A_i = 0$, so the whole group contributes no gradient \emph{through the
advantage (surrogate) term}; only the KL-penalty term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} is
unaffected. The Zero-Variance
Fraction is exactly the batch-mean of the indicator $\mathbb{1}[\sigma_g = 0]$:
\begin{equation}
\zvf \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{g \in B} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\sigma_g = 0\right].
\label{eq:zvf}
\end{equation}
This makes zero-advantage sampling a single, loggable scalar --- the measurement around
which the entire portfolio is organised.

\section{The Eight-Study Portfolio}
\label{sec:portfolio}
\input{tikz/fig7_zvf.tex}
\textbf{P1 --- Scaling / layer dynamics.} A white-box study of \emph{where} a model
adapts under GRPO-style updates, recording per-layer LoRA gradient norms to test
whether the layers that will matter can be identified early (predictive
layer-freezing). Requires per-layer gradient access, so it runs on a local GPU
rather than the managed back-end.

\textbf{P2 --- Zero-variance / advantage starvation.} Quantifies ZVF on real reward
tensors and asks whether any re-baselining scheme can recover reward contrast from
collapsed groups.

\textbf{P3 --- Group size.} Studies how collapse, reward, held-out gain, and token
cost vary with the group size $G$, and reframes $G$ as a compute budget rather than
an integer.

\textbf{P4 --- Length bias (designed).} Proposes semantic-density /
surprise-weighted token normalisation instead of uniform or static length penalties.

\textbf{P5 --- Reporting / provenance (flagship, systems).} A GRPO post-training
datasheet plus cryptographic rollout provenance, so one can prove which verifier
version and which rollouts produced a checkpoint.
\input{tikz/fig6.tex}

\textbf{P6 --- Registry.} A versioned, temporal registry of evolving run state
(verifier-logic updates, reward drift), intended to fold into the P5 standard.

\textbf{P7 --- Controller (designed).} Classical PID control of decoding temperature
and clip range, using live advantage-variance / KL-drift as sensor inputs.

\textbf{P8 --- Integrity / anomaly.} An auditor that uses the per-step telemetry
(not just reward) to detect anomalous / manipulated runs.
