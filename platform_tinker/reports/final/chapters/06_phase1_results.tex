\section{P2 --- Advantage-Signal Starvation is Large and Easy-Driven}
On real reward tensors for four methods (40 steps each, $16$ prompts $\times\,8$
samples, binary verifiable reward on GSM8K), the mean zero-variance fraction is
$\zvf = 0.72$--$0.77$: in this fixed shared-schedule, seed-0 study, roughly
three-quarters of prompt groups contribute no reward-derived surrogate signal.
The collapse is \emph{easy-driven}, not chance: about $65$--$71\%$ of
prompts are \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus only ${\sim}5\%$ all-wrong.
We then tested whether a re-baselining trick could recover gradient from collapsed
groups. A naive cross-prompt baseline (advantage against the global mean) gives
$|A|\approx0.21$, but that ``signal'' is just a proxy for prompt
easiness/difficulty --- not a learning signal --- which is precisely what
RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024rloo} already captures. A difficulty-isocline baseline
correctly recovers $|A|\approx0.012$--$0.02$, i.e.\ essentially zero, confirming that
\emph{no recoverable reward-contrast signal exists} in these groups. \textbf{Honest redirect:}
re-baselining is the wrong lever; the data point instead to difficulty-targeted
sampling / curriculum, motivating the experiments below.

\section{P3 --- A Single-Seed Sweep Does Not Establish a Group-Size Optimum}
\label{sec:p3}
\input{tikz/fig8.tex}
A single-seed sweep (Table~\ref{tab:p3}) is suggestive: $G{=}2$ sits below the
learning threshold ($50\%$ of steps collapse, $+0.000$ held-out), $G{=}4$ looks best
($+0.125$ held-out), and $G\ge8$ shows diminishing returns as token cost scales
roughly linearly with $G$ while the easy subset gets mastered and groups collapse to
all-correct anyway. But the $G{=}4$ ``win'' is literally one extra correct held-out
example ($7/8 \to 8/8$, $n=8$, single seed). When the multi-seed campaign (below)
re-tested this, the sweet spot did \emph{not} survive: at seed 0 the baseline
held-out gains were $G2\,{+}0.083$, $G4\,{0.000}$, $G8\,{+}0.083$, $G16\,{+}0.083$
--- i.e.\ $G{=}4$ was the \emph{worst} point, not the best.

\section{Curriculum --- Eliminating Waste is Not a Free Lunch}
A curriculum that trains only on mixed-variance (non-collapsed) groups does exactly
what it is designed to do: it drives the zero-loss fraction from $0.50$ to $0.00$.
But it pays $4.81\times$ the sampling cost (oversampling to \emph{find} non-collapsed
groups) and delivers an \emph{identical} held-out gain to the baseline ($+0.05$ vs
$+0.05$, held-out $n=20$, one seed, both gains equal to one extra correct answer).
Eliminating the visible zero-advantage groups does not, by itself, buy accuracy: the
sampling overhead offsets the gradient-density benefit.

\section{Matched-Token Re-Test Yields an Honest Null}
\label{sec:campaign}
The self-contained matched-token experiment in
\path{experiments/results/token\_budget/results.json} compares baseline and
curriculum at approximately $30{,}000$ rollout tokens across three seeds at $G{=}4$
(Table~\ref{tab:campaign}). Both arms average the same held-out gain, $+0.028$.
The curriculum skips 11--20 collapsed groups and therefore makes only 2, 8, and 5
optimizer steps, compared with 12, 11, and 10 for the baseline, without improving
the mean held-out outcome. With only three seeds and held-out $n=12$, these are
descriptive, noise-limited results; they support a null conclusion, not equivalence.

\section{P1 --- Step-1 Predictability Holds at Toy Scale but Collapses at 3B}
\label{sec:p1}
\input{tikz/fig4.tex}
The white-box layer profile at toy scale (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 28 layers, 5 GRPO-style
steps, a hardcoded arithmetic set) initially looked like the clearest positive in
the portfolio: the top-$k$ highest-gradient layers after a \emph{single} step were
identical to the top-$k$ over all steps
(\texttt{step1\_predicts\_final\_topk\_overlap} $=1.0$), suggesting one could decide
what to freeze after one update. \textbf{This did not survive scaling.} A four-seed
re-test (Qwen2.5-3B, 36 layers, 10 steps, \emph{real} GSM8K, seeds
$\{0,1,2,3\}$) drops the mean step-1$\to$final overlap to
$0.083\pm0.048$ --- i.e.\ ${\approx}$chance. The mid-late band that dominates by end-of-training
\emph{emerges} over steps and is invisible at step~1, so the apparent predictability
was an artifact of the toy setup. Adaptation is only \emph{moderately} concentrated:
the top $25\%$ of layers carry ${\sim}48\%$ of the total gradient norm
(\texttt{concentration\_top25pct\_share} $=0.476$ at 1.5B, $0.39$ at 3B), not the
$>90\%$ that a ``freeze almost everything'' story would need.

\section{P8 --- Telemetry Separates Method Traces in a Small Proof of Concept}
Using the per-step telemetry rather than reward alone, an exploratory classifier
separates the four method traces with row-wise cross-validated AUROC $0.838\pm0.010$,
versus $0.426\pm0.027$ for reward-only features. This shows that the stored telemetry
contains method-identifying structure beyond the reward summary; it does \emph{not}
yet establish run-integrity detection or generalisation. The result is
recomputed from the raw \texttt{n2\_reward\_tensor\_resume} tensors by
\texttt{p8\_detector.py}: four reward-optimisation algorithms each contribute $40$
per-step rows ($N=160$); the positive ``surrogate'' class is three GRPO variants
reported as GRPO ($120$ rows) and the negative class is genuine GRPO ($40$ rows), a
$3{:}1$ imbalance. Features are derived entirely from the raw reward/length/advantage
arrays (\texttt{zvf}, \texttt{reward\_mean}, \texttt{reward\_std},
\texttt{frac\_all\_zero}, \texttt{frac\_all\_one}, \texttt{mean\_len},
\texttt{cv\_len}, \texttt{len\_reward\_corr}, \texttt{adv\_abs\_mean},
\texttt{adv\_std}, \texttt{lag1\_autocorr}). A logistic-regression classifier
(median-imputed, standardised, $5$-fold stratified CV) reaches
$\mathrm{AUROC}=0.838\pm0.010$, versus $0.426\pm0.027$ for a reward-only auditor
(below chance). \textbf{Overfitting caveat:} with $N=160$ rows, $11$ features, and a
$3{:}1$ class imbalance, one trajectory per method, temporal correlation between rows,
and row-wise rather than run-wise splits, the AUROC is susceptible to severe optimism.
It should be read only as a \emph{method-classification proof of concept}. A larger,
multi-run evaluation with grouped temporal/run-level holdout (diverse base
models/tasks, thousands of steps) is required before any integrity claim. Remaining provenance caveat: the base
model/task are not recorded in the tensor rows, so provenance is incomplete --- exactly
the gap the P5 protocol is designed to close.

\section{Cross-Study Synthesis}
Read together, the eight studies tell a coherent story rather than eight isolated
results. P2 establishes that the dominant measured phenomenon in the fixed study is
advantage-signal starvation
($\zvf\approx0.72$--$0.77$), and that it is \emph{easy-driven} (the collapsed groups
are mostly already-solved prompts). That immediately rules out the tempting fix ---
re-baselining --- because P2 also shows the ``recovered'' signal is just a difficulty
proxy with no learning content. The natural next lever, a curriculum that removes the
waste, is exactly what the curriculum and matched-token studies test; both return
honest nulls. P3 tells the same cautionary tale for group size: a single-seed sweep
does not establish a universal optimum. P1 extends
the pattern to a structural claim --- step-1 predictability of layer adaptation ---
which likewise fails to survive scaling. P8 adds a narrower systems observation:
telemetry separates the four available method traces better than reward-only features,
but integrity generalisation remains untested. The synthesis, then, is that on this
compute scale the \emph{measurement} is useful while most
\emph{algorithmic} levers are noise-limited --- which is what justifies steering
Phase-2 toward the systems/provenance direction (P5) rather than chasing another
GRPO variant.

\section{Rigour and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:rigor}
The methodology is deliberately adversarial toward its own results.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed with matched baselines where feasible.} The curriculum
  intervention is re-tested across three seeds at matched token budget; the P1
  scaling check uses four seeds. P2 remains a shared-schedule seed-0 diagnostic, P3
  remains a single-seed sweep, and P8 remains one trajectory per method; these are
  explicitly labelled exploratory rather than confirmatory.
  \item \textbf{Held-out evaluation.} Gains are measured on held-out prompts, and we
  report the held-out $n$ ($8$--$20$) alongside every gain so the reader can see that
  magnitudes are noise-limited.
  \item \textbf{Recompute-from-tensors.} Published diagnostics are recomputable from
  stored raw tensors (e.g.\ \texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py}), not merely read back
  from a logged summary.
  \item \textbf{Adversarial review plus deterministic verification.} Critical review
  of the early single-seed conclusions prompted the multi-seed and matched-token
  re-tests. The evidence retained in the submission is the script-based recomputation:
  ZVF $0.72$--$0.77$, all-correct $0.65$--$0.71$, and the stored P8 row-CV output.
\end{itemize}
Known threats we do \emph{not} hide: small held-out sets; a partially-recorded P3
sweep artefact (G8/G16 summaries lost to the W\&B thread-safety bug); a deterministic
prompt schedule shared across the four P2 methods; and an invalidated
\texttt{zero\_loss\_frac} in the first campaign batch (since fixed). Re-running P3
under the process-per-run launcher yields corrected G8/G16 held-out gains of
$+0.083$ (both from $0.750\to0.833$, single seed), superseding the thread-bug-affected
values --- but these remain single-seed point estimates pending multi-seed
replication.
