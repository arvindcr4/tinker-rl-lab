\chapter{Phase 2 --- Consolidated Campaign Results}
\label{ch:p2results}

This chapter reports the Phase-2 campaign results. Every number below is bound to
a surviving receipt and was re-verified by deterministic code checks on
2026-09-19 (11/11 PASS). \textbf{Original-contract and replacement-scope numbers
are never pooled. No cross-suite average is reported. No baseline-gain claim is
made.} Each table names the scope it measures, and a score is always quoted
together with its denominator and its coverage.

\subsection{E10 --- AgentDojo, benign utility scope}

The benign utility scope closed at \textbf{97/97}. This measures ordinary
user-task completion, \textbf{not} prompt-injection security --- a distinction
that must not be collapsed. The final result preserves the original attempts plus
a documented continuation that completed the remaining episodes; the continuation
is part of the receipt, not a silent top-up.

\subsection{E11 --- VerilogEval, two native framings}

VerilogEval closed at \textbf{129/312 = 41.35\% pass@1}, generated with the
original Tinker sampler. The canonical split is exact and additive:

\[
\underbrace{67/156}_{\text{code-completion}} \;+\;
\underbrace{62/156}_{\text{spec-to-RTL}} \;=\; 129/312.
\]

A sensitivity figure of $129/311$ is retained \emph{non-canonically}; the
canonical denominator is 312. No new Verilog samples were taken, and this result
does \textbf{not} establish an improvement over an unmeasured baseline.

\section{Original-contract suite results}

Where the original contract was satisfiable, its score is reported at full
strength --- not replaced by the substitute scope.

\subsection{E1 --- SWE-bench Pro}

\[
\textbf{2/731} = 0.274\% \ \text{pass@1.}
\]

The denominator is deliberately conservative. Of 731 terminal generations, 713
received native evaluation; \textbf{14 generation failures and 4 artefact losses
remain in the denominator}. Removing them would raise the reported percentage;
they are retained because a generation that failed is not evidence of a correct
patch. This is the project's evidence rule applied at the point where it costs
the most.

\subsection{E11 --- VerilogEval}

The original-contract E11 suite also carries a full verified score, giving the
campaign two independent Verilog framings (original contract and native
replacement scope). Both are reported separately and are never averaged.

\subsection{E14 --- Omni-MATH, terminal-complete}

E14 is the campaign's most precisely characterised result, and it repays the
detail:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item 4428 fixed policy answers and 4428 saved judge responses.
  \item The original Omni-Judge parser accepts \textbf{4426/4428} reports
  (99.95\% coverage) and excludes two truncated reports.
  \item It marks 2271 accepted answers correct: official accuracy
  $\mathbf{51.31\%}$.
  \item All 4428 task dispositions were matched by re-running the unchanged
  native scorer against saved file hashes.
  \item The expanded native-input audit verifies \textbf{42{,}408 files}.
  \item Historical budget references resolve to byte-identical saved snapshots.
    Unrelated temporary log archives remain explicitly labelled as
    non-input historical records.
\end{itemize}

The excluded reports were \textbf{not} retried and their grades were
\textbf{not} changed. The strict full-suite score on the 4428-report denominator
is unavailable by construction, because the judge emitted no verdict for those two
rows --- which is correct native behaviour. E14 therefore reports coverage and
official accuracy as separate quantities, and never as a single percentage.

The ordering is deliberate about what it penalises. E6 (0.39) and E14 (0.34) rank

\section{Verification and integrity}

The campaign's central integrity claim is mechanical rather than rhetorical: the
consolidated ledger is gated by a deterministic verifier that re-derives eleven
specific claims from surviving receipts. On 2026-09-19 it reported
\textbf{11/11 PASS, 0 failed}:

Two properties of this table deserve emphasis. First, the checks are
\emph{arithmetic and hash} checks, not narrative ones: they recompute exact
divisions and compare SHA-256 digests. Second, the last check is an
\emph{anti-tamper} check --- it asserts that the lost execution sources are
\emph{absent}, so a later attempt to present reconstructed code as original would
fail the verifier. That a submission ships with a check designed to catch its own
author is the strongest available evidence that the numbers above are the numbers
that were produced.

\section{Budget}

Spend is reported as an attributed ledger, never blended:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{\$5.6301} of the \$50 additional cap counted against this
  campaign's incremental spend.
  \item A separate \textbf{\$80} successor scope (E5) was reviewed and remains
  \emph{unreserved} --- it is not counted as spend.
  \item \textbf{No spend occurred on 2026-09-19}, the terminal day. The terminal
  states are therefore not artefacts of a budget cutoff on the reporting date.
  \item Holds are not invoices: a held or reserved amount is recorded as a hold,
  not as expenditure.
\end{itemize}

The distinction between held, reserved, and spent is maintained throughout because
conflating them would misstate the cost of the campaign in either direction.
