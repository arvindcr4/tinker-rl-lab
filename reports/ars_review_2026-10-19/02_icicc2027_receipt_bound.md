Remove the internal comment block from any uploaded source.

### 2.2 R1 — Methodology

**Strengths (anchored).** (S1) Denominator discipline is exemplary and traceable: the panel independently confirmed all six later-replacement rows in `tab:later` (lines 319–324) against `outputs/finish_pending_2026-09-27/*/result.json` (E1-R 190 attempted/57 graded/1 resolved; E2-R 45/45/27; E5-R 97/72/8; E6-R 812/704/90; E9-R 34/34/10; E13-R 255/255, 26.1%), and the 11-check ledger (`LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json`: 11 PASS/0 FAIL, including `strict_completed_suites_are_E8_E10_E11`) matches lines 424–428. (S2) The E11 design-cluster bootstrap [34.3%, 48.7%] (line 259) traces exactly to `e11_design_cluster_bootstrap.json` (156 pairs; 54/81/13/8 split; CI [0.3429, 0.4872]). (S3) The manuscript repeatedly refuses causal readings its data cannot support (Omni-MATH cap split "Difficulty confounds", line 278; BankerToolBench "confounded diagnostic", lines 365–370).

**R1-W1 — Receipts do not bind to runs in any verifiable sense; validation is circular.** *Anchor:* lines 161–169 ("receipts are unsigned operator-written JSON without external timestamping, and no formal receipt schema is defined"; fail-closed gating "is the intended design, not proof that every historical launch obeyed it"); lines 424–428 (11 checks = "selected divisions… two hash bindings and missing-source probes"; "Reading this record is not rerunning"). *Problem:* the central artifact — the receipt — is a self-assertion by the same team that ran the evaluations, checked by re-dividing its own printed numbers. As *demonstrated*, this is a reporting template, not a governance mechanism; the title verb "governance" outruns the evidence tier. *Fix:* either (a) downgrade the claim throughout to "receipt-formatted reporting" and present the schema/gating as a proposal, or (b) add the missing binding: a published receipt JSON schema, one worked end-to-end re-verification by a script a reader can run against retained files, and grader/actor digests that resolve. At minimum, print one full exemplar receipt in an appendix/repository path cited by line.

**R1-W2 — Within-lane treatment heterogeneity defeats even descriptive lane scores.** *Anchor:* `tab:inference` (lines 186–208): temperatures 0–1.0, thinking on/off, output caps 384–16,384, most cells "NR" (E2-O/E5-O entirely NR); E1-O mixes "476 managed-sampler and 255 merged-vLLM responses" in one 2/731 (line 286); E13 restarts "killed or hung episodes with new seeds" (lines 332–333; `E13/result.json` shows 262 attempt records behind 255 denominators). *Problem:* a lane score aggregates materially different decoding/serving conditions, and retry policies condition on failure (survivorship): rerun-on-preemption/hang biases outcomes upward relative to a single-attempt protocol, yet attempt-level denominators are not printed for the primary lanes. *Fix:* for each headline lane, print attempt counts and the retry rule next to the score; split E1-O by serving stack or label it a two-arm mixture in the abstract-level summary; state plainly that cross-lane decoding differences (especially temperature 1.0 vs 0) preclude any lane ranking, not only capability inference.

**R1-W3 — Paired pilot is underpowered, multiplicity-unhandled, and partly circular.** *Anchor:* `tab:paired` (lines 394–412): n = 5–151, with E1/E7/E9 having k ≤ 1 discordant pairs (caption concedes "uninformative", lines 388–390); 17 primary contrasts (line 377) with no multiplicity discussion; E12's 151 rubric items cluster in 6 apps and are "scored by the base model" (line 381) — i.e., one arm of the contrast grades the other; E4's interval [−.080, 0] rests on "one nonzero pair" (line 391). Panel spot traces (E1, E2, E8, E11, E12, E14 `paired.json`) confirm the printed fractions and p-values. *Problem:* "inconclusive" is the honest headline, but printing 17 unadjusted p-values invites selective reading, and the E12 judge design biases toward the base arm in an unknown direction/magnitude. *Fix:* add a minimum-detectable-effect or discordant-pair count per lane; state the family-wise interpretation rule; flag E12 as judge-circular in the table itself (not only prose); move or visually quarantine the p ≈ 0.006 contrast as a confounded systems observation.

**R1-W4 — Conditional "sensitivity" scores are printed without verifiable subset membership.** *Anchor:* LAB-Bench 450/708 = 63.56% (lines 251–253, "unverified subset membership"); VerilogEval 129/162 = 79.63% (line 261) with the added caveat that the extractor "could accept code inside an unterminated reasoning block" (lines 265–267). *Problem:* these are the numbers secondary citations will extract, stripped of caveats — a 63.6% and a 79.6% that more than double/triple the end-to-end scores — yet by the authors' own account the denominators cannot be reconstructed from retained grade rows. *Fix:* demote both to a clearly fenced diagnostic paragraph (or supplementary), each labelled "non-reproducible sensitivity — do not cite as a score", and keep them out of any table cell or abstract-adjacent position.

**R1-W5 — Uncertainty is reported for exactly one headline proportion.** *Anchor:* only E11 carries an interval (line 259); E8-R 450/1,967, E10-R 88/97, E14-R 2,271/4,426, E1-O 2/731 and all of `tab:later` print bare point values, although the underlying `result.json` files already contain Wilson intervals the panel inspected (e.g., E1-R [0.0009, 0.0292]). *Problem:* inconsistent with the paper's own statistical care elsewhere; 88/97 (0.907) without an interval will be read as precise. *Fix:* print the retained Wilson/ bootstrap intervals in `tab:lanes`/`tab:later` or state why item-sampling intervals are not meaningful for fixed benchmark populations and apply that logic uniformly (including to E11).

**R1-W6 — Artifact-level count discrepancy in E5-R infrastructure errors.** *Anchor:* manuscript lines 321, 337–338 (97 attempted, 72 graded); `E5/result.json` claims 25 infrastructure errors (14 context-window + 11 empty-message), but `E5/infra_errors_reconstructed.json` inventories only 24 tasks and its own note says 13 context-window errors, reconstructed after an "`--auto-resume`… rewrote results.json and dropped them". *Problem:* one of the 25 ungraded tasks is unaccounted at artifact level — minor numerically (score 8/97 is unaffected) but damaging for a paper whose thesis is exhaustive disposition accounting; it also reveals a results file that was mutated post hoc. *Fix:* reconcile the 25th task ID in an erratum line in the source map and disclose the `results.json` rewrite incident in the manuscript's deviation list.

### 2.3 R2 — Domain

**Strengths (anchored).** (S1) Benchmark identity work is unusually careful: AgentDojo benign utility is explicitly severed from attack resistance and AgentHarm (lines 92–93, 272–273, 441–442); the Tau3 banking replacement is identified as the τ-Knowledge component, "not the original τ-bench" (lines 96–97, citing `shi2026tauknowledge`); count mismatches are retained rather than normalised (BinaryAudit 46-item campaign vs 33-task current project, ML-Dev-Bench 34 configs vs 30 paper tasks, lines 297–299). (S2) Failure attribution separates observation from story: SWE-bench Pro's 593 corrupt-hunk/120 parsing patches (lines 282–285) properly reframe 2/731 as end-to-end formatting failure, not issue-solving inability.

**R2-W1 — Training-overlap (contamination) remains open at exactly the lanes it matters most.** *Anchor:* `tab:training` (lines 121–122: API-Bank-RLVR and SWE-Gym, 512-row pool); lines 135–138 ("no complete content-level overlap audit against E1–E14 is established"; AgentDojo records `TRAINING_INVENTORY_ABSENT`); abstract line 36. *Problem:* SWE-Gym-style software data vs the SWE lanes (E1) and API/tool data vs tool-use lanes (E4/E5/E10) are precisely where near-duplicate leakage would inflate scores; the proposed title correctly drops "Held-Out", but the text still reports these lanes' scores without even a suite-name-level overlap matrix (which training source plausibly touches which lane). *Fix:* include a 2×14 source-by-lane plausibility matrix marking un-audited cells, so readers can discount the exposed lanes; no new audit is required to print the exposure map.

**R2-W2 — The actor premise is unverified: the "GRPO-trained" model may be functionally the base model.** *Anchor:* lines 51–57 (campaign "neither verifies the exact historical objective nor identifies its causal effect"); `tab:training` line 126 (zero reward 22/40 steps, zero loss 35/40); lines 130–134 (training logs not supplied, "Current code cannot reconstruct the historical objective"); pilot null (line 377–380, which itself notes few effective updates "would also predict small differences"). *Problem:* every lane score is a property of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B + an adapter whose training signal is mostly absent; the paper never states the simple consequence that these are, to within pilot resolution, base-model serving measurements. *Fix:* one explicit paragraph: given 35/40 zero-loss steps and the null pilot, lane results should be interpreted as base-model-plus-serving-stack behaviour unless future work shows otherwise.

**R2-W3 — Several benchmark identities rest on non-archival 2026 web sources a reader cannot verify.** *Anchor:* `refs.bib:229–268` (FrontierSWE, SDAB, BinaryAudit, WebBench, App-Bench, OpenReward as `@misc` websites/repositories, accessed Oct 2026) and 2026 arXiv entries such as `lau2026bankertoolbench`, `vidgen2026apexagents`, `shi2026tauknowledge`, `merrill2026terminalbench` (lines 180–202, 298–304). *Problem:* six of fourteen originals are "unavailable" partly because their contracts are defined by mutable web artefacts; the paper's identity claims about them are only as durable as those pages,. *Fix:* archive-pin each such source (commit SHA or Wayback capture, as already done for model revisions) in the source package and note in one sentence which lanes have no peer-reviewed benchmark reference.

**R2-W4 — Canonical anchors missing around the two most-cited lane families.** *Anchor:* Related Work (lines 73–98) cites SWE-bench Pro and Multilingual but not the original SWE-bench paper (Jimenez et al., ICLR 2024 — a canonical reference this panel is confident exists), and cites τ²-bench/τ-Knowledge without the original τ-bench lineage. Similarly, the governance framing would benefit from the construct-validity and contamination literatures (kinds of sources, not specific citations prescribed here). *Problem:* domain reviewers will read the omission as unfamiliarity with the lineage whose public split supplies the E1 headline. *Fix:* add the original SWE-bench citation and one sentence positioning the public-731 split within it; add a short contamination/validity paragraph.

**R2-W5 — AgentDojo 0.907 will be misread as a safety result despite the caveats.** *Anchor:* `tab:lanes` line 235 (88/97 = 0.9072, the highest number in the ledger); caveats at lines 271–273 and 446 ("No score or governance label certifies safe deployment"). *Problem:* benign-utility at temperature 0 with no attack arm and no pinned upstream commit (line 272) is exactly the cell most likely to be scraped into a comparison table. *Fix:* put "benign utility only — no attacks run" inside the table cell/caption, and report the AgentDojo version's missing commit pin as a reproducibility defect in the same breath.

### 2.5 Devil's Advocate

**Strongest Counter-Argument (200–300 words).** This manuscript documents, meticulously, the failure of its own proposal. The receipt-bound protocol was supposed to make benchmark claims fail-closed and verifiable; by the authors' own account the receipts are unsigned JSON written by the operators themselves, no schema exists, fail-closed gating cannot be shown to have governed the historical launches, and when the protocol blocked six lanes, the experiment lead simply overrode it and ran thinner scaffolds under changed settings. What remains after these concessions is not governance but narration: a well-kept lab notebook about a campaign in which six of fourteen benchmarks were never run, three rest on one to seven tasks, the surviving scores mix temperatures from 0 to 1.0 and two serving stacks inside a single denominator, the "trained" actor's training logs are missing and 35 of 40 steps show zero loss, and the only controlled comparison — a small same-engine pilot — finds nothing on any of seventeen contrasts. The governance framing was adopted after these results were known, converting a null training study into a methodology paper whose method was never itself tested. Every lesson the paper teaches — separate prepared/attempted/graded, distrust replacement scopes, disclose formatting failures — is already stated, with more authority, by the Agentic Benchmark Checklist and the reproducibility literature it cites. Meanwhile the paper prints, inevitably stripped of caveats by secondary citation, a 0.907 AgentDojo number, a 51.31% Omni-MATH number, and two conditional sensitivities (63.56%, 79.63%) whose denominators the authors cannot reconstruct. Publishing this in proceedings risks laundering unverifiable, non-capability numbers into the citable record under a "governance" imprimatur, while the same fourteen-lane ledger is simultaneously presented in a sibling manuscript. The honest venue for this material, absent major restructuring, is a technical report or reproducibility-track audit appendix — not a research paper claiming a governance contribution.

**Issue List.**

- **DA-CRITICAL-1 (circular / self-referential validation; claims stronger than evidence tier).** Lines 161–169, 424–428: receipts are unsigned, schema-free, operator-written; the 11 "checks" re-derive the receipts' own arithmetic. The word "governance" in the title asserts an enforcement regime the case study shows being waived by its own lead (lines 328–331). Evidence tier supports "reporting template", not "governance".
- **DA-CRITICAL-2 (salami / overlap with sibling manuscript, undisclosed).** `papers/pes_ieee_attribution/ArvindCR_GRPO_Attribution_IEEE.tex` is titled "Stack-Conditioned Measurement of GRPO Post-Training: Signal Diagnostics and a **Fourteen-Lane Evaluation Ledger**" and, on string presence alone, shares the same headline cells as this manuscript (129/312, 450/1,967, 2/731, 88/97, seed 809 actor). This manuscript nowhere cites or delineates that sibling. Whether the overlap is legitimate companion-paper partitioning or dual submission cannot be determined from this text — and that indeterminacy in a governance paper is itself the defect. Disclosure and an explicit delta statement are mandatory before publication anywhere.
- **DA-CRITICAL-3 (submission identity / venue contract).** Per `SOURCE_MAP.md:15–19` and `README.md:3–5`, ICICC accepted a differently titled "held-out GRPO" paper; this draft retitles, reframes post hoc (line 55), awaits organizer approval and AI-policy/similarity clearance, and its build artefacts disagree on pages (12 vs 13) and reference counts (37 vs 39). Accepting this text as camera-ready would ratify a paper the venue did not review.
- **DA-MAJOR-1 (null results over-read by presentation, if not by prose).** Lines 372–382, `tab:paired`: seventeen inconclusive contrasts are printed with p-values including p = 1 for lanes with zero discordant pairs; absence-of-evidence is correct in prose but the table format mimics an equivalence result. Combined with zero-loss training (line 126), a reader may conclude "GRPO did no harm" — a claim the design cannot support at these sample sizes.
- **DA-MAJOR-2 (receipts that do not bind to claimed runs).** Primary-lane headline numbers (E1-O patch audit 593/120, E8-R 1,259 unparsed/1,086 length finishes, E10-R, E14-R cap split 1,029/2,982 vs 1,242/1,446) rest on retained narrative, with raw payloads absent (`SOURCE_MAP.md:44`); response text and finish reasons for E11 are gone (lines 262–263); the original sampler "cannot be replayed from this package" (lines 421–422, 435–436). The receipts bind the *report* to itself, not to executions.
- **DA-MAJOR-3 (retry-conditioned denominators).** Lines 332–334 and E13/E2/E9 rerun policies: aborted/hung attempts are restarted with new seeds and only final outcomes are denominated (262 E13 attempt records vs 255 reported). A governance protocol that permits silent attempt censoring teaches the opposite of its stated lesson unless attempt-level accounting is printed.
- **DA-MINOR-1.** The E5-R 24-vs-25 infrastructure-error inventory mismatch and post-hoc `results.json` rewrite (see R1-W6) — small, but corrosive to the paper's premise.
- **DA-MINOR-2.** Contribution bullets (lines 59–69) list a "failure-attribution analysis" while §4 repeatedly concludes causes "cannot be apportioned" (lines 262–263) — bullet-level overclaim.

**Ignored Alternative Explanations.** (a) The campaign's anomalies (patch corruption, unparsed extractions, invented dialogue turns) may be entirely serving/scaffold defects of one immature stack, telling us little about evaluation governance in general; the paper never bounds this base rate. (b) The pilot's uniform null is equally consistent with "adapter ≈ base" as with "pilot underpowered" — the paper gestures at the former (line 379–380) but does not follow it to the conclusion that lane scores are base-model measurements. (c) High extraction failure (150/183 E11 failures) may reflect an extractor bug rather than model formatting, which would invert the failure-attribution narrative; no extractor validation on gold outputs is reported.

**Missing Stakeholder Perspectives.** Benchmark maintainers (would they accept these replacement scopes and thin scaffolds as instances of their benchmarks? CORE-Bench at 27/45 = 0.600 via a "thin OpenAI-tools loop" per `E2/result.json` deviations is liable to be read against leaderboard numbers obtained with the native scaffold); independent auditors (no external party ever held the receipts); downstream benchmark consumers and leaderboard curators who will ingest the citable numbers; and the venue itself, whose accepted-paper contract is being renegotiated by retitling.

**Observations (Non-Defects).** The paper's self-disclosure density is genuinely above the field's norm — most DA charges above are assembled from the authors' own caveats, which is to their credit. The dated-state discipline (19 vs 27 September ledgers) and refusal to pool the two SWE-bench Multilingual waves (lines 339–341) are practices worth propagating. Nothing in the panel's spot traces suggests fabrication: every number we could trace, traced.

**So-what test.** If this paper vanished, what would the field lose? A worked example — rare in print — of how a real 14-benchmark campaign degrades into fragments, and a vocabulary (prepared/attempted/graded; original/replacement; strictly vs terminally complete) for saying so.

## 3. Editorial Decision Letter

**Decision: Major Revision.**

**Consensus across seats.** All seats converge on three points: (i) the manuscript's honesty and denominator discipline are strengths, and the panel's independent traces (11-check ledger, all six later-replacement `result.json` files, E11 cluster bootstrap, spot pilot lanes, 39/39 citation-key match, arithmetic for 2,271/4,426 vs /4,428, 450/708, 129/162, and the Omni-MATH cap split) confirm the printed numbers where artefacts exist; (ii) the central claim outruns its mechanism — "governance" is demonstrated only as retrospective reporting, with circular validation (R1-W1/DA-CRITICAL-1); (iii) the manuscript is not submittable in its current identity (EIC-W1/W2, DA-CRITICAL-3), irrespective of scientific merit.

**Disagreement.** EIC and R3 would accept a repositioned experience report at this venue tier after revision; R2 and DA lean closer to reject-and-resubmit as a technical/audit report unless the sibling-overlap question resolves cleanly. The decision adopts Major Revision because every critical defect except independent re-execution is remediable by disclosure, reframing, and artefact repair — no new experiments are strictly required, though claims must shrink to fit existing evidence.

**Adjudication of every Devil's Advocate CRITICAL.**
