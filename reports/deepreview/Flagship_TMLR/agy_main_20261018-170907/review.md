## Summary

This manuscript investigates two foundational ambiguities in group-relative reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR), such as GRPO and DAPO:
1. **Decision insufficiency of homogeneous terminal rewards**: When all $G$ sampled responses to a prompt receive equal binary rewards, the centered group advantage is identically zero. The authors observe that this terminal zero-variance event cannot distinguish between an impossible prompt ($p=0$), an unlucky sample from a solvable prompt ($0 < p < 1$), a false-negative verifier, or an execution failure. They provide elementary cost-accounting corollaries of the binary-starvation model ($f_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$), showing that while increasing fixed group size $G$ reduces the expected number of groups to the first mixed contrast ($\mathbb{E}[T] = 1/q_G(p)$), complete-group sampling requires at least $G/q_G(\epsilon) \ge 1/\epsilon$ expected rollouts for success probability $\epsilon$. They also provide a two-state, two-action minimax regret witness ($\text{stop}$ vs. commit to $m$ rollouts across $E_0: p=0$ and $E_q: p=q$) and state the standard conditional Bayes value-of-information inequality.
2. **Implementation conformance vs. causal training interpretation**: Open-source RL stacks (specifically pinned TRL 1.2.0 and verl 0.3.0.post1) implement nominally similar objectives with different reward standardization, active-row selection, and token/sequence loss reductions. The authors propose a "fail-closed" conformance protocol (`S1`) that compares an explicit float64 reference implementation against framework adapters on 14 frozen objective cases and a 36-case controller matrix, classifying zero-gradient pairs as discrete logical relations (`joint_zero`, `a_zero`, `b_zero`, `nonzero`) rather than undefined cosine angles.

Finally, the paper reports a preregistered feasibility postmortem on Qwen3-1.7B across two regimes (a balanced equal-length GSM8K negative control and a filtered variable-length MATH-500 positive control over 100 replay steps). The campaign did not establish its causal hypotheses:
- All 14 filtered variable-length jobs were marked contract-infeasible because the seed-11 generator yielded an observed active completion-length coefficient of variation (CV) of 0 under the 512-token cap, failing the preregistered $\text{CV} \ge 0.35$ gate.
- Four seed-37 balanced runs remained pending due to A100 accelerator quota limits, and two seed-11 balanced runs (`intended_full` and `native_trl`) failed due to infrastructure issues.
- Across the 6 completed balanced scientific units (100 steps each), 62–65 steps produced exact `joint_zero` gradients. On the remaining 35–38 nonzero steps, intended-vs.-native gradient cosines were at least $0.999844$, but maximum relative $\ell_2$ distances reached $0.01458$–$0.01768$. Consequently, the sole completed `intended_full` unit (seed 23) satisfied the preregistered joint criterion ($\cos \ge 0.999$ and relative $\ell_2 \le 0.01$) on only $69/100$ steps ($65$ joint-zero plus $4/35$ nonzero steps), failing the $95/100$ mechanism gate.
- Across all 6 completed units, GSM8K evaluation accuracy on 128 test problems started at $20/128$ and ended between $17/128$ and $20/128$.

## Strengths

1. **Exceptional evidentiary honesty and anti-hype discipline**: The manuscript refuses to launder failed preregistered gates, infrastructure failures, or missing quota into a fabricated narrative. Explicitly separating `joint_zero` gradient steps from `nonzero` cosine comparisons (Section 3.2) prevents a subtle reporting pathology where division-by-zero floors either corrupt cosine similarity or silently inflate equivalence rates.
2. **Valuable software-semantics audit of RLVR frameworks**: Section 3.1 and Table 1 cleanly isolate concrete implementation differences between canonical equations and production codebases (`native_trl` vs. `intended_full` vs. `epsilon_only` vs. `reduction_only`), specifically regarding whether reward standardization happens before or after active-row selection, whether an additive $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ is added to the standard deviation, and whether loss reduction averages per-completion means or a global masked-token mean.
3. **Clear formalization of the distinction between group efficiency and rollout efficiency**: Proposition 2 (p. 5–6) concisely proves a practical point that is frequently confused in dynamic-sampling RLVR papers: increasing fixed group size $G$ reduces the expected number of *groups* to observe a reward contrast ($\frac{1}{G\epsilon} + O_G(1)$), but complete-group sampling cannot reduce the first-order expected *rollout* cost below $1/\epsilon$.
4. **Reproducible artifact architecture**: Binding claims to explicit statuses (`proved`, `artifact-verified`, `externally sourced`, `proposed`) and shipping a standalone verification script (`verify_anonymous_claims.py`) with SHA-256 receipts sets a strong template for auditability in RL systems research.

## Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient empirical contribution for a top archival ML venue**: By the authors' own candid admission (Abstract, Sections 5.2–5.4), the experimental campaign is an incomplete feasibility postmortem. Only 6 of 24 planned scientific training units completed (4 conditions on seed 23, 2 conditions on seed 11, 0 on seed 37, and 0 in the positive-control MATH-500 regime). In the 6 completed units, the model does not learn (test accuracy moves from $20/128$ at step 0 to $17/128$–$20/128$ at step 100, with 62–65% of steps having zero gradients and skipped optimizer updates). While the transparency is commendable, a blocked experiment due to a 512-token truncation bug (`CV = 0`) and missing GPU quota does not constitute a complete scientific study for NeurIPS/ICLR or TMLR.
2. **Disconnect between the theoretical/controller framing and the actual experiments**: The title, abstract, Introduction, Section 4, and the 36-case controller matrix (`static-8`, `static-16`, `symmetric-ZVF`, `failure-only`, `boundary-aware`, `full-triage`) focus heavily on adaptive controller interventions when facing zero-variance groups. However, zero controller interventions are evaluated in training (Table 6: *"Controller benefit: No matched intervention study / Not run"*). Conversely, the A100 campaign tests DAPO loss/standardization ablations (`epsilon_only`, `reduction_only`, `native_trl`, `intended_full`) under fixed $G=8$ replay. The paper reads as two halves—a short note on starvation/decision theory and an aborted TRL/DAPO differential replication—stitched together.
3. **Triviality of the theoretical propositions**: Propositions 1, 2, and 3 and Eq. (16) are elementary textbook exercises (geometric distribution tails, a first-order Taylor expansion / union bound $(1-\epsilon)^G \ge 1 - G\epsilon$, a 2x2 minimax linear regret intersection, and Jensen's inequality / tower property for Bayes value of information). While the authors honestly label Proposition 2 as *"elementary consequences of the known starvation model"* (p. 2), these results are too simple to carry the paper's contribution weight when the empirical campaign fails to run to completion.
4. **Flawed positive-control experimental design (`CV = 0` under 512-token cap)**: In Section 5.2, the filtered MATH-500 positive control failed because all generated completions hit the 512-token cap, yielding a completion-length coefficient of variation of exactly $0$ against a preregistered requirement of $\text{CV} \ge 0.35$. Running Qwen3-1.7B on MATH-500 with a 512-token limit virtually guarantees widespread truncation before answers terminate. Rather than being an deep scientific discovery about preregistration, this is a basic hyperparameter configuration error (setting `max_new_tokens=512` for a reasoning model on MATH) that should have been caught during preflight smoke testing before freezing the contract.
5. **Unexplained mismatch between Table 1/Appendix C case counts and concrete definitions**: In Table 1 (p. 4), TRL 1.2.0 has 14 intended cases, 4 "Native material", and 1 "Native not tested", while verl 0.3.0.post1 has 14 intended cases, 1 "Native material", and 4 "Native not tested". Those numbers ($4+1=5$) sum to 5 *arms* (`GRPO`, `DAPO`, `GSPO`, `DrGRPO`, `AERO`), not the 14 *cases/fixtures* listed in the adjacent column and Table 4. Furthermore, several arm names (`GSPO`, `DrGRPO`, `AERO`) are used in Eq. (4) and Table 4 without citation or background definition in the text.

## Anchored Comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.7: "The filtered seed-11 generator failed the frozen contract with observed completion-length CV 0, below the required 0.35, under the 512-token cap."` - A completion-length CV of 0 across 16 candidates means every single sampled completion had the exact same token length (presumably 512 tokens due to hard truncation). For Qwen3-1.7B on MATH-500, 512 tokens is far too short for chain-of-thought reasoning, causing both zero length variance (`CV = 0`) and likely all-wrong rewards. Freezing a preregistration contract before running a single corpus-generation smoke test on the positive control invalidates the experimental design. **Concrete fix**: Run and report the positive-control campaign with an appropriate generation length budget (e.g., 2048 or 4096 tokens) where natural completion-length variation occurs ($\text{CV} \ge 0.35$), and complete the missing balanced seed-11 (`intended_full`, `native_trl`) and seed-37 units so the causal hypotheses can actually be evaluated.
- `[CRITICAL] p.8: "For the sole completed intended-full balanced cell (seed 23), 69/100 receipts satisfy the frozen joint criterion, below the preregistered 95/100 requirement. Sixty-five are joint-zero; only four of the 35 nonzero receipts satisfy both cosine >= 0.999 and relative l2 <= 0.01."` - The paper diagnoses why the 95/100 gate failed only at a surface level. Because `native_trl` adds $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ to the group reward standard deviation $s$ (`s + 10^{-4}`), for binary rewards with $G=8$, the minimum nonzero sample standard deviation is $s = \sqrt{7/56} = \sqrt{1/8} \approx 0.35355$. Dividing by $s + 10^{-4}$ scales the entire gradient by $\frac{s}{s + 10^{-4}}$, producing a relative $\ell_2$ difference of $\approx \frac{10^{-4}}{0.35355} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-4}$ from $\varepsilon$ alone. Yet Table 3 reports a maximum relative $\ell_2$ of $0.01458$–$0.01768$ ($\approx 1.5\%$) even in `balanced equal-length` where all 8 rows are active, and `intended_full` vs. `reduction_only` on seed 23 have identical `Min cos` ($0.999881$) and `Max rel-l2` ($0.01544$). This implies that either (a) the "equal-length" balanced corpus does not actually have identical token lengths across the 8 completions in a group, causing per-completion mean (`intended_full`) and global masked-token mean (`native_trl` / `reduction_only`) to diverge by ~1.5%, or (b) Bessel vs. population standard deviation ($G$ vs. $G-1$, where $1 - \sqrt{7/8} \approx 0.0646$) or clipping interacts with the loss. **Concrete fix**: Explicitly decompose the $0.01458$–$0.01768$ relative $\ell_2$ discrepancy in the "balanced equal-length" regime: state whether completion lengths in the GSM8K balanced corpus are strictly equal in token count or merely unfiltered, and analytically attribute the exact source of the 31/35 nonzero relative-$\ell_2$ failures.
- `[MAJOR] p.4: "Table 1: Frozen S1 conformance receipt... Stack | Intended cases (14) | Native material (4 / 1) | Native not tested (1 / 4)"` - The columns in Table 1 mix units without explanation: "Intended cases" counts 14 fixture-level test cases (enumerated in Table 4), whereas "Native material" and "Native not tested" count 5 objective arms (`GRPO`, `DAPO`, `GSPO`, `DrGRPO`, `AERO`, as explained only in the prose of Appendix C on p. 11). **Concrete fix**: Either report all columns in Table 1 at the 14-case granularity (with arm-level counts in parentheses) or explicitly label the columns `Intended cases (14 fixtures)` vs. `Native arms: material (out of 5)` and `Native arms: unexposed (out of 5)`.
- `[MAJOR] p.3: "Within each reward group, GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO use sample standard deviation (Bessel correction); if it is at most 10^{-8}, A_i = 0. DrGRPO uses only the centered reward. AERO replaces the zero-variance branch with its frozen Beta-smoothed posterior scale."` - `GSPO`, `DrGRPO`, and `AERO` are introduced abruptly in Section 3.1 without citations or full names. **Concrete fix**: Define the acronyms and cite the original papers for Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), GSPO, and AERO when they are first introduced on page 3.
- `[MAJOR] p.8: "Table 3: Accepted balanced equal-length units. Accuracy is count/128; every start is 20/128... Condition | Seed | Start | Final | Joint 0 | Min cos | Max rel-l2 | Tokens"` - In Table 3, `epsilon_only` and `native_trl` on seed 23 have identical final accuracy (`17`), `Joint 0` (`65`), `Min cos` (`0.999894`), and `Max rel-l2` (`0.01458`); similarly, `intended_full` and `reduction_only` on seed 23 have identical values across every single column (`20`, `65`, `0.999881`, `0.01544`). This means `reduction_only` produced the exact same extrema as `intended_full`, and `epsilon_only` produced the exact same extrema as `native_trl`. **Concrete fix**: Explain in Section 5.3 why the reduction ablation (`reduction_only` vs. `intended_full`) yields bit-identical summary statistics on seed 23 (e.g., whether active completion lengths were identical on the extrema step, or what each condition's gradient is being compared against in the `Min cos` and `Max rel-l2` columns). Also clarify whether `Min cos` and `Max rel-l2` in Table 3 compare each condition's active gradient against `native_trl` or against `intended_full` at each step.
- `[MINOR] p.4: "and full-triage escalates all-wrong when the Wilson upper bound is at least 0.05 (dropping it otherwise), drops all-correct when the Wilson lower bound is at least 0.95, and keeps G = 8 otherwise."` - A Wilson score confidence interval requires specifying a confidence level $1 - \alpha$ (or $z$-score) and sample size $n$. With $n = G = 8$ and $0$ successes, the standard 95% ($z=1.96$) Wilson upper bound is $\frac{z^2}{n + z^2} \approx \frac{3.8416}{11.8416} \approx 0.324$, which is always $\ge 0.05$ unless $n$ is accumulated across historical rollouts ($n \ge 73$ for $z=1.96$). **Concrete fix**: Specify the exact sample count $n$ (single group $G=8$ vs. cumulative prompt history $n_g$) and the $z$-value used in the `full-triage` Wilson score bounds.
- `[MINOR] p.7: "Table 2: Revision r4-2 job disposition on 2026-07-27."` - The date `2026-07-27` and internal revision tag `r4-2` read like raw internal engineering logs. Additionally, in Table 5 (p. 11), the bottom row says `10/14/2/1/4 state counts`, swapping the order of `Failed validation (1)` and `Failed infrastructure (2)` relative to Table 2 (`10, 14, 1, 2, 4`). **Concrete fix**: Align the state count ordering between Table 2 and Table 5, and explain what `r4-2` denotes or replace internal revision tags with self-contained exposition.
- `[SUGGESTION] p.6: "Proposition 3 (Two-action batch-retry insufficiency). Suppose \Delta = B[1 - (1-q)^m] - cm > 0."` - Because the prior $n$ failures do not enter Proposition 3 at all, introducing $n$ failures in the setup only to ignore them in the minimax regret formula makes the statement look weaker than necessary. **Concrete fix**: Either incorporate a prior $\pi_0 = P(E_q)$ and show how the Bayes-optimal threshold on $n$ depends on $\pi_0$ (which connects directly to `full-triage`'s Wilson bound), or state explicitly in Proposition 3 that for *any* finite failure count $n$, the minimax regret over $\{E_0, E_q\}$ remains strictly positive and independent of $n$.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Reference target in Table 3**: In Table 3, what exact pair of gradients $(g_a, g_b)$ is compared to compute `Min cos` and `Max rel-l2` for each row? Since `intended_full` and `reduction_only` share identical `Min cos` ($0.999881$) and `Max rel-l2` ($0.01544$) on seed 23, while `epsilon_only` and `native_trl` share identical `Min cos` ($0.999894$) and `Max rel-l2` ($0.01458$), is every condition computing its diagnostic comparison between its own trajectory's intended vs. native gradient, and why did `reduction_only` match `intended_full` to 6 decimal places while `epsilon_only` matched `native_trl`?
2. **Source of the ~1.5% relative $\ell_2$ divergence in the balanced regime**: Why did 31 out of 35 nonzero steps in the balanced equal-length regime (`intended_full`, seed 23) have a relative $\ell_2$ distance greater than $0.01$ (up to $0.01544$)? In an equal-length regime where all 8 rows are active, per-completion mean and global token mean are algebraically identical, and adding $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ to $s \ge 0.3535$ changes the scale by at most $0.03\%$. What implementation difference between `intended_full` and `native_trl` caused a $1.54\%$ relative $\ell_2$ norm shift?
3. **Why was the 512-token truncation issue not fixed in a subsequent revision?**: The manuscript frames the `CV = 0` positive-control failure as a reason to halt and report a "registered feasibility outcome." In a non-clinical ML study using synthetic/benchmark rollouts on GSM8K and MATH-500, why not register a revised protocol (`r5`) with a 2048-token limit and sufficient compute quota so that the paper can answer its central scientific question?

## Overall Score and Confidence

- **Overall Score**: **3 / 10** (Clear Reject for NeurIPS/ICLR; also below the acceptance bar for TMLR until the empirical campaign is actually executed). While the fail-closed differential harness (`S1`) and the refusal to cherry-pick or hide negative/incomplete runs are admirable engineering practices, the paper's theoretical results are elementary exercises and its empirical section is an aborted 100-step feasibility run where the positive control failed due to a 512-token generation cap (`CV = 0`), the negative control is missing 6 of 12 runs due to GPU quota/infrastructure failures, and no controller intervention is ever tested.
- **Confidence**: **5 / 5** (Absolutely certain. All mathematical proofs, experimental tables, and protocol definitions were directly inspected and cross-checked against the text.)
