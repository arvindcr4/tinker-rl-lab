## Summary

This manuscript reports a held-out evaluation campaign for a single Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) post-trained language model (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with LoRA adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal`) across 14 agentic and reasoning benchmark lanes (E1–E14). The authors introduce a fail-closed, receipt-bound governance framework. This framework separates benchmark preparation, execution, and scoring. It binds every reported number to a write-once JSON receipt and verifies arithmetic with 11 deterministic ledger checks. Only four replacement or native scopes finish completely (`LAB-Bench public`, `AgentDojo benign`, `VerilogEval`, and `Omni-MATH`), and one original contract finishes with full coverage (`SWE-bench Pro` at 2/731 = 0.274%). The remaining lanes terminate as partial, blocked on cloud quota, or externally closed. No base-model comparison or baseline improvement is claimed.

## Strengths

- **Rigorous evaluation governance and transparency:** The separation of prepared, executed, and scored denominators prevents survivorship bias and silent subset reporting. Retaining all 18 non-native failures in the `SWE-bench Pro` denominator (731 tasks) and all 1,259 unparsed responses in `LAB-Bench` (1,967 items) sets a strong example for honest accounting.
- **Instructive diagnostic failure analysis:** Section 3.5 traces a 0.0 score across 100 `BankerToolBench` trials to a serving bridge defect that stripped special tokens and omitted stop sequences. Reporting this infrastructure bug instead of blaming model capability is valuable for practitioners.
- **Clear non-pooling rules:** The strict separation between original benchmark contracts and replacement scopes avoids conflating proxy metrics with gated or private benchmarks.
- **Candid Threats to Validity section:** Section 4 openly documents the purged sampler checkpoint on the Tinker platform, the accidental deletion of working-directory execution sources, and the lack of a matched base-model baseline.

## Weaknesses

- **Insufficient scientific and empirical signal for a top ML venue:** Out of 14 lanes, only four reach full completion (three of which are replacement scopes) and one original suite (`SWE-bench Pro`) completes at near-zero accuracy (0.274%). Nine lanes are incomplete, ungraded, or blocked by local engineering/operational issues such as AWS vCPU quotas (1-of-8 and 1-of-16 vCPUs), IAM bindings, or lost scripts. An audit log of an unfinished evaluation run does not constitute a sufficient machine learning contribution for NeurIPS or ICLR.
- **Complete absence of a paired base-model baseline:** Because no lane includes a matched base-model evaluation under identical serving conditions, the paper provides zero evidence about the effect of the GRPO post-training stage mentioned in the title and abstract.
- **Severe harness and extraction failures confound capability measurement:** In `LAB-Bench` (E8), the native pipeline failed to extract an answer on 1,259 of 1,967 items (64.0%). In `VerilogEval` (E11), no code could be extracted from 150 of 312 responses (48.1%). Without separating formatting/extraction failures from reasoning errors, the reported numbers mostly reflect prompt/parser mismatch.
- **Missing citations and definitions for majority of benchmark suites:** Twelve of the 20 original and replacement suites listed in Table 1 (`FrontierSWE`, `CORE-Bench`, `SDAB`, `BankerToolBench`, `APEX-Agents`, `Tau3`, `WebArena`, `BinaryAudit`, `LifeSciBench`, `MLE-bench`, `MLDevBench`, `AgentHarm`, `AppBench`, `BALROG`, `FrontierMath`) have no bibliographic entries in the References section.
- **Internal contradictions in ledger claims and terminal states:** The text and figures contradict each other on whether `VerilogEval` (E11) is an original contract or a replacement scope, and Table 1 disagrees with Figure 2 on whether lanes E1, E5, and E9 are complete, partial, or blocked.

## Anchored comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.7: "the claim that exactly E8, E10 and E11 are the strictly complete replacement scopes"` - Table 1 (p.5) and Figure 2 caption (p.6) explicitly classify E11 (`VerilogEval`) as an original contract (`O`), whereas E14 (`Omni-MATH`) is a replacement scope (`R`). Having a deterministic ledger check assert that E11 is a replacement scope indicates a bug in the verification specification or manuscript text. Fix the ledger assertion and text to classify E11 as an original contract and E8/E10 (plus E14 as terminal-complete) as replacement scopes.
- `[CRITICAL] p.8: "No lane has a matched base-model run, so no campaign figure is evidence of what the GRPO step contributed."` - Framing the paper around a "GRPO-Trained Language Model" is misleading when GRPO's effect is never measured and a small-scale paired check showed no significant difference. Either execute matched base-model runs (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at revision `995ad96e`) across the completed suites (E1, E8, E10, E11, E14) or remove "GRPO-Trained" from the title and reframe the paper strictly as an evaluation-governance case study.
- `[MAJOR] p.4: "For 1,259 of the 1,967 responses the native pipeline extracted no answer, and these are counted as incorrect."` - A 64.0% extraction failure rate on `LAB-Bench` (and 48.1% on `VerilogEval`, p.5) indicates a severe chat-template, stop-token, or output-formatting mismatch (similar to the serving defect found in E4). Report both the strict end-to-end score and the conditional accuracy over extracted answers, and categorize why extraction failed (e.g., truncation at max tokens vs. missing answer tags).
- `[MAJOR] p.5: "Table 1. All fourteen lanes. O = original contract, R = replacement scope."` - Table 1 omits the original contracts for E6, E8, E10, E13, and E14, listing only their `R` rows, while Section 3.4 names `LifeSciBench` (E8-original), `AgentHarm` (E10-original), and `FrontierMath` (E14-original), and leaves E6-original and E13-original unnamed across the entire paper. Add the missing `O` rows for E6, E8, E10, E13, and E14 to Table 1 so all 14 original contracts are explicitly identified.
- `[MAJOR] p.6: "Fig. 2. Terminal state of every lane: complete, partial... or blocked"` - Figure 2 assigns a single terminal state per lane using the replacement scope, which hides the original-contract results in Table 1 (e.g., E1 `SWE-bench Pro` has a full-suite score of 2/731, yet Fig. 2 marks E1 as `LAUNCH PEND` for `SWE-bench Multilingual`; E9 `MLE-bench` is 40/75 partial, yet Fig. 2 marks E9 as `QUOTA` for `MLDevBench`). Split each multi-scope lane in Figure 2 into `[Lane]-O` and `[Lane]-R` entries to match Table 1.
- `[MAJOR] p.6: "blocked on a 1-of-16 vCPU regional quota. MLDevBench (E9) is at 0/34... blocked on a 1-of-8 vCPU quota."` - Treating an 8-vCPU or 16-vCPU cloud quota or a local IAM binding (E2 `CORE-Bench`) as a publishable terminal result conflates routine infrastructure setup with benchmark evaluation. Provision the 8–16 vCPUs and resolve the IAM binding to complete E1-R, E2-R, E5-R, E6-R, E9-R, and E13-R before submission.
- `[MAJOR] p.8: "The VerilogEval scores were produced by sampling the adapter through the training platform’s managed sampler. That sampler checkpoint was later purged"` - Because the managed sampler checkpoint is gone and E4 demonstrated that serving bridges drastically alter results (from 0.0 to 0.41), the flagship 41.35% `VerilogEval` result cannot be reproduced. Run the published weight mirror (`commit 64444133`) through your current deterministic serving engine on `VerilogEval` (312 problems) and report the exact sampler divergence.
- `[MINOR] p.3: "Inference conditions are fixed per lane and recorded in that lane’s receipt rather than imposed globally"` - The paper only discloses inference hyperparameters for E10 (`AgentDojo`) and E11 (`VerilogEval`), leaving the temperature, top-p, max output tokens, and context lengths unknown for E1, E8, and E14. Add a table or appendix listing the exact decoding parameters and maximum token budgets for all evaluated lanes.
- `[MINOR] p.4: "For example, the AgentDojo receipt records TRAINING_INVENTORY_ABSENT"` - This status implies the training dataset inventory was unavailable during evaluation, meaning benchmark contamination between the GRPO training mix and the evaluation suites could not be ruled out. State explicitly in Section 2.1 or Section 4 whether the GRPO training corpus is known and audited for overlap with E1–E14.
- `[MINOR] p.6: "the gold-resolve timeout was 1,200 s rather than the official 120 s, because of an emulation constraint in the local execution environment."` - Running x86 Docker containers under architecture emulation (e.g., on Apple Silicon) can introduce non-deterministic timeouts and build failures beyond the 10x wall-clock slowdown. State the host CPU architecture, container runtime, and how many of the 713 native evaluations failed due to timeout vs. assertion failures.
- `[SUGGESTION] p.9: "References"` - Only 5 of the benchmark suites (`AgentDojo`, `SWE-Bench Pro`, `Omni-MATH`, `LAB-Bench`, `VerilogEval`) are cited in the bibliography. Add formal citations for `WebArena`, `CORE-Bench`, `MLE-bench`, `BALROG`, `FrontierMath`, `AgentHarm`, and all other named suites.

## Questions for the authors

1. **Unparsed responses in E8 and E11:** In `LAB-Bench` (1,259/1,967 unparsed) and `VerilogEval` (150/312 unparsed), did the model hit the `max_tokens` limit during reasoning blocks (`<think>...</think>`), or did it fail to follow the suite's answer-tag format? What is the accuracy on the subset of responses that finished generation before the token limit?
2. **Original contracts for E6 and E13:** What were the original benchmark contracts (`O`) for lane E6 (replaced by `WebArena`) and lane E13 (replaced by `BALROG`), and why were they replaced?
3. **Small-scale paired rerun details:** Section 4 mentions a separate small-scale paired rerun (5–151 items per lane, trained vs. base) that found no significant difference. Which lanes were included, what were the exact scores, and what did the GRPO training dataset consist of?
4. **Sampler divergence on E11:** Have you tested the archived HuggingFace LoRA adapter (`arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal`, commit `64444133`) on `VerilogEval` using vLLM or SGLang to quantify how much the score differs from the purged Tinker sampler?

## Overall score and confidence

- **Overall score:** 2 / 10 (Strong Reject — While the commitment to fail-closed accounting and auditability is commendable, the manuscript presents an unfinished evaluation campaign where 9 of 14 lanes are incomplete or blocked by minor cloud quotas, lacks any base-model baseline to evaluate the GRPO training claimed in the title, suffers from ~50–64% answer-extraction failures on two of its four completed suites, and contains internal inconsistencies between its ledger claims, tables, and figures.)
- **Confidence:** 5 / 5 (Absolutely certain — Every critique is grounded directly in the manuscript's reported denominators, terminal states, ledger descriptions, and bibliography.)
