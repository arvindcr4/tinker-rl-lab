### Summary

This manuscript presents a held-out evaluation campaign of a single Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) post-trained language model (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with LoRA adapter `arvindcr4/pavlov-portfolio-qwen36-seed809-stepfinal`) across 14 agentic and reasoning benchmark lanes (E1–E14). Rather than claiming state-of-the-art performance or improvements over a baseline, the authors propose and execute a "receipt-bound, fail-closed" evaluation governance regime. Under this regime, every reported figure must trace to a write-once JSON receipt, use the benchmark's native evaluator at a pinned revision, separate preparation from execution and scoring, and never pool original-contract results with replacement scopes. Out of the 14 lanes, 3 replacement/native scopes complete strictly (LAB-Bench public split: 0.2288; AgentDojo benign utility: 0.9072; VerilogEval: 41.35% pass@1), 1 replacement scope closes terminal-complete (Omni-MATH: 51.31%), and 1 original-contract suite finishes full-suite evaluation (SWE-bench Pro: 2/731 = 0.274% pass@1). The remaining lanes terminate as partial, cloud-quota blocked, or externally blocked due to private bundles. The paper also documents a diagnostic rerun of BankerToolBench (0.0 across 100 trials) that exposed a serving bridge bug (missing stop sequences and stripped special tokens), alongside post-hoc reproducibility failures (a purged platform sampler checkpoint and deleted execution sources).

---

### Strengths

- **Rigorous transparency on partial, blocked, and failed evaluations:** The paper directly confronts silent denominator shrinkage, benchmark substitution, and cherry-picking in LLM post-training evaluation. Reporting exact coverage denominators alongside operational bottlenecks (IAM bindings, regional vCPU quotas, unverified emails) is a refreshingly honest documentation of real-world evaluation friction.
- **Clear separation of original contracts and replacement scopes:** Refusing to conflate public subsets or alternative benchmarks (e.g., Omni-MATH for FrontierMath, AgentDojo benign utility for AgentHarm) with their gated counterparts sets a strong standard for benchmark hygiene.
- **Concrete failure analysis of evaluation infrastructure:** The Section 3.5 post-mortem of the BankerToolBench base-model rerun—tracing 100/100 zero rewards and `user user assistant ...` role-token degeneration to a serving bridge that omitted stop sequences—is a valuable empirical case study in how serving defects masquerade as model incapability.
- **Candid disclosure of post-hoc reproducibility breakdowns:** Section 4 openly admits that the managed sampler checkpoint used for VerilogEval was purged and that a working-directory deletion destroyed execution sources for several recovery launches.

---

### Weaknesses

- **No scientific or empirical insight into GRPO or the evaluated model:** For a top ML venue (NeurIPS/ICLR), a benchmark paper must provide either reusable infrastructure, broad comparative insights across models, or controlled scientific findings about a training method. Because this paper evaluates a single checkpoint with *no matched base-model baseline* across the 14 lanes, it provides zero signal about the effect of GRPO training. Furthermore, a side note in Section 4 admits that a small-scale paired rerun (5–151 items per lane) found *no significant difference* between the GRPO-trained model and the base model in any lane.
- **Severe incompleteness of the evaluation campaign:** Only 5 of the 14 lanes (E1-Original, E8-Replacement, E10-Replacement, E11-Original, E14-Replacement) yield full-coverage scores. Four replacement scopes (SWE-bench Multilingual, CORE-Bench, Tau3, BALROG) are left in "launch pending" states due to local scheduling or IAM bindings, and two (WebArena, MLDevBench) are blocked on modest cloud quotas (8 to 16 vCPUs). Submitting an evaluation campaign while 6 accessible replacement scopes remain unlaunched or mid-flight reads as a premature project status report rather than a finished research study.
- **High unparsed failure rates obscure model capability vs. harness formatting mismatches:** In E8 (LAB-Bench), 1,259 of 1,967 responses (64.0%) had no answer extracted by the native pipeline. In E11 (VerilogEval), 150 of 312 responses (48.1%) yielded no extractable code. In E1 (SWE-bench Pro), the model resolved only 2 of 731 tasks (0.274%). The paper does not analyze whether these massive extraction failures stem from GRPO training regressions, chat-template mismatches, or output truncation (e.g., the 1,024-token cap in VerilogEval).
- **Governance framework fails its own reproducibility goals in practice:** The paper's primary methodological pitch is "receipt-bound, fail-closed" governance that guarantees end-to-end provenance. Yet during this single campaign, the authors lost the execution source files to a working-directory deletion, lost the VerilogEval sampler path to a platform purge, had to patch a collector fault mid-run on AgentDojo (after 94/97 episodes), and missed a catastrophic stop-sequence bug in the serving bridge on BankerToolBench. The eleven deterministic ledger checks primarily verify basic arithmetic (such as `67 + 62 = 129`) and file hashes, which do not prevent substantive execution or serving bugs.
- **Missing citations and specifications for the majority of the benchmark suites:** Nine of the suites in Table 1 (SWE-bench Multilingual, FrontierSWE, CORE-Bench, SDAB, BankerToolBench, APEX-Agents, Tau3, WebArena, BinaryAudit, LifeSciBench, MLE-bench, MLDevBench, AgentHarm, AppBench, BALROG, FrontierMath) have no bibliographic citations, URLs, or commit hashes in the text or References.

Add a diagnostic breakdown classifying these extraction failures into (a) context/output length truncation (especially given the 1,024 max output tokens in VerilogEval for a reasoning model), (b) chat-template or stop-token misconfiguration similar to E4, or (c) genuine model refusal/malformed syntax.

- `[MAJOR] p.8: "A working-directory deletion removed the execution sources of several late recovery launches, while their sealed requests, validation receipts and test logs survive with matching hashes."` - A governance system that allows sealed requests and receipts to be generated from untracked working directories that can be accidentally deleted does not ensure end-to-end provenance. Add a concise paragraph in Section 2.1 summarizing the training datasets, reward domains, LoRA rank/alpha, and total training steps, and cite the companion study (even as an anonymous preprint).

### Questions for the authors

1. **Paired base-model comparison and training effect:** In Section 4, you note that a separate small-scale paired rerun (5–151 items per lane) found no significant difference between the GRPO-trained checkpoint and the base model in any lane. Did the GRPO training run actually modify the policy's output distribution meaningfully, or did "zero-variance signal starvation" result in near-zero gradient updates?
2. **Root cause of massive unparsed rates in E8 and E11:** In LAB-Bench (E8), 64.0% (1,259/1,967) of responses failed answer extraction; in VerilogEval (E11), 48.1% (150/312) failed code extraction. What fraction of these failures was caused by hitting the `max_tokens` ceiling during chain-of-thought reasoning versus formatting mismatches with the upstream parser?
3. **Completion of AgentDojo (E10) across two runs:** You state on p.5 that an earlier attempt stopped at 94/97 due to a collector fault, and a "continuation run closed the remaining three episodes." How does the write-once receipt schema handle stitching a 94-episode run and a 3-episode continuation run after a code repair while still classifying the lane as a single strictly complete receipt?
4. **Scope of lost execution sources:** Which specific lanes in Table 1 were affected by the working-directory deletion described in Section 4?
