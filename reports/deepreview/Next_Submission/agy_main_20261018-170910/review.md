### Summary

This manuscript is a pre-results skeleton and preregistered protocol for an empirical study of reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). The authors propose to evaluate a contrast-aware early-stop sampler (`contrast_early_stop_g2_to_g8`) against a fixed $G=8$ Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) baseline (`grpo_g8`) using Qwen3-8B in explicit non-thinking mode across GSM8K and MATH-500. Under the intervention, the sampler generates a two-completion probe per prompt, expands the group to eight completions only when the two deterministic verifier rewards disagree, and stops sampling otherwise without gradient updates or prompt replacement. The design specifies 23 paired seeds per task-arm cell (92 total runs), a blinded variance reassessment after 8 pairs (capped at 24 seeds), and an intersection-union decision rule with Holm multiplicity adjustment requiring at least a 20% reduction in charged generated tokens and held-out exact-match accuracy non-inferiority within a 1-percentage-point margin on both tasks simultaneously. Currently, 0 of 92 confirmatory rows are complete, and the manuscript contains no empirical results.

---

### Strengths

- **Rigorous statistical preregistration and multiplicity control**: The experimental design uses paired seeds, exact one-sided noncentral-$t$ power calculations, a blinded variance reassessment after eight pairs, an intersection-union cost-quality decision rule, and Holm adjustment across tasks (Section 4.1–4.3).
- **Clean isolation of the sampling intervention**: The protocol holds the model, prompt order, optimizer, reward parser, update count, checkpoint selection rule, and objective fixed across arms, isolating the effect of the two-sample early-stop check (Section 3.1, Section 3.4).
- **Transparent prospective amendments and fail-closed provenance**: Section 4.4 and Appendix A document three pre-execution protocol amendments (A001–A003) and bind all design artifacts, dataset revisions, and ledger requirements to SHA-256 hashes.
- **Explicit boundary of claims and limitations**: Sections 2 and 6 clearly distinguish verifier judgment from solution quality, account for probe tokens in skipped groups, and state that boundary-crossing intervals yield inconclusive verdicts rather than claims of equivalence.

---

### Weaknesses

- **No empirical results or executed confirmatory runs**: The submission is an unexecuted draft skeleton (`0/92 CONFIRMATORY ROWS COMPLETE`, p. 1–14). Standard NeurIPS/ICLR main-track submissions require completed empirical findings rather than a preregistration blueprint.
- **Ultra-short training horizon (30 optimizer updates)**: Each run trains for only 30 optimizer updates with a generation batch size of 16, totaling 480 prompts per run (Section 3.4). This short horizon may keep accuracy dynamics in a transient regime where held-out differences stay artificially small, helping the intervention pass the $-0.01$ non-inferiority margin simply because neither arm moves far from the initial checkpoint.
- **Confounding of effective gradient steps and batch statistics in TRL**: When two probe rewards agree, the intervention stops sampling and records a homogeneous group with no gradient update and no replacement prompt (Section 3.1). With a per-device train batch size of 2 and gradient accumulation of 8, skipping prompts changes the number of active sequences per optimizer update and alters denominator scaling or step semantics depending on how the TRL adapter pads or masks skipped groups, which is not specified in the text.
- **Severe false-homogeneity risk at $G=2$ across varied prompt difficulties**: A two-sample probe stops sampling with probability $p^2 + (1-p)^2$, where $p$ is the prompt pass rate. Even at maximum variance ($p=0.5$), the $G=2$ probe falsely classifies half of all mixed prompts as homogeneous, and at $p=0.2$ or $p=0.8$, it skips 68% of prompts where an 8-sample group would have had an 83% chance of producing reward contrast.
- **Wall-clock and KV-cache inefficiencies from two-stage expansion**: The primary cost metric counts charged generated tokens (Section 3.3), but sequential two-stage generation ($G=2$ followed conditionally by 6 additional completions) introduces two sequential decoding waves per batch, reducing GPU batching efficiency and increasing wall-clock latency relative to single-wave $G=8$ generation.

### Anchored comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.1: "[DRAFT SKELETON — no results exist; all outcome language is preregistered test language]"` - The manuscript contains zero executed confirmatory runs (0/92 rows complete) and leaves the abstract, Section 5, Section 7, Appendix A.3, and Table 3 as unpopulated stubs. Fix: Execute all 92 (or up to 96 post-reassessment) preregistered runs, populate Table 3 and the mechanism telemetry in Section 5 from the hash-validated ledger, and remove all `[TODO]` and `[RESULT PENDING]` placeholders before submitting to a conference venue.
- `[MAJOR] p.4: "2. If the two verifier rewards agree, the sampler stops sampling that prompt and records a homogeneous group (all-wrong or all-correct). The group receives no gradient update and no replacement prompt."` - In canonical GRPO, a zero-variance $G=8$ group yields zero advantage ($A_i = 0$) and zero policy gradient, so skipping it leaves the expected sum of gradients unchanged; however, in mini-batch implementations with per-token or per-sequence loss normalization, dropping 6 completions from skipped groups changes the token count denominator and KL/clipping statistics unless skipped groups are weighted identically to 8-completion zero-advantage groups. Fix: Explicitly write out the mathematical loss normalization equation in Section 3.2 and specify how `trl_sampler_adapter.py` normalizes the batch loss when a micro-batch contains a mix of 2-completion skipped groups and 8-completion expanded groups.
- `[MAJOR] p.4: "number of training steps (30 optimizer updates; generation batch size 16; per-device train batch size 2 with gradient accumulation 8)"` - Thirty optimizer updates process only 480 prompts per run (about 6.4% of GSM8K train and 6.4% of MATH-lighteval train). If 30 updates produce less than a 1–2 percentage-point improvement over the base Qwen3-8B checkpoint in the baseline arm, the $-0.01$ non-inferiority margin becomes trivial to satisfy even if the intervention learns nothing. Fix: Report the initial pre-training checkpoint accuracy alongside the 30-step baseline accuracy, justify why 30 steps is sufficient to induce meaningful policy improvement above the 1-percentage-point non-inferiority margin, or add a positive-control gate requiring the baseline arm to improve significantly over the initial checkpoint.
- `[MAJOR] p.6: "The capability planning standard deviation is 0.0128285396, the worst paired held-out standard deviation observed in the completed E1 audit"` - The text relies on an "E1 audit" and "prior audit seeds" (p. 5) to set the power calculation prior, yet never defines what the E1 audit was, what tasks or models it used, or why its variance applies to Qwen3-8B on GSM8K and MATH-500. Fix: Add a short paragraph or appendix table summarizing the E1 audit configuration (model, tasks, step count, seed count) so readers can assess the validity of theplanning prior.
- `[MINOR] p.6: "The two endpoints form an intersection-union decision within each task, and Holm adjustment (Holm, 1979) is applied across the two tasks."` - Requiring the joint decision to pass on both GSM8K and MATH-500 simultaneously ("Both conditions must hold on both tasks", p. 2) is itself an intersection-union test across tasks (where the composite null is that at least one task fails). Applying a Holm step-down adjustment across two tasks that must *both* reject their nulls to declare overall study success is overly conservative (testing at $\alpha/2 = 0.0125$ when an intersection-union intersection across tasks only requires per-task level $\alpha$ for family-wise error control of the joint claim, or conversely, Holm controls FWER for claiming *at least one* task succeeds, which the protocol forbids). Fix: Clarify in Section 4.2 why Holm adjustment is applied across tasks given that partial/single-task claims are explicitly prohibited, or note that $\alpha = 0.0125$ provides conservative per-task error control.
- `[MINOR] p.7: "justifies the single G=2 probe as the worst-case design point for the censoring mechanism, with G=4 named as the first follow-up ablation"` - The justification for $G=2$ is mentioned in the summary of Amendment A003, but the analytical tradeoff between probe size $k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$, expected token savings, and false-homogeneity rate $p^k + (1-p)^k$ is never presented in Section 3. Fix: Add a brief analytical derivation or plot in Section 3.1 showing the theoretical token savings and false-homogeneity rate as a function of prompt pass rate $p$ for $k=2$ versus $G=8$.
- `[SUGGESTION] p.5: "and wall-clock seconds. We examine mechanism telemetry; it is descriptive and model-based only"` - Because the intervention introduces a synchronization barrier between the 2-sample probe and the 6-sample expansion within each batch of 16 prompts, wall-clock time per step may decrease much less than charged tokens (or even increase if KV-cache prefix reuse or continuous batching is disrupted). Fix: Specify in Section 3.4 whether the 6 expansion completions share the prompt KV-cache from the 2-sample probe and report the ratio of wall-clock time savings to token savings in Section 5.

### Questions for the authors

1. **Baseline improvement over 30 updates**: In your preflight or E1 audit runs, how much held-out exact-match accuracy gain does fixed $G=8$ GRPO achieve over the untuned Qwen3-8B checkpoint across 30 optimizer updates (480 prompts)? If the baseline gain is near or below 1 percentage point, how does the design distinguish genuine non-inferiority of the sampler from a floor effect where 30 updates scarcely move the policy?
2. **Loss and gradient normalization with skipped groups**: When a prompt is stopped at $G=2$ with agreeing rewards, how does `trl_sampler_adapter.py` handle that prompt during the backward pass? Specifically, is the batch loss averaged over all 16 prompts in the generation batch (treating skipped prompts as zero-gradient entries) or only over the expanded mixed prompts (which effectively rescales the learning rate upward by $1/\text{GU}$)?
3. **Two-stage decoding implementation**: During the `contrast_early_stop_g2_to_g8` step, does the sampler run two separate `generate()` calls per batch of 16 prompts (first $16 \times 2$ sequences, then $m \times 6$ sequences for the $m$ mixed prompts)? How does this two-wave generation affect GPU utilization and wall-clock runtime when $m$ is small (e.g., $m=1$ or $2$)?
4. **Verifier false-negative interaction with $G=2$**: Since strict exact-match parsers have non-negligible false-negative rates on formatting variations (Section 2), a prompt with high true accuracy may appear to have moderate parser pass rate $p$, or vice versa. How do you measure "parser disagreement" in telemetry (Section 3.5) if only one deterministic parser is run? Is there a secondary parser run offline for telemetry?
