## Summary

This manuscript analyzes the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) in group-relative reinforcement learning (RL) with binary rewards. When all $K$ rollouts of a prompt group yield the same binary reward, the group-relative advantage collapses to zero and supplies no gradient. The paper presents three formal results to establish a measurement layer for ZVF:
1. **Theorem 1 (T1)** models the batch-level $\text{ZVF}_t$ as a sample mean of i.i.d. Bernoulli indicators $Z_g \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\theta_K)$, providing closed-form Wald and Wilson confidence intervals.
2. **Theorem 2 (T2)** treats group draws as a geometric waiting-time process and derives a reliability budget $N(\text{ZVF}) = K \ln(1/\delta)/(-\ln \text{ZVF})$ to observe at least one informative group with probability $1-\delta$, coupled with a one-sided Clopper–Pearson lower confidence bound (Corollary 1).
3. **Theorem 3 (T3)** audits a declared signal-per-rollout proxy objective $J(G) = \frac{1}{G}\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\phi}[(1 - p^G - (1-p)^G)p(1-p)]$ and proves that its discrete maximizer collapses universally to $G^\star \in \{2, 3\}$ for any non-degenerate prior $\phi$, demonstrating that this proxy cannot supply a data-adaptive group-size set-point.

Empirical evaluations on a frozen Qwen3-8B/GSM8K pool ($512 \times 32$ rollouts) and small-scale LoRA training runs test CI coverage, geometric waiting-time quantiles, matched-budget group-size trade-offs ($G=2$ vs. $G=16$), and advantage normalization (GRPO vs. Dr.GRPO).

## Strengths

- **Intellectual honesty and scope discipline:** The paper explicitly demotes overclaims that might otherwise entice reviewers. It clearly separates a nonzero reward-gradient event from monotone policy improvement (p.6), explicitly rejects a false higher-order U-statistic framing in favor of an elementary binomial proportion (p.4), and transparently reports that T3's proxy objective collapses to a trivial universal tie $G^\star \in \{2, 3\}$ (p.7).
- **Clear operational diagnostic for curriculum batching:** The empirical check in E-T1 (p.5, p.9) cleanly diagnoses why naive confidence intervals fail under difficulty-sorted batches (coverage drops to 0.07–0.22 for the global estimand) while remaining calibrated (0.944) for the local window estimand.
- **Concrete reliability floor:** Combining the exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson bound with the geometric quantile in Corollary 1 (p.6) gives practitioners a simple, distribution-free way to bound the rollout budget needed to avoid completely dead batches under i.i.d. sampling.
- **Informative negative result in T3 and E-R2b:** Showing that the natural variance-weighted signal-per-rollout proxy is prior-independent—paired with the matched-budget training demonstration that $G=2$ learns faster initially but hits an all-correct saturation wall ($\text{ZVF} \approx 0.75\text{–}1.0$, p.10)—nicely illustrates why static small group sizes fail late in training.

## Weaknesses

- **Severe manuscript preparation defects and unremoved internal scaffolding:** The PDF reads like an uncleaned internal working note rather than a camera-ready submission. It includes internal revision timestamps (`Resolution (2026-07-11)`, p.7; `Scope note (2026-07-11 canonicalization)`, p.8), repository file paths (`THEORY_NOTES.md`, `platform_hybrid/paper/paper_P7_zvf_controller`, `experiments-next/results/*.json`), and contradictory self-Referential disclaimers (`Superseded by Section 7`, `This draft contains no experiments of its own`, p.11).
- **Ten unreferenced, placeholder-Text figures and a contradictory auto-generated appendix:** Pages 11–14 contain ten figures (Figures 1–10, with Figures 7–8 duplicated verbatim as Figures 9–10) that appear to be text-box flowcharts never cited in the body, many depicting concepts absent from the paper (e.g., Figure 4: "Empirical Calibration Error Decomposition", Figure 5: "Multi-Task Pass@k Convergence Bounds"). Worse, Section 9 ("AntiVibe Senior Architectural & Pedagogical Audit (P10)", p.14) is an injected template that directly contradicts the paper's core findings (claiming dense verbosity rewards, $\tilde{A}_i = A_i/\sqrt{L_i}$ length normalization, and monotonic ZVF reduction).
- **Elementary mathematical depth:** While T1–T3 are clean and useful accounting identities, their mathematical content is elementary (the Lindeberg–Lévy CLT for Bernoulli trials, the quantile of a geometric distribution, and a basic polynomial inequality for degree $G \ge 4$). For a top ML venue, elementary theory must be paired with either a deeper objective analysis (which T3 defers to future work) or comprehensive closed-loop empirical validation (which Section 6 defers to "Pillar 7").
- **Disconnect between the T2 trigger and the Section 6 action rule:** In Section 6 (p.8), step 2 checks whether $N(\underline{\text{ZVF}}_t)$ exceeds the remaining per-step rollout budget, and step 3 states "raise $G$ when the current $G$'s budget-failure probability exceeds $\delta$." However, under a fixed total rollout budget $B = m G$, increasing $G$ decreases the number of prompts $m$, and it is not analyzed how changing $(m, G)$ jointly affects the probability of seeing at least one informative group across the batch.
- **Unformatted raw LaTeX tables and notation glitches:** The table in Section 7 (p.9) is rendered with raw `&` ampersands, and Corollary 1 (p.6) also prints raw `&` characters inside the piecewise definition.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Escalation mechanics in Section 6:** When the T2 reliability trigger fires at group size $K$ because $N(\underline{\text{ZVF}}_t)$ exceeds the step rollout budget, how does the controller select the new group size $K'$ without knowing the underlying prompt success rates $p_g$? Specifically, since $\theta_K = \mathbb{E}[p^K + (1-p)^K]$ is a moment of an unknown distribution $\phi$, how do you compute $N(\text{ZVF})$ at candidate $K' > K$?
2. **Batch size $m$ vs. group size $G$ under fixed rollout budget:** In E-R2b (p.10), you hold the batch size fixed at $m=8$ groups per step and vary the number of optimizer steps ($160$ steps at $G=2$ vs. $20$ steps at $G=16$). What happens if you hold the *per-step* rollout budget $B = m G$ fixed (e.g., $m=64, G=2$ vs. $m=8, G=16$ for the same number of optimizer steps)?
3. **Pre-filtering vs. group-size scaling:** Related work such as GRESO [8] and DAPO [5] changes the effective prompt distribution $\phi$ by filtering out dead or predicted-dead groups rather than increasing $G$. How does T2's wasted-compute bound compare quantitatively to dynamic sampling (where groups are drawn sequentially until $m$ informative groups are collected)?
