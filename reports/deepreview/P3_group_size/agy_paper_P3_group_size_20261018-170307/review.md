## Summary

This paper studies the effect of group size $G$ in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as part of the `TINKERRL-BENCH` suite. The authors evaluate $G$ through three lenses:
1. A measured small-scale sweep ($G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$, 3 seeds, 40 steps) on `Qwen2.5-0.5B` with a two-operand addition task. Here, held-out accuracy is near ceiling ($\approx 0.978\text{--}0.990$), $G=2$ retains $100.3\%$ of $G=16$ accuracy, and the advantage signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) grows sublinearly at $\approx 52\%$ of the $\sqrt{G}$ rate.
2. An illustrative, reconstructed token-budget grid ($G \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$, $T \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}\text{M}$ tokens) on `Qwen3-8B` / GSM8K derived from `FALLBACK_ROWS` in an analysis script rather than fresh matched-budget training runs. In this reconstruction, the accuracy-maximizing $G^*$ shifts from $G=8$ at $T=1\text{M}$ to $G=32$ at $T=64\text{M}$ ($0.88$ vs. $0.64$ for $G=4$).
3. A theoretical and diagnostic framing connecting the group-centered GRPO update to an endogenous all-pairs DPO contrast, alongside discussions of a "Critic-Degeneracy Hypothesis" (CDH) comparing PPO and GRPO, and small-scale robustness checks on `Qwen3.5-4B`.

While the conceptual distinction between Monte Carlo variance reduction and endogenous preference-pair density (zero-variance fraction, ZVF) is well-motivated, the manuscript is not in a publishable state for a top ML venue. It reads as an unedited compilation of automated iterative agent logs ("iter 3", "iter 7", "iter 15", "iter 23", "iter 27", "iter 136", "row 08", "row 16", "AntiVibe Senior Architectural & Pedagogical Audit"), contains duplicated placeholder diagrams (Figures 8–17), leaves sections empty, and relies on unmeasured "reconstructed" fallback tables for its main scaling claims while its actual measured group-size sweep is restricted to 40 steps on a trivial saturated arithmetic task with a 0.5B model.

---

## Strengths

- **Clear conceptual framing of ZVF vs. variance reduction:** The distinction between variance reduction of the baseline ($\propto 1/G$) and preference-pair yield on binary-reward tasks ($Y_G(p) = 1 - p^G - (1-p)^G$, Eq. 4) provides a clean intuitive vocabulary for why small $G$ fails when $p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$.
- **Explicit epistemic disclaimers on reconstructed data:** The text repeatedly warns the reader that the $G=32$ and $G=64$ numbers in the token-budget grid are joint-fit reconstructions rather than directly trained runs (p. 1, p. 2, p. 12).
- **Reporting of negative results on popular heuristics:** Section 6.10 (Table 15) transparently reports null results when re-testing difficulty filtering, length-bias normalizations, and step-1 layer freezing under cost-matched multi-seed checks.
- **Within-group negative correlation observation:** Table 9 (p. 10) usefully documents that empirical ZVF on the arithmetic sweep is lower than the i.i.d. Bernoulli prediction $p^G + (1-p)^G$, showing that within-group rollouts exhibit negative correlation residuals ($-0.073$ to $-0.232$).

## Weaknesses

- **Severe manuscript assembly and compilation defects:** The paper is stitched together from internal iteration logs and multi-pillar project notes without editorial integration. Sections 2.1 and 2.4 are completely empty headings (p. 3, p. 4). Figures 8–13 (pp. 23–25) and Figures 14–17 (pp. 26–27) are trivial 4-node flowcharts, and Figures 10–13 are duplicated verbatim as Figures 14–17. Section 9 (p. 26) is an out-of-place "AntiVibe Senior Architectural & Pedagogical Audit" containing claims (e.g., $\tilde{A}_i = A_i / \sqrt{L_i}$, dynamic scaling $G \leftarrow \min(2G, 64)$) that contradict the rest of the paper.
- **Core scaling claims rest on synthetic/reconstructed fallback tables rather than trained models:** The headline claim in the title ("Group Size in GRPO Is Budget- and Regime-Dependent") relies on Table 10 (p. 12) for the budget-dependence half. However, Table 10's caption admits these cells come from `FALLBACK_ROWS` in a script and "no measured $G \times T$ sweep over $\{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ was executed." Fitting exponential curves ($T_{\text{crit}}$, p. 13; Iter-19 fit, p. 16), bootstrap confidence intervals (Table 7, Table 12, Table 13), and Pareto frontiers on top of unmeasured fallback numbers creates an illusion of empirical precision where no experiment exists.
- **Measured group-size evidence is severely under-powered and saturated:** The primary measured group-size sweep (Table 11, p. 13) uses `Qwen2.5-0.5B` for 40 steps on two-operand addition with $n=3$ seeds, where all accuracies are between $0.978$ and $0.990$. As the authors note in Section 3.3 (p. 5), $n=5$ seeds only power a two-sample Welch test to detect huge effects ($d_{\min} = 2.024$); with $n=3$ seeds near a $99\%$ ceiling, statistical non-significance and TOST equivalence within $\epsilon=0.02$ cannot separate algorithmic properties from ceiling compression.
- **Mathematical error in the all-pairs contrast identity and gradient definitions:** In Section 6.7 (p. 18), the authors outsource their theoretical contribution to an "external cross-examination of two frontier reasoning systems (ChatGPT Pro Extended and Gemini Deep Think)." Equation (2) is algebraically true, but the subsequent "residual-isomorphism check" on p. 19 defines $V_{\text{mDPO}} = \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i<j} (r_i - r_j)(s_i - s_j)$, which differs from Equation (2) by a factor of 2 because $\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i,j}(r_i - r_j)s_i = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i<j}(r_i - r_j)(s_i - s_j)$. Furthermore, Equation (2) omits the $1/\sigma_r$ group-standard-deviation normalization defined in Figure 2 (p. 4), contradicting the actual GRPO update rule.
- **Internal contradictions across tables, figures, and sections:**
  - Section 6.1 (p. 11) states: "Table 6 reports G=4 as the highest last-10 reward (52.1%) under a fixed-step budget." Table 6 (p. 8) contains no such number or row.
  - Figure 6a (p. 15) shows the fit $|\Delta| = 0.395(1 - e^{-T/5.76\text{M}})$, whereas the main text on p. 13 reports $\hat{a} = 0.30, \hat{\tau} \approx 12.1\text{M}$ tokens for the exact same quantity.
  - Section 3.3 (p. 6) retains Table 5 ("Legacy 20-test BH table") while admitting in the caption that it "silently includes single-seed Tinker rows whose Welch statistics are mathematically undefined ($n_1=n_2=1$)" and reports BH-adjusted $p$-values of `0`.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Provenance of `FALLBACK_ROWS` (Table 10, p. 12):** Exactly how were the 20 accuracy numbers in `group_size_token_normalized.tsv` generated? Were they interpolated from a parametric formula, or extracted from partial training runs, and if the latter, which exact runs, models, and step counts produced them?
2. **$G=128$ in Section 6.8 vs. $G \le 16$ in Sections 1–6:** The Abstract and Introduction state that measured group sizes only extend to $G=16$. Yet Section 6.8 (p. 20) reports a 5-seed experiment on `Qwen2.5-0.5B` / arithmetic with $G=128$. Why was $G=128$ excluded from the measured group-size sweep in Section 5 / Table 11, and what were its held-out accuracy, ZVF, and SNR?
3. **Matched-budget trajectory check (p. 10):** On p. 10, you describe a 2-seed matched-budget run on `Qwen3-8B` / GSM8K (2,560 rollouts: $G=2 \times 160$ steps vs. $G=16 \times 20$ steps; `tinker-runs/results/er2b_*.json`). What were the final **held-out GSM8K test accuracies** of the $G=2$ and $G=16$ arms at the end of those 2,560 rollouts?
4. **Definition of $\text{SNR}(\text{adv})$ in Table 14 (p. 16):** Figure 7B labels the y-axis `SNR (mean|adv| / std|adv|)`, whereas Table 14's caption says `SNR is on the per-step advantage-variance trajectory`. For standardized within-group advantages $\hat{A}_i = (r_i - \bar{r})/\sigma_r$, the within-group variance is identically $1$ whenever $\sigma_r > 0$ and $0$ otherwise. How is `SNR(adv)` mathematically defined and computed across steps?
