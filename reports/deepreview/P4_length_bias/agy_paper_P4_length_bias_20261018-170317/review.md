## Summary

This manuscript investigates length bias and held-out generalization in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and its length-debiased variant, Dr. GRPO, as "Pillar 4" of the broader `TINKERRL-BENCH` benchmark suite. The authors evaluate short-horizon reinforcement learning runs (primarily 30–40 steps) across arithmetic (Qwen2.5-0.5B, $n=5$ seeds) and GSM8K chain-of-thought (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, $n=3$ seeds, 200-token cap; plus a Qwen3-8B-Instruct evaluation at 200 and 1,024 tokens). The core empirical finding is a bounded null result: within this short-horizon, generation-capped regime, GRPO and Dr. GRPO yield statistically indistinguishable held-out accuracy gains, neither algorithm inflates completion length (mean length drifts downward from 194.4 to 183.5 tokens), and the "verbosity-trap" signature (rising length coupled with stagnating or falling reward) is absent. The second half of the paper appends a sprawling sequence of 16 iterative post-hoc time-series analyses (`iter 12` through `iter 136`) on the same 16 step-level logs, a brief 2-seed tool-use diagnostic (`BFCLv4`), external theoretical proposals generated by frontier LLMs ("ChatGPT Pro Extended" and "Gemini Deep Think"), 10 placeholder diagram figures, and an "AntiVibe Senior Architectural & Pedagogical Audit" section.

While transparency around negative and null results in RL post-training is valuable, this manuscript is not ready for publication at a top ML venue. It reads as an unedited concatenation of automated research-agent iteration logs rather than a coherent scientific paper. More critically, the text contains severe mathematical contradictions regarding what algorithm was actually implemented as "Dr. GRPO," rampant multiple-hypothesis testing across dozens of post-hoc metrics on a tiny sample ($n=3$ and $n=5$ seeds), unreferenced boilerplate sections, broken LaTeX tables, and placeholder figures.

---

## Strengths

1. **Honest scoping of a negative result:** The abstract, introduction, and Section 4 clearly state that the 200-token generation cap and 30–40 step training horizon bound the null finding. The authors explicitly caution against interpreting this capped, short-horizon equivalence as a refutation of Dr. GRPO in uncapped, long-horizon regimes.
2. **Explicit statistical power accounting in Section 3.3:** The manuscript calculates minimum detectable effect sizes (MDE $d_{\min} = 2.024$ for $n_1 = n_2 = 5$ at 80% power) and openly acknowledges that single-seed API runs ($n=1$) have zero statistical power and must be treated as descriptive.
3. **Scale and cap sanity check:** The authors include a 6-arm Qwen3-8B panel (Section 5, p. 9) raising the completion cap $5\times$ to 1,024 tokens, verifying that early-step length compression (3.8–12.2% decline) still occurs when the cap is raised, even though step-0 completions begin near the raised cap ($\approx 98\%$).
4. **Actionable future experimental designs (Section 5.12):** The formalization of the Causal Length-Mediation Protocol (CLMP), the four-stratum memorization-controlled generalization ladder ($S_0$–$S_3$), and the length-adversarial truncation test provide concrete protocols for testing length bias when compute permits longer horizons.

## Weaknesses

1. **Fundamental contradiction in the mathematical definition of Dr. GRPO:** The paper gives four mutually incompatible definitions of Dr. GRPO across different sections:
   - Pages 1 and 42 state that Dr. GRPO removes the per-response $1/L_i$ length normalization factor.
   - Page 14 (`iter 20`) states that Dr. GRPO *applies* per-token advantage normalization $A_i \to A_i / |o_i|$ (which is standard GRPO's length normalization, the exact opposite of Dr. GRPO).
   - Page 26 (`iter 52`) states that Dr. GRPO drops the standard deviation normalizer $\sigma(R_x)$ *and* lacks length normalization.
   - Pages 30, 31, 32, and 35 (`iter 60`, `64`, `68`, `76`) define the Dr. GRPO estimator as clipped advantage $\hat{A}_i = \text{clip}((r_i - \mu_g)/\sigma_g, -c, c)$ and attribute all empirical differences to "outlier-censoring" and "clipping large advantage magnitudes."
   - Page 47 (Section 8.2) proposes $\tilde{A}_i = A_i / \sqrt{L_i}$.
   This invalidates the mechanistic interpretations in Sections 5.1–5.10, as the reader cannot determine what loss function was actually executed in the code.
2. **Severe $p$-hacking and post-hoc data dredging on 16 tiny runs:** In Section 3.3 (p. 5), the authors claim to control for multiple comparisons across "5 planned tests." However, Sections 5.1–5.10 run an automated discovery loop (`iter 12`, `16`, `20`, `24`, `28`, `32`, `36`, `40`, `44`, `48`, `52`, `56`, `60`, `64`, `68`, `72`, `76`, `80`, `120`, `124`, `128`, `132`, `136`) computing dozens of distinct regression slopes, lag correlations, OU unit-root tests, quantile regressions, phase-plane loop areas, and damping ratios on the *exact same* 16 short trajectories (10 arithmetic runs of 40 steps, 6 GSM8K runs of 30 steps). None of these dozens of iterative tests are corrected for multiple comparisons.
3. **Invalid statistical tests on $n=3$ and $n=5$ samples:** Throughout Sections 5.1–5.10, the authors report non-parametric paired bootstrap 95% confidence intervals ($B=2000$ or $4000$) and paired Wilcoxon tests on sample sizes of $n=3$ seeds (GSM8K-CoT) and $n=5$ seeds (arithmetic). For $n=3$ paired observations, a non-parametric bootstrap over seeds has only $3^3 = 27$ unique resamples and can never exclude zero if even one seed has an opposite sign, yet the authors report $p < 0.001$ and $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$ from 3 seeds (e.g., pp. 22, 23, 28, 29, 30, 32, 33, 36). Similarly, a one-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on $n=3$ has a minimum possible $p$-value of $(1/2)^3 = 0.125$, making claims of significant per-task Wilcoxon discovery at $n=3$ impossible.
4. **Internal contradictions across iterations:** Because Sections 5.1–5.10 are raw logs from sequential iterations (`iter 12` to `iter 136`), later subsections routinely contradict earlier subsections without reconciliation:
   - Section 4 (p. 7) states GRPO and Dr. GRPO produce held-out gains that are indistinguishable and neither inflates length; Section 5.1(F) (p. 13) says Dr. GRPO is a "measurable worsening on hard GSM8K-CoT"; Section 5.6.4 (p. 28) says "Dr. GRPO pays 46% more waste"; Section 5.7 (p. 37) reverses course and proclaims "Dr. GRPO Pareto-dominates GRPO" on the length-efficiency frontier.
   - Section 5 (p. 7) claims ten GRPO runs and eight Dr. GRPO runs (18 total), whereas page 8 and all subsequent tables state 16 runs total (5+5 arithmetic, 3+3 GSM8K-CoT).
   - Table 11 (p. 13) reports arithmetic GRPO $\rho(L,R) = -0.396$, whereas Table 6 (p. 8) and Table 14 (p. 17) report $-0.419$ on the same data.
   - Section 5.6.5 (p. 29) fits a parabola $R(L) = a(L - L^\star)^2 + b$ to GSM8K trajectories where $L \in [170, 196]$ and extrapolates an optimum $L^\star_{\text{Dr.GRPO}} = 256.2$ tokens—56 tokens *above* the hard 200-token generation cap!
5. **Unedited repository artifacts, broken cross-references, and placeholder figures:** The manuscript contains literal placeholders (`§5.X` three times on pp. 9–10), truncated table columns (Table 14 on p. 17 cuts off `Dr.GRPO sig. (` and `p=`), clipped figure titles (Figure 12 on p. 22 cuts off `hmetic` and drops $\lambda$ symbols; Figure 15 on p. 27 cuts off `sing phase`), 10 consecutive generic 3-box TikZ flowcharts on pages 44–45 (Figures 17–26) that are never cited in the text, and a bizarre Section 8 ("AntiVibe Senior Architectural & Pedagogical Audit") that describes features ("dynamic group scaling $G \leftarrow \min(2G, 64)$", $\tilde{A}_i = A_i / \sqrt{L_i}$) never used in the paper.
6. **Disconnect between Benchmark Setup (Sections 2–3) and Results (Sections 4–5):** Sections 2 and 3 describe a massive 70+ run, 7-library (SB3, CleanRL, Tianshou, PufferLib, rl_games, d3rlpy, TRL), 32-model (up to 671B DeepSeek-V3.1) benchmark, complete with a "Legacy 20-test BH table – audit trail only" (Table 5). Yet Sections 4 and 5 evaluate *only* Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and Qwen3-8B, using none of the classic RL libraries in Table 2 or frontier models in Section 3.1.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **What exact loss function was run for Dr. GRPO?** Please provide the exact mathematical formula implemented in code for both GRPO and Dr. GRPO in `drgrpo_vs_grpo.json` and `drgrpo_gsm8k_cot_full.json`. Specifically:
   - Did GRPO divide each sequence's log-probability sum by sequence length $|o_i|$, and did Dr. GRPO replace $|o_i|$ with a constant $L_{\text{max}}$?
   - Did Dr. GRPO remove the group standard deviation $\sigma_g$ from the advantage denominator?
   - Why do Sections 5.6.5–5.6.9 state that Dr. GRPO is defined by advantage clipping $\hat{A}_i = \text{clip}((r_i - \mu_g)/\sigma_g, -c, c)$?
2. **What are the exact pre-RL and post-RL held-out accuracies for the headline experiments?** Section 4 mentions Qwen3-8B-Instruct gains $+0.013$ ($p = 0.256$, $n=5$) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct gains $+6.2\text{pp}$ (GRPO) and $+5.0\text{pp}$ (Dr. GRPO) on GSM8K held-out evaluation. What were the baseline pre-RL accuracies, post-RL accuracies, and evaluation completion caps for each of these models? Why was McNemar's test (which pools items across a single run) used for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct instead of a paired test across the $n=3$ seeds?
3. **How were $p < 0.001$ values obtained from $n=3$ paired seeds?** In Sections 5.6, 5.6.4, 5.6.5, 5.6.6, 5.6.7, 5.6.8, and 5.6.10, paired bootstrap tests over $n=3$ GSM8K seeds report $p(\Delta \le 0) = 0.000$ or $p < 0.001$. When all 3 seed pairs have the same sign, a paired bootstrap simply resamples from 3 positive numbers, yielding a degenerate empirical distribution that is $100\%$ positive ($p=0$), whereas the true exact sign-test $p$-value for 3 out of 3 positive pairs is $(1/2)^3 = 0.125$ (one-sided) or $0.25$ (two-sided). Will the authors replace these small-$n$ bootstrap $p$-values with exact permutation/sign-test bounds?
