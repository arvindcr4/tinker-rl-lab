### Summary

This position paper argues that reinforcement learning for large language models (RL-for-LLM) post-training results are heavily conditioned on the underlying software and evaluation stack rather than the named algorithm label (e.g., GRPO, DAPO, Dr.GRPO, GSPO). The authors frame this as the "sandwich problem": the algorithm label is a thin layer between the training/serving substrate below and the reward/evaluation apparatus above. 

To support this thesis, the manuscript presents:
1. Empirical audits ("Exhibits") showing that a 17× reward gap (5.0% on TRL vs. 85.6% on Tinker) in a nominal "GRPO on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K" backend comparison is actually driven by an undisclosed base-vs-instruct checkpoint switch (accounting for 64% of the gap) and multiple harness confounds.
2. Evidence that a closed-stack "dapo" run was actually an advantage-clipping variant without dynamic sampling, and that in a 12-cell head-to-head comparison of four labels on a fixed stack, seed variance accounts for 96% of the sum of squares while the 3.4 percentage-point algorithm spread is underpowered at 3 seeds.
3. Variance decompositions ($\eta^2$) across a 98-cell factorial corpus (`mega_20260704`) and a 4-method same-stack corpus (`N2`), demonstrating that stack axes (`model_family`, `task_slice`, group size $G$) explain 73%–93% of telemetry variance while the algorithm axis explains $\le 6.3\%$ (or $\le 14.8\%$ at the 95% bootstrap upper bound).
4. The **MIN-REPORT-RL** standard: an eight-item minimum reportable stack checklist (loss form, reference policy/KL handling, sampler/backend/precision + base checkpoint, per-step Zero-Variance Fraction [ZVF] trajectory, group-size schedule, disjoint held-out split, decontamination + parser probe, and held-out pass@$k$ curves), accompanied by an auditor badge specification and reproducibility threat model.

---

### Strengths

- **Timely and Important Central Thesis:** The core argument—that RL-for-LLM evaluations suffer from severe under-specification where base checkpoint revisions, dynamic sampling toggles, group sizes ($G$), and answer-parser behavior dominate the nominal loss label—is highly relevant to the post-training community.
- **Radical Transparency in Self-Auditing:** The manuscript is unusually candid about its own experimental flaws. For example, in Exhibit 1 (p.8), the authors explicitly state that their own repository's "matched-configuration" YAML was written post-hoc with placeholder hashes and described neither run, and in Exhibit 2 (p.8), they admit their closed-stack "dapo" arm did not actually implement DAPO's dynamic sampling. This serves as a compelling cautionary tale.
- **Concrete, Actionable Reporting Checklist (Section 6):** Each of the eight items in the proposed MIN-REPORT-RL standard (Table 25, p.30) is justified by a specific failure mode ("documented lever"), such as ZVF aliasing both mastery ($p \to 1$) and incapacity ($p \to 0$) via $E[\text{ZVF}] = p^G + (1-p)^G$, or pass@1 conflating distribution sharpening with frontier expansion.
- **Multi-Angle Statistical Scrutiny of Manifests:** The use of predictive-sufficiency random forests (Exhibit 13, p.16) and Shannon discriminative entropy (Exhibit 16, p.19) to show that a manifest schema can have 100% key presence while remaining informatively vacuous ($H = 0$ bits on constant strings) is a sharp methodological insight for reproducibility checklists.

### Weaknesses

- **Severe Manuscript Corruption / Unedited Automated Research Log Dump:** The paper is not in a publishable state. Beyond page 10, the manuscript degenerates into a raw, unedited concatenation of internal automated agent iteration logs (`iter-13`, `iter-32`, `iter-50`, `iter-65`, `iter-85`, `iter-137`, `iter-197`, etc.). It is filled with internal repository file paths (`scripts/p5p8/...`), internal project shorthand (`P5`, `P6`, `P7`, `P8`, `F3`, `Wave-6`, `Task-4`), self-referential "Retraction notices" and "Correction notes" dated in the future (August 2026, October 2026), and verbatim prompt instructions ("The brief's vein (b) asks...", p.38).
- **Direct Internal Contradictions Left Unresolved in the Text:** Because the manuscript appends successive agent iterations without editing earlier sections, later sections actively refute earlier sections in the same paper:
  - Section 7.1 (p.35) opens with a "Retraction notice (2026-10-16)" stating that the anti-herding quantity $\delta_{\text{div}}$ is an estimator artifact and that results ranking or sizing $G$ on $\delta_{\text{div}}$ are "withdrawn", yet Sections 7.1.1–7.1.6 immediately proceed to recommend adopting $\delta_{\text{div}}$ (Item 13) as "SIGNAL-BEARING".
  - Section 7.2.4 (p.37) claims Item 16 (`max K-share`) strengthens coupling, Section 7.2.5 rejects Item 16 as a placebo, and Section 8 (p.53) claims Item 16 is one of the "most portable" signal items.
  - Section 5.5 (p.9) and Section 7.3 (p.38) claim the algorithm axis explains $\le 6.3\%$ of variance, while Section 7.7 (p.43) finds $\eta^2(\text{algo}) = 0.670$ on the exact same 4-method N2 subset when using the `zvf130` risk index.
- **Unacceptable Length and Truncation:** Even truncated at page 53 (with 113 KB of text remaining), the manuscript is roughly 5–6× the standard 9-page NeurIPS/ICLR main-text limit. Furthermore, the text cuts off mid-sentence on page 53, omitting Sections 9 through 15 (including the promised threat model in Section 10, enforcement toolchain in Section 11, call to action in Section 15, and the entire Bibliography).
- **Conflated and Incoherent Benchmark Design (Sections 2–3):** Sections 2 and 3 appear to be pasted from a different general RL benchmark paper (`TINKERRL-BENCH`). Table 2 (p.3) and Table 3 (p.4) map PPO hyperparameters across classic non-LLM RL libraries (`Stable Baselines3`, `CleanRL`, `Tianshou`, `PufferLib`, `rl_games`, `d3rlpy` CQL) on two-digit synthetic arithmetic (random baseline $1/199 \approx 0.50\%$, p.4). Classic PPO/CQL libraries on synthetic arithmetic have almost nothing to do with LLM post-training stacks (vLLM, SGLang, TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF) or the GRPO/DAPO sandwich problem introduced in Section 1.
- **Flawed Statistical Sums and Overstated Ratios:** Across multiple tables (Table 7, p.9; Table 9, p.10; Table 23, p.27), the authors compute separate univariate one-way ANOVAs on an unbalanced partial grid (98 of 120 cells) and then sum the marginal $\eta^2$ values across non-orthogonal factors to claim "stack $\eta^2$ sum = 0.927". In an unbalanced design, marginal sums of squares are not orthogonal and cannot be summed directly to represent joint variance explained. Furthermore, reporting "stack-vs-seed $\eta^2$ ratios" of $96,128\times$ (p.11, p.41) by dividing by a near-zero sample noise estimate ($\eta^2_{\text{seed}} \approx 0.00001$) is statistically meaningless ratio inflation.

### Questions for the Authors

1. **Reconciling Algorithm Variance Across Metrics (Section 7.3 vs. Section 7.7):** On the 4-method `N2` panel (`grpo`, `aero`, `areal`, `gift`), pooled per-step ZVF yields $\eta^2_{\text{algo}} = 0.0454$ (Table 31, p.39), which you use to argue that algorithm labels are nearly indistinguishable on a fixed stack. However, in Section 7.7 (p.43), evaluating the exact same 4 methods on the `zvf130` risk-index yields $\eta^2_{\text{algo}} = 0.6698$. How is `zvf130` defined, and doesn't a 67% algorithm-axis variance share on a fixed stack substantially qualify your headline claim that the algorithm axis is negligible compared to the stack?
2. **Table 7 $\eta^2(G) = 1.0000$ Calculation:** In Table 7 (p.9), how was $\eta^2(G) = 1.0000$ computed for both `ZVF` and `reward_mean` across the 3-seed Tinker sweep? Were the 3 seeds averaged prior to computing $SS_{\text{total}}$, thereby eliminating within-group variance by construction?
3. **Status of Retracted Item 13 ($\delta_{\text{div}}$):** Why does Section 7.1 (p.35) include both a retraction notice stating that $\delta_{\text{div}}$ is an estimator artifact of Jensen's inequality and a full section arguing that Item 13 is "SIGNAL-BEARING" and should be adopted into MIN-REPORT v2.1? What is the intended scientific takeaway regarding $\delta_{\text{div}}$ vs. prompt-difficulty dispersion $\text{Var}(K/G)$ (Item 17)?
