### Summary

This manuscript investigates the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)—the proportion of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) completion groups where all sampled responses receive identical binary rewards—and explores whether ZVF and related metrics can serve as feedback signals to dynamically control rollout group size ($G$). The authors formulate a binomial model of group degeneracy ($h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$) and usable learning signal ($S(p,G) = p(1-p)(1-h_G(p))$), stating three theoretical propositions (T1–T3). They pair these with a 368-run Weights & Biases retrospective audit, a confound regression demonstrating that the cross-model U-shape between ZVF and reward is largely driven by model identity and configuration ($R^2 = 0.90$) rather than reward ($+0.012$), and a micro-jitter falsification motivating Pairwise-Contrast Density ($\text{PCD} = \frac{G-1}{G}\mathbb{E}[p(1-p)]$) over raw ZVF. Finally, the paper evaluates an `adaptive-G` controller in a small open-trainer run (E3) and conducts an extensive series of post-hoc counterfactual replays across logged reward tensors (the N2 four-method corpus and N10 five-seed panel) comparing heuristic, Bayesian, Dualformer-inspired, iso-yield, and hysteresis-filtered group-size allocation policies.

While the core mechanistic question—how to allocate rollout budgets dynamically across prompts of varying difficulty in verifiable-reward RL—is timely and practical, the manuscript in its current state is not a coherent scientific paper. Instead, it reads as an unedited concatenation of automated research logs, legacy boilerplate from companion benchmark papers, in-line retractions of its own sections, and contradictory statistical analyses. It requires a complete ground-up rewrite before it can be considered for publication at a top ML venue.

---

### Strengths

1. **Clear Mathematical Intuition for Degeneracy and Jitter Sensitivity:** The elementary binomial formulation of group degeneracy (Section 5) and the distinction between existence of contrast (ZVF) and magnitude of contrast (PCD, Section 6.2) are clean and well-motivated. The micro-jitter falsification (Section 6.3)—showing that adding $\mathcal{U}(0, 10^{-4})$ noise collapses measured ZVF from $0.158$ to $0.000$ while leaving PCD invariant—is a sharp, practical demonstration of why exact-tie indicators fail under minor reward shaping (e.g., length or format penalties).
2. **Commendable Scientific Honesty and Self-Falsification:** Unlike many papers that oversell heuristic controllers, the authors rigorously audit and falsify their own preliminary hypotheses. Notably:
   - Section 6.1 uses a confound regression to show that the visually appealing population "U-shape" of ZVF vs. reward across 368 runs is an artifact of model and configuration differences ($R^2 = 0.90$) rather than an intrinsic reward effect ($\Delta R^2 = 0.012$).
   - Section 7 explicitly demotes an earlier gradient-correlation claim ($\text{corr} = +0.71$) to an unverified anecdote due to missing raw tensors, serial correlation, and mechanical tautology.
   - Section 8.3 candidly reports that on the N2 corpus, step-level `zvf-triage` escalation saves *zero* prompts because all degenerate groups at $G=8$ sit at empirical $\hat{p} \in \{0, 1\}$, where $h_G(\hat{p}) = 1$ for all $G$.
3. **Relevant Problem Domain:** Understanding compute-vs-signal trade-offs in GRPO, Dr. GRPO, and DAPO-style dynamic sampling is highly relevant for post-training reasoning models.

### Weaknesses

1. **Severe Structural Incoherence ("Iterative Log Dump"):** Starting in Section 8.5 and continuing through Section 11 (pages 15–52), the manuscript disintegrates into a chronological dump of internal iteration logs (`iter 11`, `iter 26`, `iter 31`, `iter 47`, `iter 51`, `iter 67`, `iter 71`, `iter 72`, `iter 79`, `iter 83`, `iter 87`, `iter 88`, `iter 91`, `iter 92`, `iter 103`, `iter 107`, `iter 111`, `iter 115`, `iter 119`). These subsections repeatedly re-analyze the exact same two small offline datasets (N2: 4 methods $\times$ 40 steps $\times$ 16 prompts; N10: 5 seeds $\times$ 15 steps), defining new notation, reversing previous "Pareto winners," and referencing internal issue-tracker rows (`Berkeley row 01`, `Berkeley row 19`, `P5P8_IMPROVEMENTS.md row 77`, `P5P8-SYNTH JOB B`) and agent prompts (`The frontier synthesis (Round 2, Gemini Deep Think) proposed...`).
2. **Self-Contradictory Text and Unremoved Retractions:** Rather than editing out flawed analyses prior to submission, the authors leave retracted text in the manuscript and prepend retraction notices. Page 8 states that every result using the "anti-herding" quantity $\delta_{\text{div}}$ is withdrawn, and Section 8.20 (p. 34) and Section 8.22 (p. 38) begin with formal `Retraction notice (2026-10-16)` blocks explaining that $\delta_{\text{div}}$ is a mathematical estimator artifact. Yet Sections 8.20, 8.21, 8.21.1, and 8.22 still spend seven pages presenting tables, bootstrap CIs, and "Headline" claims promoting $\delta_{\text{div}}$-triage and joint controllers built upon it.
3. **Copy-Pasted Irrelevant Boilerplate in Sections 2 and 3:** Sections 2 and 3 (pages 3–8) appear copy-pasted wholesale from a general multi-library RL benchmark paper (`TINKERRL-BENCH`) and have almost nothing to do with the paper's actual ZVF/controller content:
   - Sections 2.2–2.3 and Tables 2–3 detail PPO and CQL hyperparameter mappings across Stable Baselines3, CleanRL, Tianshou, PufferLib, `rl_games`, and `d3rlpy`—none of which are used anywhere in the ZVF controller audit.
   - Section 3.3 and Table 5 present a "Legacy 20-test BH table – audit trail only" containing undefined $n_1=n_2=1$ Welch $t$-tests and ANOVAs from a different paper, accompanied by a disclaimer that the table is invalid and superseded by an external addendum.
   - Sections 2.1 and 2.4 are completely empty headings.
4. **Statistical Methodological Flaws in Counterfactual Replays:**
   - **Plug-in Binomial Circularity:** In Sections 8.3, 8.7, 8.16, 8.17, 8.19, 8.23, and 9, the authors evaluate counterfactual policies at $G' \neq 8$ by computing the empirical success rate $\hat{p} = k/8$ from a single group of 8 rollouts and plugging $\hat{p}$ into the binomial formula $\hat{p}^{G'} + (1-\hat{p})^{G'}$. When $k \in \{0, 8\}$, $\hat{p} \in \{0, 1\}$, which mechanically forces $\hat{p}^{G'} + (1-\hat{p})^{G'} = 1$ for all $G'$, and forces any bootstrap resampling of those 8 identical binary values to size 16 (Section 8.19, p. 33) to yield 0% contrast restoration by construction. Conversely, in Section 8.11 (p. 22), switching to a $\text{Beta}(1,1)$ prior suddenly assigns $k \in \{0, 8\}$ a restoration probability of $7/25 = 0.28$, completely reversing the Pareto ranking. Both conclusions are purely mechanical consequences of the chosen plug-in vs. prior smoothing assumption, not empirical discoveries about the underlying LLM prompts.
   - **Pseudoreplication in Bootstrap CIs:** In Section 6.1 (p. 10), the authors rightly note that step-level observations within a training run are serially correlated and require run-clustered standard errors, and in Section 7 (p. 12) they reject a 30-step correlation because steps within a single run are serially dependent. However, throughout Sections 8.6–11, they compute 95% bootstrap CIs by resampling the $n=40$ steps (or $n=640$ prompt-steps) from a *single seed* ($s=0$ of N2) as if they were i.i.d., or by resampling $n=4$ methods played on the same single seed (Table 46).
5. **Lack of Prospective Validation:** As the authors acknowledge in the abstract and venue claim boundary, there is no prospective, matched-budget, multi-seed evaluation showing that any of the ~15 proposed controller variants improves training efficiency or final accuracy over static $G$ or DAPO. The only live controller run (E3, Table 9) is a single-task, small-$n$ experiment where `grpo_adaptiveG` uses 55% more rollouts (186 vs. 120) to tie `drgrpo` (+0.575 held-out gain at 120 rollouts).
6. **Unfinished Draft Artifacts:** The manuscript contains literal placeholders (`[Placeholder: pre-registered held-out test...]`, p. 11), broken character encodings (`˘g4.5` on p. 25, `˘g4.4–˘g4.13` on p. 30, `˘g4.16` on p. 51, `5 seeds Œ 15 steps` on p. 44, `]` above `ZVF` on pp. 47–49), and internal file paths/LoC counts in every subsection.

### Questions for the Authors

1. **Table 19 Arithmetic Discrepancy:** In `grpo` on the N2 corpus, $461/640 = 72.0\%$ of prompt-steps are degenerate ($k \in \{0, 8\}$), for which Eq. (9) gives $\Pr(\text{restore at } G'=16) = 7/25 = 0.280$. Even if all $179$ non-degenerate prompt-steps had $\Pr(\text{restore}) = 1.0$, the mean across all 640 prompt-steps could be at most $(461 \times 0.28 + 179 \times 1.0)/640 = 0.481$. How did Table 19 arrive at a mean restoration probability of $0.7230$ (and claim an "~82% non-degenerate majority")?
2. **Details of the E3 Open-Trainer Audit (Section 8.2):** What base model, dataset, prompt count, step count, and number of seeds were used in Table 9? How does `grpo_adaptiveG` (186 rollouts) compare against a static-$G$ GRPO or Dr. GRPO baseline given the *same* 186-rollout budget?
3. **Provenance of the N2 Four-Method Tensors:** What base model and task generated the N2 four-method (`grpo`, `aero`, `gift`, `areal`) 40-step reward tensors? Why are 72.9% of prompt-steps already at $k \in \{0, 8\}$ from the beginning of training?
