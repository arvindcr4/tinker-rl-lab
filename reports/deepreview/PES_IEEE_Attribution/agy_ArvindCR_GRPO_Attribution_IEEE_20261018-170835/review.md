## Summary

This manuscript presents an empirical measurement study of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) post-training for large language models with verifiable rewards. The authors make three primary claims:
1. **Signal Starvation via Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF):** When all $G$ completions in a group achieve identical binary rewards, the within-group variance $\sigma_g^2$ is zero, producing zero advantages and zero reward-driven gradient. On their main configuration, $\text{ZVF} \approx 0.72\text{--}0.77$, driven largely by already-solved prompts. The authors propose an analytical signal model $S(p, G) = p(1-p)(1 - p^G - (1-p)^G)$ and test an adaptive group-size controller.
2. **Stack Sensitivity vs. Algorithm Choice:** Across a 42-experiment corpus, separate one-way ANOVAs yield marginal $\eta^2 = 0.546$ for framework and $\eta^2 = 0.558$ for algorithm. Under a fixed stack, contrasts between PPO and GRPO ($\Delta = -0.002, p = 0.374$) and between GRPO and four variants (DAPO, GSPO, Dr. GRPO, AERO) are statistically inconclusive, whereas nominally matched runs across different back-ends diverge widely (5.0% to 85.6% training reward).
3. **Fourteen-Suite Held-Out Campaign (E1–E14):** The authors evaluate a single actor (`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with a Tinker-trained GRPO LoRA adapter) across 14 benchmarks under receipt-bound governance, reporting graded results on 5 suites and explicit terminal states (launch pending, quota-blocked, or externally closed) on the remaining 9 lanes.

---

## Strengths

- **Rigorous Attribution Discipline:** Holding the parser, decoding parameters, verifier, and evaluation harness fixed while varying a single factor isolates implementation confounders that routinely corrupt RLVR comparisons.
- **Simple, Recomputable Telemetry:** Defining ZVF and Pairwise Contrast Density (PCD) over persisted raw reward tensors makes gradient starvation auditable after training. The analytical form $S(p,G) = p(1-p)(1 - h_G(p))$ cleanly explains the empirical U-shaped relationship between accuracy $p$ and ZVF.
- **Valuable Negative Results on Adaptive Group Sizing:** Reporting that a symmetric ZVF controller wastes 92.3% of its group-size escalations (1,723/1,867) on all-correct prompts is an actionable finding for practitioners designing dynamic sampling rules.
- **Concrete Serving and Implementation Forensics:** Identifying that two runs both labelled "DAPO" had mean ZVF of 0.00 and 0.58 due to silent differences in dynamic sampling, and tracing a 0.0 score on BankerToolBench to missing stop sequences in the serving bridge, provides strong empirical evidence of stack sensitivity.
- **Intellectual Honesty:** The authors clearly distinguish inconclusive $p$-values from proof of equivalence, acknowledge collinearity in their marginal $\eta^2$ values, and state every threat to validity upfront.

---

## Weaknesses

- **Severe Under-Specification of Experimental Protocols:** Core experiments lack basic specification in the text. The "main configuration" yielding $\text{ZVF} \approx 0.72\text{--}0.77$ (Section IV-A), the "368-run audit across seven models" (Section IV-B), the "505-task conditional utility audit" (Section IV-B), and the "42 experiments" in the ANOVA corpus (Section IV-D) are presented as headline numbers without stating the datasets, base models, hyperparameters, training step budgets, or inclusion criteria.
- **Confounded Cross-Framework Corpus:** Although the paper argues for attribution-first multi-framework benchmarking across Tinker, TRL, veRL, and OpenRLHF, Section III-A admits that veRL and OpenRLHF are "dry-run launch plans" and only Tinker and TRL produced runs. Furthermore, the 42-experiment corpus used for the one-way ANOVAs is non-orthogonal (marginal $\eta^2$ sum to 1.897), meaning framework and algorithm effects are heavily confounded with model family and scale.
- **Underpowered Toy-Scale Training Runs:** Key algorithmic comparisons rely on 40-step runs of `Qwen2.5-0.5B` (Sections IV-B, IV-D) or 30–50 step single-seed snapshots on Tinker whose internal GRPO implementation is closed-source (Section V). Failing to reject the null hypothesis after 40 steps on a 0.5B model shows that the experiment is underpowered or too short, not that PPO, GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, and Dr. GRPO perform identically at scale.
- **Incomplete Held-Out Campaign Without a Base-Model Baseline:** In the E1–E14 campaign, 9 of 14 lanes did not complete (4 are `LAUNCH PEND`, 2 are `QUOTA`, 3 are `CLOSED EXT`), and the 5 graded suites lack full-suite unadapted base-model baselines. As Section V notes, these standalone numbers provide zero evidence regarding the effect of GRPO post-training.
- **Missing Technical Definitions:** Terms and methods central to the results—such as the "AERO" algorithm (p.3), the "eight-item minimum reportable stack" (p.3), the exact formula for "Pairwise Contrast Density (PCD)" (p.2), and the "conditional utility" objective (p.2)—are named but never defined in the manuscript.

## Anchored Comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.2: "In this release only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs; veRL and OpenRLHF entries are dry-run launch plans and are never pooled with real results."` - Claiming a "multi-framework benchmark harness" across four frameworks in the Introduction while only executing runs on two frameworks (one of which, Tinker, has closed internals per p.4) overstates the scope of the empirical study. Fix: Revise the Abstract and Introduction to state explicitly that empirical cross-stack measurements are restricted to TRL and Tinker, and move veRL/OpenRLHF adapter specifications to a brief system-design note.
- `[CRITICAL] p.3: "Across 42 experiments, four separate one-way ANOVAs give marginal η2 = 0.546 for framework, 0.558 for algorithm, 0.471 for model family and 0.322 for scale. These sum to 1.897, so they are not a variance decomposition"` - Running four separate one-way ANOVAs on an unbalanced, collinear corpus of 42 runs cannot isolate whether $\eta^2 = 0.546$ for framework is actually driven by model scale or algorithm assignment across back-ends. Fix: Replace the four marginal one-way ANOVAs with a Type II or Type III multi-factor ANOVA (or linear mixed-effects model) that estimates partial $\eta^2$ after controlling for the other factors, and provide a table listing the exact 42 runs.
- `[CRITICAL] p.3: "The matched same-stack PPO/GRPO contrast (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 40 steps, five paired seeds) gives a paired held-out difference of −0.002 (p = 0.374)"` - Forty training steps on a 0.5B model is far too brief and small-scale to support general conclusions about PPO vs. GRPO or group-size sweeps ($G \in \{2,4,8,16\}$). Fix: Report the learning curves (training reward and validation accuracy over steps) to prove whether the 0.5B model actually learned during those 40 steps, report the batch size and total token budget, and scope the claim strictly to early-phase dynamics on sub-billion-parameter models.
- `[CRITICAL] p.4: "The campaign results describe one actor at one revision; no per-suite base-model comparator exists, so they are not evidence of what the GRPO step contributed."` - Reporting a 14-suite evaluation where only 5 suites have scores (Table I, Fig. 2) and none have a matched base-model score violates the paper's "attribution-first" premise. Fix: Run the unadapted `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` base model through the exact same harness on the 5 completed suites (LAB-Bench, AgentDojo, VerilogEval, Omni-MATH, SWE-bench Pro) and report the paired base-vs-GRPO deltas in Table I.
- `[MAJOR] p.2: "On the main configuration ZVF is approximately 0.72–0.77, so roughly three quarters of each rollout batch contributes no reward-driven gradient."` - The "main configuration" is never defined. The reader does not know the base model, training dataset, group size $G$, learning rate, or step count for this headline result. Fix: Add a specification table in Section III or IV-A detailing the model, dataset, $G$, batch size, and step count for the main configuration and every audit cohort.
- `[MAJOR] p.2: "a 368-run audit across seven models shows ZVF of 0.95–0.97 at both reward extremes and 0.25–0.29 at mid-range rewards (0.35–0.50)"` - Neither the 368 runs nor the seven models are identified anywhere in the paper, and it is unclear how this relates to the 42-experiment corpus in Section IV-D. Fix: List the seven models, the tasks, and the selection criteria for the 368-run audit, and plot the empirical $(p, \text{ZVF})$ scatter against the theoretical $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ curve.
- `[MAJOR] p.2: "We model usable signal per prompt as S(p, G) = p(1 − p)(1 − hG(p))."` - The derivation and interpretation of $S(p,G)$ are stated without justification. For Bernoulli rewards $r_j \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$, $\mathbb{P}(\sigma_g = 0) = h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$, so the probability of a non-degenerate group is $1 - h_G(p)$ alone; multiplying by the population variance $p(1-p)$ conflates non-degeneracy with unnormalized reward variance, even though GRPO divides by $\sigma_g + \varepsilon$ in Eq. (1). Fix: Explain why $p(1-p)$ appears in $S(p,G)$ despite Eq. (1) standardizing rewards to unit variance whenever $\sigma_g > 0$.
- `[MAJOR] p.3: "In a same-stack audit of DAPO, GSPO, Dr. GRPO and AERO against GRPO (eight paired seeds, 500 held-out GSM8K items per unit)"` - "AERO" is introduced without a citation or mathematical definition, and the base model, framework, and training hyperparameters for this 8-seed audit are omitted. Fix: Define and cite AERO in Section II, and specify the model, stack, and training budget used in this five-algorithm comparison.
- `[MAJOR] p.3: "These findings motivate an eight-item minimum reportable stack and a machine-readable registry"` - The manuscript claims the "minimum reportable stack" as a core contribution in the Introduction and Conclusion, but never lists what the eight items are. Fix: Enumerate the eight required fields of the minimum reportable stack explicitly in Section III or IV-D.
- `[MAJOR] p.5: "Suite named is the scope the terminal state refers to (replacement or native scope where one exists). E8, E10 and E14 completed a replacement scope; their original private suites are separately closed external"` - Fig. 2 caption states that E8 (LAB-Bench), E10 (AgentDojo), and E14 (Omni-MATH) completed a "replacement scope" for "original private suites," without explaining what the original private suites were or why replacement scopes were substituted. Also, E1 is listed as "SWE-bench Multilingual" in Fig. 2 (`LAUNCH PEND`) but as "SWE-bench Pro (original)" in Table I (`731/731, 0.274%`). Fix: Add a table mapping E1–E14 from their original contract names to their replacement scopes, and reconcile the E1 naming discrepancy between Table I and Fig. 2.
- `[MINOR] p.2: "We also track the pairwise contrast density (PCD), the fraction of within-group completion pairs with differing rewards."` - PCD is defined only verbally and no PCD values are ever reported in Section IV. Fix: Write the explicit formula $\text{PCD} = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{g \in B} \frac{2}{G(G-1)} \sum_{i < j} \mathbb{1}[r_{g,i} \neq r_{g,j}]$, note that for binary rewards it equals the unbiased sample variance up to a factor of $2$, and either report PCD results in Section IV or remove it from the contributions list in Section VI.
- `[MINOR] p.3: "Fig. 1. Gradient utilisation (1 − ZVF) rises with group size G with diminishing returns (left)... power-law fit, R2=0.9996"` - Fitting a 2-parameter power law to 4 data points ($G \in \{2,4,8,16\}$) and extrapolating to $G=32$ is over-parameterized, and $1 - \text{ZVF}$ must saturate at $1$ as $G \to \infty$, which a power law violates. Fix: Fit the theoretical curve $1 - \mathbb{E}_p[p^G + (1-p)^G]$ from Eq. (3) instead of an unbounded power law.
- `[SUGGESTION] p.1: "This paper reports an individual M.Tech project that treats both issues as measurement problems."` - Mentioning degree-program metadata is unconventional for a double-blind top-tier ML venue submission (along with the non-anonymized author block). Fix: Remove academic project metadata and anonymize the submission if targeting double-blind review.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Details of the 368-Run and 42-Experiment Corpora:** What are the exact models, datasets, step budgets, and back-ends in the "main configuration" (Section IV-A), the "368-run audit across seven models" (Section IV-B), and the "42 experiments" (Section IV-D)? Why are there 368 runs in Section IV-B but only 42 experiments in the Section IV-D ANOVA?
2. **Role of $p(1-p)$ in Eq. (3):** Because standard GRPO divides by $\sigma_g + \varepsilon$, any non-degenerate binary group with empirical pass rate $\hat{p} \in \{1/G, \dots, (G-1)/G\}$ has unit advantage variance $\frac{1}{G}\sum_i A_i^2 \approx 1$. Why does your analytical signal model $S(p,G)$ multiply $(1 - h_G(p))$ by the unstandardized Bernoulli variance $p(1-p)$?
3. **Training Dynamics in the 40-Step Audits:** In the 40-step `Qwen2.5-0.5B` experiments (Sections IV-B and IV-D), what was the initial vs. final held-out accuracy? Did any arm achieve a statistically significant gain over the step-0 base model within 40 steps?
4. **Definition of the Eight-Item Minimum Reportable Stack and AERO:** What are the eight specific items in your minimum reportable stack, and what is the exact mathematical formulation and citation for the AERO estimator tested in Section IV-D?
5. **Base-Model Comparisons on E1–E14:** Why wasn't the unadapted `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` base model evaluated on the 5 completed suites (Table I) using the same native evaluators, rather than only running a small 5–151 item subset (Section V)?

## Overall Score and Confidence

- **Overall Score:** **3 / 10** (Clear Reject — The paper tackles an important measurement problem in RLVR with admirable honesty about negative and null results, and the ZVF / asymmetric escalation observations are useful. However, the empirical execution is far below NeurIPS/ICLR standards: 2 of 4 frameworks are dry-run plans, 9 of 14 evaluation suites are incomplete, the 5 completed suites lack full base-model baselines, the primary algorithmic contrasts rely on 40-step runs of a 0.5B model or collinear one-way ANOVAs, and critical experimental details for the 368-run and 42-run audits are omitted from the manuscript.)
- **Confidence:** **5 / 5** (Absolutely certain — Every methodological gap, sample size, and missing baseline is explicitly stated in the manuscript text itself.)
