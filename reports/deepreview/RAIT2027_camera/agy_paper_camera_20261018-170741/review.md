## Summary

This paper investigates "signal starvation" in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). When all $G$ completions for a prompt receive identical verifiable rewards, the within-group reward variance is zero, causing the group-relative advantage and the task-driven policy gradient to vanish. The authors define the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) to measure the share of starved prompt groups per batch and introduce Pairwise Contrast Density (PCD) as a continuous alternative. They also propose an idealized Bernoulli signal model, $S(p, G) = p(1-p)(1 - h_G(p))$ with $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$, and test three predictions across a 368-run audit and controlled sweeps on Qwen and Llama models using GSM8K and synthetic arithmetic tasks. Finally, the authors evaluate active interventions—including an adaptive group-size controller, dynamic sampling, difficulty curricula, and advantage re-baselining—and report negative or cost-inefficient results across all interventions. They conclude that ZVF and PCD should serve strictly as passive monitoring diagnostics rather than optimization targets.

## Strengths

- **Clear formulation of a practical phenomenon**: The paper cleanly isolates zero-variance group collapse in GRPO and distinguishes easy-driven saturation ($p \to 1$) from incapacity ($p \to 0$).
- **Honest reporting of null results**: The counterfactual trace (showing 92.3% of escalations fire on already-solved groups) and the cost-matched curriculum evaluation provide valuable negative evidence against naive variance-recovery heuristics.
- **Simple analytical baseline**: The Bernoulli signal model $S(p, G)$ offers a closed-form reference to separate i.i.d. sampling noise from prompt-level heterogeneity (Jensen gap).
- **Recomputable telemetry**: Persisting raw per-group reward tensors to verify logged diagnostics against micro-jitter and threshold sensitivity ($\epsilon$) is good empirical practice.

## Weaknesses

- **Severe mathematical and empirical contradictions in the core model**: Prediction T1 claims that the expected ZVF indicator equals $h_G(p)$, yet Section IV-C states that binning by empirical success rate $\hat{p}$ gives a ZVF of $0.000$ in every interior bin. That is a tautology of conditioning on $\hat{p} \in (0, 1)$, not a test of $E[\text{ZVF} \mid p] = h_G(p)$. Furthermore, the residual signs and theory explanations between Table I ( p. 3) and Section V (p. 4) directly contradict Jensen's inequality and each other.
- **Tautological definition of the proposed control signal (PCD)**: For binary rewards $r \in \{0, 1\}$, the unbiased sample variance of a group is $\widehat{\text{Var}}(r) = \frac{G}{G-1}\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})$. The empirical estimator of $\text{PCD} = \frac{G-1}{G} E[p(1-p)]$ is simply $\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})$ (or the U-statistic $\widehat{\text{Var}}(r)$). PCD does not resolve the symmetry problem between mastery ($p=1$) and incapacity ($p=0$) because $p(1-p)$ is completely symmetric around $p=0.5$.
- **Insufficient experimental rigor and opaque reporting**: Key experimental parameters (exact tasks, prompt counts, learning rates, rollout budgets, and model sizes for Table II and the curriculum experiments) are omitted. Many runs are only 30–50 steps with a single seed, and the main $G$-sweep is conducted on a near-ceiling task (~0.98 accuracy) where no learning signal is needed.
- **Overstated novelty relative to existing RLVR literature**: Prior works such as DAPO [5] and Dr. GRPO [7] already identify zero-variance groups and standard-deviation normalization biases in GRPO. This manuscript reads as an internal telemetry audit rather than a complete scientific paper suitable for a top ML venue.

## Anchored comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.1: "On our main configuration roughly 0.72–0.77 of each batch is starved... three stored GSM8K group tensors give 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155 (pooled 0.1583)"` - The "main configuration" is never defined, and the first concrete numbers reported in Section IV-A (0.130–0.190) are five times lower than the headline 0.72–0.77 range without explanation. Specify the exact model, dataset, group size $G$, batch size $|B_t|$, and training step range for both the 0.72–0.77 claim and the three stored tensors.
- `[CRITICAL] p.2: "ZVF is unsigned: an all-wrong group and an all-correct group score identically. We therefore also track the pairwise contrast density, PCD = \frac{G-1}{G} E[p(1 - p)]"` - PCD is also unsigned and symmetric under $p \leftrightarrow 1-p$: an all-wrong group ($\hat{p}=0$) and an all-correct group ($\hat{p}=1$) both yield zero pairwise contrast. To distinguish mastery from incapacity, track the directional components $p^G$ (all-correct fraction) and $(1-p)^G$ (all-wrong fraction), or condition PCD on $\hat{p} < 0.5$, and rewrite Section III-B to state explicitly how PCD is estimated per group from binary rewards.
- `[CRITICAL] p.2: "with unnormalised group-centred advantages, the mean absolute advantage is 2\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p}), so gradient magnitude should track p(1 - p)."` - Standard GRPO in Eq. (1) divides the centered reward by $\sigma_g + \varepsilon = \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})} + \varepsilon$, which makes the mean absolute advantage $2\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}$ for non-degenerate binary groups (and corresponds to Dr. GRPO [7] only when $\sigma_g$ is removed). Derive the expected advantage magnitude for both normalized GRPO and unnormalized Dr. GRPO, and test T3 on standard normalized GRPO.
- `[CRITICAL] p.2: "T1. Binning per-group reward tensors by empirical success rate gives a ZVF indicator of 1.000 at \hat{p} \in \{0, 1\} and 0.000 in every interior bin."` - Conditioning a binary group on its empirical mean $\hat{p} = \frac{1}{G}\sum_j r_j$ makes ZVF deterministic by algebra ($\hat{p} \in \{0,1\} \iff \widehat{\text{Var}}(r) = 0$); this tests an identity, not the probabilistic prediction $E[\text{ZVF} \mid p] = h_G(p)$. Estimate each prompt's underlying $p$ using an independent validation rollout set (or leave-one-out rollouts) before computing ZVF on training groups of size $G$.
- `[CRITICAL] p.3: "Table I: Group-size sweep... G=2: ZVF (measured) 0.838, h_G (theory) 0.964, Residual -0.126... G=16: ZVF 0.631, h_G 0.704"` - Because $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ is strictly convex on $[0,1]$ for all $G \ge 2$, Jensen's inequality guarantees $E[h_G(p)] \ge h_G(E[p])$, meaning heterogeneous prompt difficulty must produce a positive residual above the pooled-rate theory $h_G(\bar{p})$, not a negative one; moreover, $h_G(\bar{p})$ in Table I does not match any single constant $\bar{p}$ across $G=2,4,8,16$ (e.g., $h_2(\bar{p})=0.964 \implies \bar{p} \approx 0.982$, which gives $h_{16}(0.982) = 0.746 \neq 0.704$). State how $\bar{p}$ was computed for each row of Table I, verify the code for $h_G(\bar{p})$, and reconcile the negative residuals in Table I with the claim on p. 2 that "measured ZVF sits above $h_G(\bar{p})$ at every $G$."

## Questions for the authors

1. **Table I vs. Jensen's Inequality**: How were the theoretical values $h_G$ in Table I computed? Since $h_G(p)$ is convex on $[0,1]$, why is the measured ZVF in Table I lower than $h_G$ (negative residual), while Section IV-C (p. 2) states that "measured ZVF sits above $h_G(\bar{p})$ at every $G$"?
2. **Disambiguating mastery vs. incapacity with PCD**: Since $\text{PCD} \propto E[p(1-p)]$ equals $0$ at both $p=0$ and $p=1$, how did the Adaptive-$G$ controller in Section IV-E use PCD as an "aliasing guard" if 92.3% of its escalations still fired on all-correct groups? Why not trigger escalation only when the group reward mean is zero ($\mu_g = 0$)?
3. **Experimental details of Table II**: What model, dataset, prompt count, number of training steps, and number of random seeds were used in Table II? What does a held-out gain of $+0.500$ to $+0.575$ represent (e.g., 50.0 to 57.5 percentage points on synthetic arithmetic), and how only 120 total rollouts were needed to achieve it?
4. **Performance on unsaturated benchmarks**: Since the Qwen2.5-0.5B sweep is already at ~98% accuracy on its task, what happens to the held-out accuracy across $G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ on a benchmark where initial accuracy is in the interior regime (e.g., 20%–50% on MATH or GSM8K)?
