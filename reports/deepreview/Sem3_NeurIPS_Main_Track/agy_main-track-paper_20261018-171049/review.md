## Summary

This manuscript conducts a diagnostic and methodological audit of critic-free, group-relative reinforcement learning (RLVR / GRPO-style loops) for large language models across scales ranging from 0.6B to 671B parameters. The authors organize their work around four central claims:
1. **Reward-Contrast Mechanism (C1):** Under binary or verifier rewards, a group-relative update carries a non-zero gradient only when the sampled group of $G$ completions contains mixed rewards, occurring with probability $P(\text{usable}) \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_x [1 - (1-p_x)^G - p_x^G]$.
2. **Triage Diagnostics (C2):** Zero-Variance Fraction ($\text{ZVF}_t$) and Gradient Utilization ($\text{GU}_t = 1 - \text{ZVF}_t$) distinguish cold-start collapse (high ZVF, low reward), saturation (high ZVF, high reward), and active learning (low ZVF). A first-5-step heuristic ($\text{ZVF} \ge 80\%$, reward $\le 5\%$) catches both collapsed runs in a 22-run validation subset.
3. **Training Reward vs. Capability (C3):** Online training reward reflects rollout dynamics rather than held-out generalization. In a paired 5-seed, 200-prompt held-out GSM8K evaluation on Qwen3-8B, accuracy moves from $82.0\%$ to $83.3\%$ ($p = 0.26$), while top-checkpoint selection on training last-10 reward induces post-selection over-optimism ($87.2\%\text{--}95.0\%$).
4. **Under-Specification of Algorithm Labels (C4):** Nominal algorithm labels (PPO, GRPO, DPO) hide critical implementation variables. Under nominally matched visible configurations on Qwen3-8B, the Tinker runner reaches $84.4\%$ (or $85.6\%$) last-10 reward while TRL on Modal H100 reaches $5.0\%$, demonstrating severe sensitivity to the underlying training stack.

While the core methodological warnings—that online reward is not capability and that RL post-training comparisons are heavily confounded by stack details—are timely and well-taken, the manuscript fails to meet the standards of a top ML venue. The paper reads like an unedited concatenation of an earlier overclaiming benchmark draft and a series of automated defensive disclaimers, leaving behind severe anonymity violations, synthetic or dry-run data in main figures/tables, pseudoreplicated hypothesis tests, internal contradictions across tables and figures, and numerous fabricated or corrupted citations.

## Strengths

- **Clear Mechanistic Framing of Group-Relative Dead Zones:** Framing critic-free group-relative updates via the binomial mixed-group probability $1 - p_x^G - (1-p_x)^G$ and operationalizing it via $\text{ZVF}_t$ and $\text{GU}_t$ gives practitioners an intuitive, zero-overhead telemetry metric to diagnose cold-start collapse versus saturation (pp. 1–3, 25).
- **Lagged Regression Analysis Against Tautology:** In Section 5.16 (p. 26, Table 20), the authors test whether $\text{ZVF}_t$ predicts future reward $R_{t+k}$ after controlling for current reward $R_t$. Showing that the partial correlation at lag 5 drops to $r = 0.059$ ($p = 0.096$) and that within-run coefficients are null ($p > 0.16$) is a rigorous piece of self-skeptical analysis that prevents over-selling ZVF as a forecasting metric.
- **Separation of Post-Selection Over-Optimism from Paired Controls:** The contrast between the post-selected top-10 checkpoint table (Table 12, mean $92.08\%$ held-out accuracy) and the pre-specified 5-seed paired Qwen3-8B control ($82.0\% \to 83.3\%, p = 0.26$) clearly illustrates how checkpoint selection on noisy 16-sample rollout batches inflates perceived RLVR gains (p. 15).
- **Candid Accounting of Compute, Failures, and Implementation Flaws:** The manuscript transparently documents that $61.6\%\text{--}89.6\%$ of the full-sequence loss magnitude in its runner comes from unmasked prompt tokens (p. 2), discloses broken HumanEval sandboxes that strip `len`/`range`/`sum` (p. 19), and provides itemized financial and carbon accounting that includes failed runs (Tables 23–24, pp. 39–40).

## Weaknesses

- **Double-Blind Anonymity Violations:** The manuscript is not anonymized. Page 1 lists all eight authors, their affiliations (PES University, Great Learning, Northwestern University), and their personal/institutional email addresses. Furthermore, Section 4.4 (p. 11), Section 6 (p. 30), Section 9.1 (p. 38), Section 9.4 (p. 41), Section 9.5 (p. 42), Section 9.6 (p. 43), and the Acknowledgments (p. 43) repeatedly identify the authors (`arvindcr4`, `Balasandhya`, `Madhu2133`, `MohammadRafiML`, `dhruvanmurthy`), their collaborators, their institution in Bengaluru, and their advisors.
- **Severe Statistical Pseudoreplication and Contradictory Test Accounting:** Despite repeatedly stating in prose that single-seed runs have zero statistical power (p. 10) and that step-level rewards are autocorrelated telemetry (p. 12), the paper bases its main inferential tables (Table 7, p. 11; Table 19, p. 23; Figure 13, p. 24; Table 27, p. 54; Table 30, p. 55) on two-sample Welch $t$-tests and Mann–Whitney $U$ tests over the last-10 or 30 autocorrelated steps of single-seed trajectories. Moreover, the paper contradicts itself on the family of tests: p. 10 claims $k=5$ planned tests ($\alpha' = 0.0100$) in one paragraph and $m=20$ tests in the next, whereas Appendix C/D (pp. 53–54) applies Bonferroni correction over $k=38$ tests, resulting in wildly different $p$-values for the same comparisons across Table 7, Table 19, and Table 30.
- **Synthetic and Dry-Run Data in Main Tables and Figures:** In Table 28 (p. 54), the `Tinker (GRPO)` row ($0.999 [0.997, 1.000]$) lists its source as `synth. (mean ± SE, n=5)`, which was also used in Table 10 (p. 14) to claim a 5-seed empirical result. Similarly, Section 5.2 (p. 12) and Table 9 (p. 13) state that the `verl` and `OpenRLHF` rows in the Task 4 framework comparison are "deterministic dry-run fallbacks" and refer the reader to Figure 4, which does not even plot `verl` or `OpenRLHF`.
- **Pervasive Figure–Text Mismatches and Internal Contradictions:** The manuscript looks stitched together from incompatible versions. Figure 4 (p. 14) is captioned as the "Task 4 stack-sensitivity probe" comparing Tinker, TRL, verl, and OpenRLHF on Qwen3-8B, but the actual graphic plots CleanRL PPO, Tianshou PPO, SB3 PPO, Modal PPO-REINFORCE, Tinker GRPO, and TRL GRPO on Qwen2.5-0.5B. Figure 5 (p. 15) is captioned as "Learning curves for the arithmetic sanity task across RL libraries" ($\pm 1$ SD across 5 seeds), but the graphic plots single-seed GSM8K and `tool_use` curves (`GRPO vs PPO-REINFORCE learning curves (GSM8K, tool\_use)`). Hyperparameters, step counts, and accuracy values contradict each other across sections (detailed in Anchored Comments).
- **Non-Canonical Runner Confounds the Diagnostic Audit:** The primary runner omits the PPO probability ratio and clipping, omits a reference-policy KL penalty, and fails to mask prompt tokens during loss computation (so $61.6\%\text{--}89.6\%$ of the loss magnitude comes from prompt tokens, p. 2). Auditing a broken implementation that trains on prompt tokens and lacks importance sampling clipping undermines the paper's ability to attribute observed instabilities (such as the Nemotron-120B collapse or the 17$\times$ Tinker-vs-TRL gap) to standard group-relative RL mechanisms.
- **Fabricated and Corrupted Bibliography:** Numerous references in the bibliography contain fabricated arXiv identifiers (e.g., `2510.12345`, `2510.08765`, `2601.12034`, `2603.04567`), impossible future publication venues (e.g., "NeurIPS 2026" on p. 45), or mismatched titles and author lists.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Origin of the Paired Qwen3-8B Control ($82.0\% \to 83.3\%$ vs. Table 11's $89.8\% \to 94.2\%$):** What is the exact experimental protocol for the 5-seed, 200-prompt Qwen3-8B evaluation that yielded $82.0\% \to 83.3\%$ ($p = 0.26$), and how does it differ from the 5-seed Qwen3-8B evaluation in Table 11 / Table 29 that yielded $89.8\% \to 94.2 \pm 0.5\%$ ($p = 0.006$)?
2. **Impact of Prompt-Token Loss Masking:** Since $61.6\%\text{--}89.6\%$ of the loss magnitude in your runner comes from unmasked prompt tokens (p. 2), how do the ZVF trajectories, the Qwen3-8B group-size ablation, and the Tinker-vs-TRL comparison change when prompt tokens are properly masked out of the policy gradient loss?
3. **Synthetic and Dry-Run Entries:** Why was a synthetic entry (`synth. (mean ± SE, n=5)`) used for Tinker (GRPO) in Table 10 and Table 28, and why were dry-run fallback rows included for `verl` and `OpenRLHF` in Section 5.2 and Table 9?
4. **Reconciliation of Checkpoint Last-10 Scores:** Why do the training last-10 numbers for `Qwen3-8B-Base (s=42)`, `GPT-OSS-120B (s=42)`, and `Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (s=42)` differ between Table 9 ($85.6\%$, $87.5\%$, $97.9\%$) and Table 12 ($98.8\%$, $91.9\%$, $91.9\%$)?
