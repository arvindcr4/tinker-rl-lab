### Summary
This manuscript investigates the division of labor between gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost) and large language models (LLMs) in credit-card fraud detection. The authors frame their investigation around a head-to-head experiment on a 50,000-row custom synthetic dataset generated via `scikit-learn`'s `make_classification` (20 numerical features $V_1, \dots, V_{20}$ plus 4 row-level summary statistics: mean, standard deviation, maximum, and minimum). On the leakage-free test split, stock XGBoost achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.7955 (10,000 rows), whereas a Qwen3.5-4B model fine-tuned on serialized row text achieves an accuracy of 0.792 and a chance-level ROC-AUC of 0.48268 (on a 500-row positive-enriched subset). From this negative result and four operational constraints (latency, cost, calibration, and prompt-injection exposure), the authors argue that LLMs should not serve as real-time tabular scorers. Instead, they propose a hybrid architecture where XGBoost remains the real-time scorer and LLMs act as a *sensor* (extracting numerical features from unstructured evidence), a *scribe* (drafting FinCEN Suspicious Activity Reports and ECOA/Regulation B adverse-action notices), a cold-start triager, and an alert-queue investigator. Sections 4 through 7 present an extensive series of bootstrap ablations, calibration audits, cost-per-decision frontiers, label-noise sensitivity sweeps, and cross-project density comparisons.

Despite a sensible high-level thesis, the manuscript is not in a publishable state for a top machine learning venue. It reads as an unedited compilation of automated research logs ("iter-4", "iter-144 JOB A", "P5P8-SYNTH") rather than a coherent scientific paper. Furthermore, the empirical methodology suffers from severe flaws: train-test leakage in Sections 4–7, an invalid proxy for the LLM sensor, self-contradictory conclusions across consecutive subsections, and undefined references to external projects. Note also that the manuscript text truncates mid-sentence on page 55 before Sections 8–12 appear.

---

### Strengths
- **Practical architectural framing:** Separating the fraud detection pipeline into distinct functional tasks (tabular scoring, multimodal perception, regulatory narration, cold-start triage, and alert investigation) is a clear, practical mental model for practitioners evaluating LLM adoption (pp. 1–2).
- **Attention to operational constraints:** The discussion of real-time card authorization constraints—specifically sub-100ms latency budgets, token costs scaling linearly with transaction volume, probability calibration, and indirect prompt-injection surfaces in free-text transaction fields (pp. 3–4)—highlights critical deployment realities often ignored in academic tabular ML benchmarks.
- **Transparent disclosure of negative results and leakage:** The authors candidly report that their fine-tuned LLM performs at chance level (AUC 0.48268, p. 3) and explicitly disclose where training and evaluation splits overlap in their calibration scripts (p. 4), as well as where library version changes altered baseline recall (p. 44).

### Weaknesses
1. **Severe train-test data leakage invalidates Sections 4–7:** On page 4, the authors disclose that the pipeline generating Tables 2–30 and 32–52 fits XGBoost on the full `fraud_data.csv` and evaluates on `test_data.csv`, which is a subset of `fraud_data.csv`. This in-sample evaluation inflates XGBoost's AUC from 0.7955 to 0.9988–0.9999. Evaluating calibration (Brier, ECE), top-$K$ precision/recall, threshold transfer, and decision regret on training data is methodologically invalid; tree ensembles overfit in-sample probabilities and rankings in ways that do not generalize to held-out data.
2. **Invalid empirical surrogate for the "LLM-as-sensor":** Throughout Sections 4–6, the "LLM sensor" is modeled by computing four deterministic row-wise summary statistics (`V_mean`, `V_std`, `V_max`, `V_min`) of the 20 synthetic numerical features $V_1, \dots, V_{20}$ (p. 5). An LLM extracts semantic signals from unstructured text or images (e.g., receipts, merchant memos); computing the mean and standard deviation across 20 synthetic Gaussian covariates generated by `make_classification` has no mathematical or domain connection to LLM feature extraction.
3. **Unedited automated agent logs and internal jargon:** From Section 4.5 onward, the manuscript degenerates into pasted iteration logs ("iter-68 row 79", "iter 100 JOB A", "P5P8-SYNTH", "H1 (PASS)", "Verdict (H1 REFUTED)"). It is filled with undefined cross-paper references ("Pillar 3 ZVF controller", "P5 mega-manifest", "P6 outcomes.zvf_antiherding", "N2 four-method panel: grpo, aero, areal, gift") that have nothing to do with credit-card fraud detection.
4. **Direct internal contradictions across subsections:** Because sequential log entries were appended without synthesis, the paper repeatedly contradicts itself. For example:
   - Section 5.8 (Table 48, p. 39) claims per-cohort isotonic calibration reduces worst-cohort ECE on the 1.44% release split from 0.115–0.176 to `0.0000` ("closes the compliance gap"). Yet Section 6.5.2 (Table 52, p. 47) and Section 7.5.4 (p. 55) state that at the exact same 1.44% rate, `iso_per_cohort` amplifies worst-cohort ECE to `0.990` ("Per-cohort PAVA fails to close ECE at any rate").
   - Section 4.6 (p. 9) claims the 4 aggregate features are a "free oracle LLM sensor" that improves true positives at $K \ge 1\%$, Section 4.9 (p. 13) says "the sensor never pays for itself (0/25 cells)", Section 4.14 (p. 19) says "when does the sensor pay for itself? The answer is: at $K \ge 2\%$", and Section 5.11.1 (p. 42) says "`xgb-only` Pareto-dominates at every cell."
   - Baseline recall@K=2% for `XGB-24full` jumps between `0.778` (Table 21, p. 18), `0.9931` (p. 23), `0.951` (Table 49, p. 39), `0.9792` (p. 40), `0.389` (p. 44), `0.368` (p. 45), and `1.000` (Table 51, p. 46).
5. **Unverifiable core claims and missing sections:** Of the four central capability gaps claimed in the Abstract and Introduction (VLM document fraud, FinCEN/ECOA narration, cold-start triage, and 85$\times$ cheaper agentic investigation), none are empirically evaluated in the provided 55 pages. Sections 8–12 are completely missing due to manuscript truncation at page 55, and the 55 pages present are bloated (55 pages vs. the standard 9-page NeurIPS/ICLR main-text limit) with redundant tables.
6. **Weak head-to-head baseline setup:** Comparing XGBoost on 10,000 natural-rate test rows (1.44% positives) against Qwen3.5-4B SFT on a 500-row positive-enriched subset (20% positives) (p. 3, Table 1) violates basic experimental control, even if ROC-AUC is invariant to class prior shifts in expectation. Furthermore, serializing 20 unnamed synthetic floats (`V1`...`V20`) into text strings strips LLMs of the semantic column/value priors that methods like TabLLM rely on.

### Anchored Comments

- **[CRITICAL] p. 4:** `"The calibration and robustness pipeline that produces these tables fits on the full fraud_data.csv and scores test_data.csv, whose rows are a subset of fraud_data.csv; the training set therefore overlaps the evaluation rows, which inflates the absolute AUC toward 1.0"` - Training on the test set invalidates all downstream empirical evaluations of calibration, precision-recall, operating thresholds, and decision regret in Sections 4–7. **Concrete fix:** Modify `p8_calibration_cis.py` and all downstream scripts in `scripts/p5p8/` to fit strictly on `train_data.csv` (40,000 rows) and evaluate on the disjoint `test_data.csv` (10,000 rows), then regenerate all tables and figures.
- **[CRITICAL] p. 5:** `"XGB-4sensor fits a tree on the four aggregate columns alone – an empirical surrogate for what an LLM-as-sensor would produce when it returns only a small tree-readable numerical vector."` - Using the row-wise mean, std, min, and max of 20 synthetic `make_classification` features is not a valid proxy for an LLM extracting features from unstructured text or images; those four quantities are deterministic functions of the raw features already visible to the tree. **Concrete fix:** Either evaluate an actual LLM extracting features from a dataset with text/multimodal fields, or explicitly simulate an unstructured modality by holding out a subset of informative latent features from `XGB-20raw` that only the sensor can noisy-recover (similar to the synthetic 25th feature in Section 5.2, Table 37).
- **[CRITICAL] p. 26:** `"The task is to separate genuine RL (grpo) from three surrogate methods (aero, areal, gift); positive = surrogate. Each of the 4 methods contributes 40 training steps, giving N=160 rows"` - Section 4.18 ("Reproducible re-derivation of the telemetry detector") evaluates a logistic regression classifier on RL training-step reward tensors (`zvf`, `adv_std`), which is an entirely different paper and domain. **Concrete fix:** Delete Section 4.18, Section 6.8, Section 6.9, and Section 7 entirely from this manuscript, and remove all cross-paper couplings to `P5`, `P6`, `P7`, `GRPO`, and `ZVF`.
- **[CRITICAL] p. 39 & p. 47:** `"The amount Q0 XGB-20raw worst-cohort ECE drops from 0.176 to 0.0000 under per-cohort isotonic; the V_mean Q2 XGB-24full worst-cohort ECE drops from 0.157 to 0.0000."` (p. 39) vs. `"1.44 | XGB-20raw | iso_per_cohort | 0.990 | 0.941"` (Table 52, p. 47) and `"Across all 5 positive rates {0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.00, 1.44}%, 26/26 cohort-cells have worst-cell ECE > 0.10 under iso_per_cohort"` (p. 55) - Section 5.8 and Sections 6.5/7.5 report opposite results (0.0000 vs. 0.990) for the exact same method (`iso_per_cohort`), model (`XGB-20raw`), and positive rate (`1.44%`). **Concrete fix:** Audit the PAVA implementation difference between the iter-104 script and the iter-136 script (likely a bug in label/prediction sorting or fold recombination in iter-136 that inverted probabilities), fix the bug, and report a single verified result.
- **[CRITICAL] p. 55:** `"H4 — Density <truncated 138611 bytes>"` - The manuscript is 55 pages long (exceeding NeurIPS/ICLR length limits by ~6$\times$) and cuts off mid-sentence before Sections 8, 9, 10, and 12 (the core taxonomy, hybrid architecture, regulatory grounding, and bibliography) even appear. **Concrete fix:** Condense Sections 4–7 into 2–3 pages of leakage-free, non-contradictory results, restore the missing Sections 8–12 and References, and bring the main text within the 9-page venue limit.
- **[MAJOR] p. 1 & p. 3:** `"while a Qwen3.5-4B SFT row-serialization arm reaches accuracy 0.792 but AUC 0.48268 on a 500-row positive-enriched held-out evaluation."` - Evaluating XGBoost on 10,000 rows (1.44% fraud) and Qwen3.5-4B on 500 rows (20% fraud) makes Table 1 an uncontrolled comparison, and it is unclear how probabilities were extracted from Qwen3.5-4B to compute ROC-AUC (token log-probs vs. verbalized numbers vs. binary hard labels). **Concrete fix:** Evaluate Qwen3.5-4B on the exact same 10,000-row test split as XGBoost, and specify in Section 2 how continuous anomaly scores are extracted from the SFT model (e.g., softmax over the `fraud`/`legitimate` token logits).
- **[MAJOR] p. 32:** `"LLM-as-scribe surrogate: XGB-24full tree at $0.001/decision, modeling the LLM extraction cost."` - In Section 5.4, the authors rename `XGB-24full` to "LLM-as-scribe surrogate" and then report in Table 39 as a "sharpest finding" that `XGB-24full` vs. `LLM-scribe` has a `0.00%` decision flip-rate. Since both arms are literally the exact same `XGB-24full` model predictions with a different cost constant attached, a 0.00% flip rate is a tautology, not an empirical finding. **Concrete fix:** Remove the tautological comparison in Table 39 and stop conflating the "sensor" (feature extractor) with the "scribe" (regulatory narrative generator).
- **[MAJOR] p. 44:** `"The XGB-24full backbone on the current test_data.csv achieves recall@K=2 % = 56/144 = 0.389 under xgboost 3.x + numpy 2.x. This is lower than the iter-108 headline of 141/144 = 0.979"` - A shift in recall@K=2% from 0.979 to 0.389 is not caused by minor `hist` default changes in XGBoost 3.x; `0.389` is the leakage-free test performance (matching Section 4.1's disclosure that leakage-free top-2% caught-recall is $\approx 0.40$), whereas `0.979` was the leaked in-sample performance. **Concrete fix:** Acknowledge that the drop from 0.979 to 0.389 is due to fixing train-test leakage (or changing the train/test split), not library version drift, and unify the entire paper on a single pinned environment and leakage-free split.
- **[MINOR] p. 3 & p. 7:** `"An older 21 June 2026 internal record"` and `"at internal June 2026 token prices"` and `"quick_20260704"` - The manuscript repeatedly cites dates in June and July 2026 as historical internal records, alongside internal file paths (`experiments/results/quick_20260704/qp8_fraud.tsv`). **Concrete fix:** Remove internal file paths, iteration numbers, and anomalous future-dated provenance notes from the main text; move code reproduction commands to an Appendix or README.
- **[MINOR] p. 24:** `"∆-gap 95% CI | [-.034, -.001] | ... | [-.2"` (Table 29) - Table 29 is clipped on the right margin, cutting off the $\sigma_{\text{mult}} = 1.00$ column header and its values (`-`, `+`, `[-.2`). **Concrete fix:** Wrap or resize Table 29 using `\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...}` or reduce column spacing so the final column is legible.
- **[MINOR] p. 30:** `"[figure p8_noisy_sensor.pdf pending regeneration]"` - Figure 3 is missing and replaced by a placeholder string. **Concrete fix:** Generate and embed `p8_noisy_sensor.pdf` before submission.
- **[SUGGESTION] p. 36:** `"Amount quintile (proxy: rank of Vstd; the corpus has no native Amount column), Time tertile (proxy: rank of Vmax; operational magnitude)."` - Labeling `V_std` as "Amount" and `V_max` as "Time" in Tables 44, 45, and 48 is misleading because `V_std` and `V_max` are just row-wise summary statistics of synthetic Gaussian features. **Concrete fix:** Refer to these strata honestly as `V_std quintile` and `V_max tertile` rather than relabeling them as `Amount` and `Time`.

### Questions for the Authors
1. **Train-test leakage in Sections 4–7:** Why were the experiments in Sections 4–7 not re-run on the disjoint `train_data.csv` $\rightarrow$ `test_data.csv` split once you discovered (Section 4.1, p. 4) that fitting on `fraud_data.csv` leaked the test set into the training set and inflated AUC from 0.7955 to 0.9991?
2. **LLM scoring methodology:** In Section 2 (p. 3), how was the ROC-AUC of `0.48268` computed for the Qwen3.5-4B SFT model? Did you extract the logit probability of the target token ("fraud" vs. "legitimate"), or did you ask the model to generate a numerical confidence string, or was ROC-AUC computed from hard binary predictions?
3. **Contradiction in per-cohort isotonic calibration:** In Section 5.8 (Table 48, p. 39), 5-fold OOF per-cohort isotonic calibration on the 1.44% split yields a worst-cohort ECE of `0.0000`. In Section 6.5.2 (Table 52, p. 47), 5-fold OOF per-cohort isotonic calibration on the same 1.44% split yields a worst-cohort ECE of `0.990`. What changed between these two scripts to cause an ECE difference of 0.99?
4. **Empirical evidence for the four capability gaps:** In the missing Sections 8–12 (truncated after p. 55), are there actual experiments backing the VLM document fraud, FinCEN/ECOA compliance narration, cold-start typology triage, and 85$\times$ analyst cost reduction claims, or are those sections purely conceptual/analytical arguments?
