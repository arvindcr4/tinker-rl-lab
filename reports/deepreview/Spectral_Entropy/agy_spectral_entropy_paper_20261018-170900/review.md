## Summary

This manuscript proposes a two-part framework to mitigate zero-variance gradient starvation (advantage collapse) in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): **Spectral Legendre Routing (SLR)** and **Quantum-Inspired Givens Entropic Attention (QIG-EA)**. SLR projects discrete sequence hidden states onto continuous orthogonal Legendre polynomials over $[-1, 1]$, computes a spectral $L^2$ trajectory distance $d_{\text{spec}}(y_i, y_j)$, and applies a linear-SiLU routing block over low- and high-frequency reconstructions. QIG-EA monitors attention logit Shannon entropy $H(p)$ and applies planar Givens rotations $G(i, j, \theta)$ followed by hard coordinate zeroing when average entropy drops below a threshold $\tau_{\text{entropy}}$. The authors report reductions in Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) from 64.2% to 1.1% and accuracy gains of +7.9% on GSM8K and +7.8% on MATH.

Despite the ambitious framing, the manuscript suffers from fatal mathematical contradictions, broken architectural links between the proposed modules and the GRPO objective, buggy reference implementations that directly refute the paper's core theorems, and a complete omission of basic experimental details (e.g., the base LLM used). It is not suitable for publication.

---

## Strengths

- **Relevant Problem Domain:** Zero-variance starvation ($s_R = 0$ when all group completions receive identical verifiable rewards) is a genuine, well-known bottleneck in RLVR and GRPO training pipelines.
- **Self-Contained Code Listings:** Including the exact PyTorch code in Listings 1 and 2 makes the proposed methods completely transparent and allows direct verification of the mathematical claims against the implementation.

## Weaknesses

1. **Missing Theoretical and Architectural Bridge to Advantage Estimation:** Theorem 1 proves that when $R_1 = \dots = R_G = c$, the advantage $A_i = 0$ and the policy gradient vanishes. However, the paper never defines how SLR or QIG-EA modify $R_i$ or $A_i$ in Equation (1) or (2). If $R(x, y_i) \in \{0, 1\}$ is still evaluated by an external deterministic verifier, changing internal representations via SLR or QIG-EA can only lower ZVF if it causes the policy to sample mixed successes and failures within a group of $G=4$. Yet a model improving from 74.2% to 82.1% accuracy on GSM8K will inevitably produce *more* all-correct groups ($p^4$ increases from $\approx 0.30$ to $\approx 0.45$), making a drop in ZVF to 1.1% mathematically impossible under unmodified binary rewards. Conversely, if $d_{\text{spec}}$ or $S_{\text{route}}$ is injected into $A_i$, that formula is completely missing from the paper.
2. **Direct Contradiction on Norm Preservation (QIG-EA):** The abstract, Figure 1, Theorem 2, and Table 3 repeatedly claim that QIG-EA strictly preserves vector norms ($\|Gv\|_2 = \|v\|_2$, with $0.00 \times 10^{-7}$ norm error in Table 3). However, QIG-EA's defining operation in Algorithm 2 (Line 11) and Listing 2 (Lines 26–29) chooses $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(x_j, x_i)$—which specifically rotates the entire magnitude $\sqrt{x_i^2 + x_j^2}$ into coordinate $i$ and leaves $x'_j = 0$—and then executes hard projection `x_projected[..., (d - n_noise_dims):] = 0.0`. For $N_{\text{noise}} = 1$, the rotation places all energy into index $i = d - 2$ and sets index $j = d - 1$ to zero, so the subsequent hard projection of index $d - 1$ is a vacuous no-op! Even worse, for $N_{\text{noise}} = 2$ (Table 3, row 4), the loop rotates energy from $j = d - 2$ into $i = d - 4$, but on the $k=0$ iteration it rotated energy from $d - 1$ into $i = d - 3$. Then Line 29 zeros out the last two coordinates (`d-2` and `d-1`), which were *already* zeroed by `atan2`! And if any non-zero energy were actually zeroed out by hard projection, the norm $\|x_{\text{proj}}\|_2$ would strictly decrease, contradicting Table 3's claim of $0.00 \times 10^{-7}$ norm error.
3. **Incorrect Quadrature Scaling and Basis Discretization in SLR:** In Definition 2 (Eq. 10), the Legendre expansion coefficient is defined as $c_{i,n} = \frac{2n+1}{L_i} \sum_{l=0}^{L_i-1} x_i(l) P_n(t(l))$. Because the continuous Legendre integral is over $[-1, 1]$ of length $2$, a uniform Riemann sum with $L_i$ points has step size $\Delta t \approx \frac{2}{L_i}$, meaning the continuous forward projection requires a factor of $\frac{2n+1}{2} \Delta t \approx \frac{2n+1}{L_i}$, which is missing a factor of $1/2$ at the endpoints (trapezoidal rule) and furthermore: discrete Legendre polynomials on anispaced grid are *not* orthogonal with respect to uniform summation, nor can continuous completeness be invoked when reconstructing $X_{\text{low}}$ and $X_{\text{high}}$ without severe Runge-phenomenon aliasing at the boundaries.
4. **Misleading "Quantum-Inspired" and "Entropic" Terminology:** Standard planar Givens rotations $G(i, j, \theta) \in \mathrm{SO}(D)$ are classical numerical linear algebra primitives (Jacobi/Givens QR factorization) with no quantum mechanical content. Moreover, QIG-EA claims to trigger when attention is "collapsed" into "low-entropy, highly concentrated states" ($H < \tau_{\text{entropy}}$). In Transformers, low-entropy (sharp/peaked) attention on specific reasoning tokens is often *desirable* signal, whereas high-entropy (uniform) attention corresponds to uninformative/dispersed attention or attention sinks. Treating sharp attention ($H < \tau_{\text{entropy}}$) as "entropic starvation" and arbitrarily rotating the last $N_{\text{noise}}$ hidden channels has no theoretical justification.
5. **Unreproducible Experimental Setup and Undefined Metrics:** The manuscript never states what base language model is trained (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B, etc.), what learning rate, optimizer, rollout temperature, or training dataset is used, or how many steps are run. Furthermore, the "ground-truth reference gradient $g_{\text{ref}}$ on non-starved prompts" used to compute $\cos \phi$ in Table 1 is undefined.

## Anchored Comments

- **[CRITICAL] p.1, Abstract & p.2, Section 1:** `"reduces zero-variance starvation from 64.2% to < 1.5%"` — As defined in Definition 1 (Eq. 3), $\text{ZVF} = \mathbb{P}(R_1 = \dots = R_G)$. With binary verifiable rewards $R_i \in \{0, 1\}$ and group size $G = 4$, for any prompt with success rate $p \in [0, 1]$, the probability of a homogeneous group is $p^4 + (1-p)^4$, which has a mathematical global minimum of $2 \times (0.5)^4 = 12.5\%$ at $p = 0.5$. It is mathematically impossible for $\mathbb{E}_x[p(x)^4 + (1-p(x))^4]$ to be $1.1\%$ (Table 1) under binary verifiable rewards. **Fix:** Explicitly state whether ZVF in Table 1 is measured *after* replacing $R_i$ with a continuous surrogate reward based on $d_{\text{spec}}$, and write out the exact modified advantage equation that replaces Eq. (1).
- **[CRITICAL] p.4, Section 2.2, Eq. (16) & p.6, Algorithm 1:** `"S_route(y_i) = SiLU(W_low X_i,low + W_high X_i,high)"` — The paper defines $d_{\text{spec}}(y_i, y_j)$ in Eq. (11) and $S_{\text{route}}(y_i)$ in Eq. (16), but neither quantity ever appears in the GRPO loss (Eq. 2) or advantage (Eq. 1), and `S_route` is completely absent from Listing 1. **Fix:** Provide the exact mathematical equations and code showing where $S_{\text{route}}(y_i)$ and $d_{\text{spec}}(y_i, y_j)$ are inserted into the Transformer forward pass and/or the GRPO advantage calculation.
- **[CRITICAL] p.5, Section 2.3 & p.7, Listing 2 (Lines 12–30):** `"theta = torch.atan2(x_rotated[..., j], x_rotated[..., i]) ... x_projected[..., (d - n_noise_dims):] = 0.0"` — Applying `apply_givens_rotation_pair` with $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(x_j, x_i)$ computes $x'_i = x_i \cos\theta + x_j \sin\theta = \sqrt{x_i^2 + x_j^2}$ and $x'_j = -x_i \sin\theta + x_j \cos\theta = 0$. Therefore, coordinate $j = d - 1 - k$ is *already* set to zero by the unitary rotation itself! Zeroing `x_projected[..., (d - n_noise_dims):] = 0.0` at Line 29 does nothing at all, no noise energy is removed, and the operation simply folds the last $N_{\text{noise}}$ coordinates into coordinates $d - 2N_{\text{noise}}, \dots, d - N_{\text{noise}} - 1$. **Fix:** If the intention is to project out a noise subspace, explain how noise vs. signal coordinates are identified (arbitrary fixed indices $d-1$ and $d-2$ do not separate signal from noise) and fix the rotation/projection logic; if energy is genuinely projected out, remove the false claim that the full gate preserves $\|x\|_2$.
- **[CRITICAL] p.8, Section 4.1 & Table 1:** `"All experiments use group size G = 4 and batch size B = 64."` — The manuscript omits the base LLM architecture and size, training corpus, number of training iterations, decoding temperature, max token length $L$, and hardware used. **Fix:** Add a complete implementation details paragraph specifying the exact pretrained model checkpoint, training dataset, hyperparameters ($\beta$, $\epsilon$, learning rate), and compute budget.
- **[MAJOR] p.7, Listing 1 (Lines 36–45):** `"x_eff = x_3d * mask_2d.unsqueeze(-1) ... proj = torch.einsum('bld,ln->bnd', x_eff, basis)"` — In `legendre_spectral_projection`, the Legendre grid `t` is constructed once across `seq_len` (the padded batch length $L$), and padded positions are simply zeroed out via `x_eff` while dividing by `l_eff`. However, in Eq. (6), $t(l) = \frac{2l}{L_i - 1} - 1$ maps each trajectory's *actual* length $L_i$ to $[-1, 1]$. Evaluating $P_n(t(l))$ on the padded grid $[-1, 1]$ means a trajectory of length $L_i < L$ only occupies $[-1, \frac{2L_i}{L-1}-1]$, breaking Legendre orthogonality over $[-1, 1]$ and contradicting Eq. (6). **Fix:** Construct the per-sequence grid $t_i(l) = \frac{2l}{L_i - 1} - 1$ over active tokens $0 \le l < L_i$ before evaluating $P_n(t_i(l))$.
- **[MAJOR] p.8, Section 4.1 & Table 1:** `"Gradient Cosine Fidelity (cos \phi): The cosine similarity between the actual policy update gradient g_impl and the ground-truth reference gradient g_ref on non-starved prompts"` — On non-starved prompts ($s_R > 0$), what is $g_{\text{ref}}$? If $g_{\text{ref}}$ is the standard GRPO gradient, modifying the model architecture by adding SLR and QIG-EA changes the policy parameterization and hidden states, so achieving $\cos \phi = 0.999912$ would imply SLR and QIG-EA have virtually zero effect on the gradient direction. Conversely, if SLR + QIG-EA only alters starved groups, why is $\cos \phi$ defined "on non-starved prompts"? **Fix:** Rigorously define $g_{\text{impl}}$ and $g_{\text{ref}}$, specify which parameter subset is compared, and explain how $\cos \phi$ is computed when group rewards are homogeneous ($g_{\text{GRPO}} = 0$, where cosine similarity with zero is undefined).
- **[MAJOR] p.8, Listing 2 (Lines 39–48):** `"if float(h.mean()) < self.tau_entropy:"` — Taking `h.mean()` collapses the entropy across the entire batch $B$ and sequence length $L$ into a single Python scalar, meaning gating is triggered all-or-nothing per batch rather than per token or per trajectory. Furthermore, when `attention_logits is None` (Line 44), the code runs `prob = F.softmax(x, dim=-1)`, treating hidden state features $x \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times D}$ as attention logits over $D$. **Fix:** Compute and apply the entropy mask per token/head rather than across the batch mean, and remove the invalid fallback that applies `softmax` to hidden representation vectors $x$.
- **[MINOR] p.1, Abstract:** `"establish bounds on gradient starvation probability"` — The abstract claims the paper establishes bounds on gradient starvation probability, but Section 2 contains no such theorem or bound (only the inline expression $f_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ in the introduction and Theorem 1 showing $\nabla_\theta = 0$ when $R_i = c$). **Fix:** Either add the formal probability bound theorem to Section 2 or remove this claim from the abstract.
- **[MINOR] p.9, Table 3:** `"Norm Error || ||x'|| - ||x|| ||"` — The table reports `0.00 x 10^-7` across all rows. Because `eliminate_noise_components` in Listing 2 zeros out coordinate $j$ via `atan2` and rotates its magnitude into $i$, $\|x_{\text{projected}}\|_2 = \|x_{\text{rotated}}\|_2 = \|x\|_2$ holds *only* because the hard projection at Line 29 fails to zero out the rotated energy in coordinate $i$. Thus Table 3 is measuring an implementation bug rather than a meaningful projection invariant. **Fix:** Report the norm before and after actual subspace projection separately.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **How does SLR or QIG-EA reduce ZVF in Table 1?** Given Definition 1 ($\text{ZVF} = \mathbb{P}(R_1 = \dots = R_G)$) and binary rewards on GSM8K/MATH with $G=4$, the theoretical minimum of $\text{ZVF}$ is $12.5\%$ (when $p(x) = 0.5$ for every prompt). How is $\text{ZVF} = 1.1\%$ computed? Are you redefining $R_i$ using $d_{\text{spec}}$, and if so, what is the exact formula?
2. **Why does `eliminate_noise_components` zero out coordinates that `atan2` already set to zero?** In Listing 2 (Lines 23–29), rotating $(x_i, x_j)$ by $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(x_j, x_i)$ sets $x'_i = \sqrt{x_i^2 + x_j^2}$ and $x'_j = 0$. Why does Line 29 then set `x_projected[..., (d - n_noise_dims):] = 0.0` (which are the $j$ indices that are already $0$), leaving the rotated magnitude intact in the $i$ indices?
3. **What base model and training configuration produced Tables 1–3?** Which pretrained LLM was used for the GSM8K and MATH experiments, how were SLR and QIG-EA inserted into the pretrained weights without destroying initialization, and how many RLVR steps were trained?
4. **How is $\cos \phi$ defined and measured?** What is the exact definition of $g_{\text{ref}}$, and why would modifying the network architecture and routing keep the policy gradient within $\cos \phi \ge 0.999844$ of unmodified GRPO while simultaneously changing test accuracy by $+7.9\%$?
