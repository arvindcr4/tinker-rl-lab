### Summary

This manuscript is a 300-page Master of Technology dissertation presented in the form of an extended empirical audit and methodological study of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for language model post-training. Rather than proposing a new state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithm, the work focuses on instrumentation, attribution, and evaluation governance across an eight-study portfolio (P1–P8) and a 14-lane benchmark evaluation campaign (E1–E14), supplemented by a frozen-policy reference diagnostic (C1).

The manuscript organizes its work into four primary contributions:
1. **A recomputable signal-starvation diagnostic and loss-form audit:** It formalizes the Zero-Variance Fraction ($\text{ZVF}$)—the proportion of prompt rollout groups in which all $G$ sampled completions receive identical rewards (all-correct or all-wrong), resulting in zero group-relative advantage—alongside a closed-form independent Bernoulli baseline $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ and a runner-level audit of executed loss formulations across training scripts (Table 4.2).
2. **Stack-conditioned reporting and registry tooling:** Motivated by severe confounding across RL frameworks (e.g., a nominal Qwen3-8B GSM8K comparison between Tinker and TRL yielding $0.856$ vs. $0.050$ last-10 mean training reward due to unpinned base-vs-instruct checkpoint differences and backend defaults), the work introduces an eight-item minimum reporting standard (`MIN-REPORT-RL`), a machine-readable stack registry, and a `stackdiff` tool.
3. **Calibrated negative and null results:** Across multi-seed controlled experiments, the work reports that: (a) no reliable cross-scale scaling law is recovered from GRPO reward curves (P1); (b) $\text{ZVF}$ is mechanically coupled to accuracy and $G$, and its historical predictive correlations with training collapse or held-out performance are invalidated due to mixing synthetic projections with heterogeneous empirical runs (P2); (c) on a saturated 0.5B two-digit addition task, PPO and GRPO ($0.992$ vs. $0.990$, $p = 0.374$), group sizes $G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$, and GRPO vs. Dr. GRPO ($0.987$ vs. $0.992$, $p = 0.35$) show no detectable differences; (d) on an unsaturated Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT rerun (200-token cap), GRPO ($G=8$) outperforms a minimal untuned-critic PPO by $+5.0$ percentage points ($0.245$ vs. $0.195$, paired-$t$ $p = 0.016$, exact sign-flip $p = 0.0625$ over 5 seeds), while $G=8$ vs. $G=2$ ($0.245$ vs. $0.250$) and GRPO vs. Dr. GRPO remain indistinguishable; and (e) a 5-arm, 8-seed variant audit (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, Dr. GRPO, AERO) on Qwen3-8B GSM8K yields no Benjamini–Hochberg significant differences after correction ($p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.84$).
4. **An evaluation-governance protocol:** A single lightly trained checkpoint (`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with a LoRA adapter trained for 40 steps) is evaluated across 14 benchmark suites (E1–E14) using pinned native graders, cryptographic receipts, a strict no-pooling rule between original contracts and replacement scopes, and explicit terminal-state tracking. In paired small-scale evaluations on identical items, the trained actor shows no statistically significant improvement over the base model in any lane.

### Strengths

- **Exceptional epistemic honesty and self-auditing:** In a subfield prone to overclaiming from single-seed runs and confounded baselines, this manuscript stands out for its rigorous self-correction. The author systematically audits prior internal claims, withdraws a historical cross-scale architectural contrast and a mixed-source $\text{ZVF}$ correlation ($\rho \approx 0.27$ over 17 finite cells, 9 of which were synthetic), explicitly flags synthetic dry-run baselines (`variance_mitigation_integration.py`) as unusable as empirical evidence, and overturns an internal "differences disappear" equivalence claim because the exact non-central paired-$t$ minimum detectable effect ($\text{MDE} = 0.010115893$) exceeded the locked $0.010$ equivalence margin.
- **Concrete demonstration of stack-level confounding:** The manuscript provides compelling, code-audited evidence that algorithm labels ("GRPO", "PPO", "DAPO") do not identify an executed training procedure. Table 4.2 is a valuable artifact: by inspecting actual runner code rather than paper prose, it shows that several nominal "GRPO" runners omit importance ratios, clipping, or KL penalties (reducing to REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline), omit $\sigma_g$ division, or mask full sequences rather than completions only. Similarly, demonstrating that "DAPO" yields $\text{ZVF} = 0.00$ on an open trainer with dynamic filtering but $\text{ZVF} = 0.58$ on a managed backend where only asymmetric clipping is exposed cleanly motivates stack-conditioned reporting.
- **Rigorous statistical framing and denominator discipline:** Every headline figure in Table 1.1 is tied to its exact model, task, group size $G$, seed count, evaluation item denominator $n$, and source file. The manuscript carefully distinguishes between item-level pairing (McNemar) and seed-level pairing (paired $t$-test and exact sign-flip test), rightly rejecting pooled item-level $p$-values ($p < 10^{-3}$) when the seed ($n=3$ or $n=5$) is the true unit of replication.
- **Transparent evaluation governance:** The E1–E14 campaign treats benchmark execution failures, quota blocks, and serving bugs (such as missing stop sequences in the E4 BankerToolBench serving bridge) as first-class diagnostic outcomes rather than silently dropping failed suites or pooling incompatible replacement splits.

### Weaknesses

- **Severe experimental flaws in core controlled comparisons:** While the manuscript admirably discloses its experimental limitations after the fact, those limitations remain fatal to the scientific utility of several core experiments:
  - *Task saturation and train–test leakage in Pillar 1 & Pillar 3:* The primary same-stack PPO vs. GRPO comparison, the primary group-size sweep ($G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$), and the arithmetic GRPO vs. Dr. GRPO test were run on Qwen2.5-0.5B two-digit addition, where accuracy is at ceiling ($0.978$–$0.992$) and completions average $4.7$ tokens. Worse, the 200 evaluation items were drawn from the exact same random generator used during training rather than a disjoint test split; because PPO consumed 128 prompts/step while GRPO consumed 16 prompts/step ($\times 8$ rollouts), PPO saw $\sim 40\%$ of its evaluation items during training compared to $\sim 7\%$ for GRPO (p. 28). A ceiling task with unequal train–test contamination cannot adjudicate estimator quality.
  - *Confounded prompt exposure and untuned baseline in the unsaturated rerun:* When rerun on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT (§6.5.5, Table 1.1), the +5.0 point advantage of GRPO ($G=8$) over PPO ($0.245$ vs. $0.195$) is confounded by both an untuned linear value head on the prompt-end hidden state (no GAE, one-step MSE) and an $8\times$ difference in unique prompt exposure per step (64 generations/step fixed: 8 prompts $\times 8$ for GRPO vs. 64 prompts $\times 1$ for PPO).
  - *Artificial 200-token truncation in the length-bias study (P4):* Testing Dr. GRPO's length-bias mitigation on GSM8K-CoT under a hard 200-token generation cap (where mean completion lengths are already $180$–$195$ tokens, bumping directly against the cap) prevents length inflation by construction. A null result under a 200-token ceiling says very little about whether Dr. GRPO mitigates verbosity in standard uncapped reasoning regimes.
- **Incomplete execution and missing provenance across the portfolio:** Large portions of the proposed system and experimental matrix are either unexecuted placeholders or lack recoverable source code:
  - In the multi-framework harness (O1, FR-2), only 2 of the 7 launcher paths (Tinker and TRL) completed runs; veRL and OpenRLHF are dry-run placeholders, and the single Tinker vs. TRL run used different base models (`Qwen3-8B-Base` vs. `Qwen3-8B` Instruct) and unrecoverable managed configurations (p. 25, p. 68).
  - In Table 4.2, the execution sources and exact loss forms for the E1–E14 campaign actor, the P1 frontier anchors, the P3 Qwen3-8B matched-budget panel, and the Tinker side of the framework comparison are listed as "not recoverable" or "unknown".
  - In P7 (O6), the adaptive group-size controller's decisive matched-token bakeoff against static $G=16$ remains unrun (p. 35, p. 47).
  - In the E1–E14 campaign (O7), the evaluated actor was trained for only 40 steps with unrecoverable training code and unverified content decontamination, does not outperform the base model on any tested subset, and left 7 of 14 original benchmark contracts externally blocked.
- **Limited technical novelty of the positive contributions:** The central mechanism—that within-group reward standardization yields zero advantage when all completions in a group achieve identical binary rewards, and that this occurs with probability $p^G + (1-p)^G$ under i.i.d. Bernoulli draws—is an elementary algebraic property of GRPO already targeted by prior work (e.g., DAPO's dynamic sampling, GRESO, RL-ZVP, AERO, and Dr. GRPO, all cited in §2.3). Because the manuscript's own empirical audits invalidate $\text{ZVF}$'s predictive correlation with training collapse and leave the adaptive controller's benefit unestablished, the manuscript's durable contribution reduces primarily to an engineering cautionary tale and a documentation schema.
- **Format and presentation:** The manuscript is written and structured as an institutional degree dissertation and forensic repository ledger (complete with institutional declarations, Semester-3 vs. Semester-4 commit boundary accounting, internal file path maps, and software requirements tables FR-1–FR-16 / TC-1–TC-10) rather than a cohesive machine learning research paper. Furthermore, the manuscript copy ends mid-sentence on page 89 (`"becaus"`), omitting the remainder of Chapter 4 and Chapters 5–9.

### Anchored comments

- `[CRITICAL] p.28: "the 200 evaluation prompts are fresh random draws from the same generator used in training rather than a disjoint split; the two arms of a seed share only 3–7 of their 200 evaluation items, and PPO had drawn about 40% of its evaluation items during training against about 7% for GRPO"` - The primary Pillar 1 same-stack PPO vs. GRPO experiment suffers from severe, asymmetric train-on-test leakage in addition to a ceiling effect ($0.992$ vs. $0.990$). Because the evaluation sets are neither disjoint from training nor shared across the paired arms within a seed, this experiment cannot support even a bounded null contrast between estimators. **Concrete fix:** Remove the Qwen2.5-0.5B addition run from Pillar 1's headline claims (demote it to an appendix note on experimental pitfall discovery) and re-run the same-stack comparison on a strictly disjoint, identical held-out evaluation split with matched unique prompt exposure per step.
- `[CRITICAL] p.65: "the historical actor execution source is unrecoverable, so its preset is not proof of its executed loss or mask... “not recoverable” means the repository does not hold the code or configuration that ran."` - A core thesis of the manuscript is stack-conditioned attribution and cryptographic provenance (FR-8, NFR-9), yet Table 4.2 reveals that the exact training code, loss form, token mask, and KL configuration for the flagship E1–E14 campaign actor, the P1 cross-scale anchors, and the Tinker framework comparison run were lost or unrecorded. **Concrete fix:** Do not claim that the E1–E14 campaign or P1 roster satisfies NFR-9 ("Lost-source auditability") in Table 3.3/3.4; explicitly mark NFR-9 as *Not Met* for historical runs and restrict reproducibility claims strictly to the post-recovery scripts where code, config, and output hashes are jointly bound.
- `[MAJOR] p.39: "Same-stack GRPO vs PPO, unsaturated post-training accuracy ... Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GSM8K-CoT, 200-token cap, 64 generations per step, 30 steps"` - Combining a fixed budget of 64 generations per step (8 prompts $\times 8$ completions for GRPO vs. 64 prompts $\times 1$ completion for PPO, or 32 prompts $\times 2$ for $G=2$) with a 30-step horizon means PPO sees $8\times$ as many unique prompts as GRPO ($G=8$), while simultaneously using a minimal one-step value head on the prompt-end hidden state without GAE (Table 4.2, p. 67). Furthermore, a 200-token cap truncates standard GSM8K chain-of-thought reasoning. **Concrete fix:** To isolate the advantage estimator on GSM8K-CoT, raise the generation cap to at least 512–1024 tokens, include a separate control matching unique prompts per step (or run both to convergence under a matched total token budget), and use a standard token-level value critic with GAE for PPO.
- `[MAJOR] p.48: "In a second regime — Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on GSM8K with a 200-token chain-of-thought cap and 3 seeds — GRPO moved from 20.2% to 26.3% and Dr. GRPO from 20.5% to 25.5% ... Neither arm inflated length over 30 steps (GRPO 195 → 180 tokens; Dr. GRPO 194 → 187)."` - Evaluating length bias when the pre-training mean completion length is already 194–195 tokens under a hard 200-token maximum generation cap makes positive length inflation impossible to observe. **Concrete fix:** Do not frame the 200-token capped experiment as a valid test of Dr. GRPO's length-bias hypothesis; promote the uncapped or 1,024-token raised-cap extension (listed in Table 4.2, p. 67) to the primary text and report its exact multi-seed length trajectories and held-out accuracy intervals.
- `[MAJOR] p.63: "where \widehat{\text{Var}} is the unbiased sample variance and \varepsilon = 10^{-6} for rewards normalised to [0, 1]."` - There is a persistent inconsistency across the manuscript between population variance ($1/G$, `ddof=0`) and unbiased sample variance ($1/(G-1)$, `ddof=1`), as well as between exact ties ($\text{ZVF}_0$) and tolerance-thresholded variance ($\text{ZVF}_\varepsilon$). Table 4.2 (p. 66) notes that P2's recomputed tensors ($0.130 / 0.190 / 0.155$) used population variance, whereas the reference implementation on p. 63 specifies `ddof=1`. While identical for exact binary ties, they diverge under reward shaping, length penalties, or jitter, and `ddof=0` vs. `ddof=1` also scales the advantage $A_i = (r_i - \mu_g)/(\sigma_g + \varepsilon)$ by $\sqrt{(G-1)/G}$ (a $\sim 29\%$ reduction at $G=2$). **Concrete fix:** Standardize on one variance convention across all definitions, figures (e.g., Figure 1.1 vs. Figure 4.4), and code paths, and explicitly report whether the $\sqrt{(G-1)/G}$ advantage scaling difference in $G$-sweeps was absorbed by the learning rate or affected update magnitudes.

### Questions for the authors

1. **Disentangling prompt diversity from the critic in the unsaturated GSM8K-CoT rerun (§6.5.5, Table 1.1):** In the Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT experiment where GRPO ($G=8$) achieves $0.245$ vs. PPO's $0.195$ ($p = 0.016$ paired-$t$, $p = 0.0625$ sign-flip), PPO was implemented with a linear value head on the prompt-end hidden state (one-step MSE, no GAE) and sampled 64 unique prompts $\times 1$ completion per step, whereas GRPO sampled 8 unique prompts $\times 8$ completions per step. Since GRPO ($G=2$, 32 prompts $\times 2$) achieved $0.250$, why did PPO fail to improve over the pre-training baseline ($\sim 0.20$)? Did you test whether PPO's stagnation was caused by poor value-head initialization/critic variance or by the sequence-level ratio clipping on single samples?
2. **Raised-cap results for GRPO vs. Dr. GRPO (Table 4.2, p. 67):** Table 4.2 lists a "P4 Qwen3-8B raised-cap extension (1,024 tokens)" using `T/live_zvf_probe.py (--loss drgrpo)` over 30 steps with $G=8$, which also uses a full-sequence token mask rather than a completion-only mask. What were the held-out accuracy and completion-length trajectories for this 1,024-token run, and how did the full-sequence token mask affect Dr. GRPO's length dynamics compared to the completion-only mask?
3. **Evaluation governance and the 40-step training budget in E1–E14 (Chapter 8, Table 8.B):** Why was the flagship E1–E14 evaluation actor (`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` + LoRA) trained for only 40 steps at $G=4$ before launching a 14-benchmark evaluation campaign? Given that 40 steps at $G=4$ was insufficient to produce a statistically detectable gain over the base model on any lane in Table 8.B, was the 40-step limit dictated by compute/API cost constraints, or was the campaign designed strictly to test the evaluation harness rather than post-training efficacy?
4. **C1 frozen-policy diagnostic interpretation (p. 7, p. 34, Table 7.2):** In the C1 study on GSM8K train, 64 questions that initially scored $0/8$ under `parser-v2` received 24 fresh rollouts, yielding 11/64 ($17.19\%$) parser-positive events, of which only 3/64 are corroborated as unambiguous numerical witnesses after accounting for reference errors, unit mismatches, and formatting. How should practitioners incorporate this finding into online RLVR training—specifically, does filtering out "all-wrong" groups (as in DAPO or GRESO) disproportionately discard prompts with defective reference keys, or does it discard genuinely solvable high-difficulty prompts?
