## Summary

This excerpt (PDF pp. 155–199; internal thesis pp. 135–179) covers the conclusion of the P7 adaptive group-size controller audit (§7.3 continuation, PDF pp. 155–158), the Historical V2 and C1 frozen-policy fresh-rollout diagnostics on GSM8K (§7.4–§7.5, PDF pp. 159–163), the E1–E14 multi-suite evaluation campaign of a single Tinker-trained actor (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with a LoRA adapter trained for 40 GRPO steps; Chapter 8, PDF pp. 164–187), and the cross-study synthesis and threats-to-validity analysis (Chapter 9, §9.1–§9.2, PDF pp. 189–199).

The thesis makes a methodological and auditing contribution rather than an algorithmic capability claim. In Chapter 7, the author shows that point-estimate plug-in rules fail to rescue homogeneous reward groups ($\hat{p} \in \{0,1\}$ yields zero estimated rescue), that 92.3% (1,723/1,867) of symmetric ZVF escalations fire on all-correct groups, and that on fresh rollouts of `Qwen/Qwen3-8B` (§7.4), 33/461 (7.16%) initially homogeneous groups become mixed over 24 extra draws—but 20/33 are strict-parser format failures and only 13/461 (2.82%) are genuine numeric changes (2 wrong-to-correct recoveries, 11 correct-to-wrong changes). In §7.5 (C1), across 64 all-wrong screening questions from GSM8K, 11/64 (17.19%) trigger primary parser recovery events, but semantic reconciliation corroborates only 3/64 (4.69%) genuine numeric recoveries alongside 16/64 clear wrong reference final values and 19/64 materially ambiguous questions. In Chapter 8, the E1–E14 campaign evaluates a single checkpoint trained with only 40 steps of GRPO (80 prompt draws, 320 completions total, with zero loss on 35/40 steps). Across 14 suites, the work documents pervasive harness, truncation, and serving defects (e.g., 593/713 corrupt `git apply` patches in E1 SWE-bench Pro; a Modal OpenAI bridge omitting stop sequences in E2/E4/E5/E7/E9; 64.0% unparsed outputs in E8 LAB-Bench; 48.1% extraction failures in E11 VerilogEval; 67.3% length-capped responses in E14 Omni-MATH). A small-scale paired rerun on a unified vLLM engine (Table 8.B) finds no statistically significant difference between the trained actor and the untrained base model on any of the 14 lanes.

## Strengths

- **Exemplary epistemic honesty and negative-result reporting:** The manuscript refuses to overclaim. Every null result, broken script, confounded protocol change, and missing artifact is disclosed directly in the text and tables (e.g., explicitly retracting four internal "DECISIVE" grades in §9.1 on PDF p. 190 after applying exact non-parametric floors and Holm family-wise error corrections).
- **Separation of parser artifacts from semantic reasoning:** Sections 7.4 and 7.5 provide a rigorous decomposition of reward variance on GSM8K. Demonstrating that 20 of 33 reward-flipping groups in Historical V2 are format-induced parser failures, and that 8 of 11 C1 recovery events reflect reference errors, unit ambiguities, or convention disputes rather than mathematical recovery, is a concrete contribution to RLVR evaluation hygiene.
- **Forensic systems auditing in agentic benchmarks:** Chapter 8 identifies concrete failure modes that routine benchmark reporting conceals: patch hunk-header corruption (`git apply` vs. `git apply --recount` rescuing 591 of 593 rejected patches in E1, PDF p. 177), bridge-level stop-token omission causing multi-turn role degeneration in E4 (PDF p. 181–182), reasoning-block draft leakage in E11 (PDF p. 176), and engine-level numerical divergence matching the magnitude of the LoRA adapter update (§8.7, PDF p. 183).
- **Strict non-pooling governance:** The discipline of separating original contracts, replacement scopes (27 September override runs), and small-scale controlled reruns (26 September paired runs), alongside explicit terminal-state taxonomies (Figures 8.1 and 8.6), prevents meaningless cross-suite aggregation.

## Weaknesses

- **Critically under-trained campaign actor invalidates post-training evaluation:** The E1–E14 campaign evaluates an adapter trained for 40 steps at $G=4$ and batch size 2 (80 prompt draws, 320 total completions), where reward was zero on 22/40 steps and **loss was zero on 35/40 steps** (Table 8.1, PDF p. 169). Evaluating an actor that received only 5 non-zero gradient updates across 14 heavy agentic benchmarks cannot test GRPO post-training efficacy; as Table 8.B confirms, the adapter is statistically indistinguishable from the base model everywhere.
- **Pervasive artifact loss and unverifiable provenance in the frozen checkout:** Central claims in Chapter 8 rely on files absent from the frozen repository—including the dirty training source diff, the exact 80 sampled training prompts, the deployed bridge script (`sha256 f59c08b9...`), the raw E4 100-trial receipt, and six core receipts only recovered via a "withheld source archive" on 3 October 2026 (PDF p. 166).
- **Chronological and ledger contradictions:** In §8.5 (PDF p. 181–182), the text and Figure 8.5 report a 100-trial base-model E4 rerun dated 2026-09-21, whereas the recovered `Pending_Experiments.md` snapshot through 21 September explicitly states the rerun was *not launched*. Furthermore, self-issued protocol overrides on 27 September (§8.4, PDF p. 178) bypassed the fail-closed governance framework that Chapter 8 claims to exemplify.
- **Confounded small-scale reruns and judge self-preference bias:** In Table 8.B (PDF p. 185–186), E12 (AppBench) uses the base model itself (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B`) as the LLM judge to compare the trained actor against the base model, introducing direct self-evaluation bias. In E10 (AgentHarm), Table 8.B uses the identical label `30 harmful, 30 benign` across three different metric rows without specifying which subset forms the denominator of each metric.
- **Broken scripts retained in the narrative:** Section 7.3 (PDF p. 157) devotes a paragraph to two "N10" scripts that inverted the Bayesian firing inequality ($m < \tau$ instead of $m > \tau$), computed fractional pseudo-successes from aggregate step rewards, and inverted the ZVF triage branch ($\text{ZVF} < \tau$). Reporting buggy script outputs in the main thesis text adds confusion rather than scientific insight.

## Questions for the Authors

1. **Rationale for evaluating a 40-step, 35-zero-loss-step checkpoint (Chapter 8, Table 8.1, p. 169):** Why was the massive 14-suite evaluation campaign executed on a checkpoint that experienced only 5 active gradient steps (320 total training completions) rather than a checkpoint trained to convergence or at least non-trivial KL/reward progression?
2. **Resolution of the E4 21 September chronology conflict (§8.5, pp. 181–182):** How could the 100-trial BankerToolBench base-model rerun be dated `2026-09-21` in the earlier graphic when the recovered `Pending_Experiments.md` snapshot updated through 21 September explicitly states the rerun was `NOT launched`? What is the exact filesystem creation timestamp of `outputs/e1_e14_small_scale_2026-09-26/E4/result.json` (`prior_base_run`)?
3. **Mechanics of the `zvf-triage` vs. Dualformer-Auto efficiency metric (§7.3, p. 156):** How is "contrast restored per 1,000 extra rollouts" (84.00 units for Dualformer-Auto) defined when Dualformer-Auto is a shrink-only de-escalation rule with a net cost ratio of $0.657$ (i.e., negative extra rollouts relative to static $G=8$)?
4. **Hunk-header repair in E1 SWE-bench Pro (§8.3, p. 177):** Since 593 of 713 patches failed plain `git apply` due to line-count headers but 711 of 713 succeeded with `git apply --recount`, did you run the SWE-bench Pro evaluation harness on those 591 recovered patches to determine whether the model's underlying code edits were semantically valid?
5. **Jev (TypeSafe) judgment harness degradation (§9.2, p. 199):** What caused the Jev blocker-classification harness to lose reliability at `13:10Z on 2026-09-19` (where the controlled yes-probe fell from $\sim 1.0$ to $0.3$), and was that caused by an external API model update or a local bridge/prompt regression?
