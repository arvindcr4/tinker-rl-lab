## Summary

This excerpt covers the concluding synthesis (§9.3–§9.4, PDF pp. 200–205), the complete bibliography ([1]–[88], PDF pp. 206–214), Appendix A on the historical run registry and coverage reconciliation (PDF pp. 216–231), and the opening sections of Appendix B on reproducibility and provenance (§B.1–§B.4, PDF pp. 232–234, corresponding to thesis pp. 180–214).

The authors summarize four research questions (RQ1–RQ4) around GRPO-style post-training of large language models:
1. **RQ1 (Zero-Variance Fraction, ZVF):** Recomputable on three stored Qwen3-8B tensors (`0.130`, `0.190`, `0.155`), with regime-dependent means (`0.16` to `0.84`), but without established predictive validity because historical pooled correlations ($\rho = 0.27$, $n = 23$ reported, 17 finite, 9 synthetic) mix synthetic and measured rows and non-comparable outcomes.
2. **RQ2 (Algorithm comparisons on a fixed stack):** Saturated at ceiling (`0.98–0.99`) on 0.5B arithmetic. An unsaturated Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K-CoT rerun (5 seeds, 30 steps) finds GRPO at $G = 8$ exceeds an untuned learned-critic PPO baseline by `+0.050 [+0.015, +0.085]` ($p = 0.016$ paired-$t$; $p = 0.0625$ exact sign-flip), while $G = 8$ minus $G = 2$ is `-0.005 [-0.038, +0.028]`. An 8-seed matched-stack block (P11, 5 arms $\times$ 8 seeds on Qwen3-8B, 30 steps) finds DAPO minus GRPO is $\Delta = +0.001$ despite DAPO reducing ZVF from `0.693` to `0.000` at $3.61\times$ rollout cost.
3. **RQ3 (Manifest comparison):** Assigns heuristic divergence code `R5` to an open-versus-closed DAPO pair without calibrated outcome prediction.
4. **RQ4 (E1–E14 evaluation campaign):** Evaluates a single 40-step checkpoint (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` + adapter) across 14 lanes, yielding receipt-bound scores on three strictly complete scopes and explicit closure states (`CLOSED_EXTERNAL`, `CLOSED_PARTIAL`), but inconclusive paired base-model deltas across all lanes.

Appendices A and B audit the empirical provenance of the thesis, exposing seeded dry-run fallbacks (`verl-dryrun`, `openrlhf-dryrun`), non-LLM gym runs (`sb3_ppo_math`, `cleanrl_ppo_math`, `tianshou_ppo_math`), model-identity conflicts (`P4-C1`, `P5-C1`), zero-step sampling cells (`P3-C2`), unexecuted architectures (`P8-C3`), and broken verification scripts (`verify_results.py`, `stats.py`).

## Strengths

- **Uncompromising self-audit and epistemic honesty:** Appendix A and §9.3 explicitly document every methodological flaw in the historical campaign rather than hiding negative or invalid runs. Table A.5 classifies all 18 headline claims from the Semester-4 P1–P8 series and shows that zero empirical claims meet Tier A or Tier B standards (`C = 12, R = 2, X = 4`).
- **Clear separation of evidence units and anti-pooling discipline:** The manuscript enforces a strict "never-pool" rule between original-contract and replacement-scope benchmarks (§9.3, §9.4, Table A.8) and separates provider ledger entries, seeded dry-run fallbacks, zero-step probes, and actual training runs (§A.5).
- **Mechanistic decoupling of ZVF reduction from accuracy gain:** The P11 8-seed audit (p. 223) provides a clean empirical demonstration that driving ZVF from `0.693` (GRPO) to `0.000` (DAPO) via $3.61\times$ more rollouts (`1,734` vs. `480`) yields zero detectable held-out gain ($\Delta = +0.001$, 95% paired-$t$ CI `[-0.0063, +0.0083]`).
- **Concrete failure analysis of reproducibility tooling:** Appendix B (§B.3–§B.4) documents specific bugs in the evaluation and reproduction scripts—such as `verify_results.py` previously parsing a non-existent log format and exiting `0` on empty matches, and still checking all seeds against the seed-42 baseline (`0.344 / 0.625`)—giving reviewers an accurate picture of what works and what fails.

## Weaknesses

- **Severe provenance and execution defects across the underlying experimental corpus:** While the post-hoc audit is exemplary, the underlying experiments suffer from fundamental execution flaws:
  1. Two of the four rows in the cross-framework benchmark (Table A.2, p. 217–218) are deterministic dummy fallbacks (`verl-dryrun`, `openrlhf-dryrun`) generated when the packages were missing from the sandbox, and the remaining two rows (`Tinker` vs. `TRL`) use different base checkpoints (`Qwen3-8B-Base` vs. `Qwen3-8B`).
  2. Fifteen of the 20 runs in the Modal campaign (Table A.3, p. 218–219) use classical RL libraries (`Stable-Baselines3`, `CleanRL`, `Tianshou`) for `~100k env. steps` in `1.67–32.4 s` with no LLM recorded and `0.002–0.014` accuracy.
  3. All Semester-4 opening runs (Table A.6, p. 224–226) and P1–P8 runs (Table A.5, p. 220–222) run for only `5–30` steps, often with a single seed or tiny held-out sets ($n = 8\text{--}30$).
  4. The E1–E14 actor (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` + adapter, p. 227) was trained for only 40 steps, and its exact loss, masking, and KL path cannot be established due to a "missing dirty source binding."
- **Systematic bibliography corruption and unverifiable/future-dated citations:** The References section ([1]–[88], pp. 206–214) contains multiple misattributed author lists, fabricated or future-dated 2025–2026 arXiv identifiers, and incorrect venue metadata. Well-known 2024–2025 papers (`VAPO` [24], `CPPO` [26], `LIMR` [86], `PRMBench` [77], `Evaluating Mathematical Reasoning Beyond Accuracy` [79], `Scaling Behaviors of LLM RL Post-Training` [72]) are assigned wrong author teams or wrong titles, and entries [32], [35], [37], [38], [39] cite `arXiv:2601.*`–`2605.*` preprints alongside impossible venue combinations (e.g., [28] and [31] in `ICLR 2026` with `arXiv:2510.*` and `2509.*`).
- **Unverifiable software and model versions in Appendix B:** Section B.2 (p. 233) claims a frozen runtime validated `106/106` with `transformers==5.5.4`, `datasets==4.8.4`, `accelerate==1.13.0`, and `peft==0.19.1` on `cuda:12.4.1`, while §A.3–§A.4 cite models such as `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B`, `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B`, and `nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-BF16`. Several of these package versions and model identifiers do not match standard public releases or conflict with the `cuda:12.4.1` base image (`torch==2.7.1` targets CUDA 12.6/12.8 by default).
- **Redacted operational identifiers and missing primary receipts prevent independent verification:** Throughout Appendix A (`Table A.5`, `§A.4`, `§A.5`), run IDs, adapter commits, and campaign ledgers are replaced with `[operational identifier withheld]` or `withheld-adapter-revision…`, and key receipts (e.g., the E4 base-model rerun receipt and the E1–E14 actor dirty source tree) are unrecovered.
