# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper investigates the role of group size $G$ in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), framing $G$ as a preference-density dial rather than a pure Monte Carlo variance-reduction knob. As part of the broader `TINKERRL-BENCH` benchmark, the authors combine a measured small-scale sweep ($G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ on `Qwen2.5-0.5B` for two-operand arithmetic), an illustrative token-budget-normalized reanalysis ($G \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ across budgets $T \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}\text{M}$ on `Qwen3-8B` / GSM8K), and theoretical syntheses linking group-centered updates to all-pairs Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and examining PPO critic behavior under outcome rewards. The study argues that while small group sizes ($G=2$) match larger groups ($G=16$) on easy, near-ceiling tasks, the advantage signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scales sublinearly at roughly $52\%$ of the $\sqrt{G}$ ideal, and the reconstructed token-budget grid hypothesizes a shift toward larger group sizes ($G \approx 32$) at canonical training budgets.

## Strengths
* The paper addresses a highly practical and timely question in LLM reinforcement learning post-training: how to choose the group rollout size $G$ in GRPO to balance rollout generation compute against zero-variance batch collapse and gradient quality.
* The authors maintain commendable transparency regarding the evidence boundaries of their study, repeatedly distinguishing between directly measured multi-seed runs and unmeasured, reconstructed point estimates used for hypothesis generation.
* The empirical diagnostic tracking—connecting logged zero-variance fractions (ZVF), advantage variance SNR scaling, and Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) to reconcile fixed-step versus fixed-token-budget comparisons—offers a useful diagnostic lens for RLVR practitioners.
* Section 6.10 provides an honest report of negative results across popular GRPO heuristics at small scale, reinforcing the value of cost-matched, multi-seed verification disciplines in benchmark design.

## Weaknesses
* The central claim that the optimal group size shifts toward $G=32$ at canonical token budgets ($T \ge 16\text{M}$) relies entirely on unmeasured, reconstructed fallback estimates (`FALLBACK_ROWS`) rather than directly trained matched-budget runs, while the only directly measured multi-seed sweep ($G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$) is conducted on a near-ceiling two-operand arithmetic toy task ($\sim 98\text{--}99\%$ accuracy); evaluating directly on standard unsaturated mathematical reasoning benchmarks in the low-pass-rate regime—such as MATH-500 or AIME 2024—and situating the findings against established empirical iso-compute scaling laws would substantially strengthen the paper's empirical foundation (Reference Paper: It Takes Two: Your GRPO Is Secretly DPO, Yihong Wu et al., 2025; Reference Paper: IsoCompute Playbook: Optimally Scaling Sampling Compute for LLM RL, Zhoujun Cheng et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Scaling Behaviors of LLM Reinforcement Learning Post-Training: An Empirical Study in Mathematical Reasoning, Zelin Tan et al., 2025).
* The theoretical formulations in Section 6.7—characterizing GRPO's group-centered update as an endogenous all-pairs DPO contrast and its $1/\sigma_r$ standard-deviation whitening as an adaptive-margin curriculum—largely repackage known U-statistic identities and difficulty-bias derivations from prior RLVR literature and external LLM prompting, and the paper lacks empirical comparisons against unwhitened, batch-normalized, or dynamic zero-variance-filtering baselines across the group-size sweep to isolate the effect of $1/\sigma_r$ whitening from leave-one-out variance and ZVF collapse (Reference Paper: Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective, Zichen Liu et al., 2025; Reference Paper: What is the Alignment Objective of GRPO?, Milan Vojnovic and Se-Young Yun, 2025; Reference Paper: Back to Basics: Revisiting REINFORCE Style Optimization for Learning from Human Feedback in LLMs, Arash Ahmadian et al., 2024; Reference Paper: DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale, Qiying Yu et al., 2025; Reference Paper: REINFORCE++: A Simple and Efficient Approach for Aligning Large Language Models, Jian Hu et al., 2025).
* The "Critic-Degeneracy Hypothesis" in Section 6.8—claiming that PPO's value head is fundamentally a noise-amplifying re-parameterization of GRPO's stateless group-mean baseline—is tested only over 40 steps on a 0.5B arithmetic task with an unconverged critic, overlooking prior work that already documented standard PPO critic failures on reasoning tasks as well as recent value-based RLVR methods showing that properly pretrained and GAE-calibrated critics outperform critic-free group baselines on hard reasoning benchmarks (Reference Paper: VinePPO: Refining Credit Assignment in RL Training of LLMs, Amirhossein Kazemnejad et al., 2024; Reference Paper: VAPO: Efficient and Reliable Reinforcement Learning for Advanced Reasoning Tasks, Yu Yue et al., 2025).
* The quantitative analyses contain several mathematical and statistical inconsistencies—including conflicting exponential decay parameters ($\hat{\tau} = 12.1\text{M}$ vs. $\tau = 5.76\text{M}$), underpowered $n=3$ seed sweeps that fall short of the paper's own power requirements ($d_{\min} = 2.024$ at $n=5$) alongside implausible $p < 10^{-9}$ TOST values at 2 degrees of freedom, and a theoretical misattribution in Table 9 that conflates prompt-difficulty clustering with negative within-group correlation while comparing trajectory-averaged training ZVF against terminal held-out accuracy.
* The manuscript exhibits structural and editorial artifacts from multi-iteration script aggregation, including empty subsection headers (Sections 2.1 and 2.4), unresolved references to external files and companion pillars, redundant figures and tables across Sections 5–6, duplicated unreferenced block diagrams (Figures 8–17), and an unintegrated template audit section (Section 9) whose rules contradict the main text.

1. **Unreported "505-Task Conditional Utility Audit" ($G=4$ vs. $G=5$) in the Abstract and Introduction:**
   - **Potential Concern:** Both the Abstract (page 1) and the "Evidence boundary for $G=32$" paragraph in Section 1 (page 2) highlight a specific quantitative result: *"A separate 505-task conditional utility audit, using $(1 - \text{ZVF})/\sqrt{G}$ as the declared cost proxy, selects $G=4$ over $G=5$ by $0.00177$ with a 95% bootstrap interval $[0.00082, 0.00270]$; that result is cohort- and objective-conditional, not a universal optimum."* However, this 505-task audit, the definition of its 505-task cohort, the $(1 - \text{ZVF})/\sqrt{G}$ cost proxy, and the evaluation of $G=5$ are never presented, defined, or analyzed anywhere in the body of the manuscript (aside from unreferenced placeholder flowcharts in Figures 11 and 15). 4. **Statistical Methodology Discrepancies in Power Analysis, Multiple Testing, and ANOVA (Section 3.3, Pages 5–7):**
   - **Potential Concern (Pooled Student's $t$-test vs. Welch's $t$-test and Column 4 Values in Power Analysis, Table 4):** Section 3.3 (page 5) states that `statsmodels.stats.power.TTestIndPower` was used to compute statistical power and minimum detectable effect sizes (MDE) for a *"two-sample Welch $t$-test"* in Table 4 (page 6). In `statsmodels`, `TTestIndPower` computes power for a standard pooled-variance Student's $t$-test ($\text{df} = n_1 + n_2 - 2$), which assumes equal variances rather than Welch's unequal-variance $t$-test. Under unequal sample sizes ($n_1 = 5, n_2 = 15$ in Column 4), Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom depend on the variance ratio and can drop as low as $\text{df} \approx 4$ when variance is concentrated in the smaller $n_1 = 5$ group (or $\approx 6.62$ under equal variances), increasing $d_{\min}$ above the pooled-variance value of $1.530$. Furthermore, while the footer $\text{MDE } d = 1.530$ matches `TTestIndPower` with $n_1 = 5, n_2 = 15$ (`nobs1=5, ratio=3.0`, $\text{df}=18$), the achieved power values printed in Column 4 of Table 4 ($0.066, 0.151, 0.311, 0.450, 0.594, 0.784, 0.955$) do not match $n_1 = 5, n_2 = 15$ (which yields $0.065, 0.148, 0.302, 0.436, 0.577, 0.768, 0.948$); instead, they correspond to $\text{df} \approx 28$. Finally, pooling 3 different Classic-RL libraries ($5\text{ seeds} \times 3 = 15$) into a single $n_2 = 15$ reference group violates the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption of a two-sample $t$-test given that library/framework accounts for $\eta^2 = 0.546$ of performance variance (page 6).
   - **Potential Concern (Mathematically Undefined $n_1 = n_2 = 1$ Tests and Planned Family Size in Table 5):**

Because a two-sample $t$-test with $n_1=n_2=1$ has zero degrees of freedom and yields `NaN`, printing numerical $p$-values without explaining the underlying unit of observation (e.g., whether evaluation prompts or training steps within a single seed were treated as independent replicates via pseudoreplication) is confusing.
     - On page 5, Section 3.3 states that Bonferroni ($\alpha' = \alpha/k = 0.0100$) and Benjamini–Hochberg corrections are applied *"across 5 planned tests,"* and defines superscript markers $^*$ and $^\dagger$ for surviving tests. However, those 5 planned tests (or the restricted Tier-A/B family where *"only $F_3$ survives"*) are never explicitly listed in Section 3.3, and the markers $^*$ and $^\dagger$ are never used (Table 5 instead uses $\checkmark$, $\times$, and an undefined $\ddagger$ symbol).
   - **Potential Concern (Confounded One-Way ANOVAs in Variance Decomposition, Pages 6–7):** At the bottom of page 6, the text reports separate one-way ANOVAs across 42 experiments yielding $\eta^2 = 0.546$ for library/framework, $\eta^2 = 0.558$ for training algorithm, $\eta^2 = 0.471$ for model family, and $\eta^2 = 0.322$ for model scale. Because these four marginal $\eta^2$ values sum to $1.897 > 1.0$, they derive from separate marginal one-way ANOVAs on an unbalanced, non-orthogonal observational set where factors are heavily confounded (e.g., Table 2 shows CQL is only in `d3rlpy`, DPO/SFT are only in `TRL`, classic RL libraries only run PPO, and frontier models are only run on `Tinker`). Separate marginal one-way ANOVAs share overlapping variance across confounded factors and cannot isolate each factor's independent contribution without a multi-factor ANOVA (e.g., Type II/III sums of squares) or linear mixed-effects model.

### [Group Size Empirical Sweeps, Token-Budget Reconstructions, and Iterative Syntheses] (Pages: 7-17)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Methodological Validity of Inferential Statistics, Parametric Curve Fitting, and Extrapolations on Unmeasured Fallback Grids (Sections 5, 6.3, 6.5.1, and 6.6; Tables 6, 7, 10, 12, 13; Figures 4–7)**
   - **Potential Concern:** A major portion of the empirical analysis in this segment relies on an "illustrative reanalysis" for Qwen3-8B / GSM8K across group sizes $G \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ and token budgets $T \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}\text{M}$. As candidly disclosed in the caption of Table 10 (p. 12), these 20 accuracy cells are *"reconstructed point estimates from existing GRPO ablation logs (the `FALLBACK_ROWS` table in the script), not freshly measured per-seed runs; per-cell seed counts are not retained and no measured $G \times T$ sweep over $\{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ was executed."* While the text transparently caveats that these values are hypotheses rather than direct measurements, Sections 5, 6.5.1, and 6.6 build a multi-stage statistical and inferential pipeline on top of these static fallback values:
     - **Bootstrap Confidence Intervals Without Seed-Level Observations:** Table 7 (p. 9), Table 12 (p. 14), Table 13 (p. 14), and Figures 4, 6a, and 7C report 95% confidence intervals on $\Delta$ (uniform half-width of $\pm 0.06$ across rows in Table 7), conditional bootstrap 95% intervals on $\text{acc}(G^\star)$ (uniform half-width of $\pm 0.03$ across rows in Table 12), and paired-bootstrap crossover thresholds $T_{\text{cross}}$. Because per-cell seed counts and trial-level observations are not retained in `FALLBACK_ROWS`, bootstrap resampling cannot validly quantify experimental or optimization variance without injecting synthetic noise assumptions. Furthermore, the exact mathematical formula or interpolation procedure used to construct `FALLBACK_ROWS` from underlying logs is never stated.
     - **Over-Parameterized Curve Fitting and Out-of-Range Extrapolation:** Section 6.5.1 (p. 13) fits a two-parameter saturating exponential $|\Delta(T)| = a \cdot (1 - e^{-T/\tau})$ to the 4 reconstructed $G=4$ vs. $G=32$ budget points to estimate a continuous breaking threshold $T_{\text{crit}}^{5\%} = 2.21\text{M}$ with a bootstrap 95% CI of $[1.29\text{M}, 4.77\text{M}]$, and solves for diversity amplification bounds ($\delta_{\text{div}}$, p. 14). 2. **Theoretical Contradiction in the Within-Group Correlation Claim and Metric Conditioning Mismatch in Table 9 (Sections 5 and 6.5; Pages 8–10 and 13)**
   - **Definitive Flaw / Potential Concern:** In Section 5 (Item 3 on p. 8, "ZVF-vs-G: empirical vs theory" on p. 9, and Table 9 on p. 10), the text compares the empirical Zero-Variance Fraction ($\text{ZVF}_{\text{emp}} \in [0.631, 0.838]$) against the homogeneous Bernoulli-independent closed form $\text{ZVF}(p, G) = p^G + (1 - p)^G$ evaluated at the empirical accuracy $p \in [0.978, 0.990]$. Observing negative residuals ($\text{ZVF}_{\text{emp}} - \text{ZVF}_{\text{theory}} \in [-0.232, -0.073]$), the text concludes on page 10: *"This means the empirical contrastive signal is stronger than the Bernoulli-independent prediction: rollouts within a group are negatively correlated (correct rollouts cluster, incorrect rollouts cluster) rather than independent."* This analysis contains three mathematical issues:
     1. **Clustering vs. Correlation Sign:** Having correct rollouts cluster together and incorrect rollouts cluster together across prompts (which occurs when prompt difficulty $p_x$ varies across the dataset) describes **positive** within-group correlation (overdispersion relative to a homogeneous $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ distribution), not negative correlation. By strict convexity of $f(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ on $[0, 1]$ for all $G \ge 2$, Jensen's inequality guarantees that prompt-difficulty heterogeneity strictly *increases* the expected zero-variance fraction above the homogeneous baseline: $\mathbb{E}_x[p_x^G + (1-p_x)^G] > (\mathbb{E}[p_x])^G + (1-\mathbb{E}[p_x])^G$. Thus, clustering of correct and incorrect rollouts produces *positive* residuals ($\text{ZVF}_{\text{emp}} > \text{ZVF}_{\text{theory}}$), the exact opposite of Table 9.
     2. **Conditional Independence Under Standard Decoding:** For a fixed prompt $x$ and fixed policy parameters $\theta$, $G$ completions sampled via standard i.i.d. temperature decoding are conditionally independent Bernoulli trials with success probability $p_x$; their within-group outcome correlation is zero, not negative (unless sampling without replacement or stateful cross-rollout diversity penalties are actively applied). Note that Proposition 4.1 of Wu et al. [23] concerns the covariance between positive and negative gradient vectors $\text{Cov}(g^+, g^-)$, not negative correlation among binary rollout outcomes.
     3. **Mismatch Between Trajectory-Averaged Training ZVF and Terminal Held-Out Accuracy $p$:** Comparing Table 9 (`p (empirical)`: $0.982, 0.988, 0.990, 0.978$; `ZVF (empirical)`: $0.838, 0.764, 0.691, 0.631$) with Table 11 (p. 13) shows that `p (empirical)` is the *terminal held-out accuracy* after 40 steps, whereas `ZVF (empirical)` is the *mean ZVF averaged across all 40 training steps*. Indeed, at $G=2$, achieving $\text{ZVF} = 0.838$ on any set of binary pairs whose mean accuracy is $p = 0.982$ is mathematically impossible: since each non-degenerate pair at $G=2$ has 1 success and 1 failure (mean $0.5$), the fraction of non-zero-variance groups cannot exceed $2(1 - p) = 2(1 - 0.982) = 0.036$, which bounds $\text{ZVF} \ge 1 - 0.036 = 0.964$. Evaluating $p^G + (1-p)^G$ at the near-ceiling terminal held-out accuracy while averaging $\text{ZVF}_{\text{emp}}$ over earlier training steps (when pass rates $p_t$ were lower) artificially inflates $\text{ZVF}_{\text{theory}}$ and creates negative residuals.
   - **Recommendation:** Recompute the theoretical baseline by averaging $\mathbb{E}_{t,x}[p_{t,x}^G + (1-p_{t,x})^G]$ over the per-step, per-prompt training accuracies from the same rollouts used to measure $\text{ZVF}_{\text{emp}}$, and revise the discussion on pages 8–10 so that positive prompt-difficulty clustering is not conflated with negative correlation.

3. **Statistical Invalidity of Reported TOST Equivalence $p$-Values at $n = 3$ Seeds and Fixed-Step Confounding (Sections 5 and 6.6; Tables 8, 11, 14; Pages 8–10 and 15–16)**

1. **Undefined Terms, Inverse Pair-Count Scaling in $g_{\text{GRPO}}(x)$, and Unwhitened vs. $1/\sigma_r$-Whitened GRPO Across Section 6.7 (Pages 18–19)**
   - **Verification Summary:** The primary mathematical identities in Section 6.7—specifically the all-pairs contrast-projection identity in Equation (2), the leave-one-out (LOO) control-variate decomposition $r_i - \bar{r} = \frac{G-1}{G}(r_i - b_{-i})$ with $\text{Var}(b_{-i}) = p_x(1 - p_x)/(G - 1)$, the population-whitened binary advantage scalars $A_+ = \sqrt{(G-K)/K}$ and $A_- = -\sqrt{K/(G-K)}$ with adaptive margin $\Delta_{\text{margin}} = \frac{G}{\sqrt{K(G-K)}}$ in Equation (3), the useful-signal rate $Y_G(p) = 1 - p^G - (1 - p)^G$ in Equation (4), and the Baseline-Equivalence-Index $\text{BEI}_G$ in Equation (5)—are algebraically exact under their stated definitions.
   - **Potential Concern in $g_{\text{GRPO}}(x)$ Scaling (Page 18):** Immediately following Equation (2), the text states: *"For binary rewards with $K$ successes the frontier models give the closed form $g_{\text{GRPO}}(x) = \frac{1}{G K(G-K)} g_{\text{pair}}(x)$, exposing the $K(G-K)$ pair count as the mechanistic driver."* Neither $g_{\text{GRPO}}(x)$ nor $g_{\text{pair}}(x)$ is formally defined, and placing $K(G-K)$ in the denominator conflicts with both the algebraic expansion of Equations (2) and (3) and the claim that a larger pair count $K(G-K)$ drives a stronger gradient signal. Let $\mathcal{W}$ ($|\mathcal{W}| = K$) and $\mathcal{L}$ ($|\mathcal{L}| = G - K$) denote the winning ($r_i = 1$) and losing ($r_j = 0$) rollouts, let $S_{\text{pair}}(x) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{W}, j \in \mathcal{L}} (s_i - s_j)$ denote the sum over all $K(G-K)$ ordered winner–loser pairs, and let $\bar{g}_{\text{pair}}(x) = \frac{1}{K(G-K)} S_{\text{pair}}(x)$ denote the mean pairwise score difference:
     - For the **unwhitened** group-centered update in Equation (2), $\sum_{i=1}^G (r_i - \bar{r}) s_i = \frac{1}{G} S_{\text{pair}}(x) = \frac{K(G-K)}{G} \bar{g}_{\text{pair}}(x)$ (or $\frac{1}{G^2} S_{\text{pair}}(x) = \frac{K(G-K)}{G^2} \bar{g}_{\text{pair}}(x)$ if the outer $\frac{1}{G}$ group-mean loss normalization is included), placing $K(G-K)$ in the numerator.

     In neither convention does a prefactor of $\frac{1}{G K(G-K)}$ arise unless $g_{\text{pair}}(x)$ is non-standardly scaled by $[K(G-K)]^2$ or $[K(G-K)]^{3/2}$.
   - **Population vs. Sample Standard Deviation and $G=2$ Scalars (Pages 18–19):** Equation (3) uses the biased population standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{pop}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^G (r_i - \bar{r})^2} = \frac{\sqrt{K(G-K)}}{G}$, whereas many RL libraries (such as PyTorch `torch.std(unbiased=True)` used in several TRL implementations, and matching the $G-1$ denominator in $\text{Var}(b_{-i}) = p_x(1-p_x)/(G-1)$ immediately preceding Equation 3) use the Bessel-corrected sample standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{samp}} = \sqrt{\frac{G}{G-1}}\sigma_{\text{pop}}$, which scales $A_+$, $A_-$, and $\Delta_{\text{margin}}$ by $\sqrt{\frac{G-1}{G}}$. Furthermore, on page 19, the text states that *"at $G=2$ the centred update assigns $+\frac{1}{2}$ to the winner and $-\frac{1}{2}$ to the loser"*; this holds for the *unwhitened* centered advantage $r_i - \bar{r} = \pm\frac{1}{2}$, whereas Equation (3) gives $A_{\pm} = \pm 1$ under population whitening (or $\pm 1/\sqrt{2}$ under sample whitening).
   - **Impact on the Residual-Isomorphism Check (Page 19):** The single-batch residual-isomorphism check subtracts a synthetic margin-scaled DPO gradient $V_{\text{mDPO}} = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i<j}(r_i - r_j)(s_i - s_j)$ from the empirical GRPO gradient $V_{\text{GRPO}}$ and claims that $\cos(V_{\text{GRPO}} - V_{\text{mDPO}}, V_{\text{KL}}) < 0.99$ *"would prove the group baseline carries stabilizing structure orthogonal to pairwise preference."* Because $V_{\text{mDPO}}$ is algebraically identical to the unwhitened, unclipped sum $\sum_{i=1}^G (r_i - \bar{r}) s_i$ by Equation (2), subtracting an unwhitened $V_{\text{mDPO}}$ from a standard $1/\sigma_r$-whitened, length-normalized $V_{\text{GRPO}}$ across a multi-prompt batch with $G > 2$ will leave a non-zero residual simply because each prompt $x$ is rescaled by a different within-group standard deviation $1/\sigma_r(x)$—even when every prompt's update is still an exact all-pairs contrast with whitened pairwise margin $\hat{A}_i - \hat{A}_j = (r_i - r_j)/\sigma_r(x)$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly define $g_{\text{GRPO}}(x)$ and $g_{\text{pair}}(x)$ and correct the placement/exponent of $K(G-K)$; state the population standard-deviation convention for Equation (3); and specify that $V_{\text{mDPO}}$ in the residual-isomorphism check must incorporate the same per-prompt $1/\sigma_r(x)$ and token-length normalizers as $V_{\text{GRPO}}$ to isolate orthogonal baseline structure cleanly.

2. **Monotonicity of the Compute-Optimal Group-Size Objective $G^\star = \arg\max_G Y_G(p)/G$ and Asymmetric ZVF Bound (Section 6.7, Page 19)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Following Equation (4), $Y_G(p) = 1 - p^G - (1 - p)^G$, page 19 states that increasing $G$ trades pair generation, baseline variance, and linear inference compute against one another so that *"the compute-optimal group size is finite, $G^\star = \arg\max_G Y_G(p)/G$, not 'as large as possible'"*, and argues that this *"reinforces our budget-dependent optimum (Table 6)."* However, for every $p \in (0, 1)$, $p^G$ and $(1-p)^G$ are strictly convex in $G$, so $Y_G(p) = 1 - p^G - (1 - p)^G$ is strictly concave in $G > 0$. Evaluating $Y_G(p)/G$ at integer group sizes gives:
     $$\frac{Y_2(p)}{2} = \frac{1 - p^2 - (1-p)^2}{2} = p(1-p), \qquad \frac{Y_3(p)}{3} = \frac{1 - p^3 - (1-p)^3}{3} = p(1-p),$$
     and by strict concavity of $Y_G(p)$, $Y_G(p)/G < p(1-p)$ and decreases strictly monotonically for all $G \ge 3$ (e.g., $Y_4(p)/4 = p(1-p)\left[1 - \frac{1}{2}p(1-p)\right] < p(1-p)$). Consequently, $\arg\max_{G \ge 2} Y_G(p)/G$ is identically $\{2, 3\}$ (and uniquely $G = 2$ over power-of-two grids $G \in \{2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$) for *every* $p \in (0, 1)$, independent of prompt difficulty $p$ and token budget $T$. Additionally, the immediately following sample-complexity bound $G \gtrsim \log \epsilon / \log(1 - p_{\min})$ only bounds the all-failure term $(1-p)^G \le \epsilon$ as $p \to 0$; because $\text{ZVF}(p, G) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ is symmetric, near-ceiling prompts ($p \to 1$) require $p_{\min} = \min(p, 1-p)$ so that $\max(p^G, (1-p)^G) \le \epsilon$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify that maximizing per-rollout non-degenerate group yield $Y_G(p)/G$ alone always selects the minimal group size $G \in \{2, 3\}$ (mirroring the monotone decreasing behavior of $\widehat{\text{GE}}_{\text{tok}}$ in Table 10), and that obtaining an interior or budget-dependent optimum $G^\star > 3$ requires either enforcing the ZVF threshold constraint ($Y_G(p) \ge 1 - \epsilon$) or modeling how per-step gradient SNR trades off against the reduced step count $n_{\text{step}}(G) = T/(G \cdot K \cdot \bar{L})$ over a finite budget $T$. Also define $p_{\min} = \min(p, 1-p)$ in the ZVF bound.
