# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper investigates the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)—the proportion of prompt groups in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) where all $G$ sampled completions receive identical binary rewards, yielding zero group-centered advantages—as a descriptive diagnostic within the TINKERRL-BENCH suite. The authors provide a cross-framework logging and auditing pipeline, analyze the training-time temporal dynamics of ZVF (highlighting its high lag-1 autocorrelation and upward drift), and bound its interpretive reach by showing that unsigned ZVF aliases all-correct task mastery ($\text{ZVF}^+$) with all-wrong incapacity ($\text{ZVF}^-$) and cannot serve as a standalone predictor of final held-out generalization.

## Strengths
* The paper offers a valuable corrective and bounding analysis for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) practitioners by rigorously demonstrating why unsigned zero-variance group statistics fail as standalone predictors of held-out generalization due to mastery–incapacity aliasing and vulnerability to sub-reward micro-jitter.
* The decomposition of raw ZVF into signed all-wrong starvation ($\text{ZVF}^-$) and all-correct saturation ($\text{ZVF}^+$) channels, combined with the release of a unified cross-framework reward-log parser and exact per-group GSM8K reward tensors, provides a practical and cheap operational sanity check for diagnosing gradient starvation.
* The authors demonstrate commendable scientific transparency in auditing their own earlier analyses—explicitly identifying and correcting the mathematical sign-guarantee of the same-group plug-in estimator residual ($\delta_{\text{div}}$) and applying Jensen's inequality to separate prompt-difficulty dispersion from homogeneous Bernoulli baselines.

## Weaknesses
* While the paper transparently acknowledges in the Abstract and Section 5.5 that the 45 trajectories in the 9-method variance-mitigation corpus (`variance_mitigation.tsv`) are a synthetic "simulation projection" rather than actual model-training runs, Sections 4.1–4.3, 6, 8, 9, and 10.10–10.16 repeatedly analyze these exact simulated rows as if they were live LLM training deployments, making empirical claims about algorithm collapse resistance, temporal autocorrelation ($\rho_1 \approx 0.94$), and forecasting ROC-AUCs that merely recover the assumptions of the synthetic generator.
* The manuscript reads as an unharmonized concatenation of sequential research iterations (`iter14` through `iter130`), leaving multiple explicitly retracted or mathematically invalidated analyses—including the $\delta_{\text{div}}$ Iso-Yield/Iso-$G$ cost-saving tables (Sections 10.7, 10.9, 10.11–10.12), the withdrawn `iter130` fused-risk index (Section 4.5), and the legacy 20-test table with undefined $n_1=n_2=1$ Welch $t$-tests (Table 5)—intact in the main body alongside contradictory failure tallies, trajectory lengths, and pre-collapse ZVF behavior descriptions across sections.
* Several statistical and machine-learning evaluations suffer from circularity or methodological flaws: most notably, the claimed $\text{AUC} = 1.000$ failure separability of $\text{ZVF}^-$ across the 15 unlogged runs in Section 10.15 is an algebraic artifact of imputing $\text{ZVF}^-$ via an odds-split factor $(1-\bar{r})^G / [(1-\bar{r})^G + \bar{r}^G]$ that gates directly on the outcome reward $\bar{r} \lessgtr 0.5$ (and over-estimates rather than lower-bounds $\text{ZVF}^-$ when $\bar{r} < 0.5$), while the Section 10.2 nearest-centroid LOO classifier suffers from test-set centroid leakage on a singleton class ($n=1$) and Section 10.16 conflates training-step competence with intrinsic prompt difficulty.
* The core diagnostic and its theoretical properties—including the Advantage Collapse Rate (ACR), the Jensen inequality excess under prompt-difficulty heterogeneity, the temporal persistence of zero-variance prompts, and the disaggregation of all-fail starvation from all-pass saturation or variance-based filtering—have already been established in prior work, and the manuscript omits comparison or discussion of the companion AVSPO baseline from the primary ACR reference (Reference Paper: Advantage Collapse in Group Relative Policy Optimization: Diagnosis and Mitigation, Xixiang He et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Gradient Starvation in Binary-Reward GRPO: Why Group-Mean Centering Fails and Why the Simplest Fix Works, Wen-Hua Nie et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Train Less, Learn More: Adaptive Efficient Rollout Optimization for Group-Based Reinforcement Learning, Zhi Zhang et al., 2026; Reference Paper: No Prompt Left Behind: Exploiting Zero-Variance Prompts in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Entropy-Guided Advantage Shaping, Thanh-Long V. Le et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Act Only When It Pays: Efficient Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning via Selective Rollouts, Haizhong Zheng et al., 2025; Reference Paper: Online Difficulty Filtering for Reasoning Oriented Reinforcement Learning, Sanghwan Bae et al., 2025; Reference Paper: VCRL: Variance-based Curriculum Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models, Guochao Jiang et al., 2025).
* The manuscript contains notable completeness and bibliographic gaps, including empty subsection headings for key benchmark specifications (Subsections 2.1, 2.4, and 5.6), an unexecuted counterfactual sweep with empty result cells in Appendix B (Table 24), a corrupted citation for Reference [18] whose arXiv ID points to an unrelated physics paper, and missing bibliography entries for core evaluated methods (`NGRPO`, `SCAFGRPO`, `MCGRPO`, `AREAL`).

Clarifying in Section 2 that Tables 1–3 describe the broader multi-pillar `TINKERRL-BENCH` suite, explicitly defining "$F_3$" upon first mention, and introducing the nine variance-mitigation methods and their simulation protocol in Section 2 or Section 3 would make the benchmark design self-contained.

3. **Inclusion of Mathematically Undefined Single-Seed Tests in Table 5 and Unenumerated Planned Tests in Section 3.3**
   - **Observation:** On page 6, Section 3.3 includes *Table 5: Legacy 20-test BH table – audit trail only*, while explicitly noting in the caption and text that the table *"pre-dates the Tier-A/B/C partition and silently includes single-seed Tinker rows whose Welch statistics are mathematically undefined ($n_1=n_2=1$)"* and that under the restricted Tier-A/B family computed in the addendum, *"only $F_3$ survives."* In addition, on page 5, Section 3.3 states that to address multiple comparisons *"across 5 planned tests, we apply both the conservative Bonferroni correction (family-wise error rate: $\alpha' = \alpha/k = 0.0100$) and the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure."*
   - **Potential Concern:** While transparency regarding earlier revisions is constructive, retaining a full 20-row table of "significant" $p$-values in the main text that the paper simultaneously identifies as mathematically invalid or withdrawn raises several validity and clarity issues:
     - **Invalid Test Statistics and Pseudo-Replication:** For a two-sample Welch $t$-test with $n_1=n_2=1$, sample variances and degrees of freedom ($\nu = 0$) are strictly undefined (`NaN`), yet Table 5 reports extreme floating-point $p$-values (e.g., Rank 1 `Dense vs MoE Architecture`, $\text{Raw } p = 2.357\times 10^{-21}$, $\text{BH-adj. } p = 0$; Rank 2 `PPO vs GRPO on Llama-3.1-8B`, $\text{Raw } p = 3.924\times 10^{-10}$). Similarly, a two-sample Mann–Whitney $U$ test on $n_1=n_2=5$ seeds has a minimum possible two-tailed exact $p$-value of $\frac{2}{\binom{10}{5}} = \frac{2}{252} \approx 0.00794$, making the reported Mann–Whitney $p = 3.29\times 10^{-5}$ in Rank 6 impossible for a 5-seed run-level comparison unless autocorrelated per-step trajectory rows were pooled as independent observations (which Sections 4 and 5.7 explicitly warn against due to lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho \approx 0.9$). Moreover, Ranks 8 and 9 report highly significant correlations between ZVF and Final Performance ($p = 0.00054$ and $p = 0.00081$), contradicting the Abstract, Section 4, and Section 5.4, which withdraw the ZVF–outcome correlation.
     - **Missing Authoritative Test Table and Bonferroni Power:** The 5 planned Tier-A/B hypothesis tests referenced on page 5 are never enumerated in Section 3.3, nor are their test statistics, effect sizes (Cohen's $d$), or restricted-family adjusted $p$-values shown in the main text. Additionally, the power calculations in Table 4 (page 6) are evaluated at the unadjusted $\alpha = 0.05$ ($d_{\min} = 2.024$ for $n_1=n_2=5$; $d_{\min} = 1.530$ for $n_1=5, n_2=15$); under the Bonferroni-adjusted $\alpha' = 0.0100$, the minimum detectable effect size at 80% power for $n_1=n_2=5$ rises to $d_{\min} \approx 2.68$. Replacing the deprecated Table 5 in the main text with the authoritative restricted Tier-A/B test table (and moving Table 5 to a changelog appendix) would substantially strengthen the statistical presentation.

4. **Confounded Marginal One-Way ANOVAs in Variance Decomposition (Section 3.3)**
   - **Observation:** On page 7 (*Variance decomposition*), the text reports four separate one-way ANOVAs across 42 experiments with valid performance observations: library/framework ($\eta^2 = 0.546$), training algorithm ($\eta^2 = 0.558$), model family ($\eta^2 = 0.471$), and model scale ($\eta^2 = 0.322$), concluding that library/framework and training algorithm are the dominant variance sources.
   - **Potential Concern:** These four marginal $\eta^2$ values sum to $1.897 > 1.0$ because the experimental design across libraries, algorithms, model families, and model scales is unbalanced and non-orthogonal (collinear)—for example, classic RL libraries exclusively run PPO/CQL on synthetic arithmetic, whereas LLM launchers run GRPO on GSM8K/tool-use across specific model families and scales. Inferring that library/framework and training algorithm are the dominant variance sources from separate marginal one-way ANOVAs without accounting for factor confounding is potentially misleading, as each marginal $\eta^2$ absorbs shared variance from the other collinear factors. Furthermore, it is unclear whether the "42 experiments" pool multi-seed Tier-A runs (treating individual seeds as independent rows) alongside single-seed Tier-C runs. The authors should clarify the exact composition of the 42 observations and either use a multi-way decomposition (e.g., Type II/III partial $\eta^2$) or explicitly caveat that the one-way $\eta^2$ values reflect overlapping, confounded marginal variance.

6. **Ambiguity in Advantage Normalization Formula Under Zero Variance (Figure 2)**
   - **Observation:** Figure 2 (page 4) defines the group-relative advantage as $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \bar{r}}{\sigma_r}$ where $\sigma_r = \text{std}(\{r_i\})$, without a numerical stabilizer $\varepsilon$ in the denominator or an explicit indicator for $\sigma_r = 0$ (and without specifying whether $\sigma_r$ uses sample or population standard deviation, whereas Appendix A.1 uses unbiased sample variance $\widehat{\text{Var}}$ with threshold $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$).
   - **Potential Concern:** When all rewards in a group are identical ($\sigma_r = 0$, the exact condition defining ZVF), the expression $\frac{r_i - \bar{r}}{\sigma_r}$ evaluates to $0/0$ (`NaN`) unless a stabilizer $\varepsilon$ is added to the denominator, standard-deviation division is omitted (unnormalized centering $r_i - \bar{r}$), or zero-variance groups are explicitly masked to zero. Moreover, while the Abstract and Section 1 carefully state that homogeneous rewards zero the centered reward-contrast term rather than necessarily the total gradient under KL or clipping, Section 10.19 (page 39) later notes that if a KL penalty is folded into the reward prior to normalization ($r_i = r_i^{\text{task}} - \beta\,\text{KL}_i$), dividing by $\sigma_r$ when $\text{Var}(r^{\text{task}}) = 0$ cancels $\beta$ and amplifies the KL term into a full-magnitude update $\hat{A}_i = -(\text{KL}_i - \overline{\text{KL}})/\sigma_{\text{KL}}$. Specifying the exact stabilized advantage formula implemented in the submitted runner (including how $\sigma_r = 0$ and KL regularization are handled) in Section 1 or Figure 2 would clarify this mechanism early on.

2. **Retention of Withdrawn Fused-Risk Index Analysis and Unrelated Retrieval Text in Section 4.5 (Pages 9–11):**
   A substantive concern for manuscript coherence is that Section 4.5 (*"Iter 130: Unified ZVF Risk Index"*, pp. 9–11) presents an affirmative evaluation of a three-channel composite risk index (`zvf_risk` and `zvf_risk_max`), reporting a cross-experiment Mann–Whitney AUROC of $0.929$ $[0.83, 1.00]$, a within-methods AUROC of $0.805$ $[0.62, 0.95]$, and specific practitioner intervention thresholds (`zvf_risk_max > 0.55` as failure-positive, `< 0.30` as safe). However, later in the manuscript (Section 10.18, p. 37), the authors explicitly retract this analysis: *"The earlier fused-risk AUROC and threshold analysis is withdrawn. 4. **Mathematical Inversion and Mean-Reward Confounding in the "Conservative Odds-Split Lower Bound" for Signed ZVF (Section 10.15; Pages 33–35):**
   - **Definitive Mathematical and Methodological Issue:** In Section 10.15 (p. 33), the authors decompose $\text{ZVF}_G = \text{ZVF}^- + \text{ZVF}^+$, where $\text{ZVF}^- = \mathbb{E}_x[\Pr(K_x=0)]$ (all-wrong starvation) and $\text{ZVF}^+ = \mathbb{E}_x[\Pr(K_x=G)]$ (all-correct saturation). Because per-group reward vectors are only logged for the 3 GSM8K runs, for the remaining runs in Table 21 (p. 34; referred to as "15 non-GSM8K runs" in the text) where only aggregate $(\text{ZVF}, \bar{r}, G)$ are available, the authors impute $\text{ZVF}^-$ using the homogeneous i.i.d. odds-split formula evaluated at the aggregate mean accuracy $\bar{r}$:
     $$\widehat{\text{ZVF}}^- = \text{ZVF} \cdot \frac{(1-\bar{r})^G}{(1-\bar{r})^G + \bar{r}^G}.$$
     The authors claim (pp. 33–34) that this homogeneous odds-split is a *"conservative lower bound on $\text{ZVF}^-$"* across all runs and conclude that *"$\text{ZVF}^-$ for the 15 non-GSM8K runs is the odds-split lower bound, so the true $\text{ZVF}^-$ separation is at least as clean as reported,"* yielding a *"perfect failure separator"* ($\text{Mann–Whitney AUC} = 1.000$ between $n=7$ healthy `converged` runs and $n=13$ unhealthy `plateau`/`drift`/`collapse` runs). Three mathematical problems affect this conclusion:
     1. **The Odds-Split Is an Upper Bound (Over-Estimator), Not a Lower Bound, When $\bar{r} < 0.5$:** Evaluating the odds ratio at the pooled mean $\bar{r}$ under-predicts $\text{ZVF}^-$ *only* when $\bar{r} > 0.5$ (as in the converged GSM8K runs with $\bar{r} \approx 0.70$, where $(1-\bar{r})^8 \ll \bar{r}^8$ assigns nearly $100\%$ of ZVF mass to $\text{ZVF}^+$). Conversely, when $\bar{r} < 0.5$—which holds for **every** unhealthy `plateau` and `drift` run in Table 21 ($\bar{r} \in [0.265, 0.428]$ at $G=8$)—we have $(1-\bar{r})^8 \gg \bar{r}^8$, so the factor $\frac{(1-\bar{r})^8}{(1-\bar{r})^8 + \bar{r}^8}$ is between $0.90$ and $0.999$, assigning $90\%\text{--}99.9\%$ of raw ZVF to $\text{ZVF}^-$. Under prompt-difficulty heterogeneity, if a run with $\bar{r} = 0.391$ has mastered a subset of easy prompts ($p_x \approx 1$, which actually belong to $\text{ZVF}^+$) while failing on others, the homogeneous formula at $\bar{r} < 0.5$ *over-estimates* $\text{ZVF}^-$. Because it under-estimates $\text{ZVF}^-$ for all healthy runs ($\bar{r} > 0.5$) and over-estimates $\text{ZVF}^-$ for all unhealthy runs ($\bar{r} < 0.5$), the imputation artificially widens the gap between the two groups in the exact direction of the hypothesis.
     2. **Algebraic Confounding with Mean Reward $\bar{r}$:** Because all 7 `converged` runs have $\bar{r} \in [0.697, 0.984]$ ($> 0.5$) and all 13 `unhealthy` runs have $\bar{r} \in [0.000, 0.428]$ ($< 0.5$, since `plateau` is defined by $\text{peak} < 0.5$), multiplying ZVF by the step-like sigmoid $\frac{(1-\bar{r})^G}{(1-\bar{r})^G + \bar{r}^G}$ simply gates ZVF by whether $\bar{r} < 0.5$. On these 20 runs, mean reward $\bar{r}$ alone already achieves $\text{Mann–Whitney AUC} = 1.000$.
     3. **Inconsistency Between Stated Formula and Table 21 Values:** In Table 21 (p. 34), the values reported for `sweep G=2` ($\bar{r} = 0.977, \text{Raw ZVF} = 0.838, \text{ZVF}^- = 0.072$) and `sweep G=16` ($\bar{r} = 0.984, \text{Raw ZVF} = 0.631, \text{ZVF}^- = 0.003$) do not match the stated power-$G$ odds-split formula: at $\bar{r} = 0.977, G = 2$, $(0.023)^2 / [(0.023)^2 + (0.977)^2] = 0.00055$, which gives $\text{ZVF}^- = 0.838 \times 0.00055 = 0.00046$ (not $0.072$), and at $G = 16, \bar{r} = 0.984$, the power-16 odds factor is $\sim 10^{-29}$ (not $0.003$).
   - **Recommendation:** Correct the mathematical claim that the i.i.d. odds-split is a lower bound on $\text{ZVF}^-$ when $\bar{r} < 0.5$, clarify how the `sweep` rows in Table 21 were computed, explicitly acknowledge that for unlogged runs $\widehat{\text{ZVF}}^-$ is an algebraic transform gated by $\bar{r} \lessgtr 0.5$ (where $\bar{r}$ alone achieves $\text{AUC} = 1.000$), and restrict empirical separability claims for Signed ZVF to runs where exact per-group reward vectors are logged.

5. **Confounding of Training Time with Intrinsic Prompt Difficulty in "Difficulty-Stratified ZVF" (Section 10.16; Pages 35–36, Tables 22–23):**
   - **Definitive Methodological Issue:** In Section 10.16 (p. 35), the authors ask whether variance-mitigation libraries lower ZVF *"on the hard-prompt tail (the pathological starvation channel) or on the easy-prompt tail (the benign saturation channel)."* However, rather than stratifying individual prompts by their intrinsic problem difficulty $p_x$, they *"bin every per-step observation of each library's training run by its own heldout-accuracy into five equal-mass quintiles, defined per method... Quintile 0 is the hardest-prompt-dominated training window; quintile 4 is the easiest-prompt-dominated one."*
     - Because `heldout-accuracy` is a global evaluation of the policy checkpoint at training step $t$ on a fixed task, and policy accuracy increases over training steps, binning optimizer steps by step-level `heldout-accuracy` does not stratify prompts by difficulty—it stratifies the trajectory by **training time / policy competence stage** (`q0` = earliest training steps when accuracy is near its initial baseline $p \approx 0.25\text{--}0.35$; `q4` = latest training steps when accuracy is higher).
     - In `q0` (the start of training, where mean accuracy is around $0.25\text{--}0.35$), the binomial collision probability $p^8 + (1-p)^8$ is naturally near its minimum ($\approx 0.01\text{--}0.03$), which explains why all libraries have near-zero ZVF in `q0` ($0.010\text{--}0.020$, Table 22)—not because "hard-prompt groups" produce no zero-variance collisions (a truly hard prompt with $p_x \to 0$ has $(1-p_x)^8 \to 1$ and $\text{ZVF} \to 1$), but because `q0` reflects intermediate batch-level accuracy early in training. Likewise, `q0` through `q4` restate the early/mid/late temporal trajectory decomposition already reported in Section 6.2 (Table 9, p. 17: $\bar{z}_e, \bar{z}_m, \bar{z}_l$).
     - Additionally, Table 23 (p. 36) computes 95% confidence intervals via a *"2,000-rep step-level bootstrap"* across the 5,540 strongly autocorrelated step rows ($\rho_1 \approx 0.94$) without trajectory/block resampling, which artificially shrinks the CIs (contrary to the paper's own warning in Section 5.7, p. 14).
   - **Recommendation:** Reframe Section 10.16 and Tables 22–23 as **competence-stratified (or heldout-accuracy-stratified) ZVF across training stages** rather than "prompt-difficulty stratification," correct the interpretation of `q0` (which represents early-training intermediate accuracy rather than $p_x \to 0$ hard prompts), and compute bootstrap CIs in Table 23 by resampling at the seed/trajectory level rather than the independent step level.

2. **Notation and Scaling Factor Distinction Between Biased and Unbiased Within-Group Variance in Equation (11) vs. Equation (13)**
   - **Location:** Section 10.19, Page 38, Equation (11) vs. Appendix A.1, Page 40, Equation (13) and Appendix C.1, Page 43.
   - **Potential Concern:** Equation (11) defines Pairwise Contrast Density (PCD) via the chain of equalities:
     $$\text{PCD} = \mathbb{E}_x\big[\text{Var}(r \mid x)\big] = \mathbb{E}_x\left[\frac{1}{G^2}\sum_{i<j}(r_i - r_j)^2\right] = \frac{G-1}{G}\mathbb{E}_x\big[p_x(1 - p_x)\big].$$
   - **Mathematical Clarification:** For conditionally i.i.d.
