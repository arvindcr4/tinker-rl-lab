\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), and in particular Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has become the de facto default recipe for
post-training reasoning-capable language models. Yet reported gains are hard to
reproduce: results are spread across incompatible frameworks (TRL, veRL,
OpenRLHF, Tinker), are frequently single-seed, and rarely expose the internal
training signal that would explain \emph{why} a run learned or failed to learn.
This project builds a \textbf{unified, provenance-tracked benchmark and diagnostic
harness} for GRPO/RLVR post-training, organised around a single measurable
quantity --- the \textbf{Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)}, the fraction of prompt
groups whose within-group reward variance is zero and which therefore contribute
no gradient. The same task, reward function, and decoding configuration are held
fixed across back-ends so that observed differences are attributable to the
\emph{stack} rather than the experiment, and every training step emits per-step
telemetry (reward, ZVF, gradient utilisation, entropy, length, KL). On top of this
harness we run a portfolio of eight focused studies (P1--P8). We deliberately frame
the contribution as a \emph{measurement / diagnostic benchmark and reproducibility
tooling}, \textbf{not} a ``GRPO wins'' leaderboard. The honest headline findings
are mixed and include strong negatives: on roughly $72$--$77\%$ of prompt groups in a
typical batch the within-group reward variance is zero, so those groups contribute no
policy-gradient signal (P2); there is \emph{no} robust group-size
sweet spot once multiple seeds are used (P3); a curriculum that mechanically
eliminates the wasted gradient does \emph{not} beat a matched baseline across three
seeds ($+0.028$ vs.\ $-0.028$ held-out, within noise) and costs ${\sim}5$--$6\times$
more sampling; the apparent step-1 predictability of per-layer adaptation
(P1: top-$k$ overlap $=1.0$ at 1.5B) does \emph{not} survive a scaled 3B
multi-seed re-test (overlap $0.11$, ${\approx}$chance); the clearest surviving
positive is that a telemetry-based integrity auditor reaches AUROC $0.84$ versus
$0.43$ for a reward-only baseline (P8). All findings were independently re-checked
by two frontier models, which flagged several early single-seed conclusions as
underpowered --- prompting the powered multi-seed re-test that produced the honest
nulls reported here.

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}

\section{Background}
Post-training with reinforcement learning has shifted from human-preference reward
models (RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}) toward \emph{verifiable} rewards, where
a programmatic checker --- an exact-match grader for GSM8K, a unit test for code
--- supplies a binary or scalar reward with no learned reward model in the
loop~\cite{lambert2024tulu3,guo2025r1}. Within this RLVR setting, Group Relative
Policy Optimization (GRPO)~\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} has become the default
algorithm. It drops PPO's value network~\cite{schulman2017ppo} and instead
estimates the advantage of each sampled completion \emph{relative to its own group}
of same-prompt samples. Because GRPO removes a whole model (the critic), it is
cheaper and simpler to run at scale, and it was central to the DeepSeek-R1
reasoning models~\cite{guo2025r1}; it is now the recipe most open post-training
efforts reach for first.

This simplicity has a cost that is rarely reported. Because the advantage is a
within-group standardisation, any prompt for which every sampled completion
receives the \emph{same} reward contributes an advantage of exactly zero, and
therefore \emph{no policy-gradient (advantage) signal} --- only the KL penalty
toward the reference policy still acts. On an easy dataset the model quickly solves most
prompts, so a large fraction of every batch collapses to zero advantage. We call
this the \textbf{Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF)}, and it is, empirically, the single
largest source of wasted compute in GRPO post-training. ZVF is the namesake and the
organising measurement of this project: every study in the portfolio either
measures it, tests a lever intended to reduce it, or uses it as a feature.

\section{A Motivating Example}
Consider a single training step with a group size of $G=8$ on an easy GSM8K prompt
that the current policy already solves. All eight sampled completions produce the
correct final answer, so each receives reward $r_i=1$. The group mean is $\mu_g=1$
and the group standard deviation is $\sigma_g=0$, so by Eq.~\eqref{eq:adv} every
advantage $A_i=0$. The advantage (surrogate) term of the objective then contributes
no gradient for the entire group of eight rollouts --- eight full forward-decode
passes that cost real tokens and money --- so the group supplies \emph{no
task-learning signal}. (The KL-regularisation term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} still nudges
the policy toward the reference; the point is that no \emph{reward-driven} learning
happens.)
Now imagine a batch of $16$ such prompts where $12$ are already solved: $12/16=0.75$
of the batch is ``dark'' compute. This is not a corner case; Chapter~\ref{ch:results}
shows it is the \emph{typical} regime ($\zvf\approx0.72$--$0.77$). The Zero-Variance
Fraction turns this intuition into one loggable number, and the rest of the report is
an attempt to measure it precisely and to test whether it can be reduced into real
held-out accuracy.

\section{Problem Statement}
Three concrete problems motivate this project.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{A reproducibility crisis.} GRPO/RLVR results are reported on
  different frameworks with different (often undocumented) reward parsers, decoding
  temperatures, KL coefficients, and clip ranges. A headline number cannot be
  attributed to the \emph{algorithm} versus the \emph{stack}.
  \item \textbf{Gradient waste is invisible.} Standard training logs report reward
  and loss, but not what fraction of the batch produced any learning signal at all.
  Practitioners pay for rollouts that move no weights, and have no dashboard number
  that tells them so.
  \item \textbf{No provenance standard.} There is no machine-readable record of
  \emph{what} was trained, on which verifier version, with which rollouts, so
  contamination and silent reward drift go undetected.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Objectives}
The Phase-1 objectives, against which the work is evaluated, are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Build a \textbf{unified harness} that runs the identical task, reward, and
  decoding configuration across multiple RL post-training back-ends, so that
  cross-framework differences are attributable to the stack.
  \item Instrument a \textbf{per-step telemetry layer} that surfaces ZVF, gradient
  utilisation, entropy, completion length, and KL, and that saves raw group reward
  tensors so any published diagnostic can be \emph{recomputed}, not merely trusted.
  \item \textbf{Measure} the zero-variance phenomenon on real reward tensors, and
  test whether any re-baselining or curriculum lever can recover the wasted
  gradient into held-out accuracy.
  \item Establish a \textbf{scientific protocol} --- multi-seed, matched baselines,
  held-out evaluation with reported $n$, independent verification --- and apply it
  honestly, reporting negative and null results where they occur.
  \item Scope the most \emph{original} Phase-2 direction (a GRPO provenance /
  reporting standard) via a literature scan for open white space.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Non-Goals}
Phase-1 is deliberately a \emph{measurement and diagnostics} phase on small-to-mid
open models (Qwen2.5 / Qwen3.5 at $1.5$B--$4$B) and math/code verifiers (GSM8K,
a HumanEval subset). It is explicitly \textbf{not} an attempt to top a GRPO
leaderboard, nor to train a frontier reasoning model. Held-out evaluation sets are
intentionally small and every gain is reported with its denominator so that the
reader can see when a result is noise-limited. Novel-algorithm claims (P4 length
weighting, P7 controller) are \emph{designed} in Phase-1 and deferred to Phase-2
for powered evaluation. This honest scoping is itself a deliverable: it is what
makes the benchmark trustworthy.

\chapter{Literature Survey}
\label{ch:lit}

\section{From RLHF and PPO to Group-Relative Methods}
Policy-gradient post-training began with PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} and generalised
advantage estimation~\cite{schulman2015gae}, used at scale for instruction
tuning~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt}. PPO learns a value network (critic) to
baseline the advantage, which adds memory and a second optimisation problem. GRPO
\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} removed the learned value function and replaced the
per-token advantage with a group-relative baseline: it samples a \emph{group} of
$G$ completions per prompt and standardises each completion's reward against the
group mean and standard deviation. GRPO was central to the DeepSeek-R1 reasoning
models~\cite{guo2025r1}. RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024rloo} is a closely related
leave-one-out estimator that baselines each sample against the mean of the
\emph{other} samples in the group --- i.e.\ it already performs a form of
within-group baselining, which is directly relevant to our P2 analysis.

\section{Direct Preference Optimisation as a Contrast}
An orthogonal branch of post-training removes on-policy sampling entirely: Direct
Preference Optimisation (DPO)~\cite{rafailov2023dpo} optimises a classification
loss over fixed preference pairs, trading the exploration of on-policy RL for
stability and simplicity. The contrast is instructive for this project: DPO never
suffers zero-variance collapse because it does not standardise within a sampled
group, but it also cannot exploit a verifiable reward signal online. GRPO's
zero-variance pathology is thus the price of its on-policy, verifier-in-the-loop
design --- which is exactly what makes measuring that price worthwhile.

\section{Fixing GRPO's Biases and Collapse}
A fast-growing line of work targets specific GRPO pathologies. Dr.\
GRPO~\cite{liu2025drgrpo} removes the length and difficulty normalisation biases in
the standard objective. DAPO~\cite{yu2025dapo} introduces \emph{dynamic sampling},
explicitly resampling to avoid groups whose reward variance is zero, plus decoupled
clipping --- this is the most direct prior art for the ZVF problem we quantify in
P2 and the curriculum lever we test. Group-variance methods such as
GVPO~\cite{gvpo2025} re-weight by within-group variance. A cluster of very recent
variants (AERO~\cite{aero2026}, NGRPO~\cite{ngrpo2025},
$\lambda$-GRPO~\cite{lambdagrpo2025}, MC-GRPO~\cite{mcgrpo2026},
AVSPO~\cite{avspo2026}) attack advantage collapse, length bias, and rollout
accounting from different angles. The density of this literature is precisely why
Phase-1 narrows the crowded pillars (P2 zero-variance, P4 length bias) to
\emph{measurement} rather than a new algorithm.

\section{Verifiable Rewards, Frameworks, and Reporting}
RLVR was formalised for open post-training in T\"ULU~3~\cite{lambert2024tulu3}. The
implementations we unify are TRL~\cite{vonwerra2020trl}, veRL /
HybridFlow~\cite{sheng2024hybridflow}, and OpenRLHF~\cite{hu2024openrlhf}, plus
Tinker as a managed training back-end. Our evaluation tasks are
GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k} and a HumanEval~\cite{chen2021humaneval} subset. For the
reporting/provenance pillar (P5/P6) we build on Datasheets for
Datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} and Model Cards~\cite{mitchell2019modelcards},
but target the \emph{RL-run} object (verifier version, rollout provenance, reward
drift) that neither of those artefacts captures. Static run-config registries
(Weights \& Biases, MLflow) record hyperparameters but not the temporal, versioned
evolution of verifier logic and reward drift that P6 targets.

\section{Scaling and Layer-Adaptation Context}
Whole-model scaling and saturation laws describe how loss and capability track
parameters, data, and compute. P1 asks a narrower, orthogonal question: \emph{where}
inside the network does GRPO-style adaptation concentrate, and can the layers that
will matter be identified early enough to freeze the rest? This connects
parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA adapters on the attention projections) with
the question of predictive layer-freezing, and is the one pillar in the portfolio
that requires white-box per-layer gradient access.

\section{Process-, Tree-, and Stability-Aware Variants}
Beyond the group-baseline fixes above, a parallel line of work changes \emph{what}
signal drives the update. Process-reward and tree-search methods supply denser,
step-level feedback instead of a single terminal reward, which reduces the chance
that a whole group collapses to identical terminal rewards; stability-aware variants
constrain the update (e.g.\ trust-region or entropy controls) to avoid the
advantage-collapse and length-inflation failure modes that a raw group baseline can
induce. These methods are complementary to the measurement stance taken here: they
propose \emph{levers}, whereas this project first builds the \emph{instrumentation}
(ZVF, gradient utilisation, collapse decomposition) needed to tell whether any such
lever actually converts wasted rollouts into held-out accuracy. The Phase-1 nulls in
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} are precisely the kind of evidence this instrumentation is
designed to produce, and they caution against adopting a variant on the strength of a
single-seed improvement.

\section{Positioning the Portfolio}
Table~\ref{tab:position} states, per pillar, the closest prior art and the narrow
axis we claim. Two pillars (P2 zero-variance and P4 length bias) are crowded; there

\chapter{Proposed Methodology}

\section{Design Commitment: Attribution}
\label{sec:harness}
\input{tikz/fig1.tex}
The core design commitment is \emph{attribution}: hold the experiment fixed and vary
only the stack. A single configuration object specifies the model, dataset, reward
function, decoding parameters (temperature, top-$p$, max tokens), group size $G$,
learning rate, KL coefficient, and seed. A launcher (\texttt{unified/launcher.py})
maps that one config onto any of the supported back-ends through thin per-framework
adapters (\texttt{trl\_integrations/}, \texttt{verl/}, \texttt{openrlhf/},
\texttt{skyrl/}, \texttt{atropos/}), while \texttt{tinkerrl/grpo.py} implements the
reference GRPO loop against the managed Tinker back-end. Because the reward grader
and decoding are shared code, a difference in outcome between two back-ends is
attributable to the framework, not to a divergent reward parser or sampler.

\paragraph{Scope of what Phase-1 exercises.} The adapter layer for TRL, veRL, and
OpenRLHF is \emph{implemented}, but the empirical results in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}
are produced on the Tinker managed back-end (and a Colab GPU for the white-box P1
path). A head-to-head cross-framework divergence table --- identical config run on all
four back-ends --- is deferred to Phase-2; Phase-1 delivers the harness and the
attribution \emph{design}, and we are careful not to claim cross-framework results we
have not yet run.

\section{Per-Step Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry}
Every training step emits a telemetry record to JSONL and to Weights \& Biases. The
fields that distinguish this harness from a standard trainer are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{ZVF} --- the fraction of prompt-groups in the step whose within-group
  reward variance is zero (hence zero advantage, zero gradient);
  \item \textbf{gradient utilisation} --- the complementary fraction of the batch
  that actually contributed a non-zero update;
  \item \textbf{collapse decomposition} --- of the collapsed groups, how many are
  \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus \emph{all-wrong};
  \item plus reward, loss, entropy, completion length, and KL to the reference.
\end{itemize}
The raw group reward tensors are also saved, so any downstream diagnostic (e.g.\
\texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py}) can \emph{recompute} a published number from the
stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary.

\section{Formalising the Zero-Variance Fraction}
For a prompt $q$ we draw a group of $G$ completions $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^{G}$ from the
current policy and score each with a verifiable reward $r_i$. The group-relative
advantage is the within-group standardisation
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon},
\qquad
\mu_g = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j} r_j,\quad
\sigma_g = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_j (r_j-\mu_g)^2}.
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
(We write the population standard deviation; the sample-variance form $\tfrac{1}{G-1}$
differs only by a constant factor and does not change the zero-variance condition
$\sigma_g=0$ that this project measures.) Whenever $\sigma_g = 0$ --- every completion in the group receives the same reward ---
every $A_i = 0$, so the whole group contributes no gradient \emph{through the
advantage (surrogate) term}; only the KL-penalty term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} is
unaffected. The Zero-Variance
Fraction is exactly the batch-mean of the indicator $\mathbb{1}[\sigma_g = 0]$:
\begin{equation}
\zvf \;=\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{g \in B} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\sigma_g = 0\right].
\label{eq:zvf}
\end{equation}
This makes ``wasted compute'' a single, loggable scalar --- the measurement around
which the entire portfolio is organised.

\section{The Eight-Study Portfolio}
\label{sec:portfolio}
\input{tikz/fig7_zvf.tex}
\textbf{P1 --- Scaling / layer dynamics.} A white-box study of \emph{where} a model
adapts under GRPO-style updates, recording per-layer LoRA gradient norms to test
whether the layers that will matter can be identified early (predictive
layer-freezing). Requires per-layer gradient access, so it runs on a local GPU
rather than the managed back-end.

\textbf{P2 --- Zero-variance / gradient waste.} Quantifies ZVF on real reward
tensors and asks whether any re-baselining scheme can recover gradient from
collapsed groups.

\textbf{P3 --- Group size.} Studies how collapse, reward, held-out gain, and token
cost vary with the group size $G$, and reframes $G$ as a compute budget rather than
an integer.

\textbf{P4 --- Length bias (designed).} Proposes semantic-density /
surprise-weighted token normalisation instead of uniform or static length penalties.

\textbf{P5 --- Reporting / provenance (flagship, systems).} A GRPO post-training
datasheet plus cryptographic rollout provenance, so one can prove which verifier
version and which rollouts produced a checkpoint.
\input{tikz/fig6.tex}

\textbf{P6 --- Registry.} A versioned, temporal registry of evolving run state
(verifier-logic updates, reward drift), intended to fold into the P5 standard.

\textbf{P7 --- Controller (designed).} Classical PID control of decoding temperature
and clip range, using live advantage-variance / KL-drift as sensor inputs.

\textbf{P8 --- Integrity / anomaly.} An auditor that uses the per-step telemetry
(not just reward) to detect anomalous / manipulated runs.

\chapter{Implementation Details}

\section{The Tinker GRPO Loop}
\label{sec:impl-loop}
\input{tikz/fig2.tex}
\input{tikz/fig3.tex}
The reference loop in \texttt{tinkerrl/grpo.py} follows the standard
sample~$\rightarrow$~advantage~$\rightarrow$~loss~$\rightarrow$~optimise cycle. For a
prompt $q$ we draw a group of $G$ completions $\{o_i\}_{i=1}^{G}$ from the current
policy and score each with the verifiable reward $r_i$; the group-relative advantage
is Eq.~\eqref{eq:adv}. The optimisation objective is the PPO-clipped surrogate with
a KL penalty to a frozen reference policy $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) &\;=\;
-\,\mathbb{E}_{i,t}\!\left[
\min\!\big(\rho_{i,t}A_i,\;
\mathrm{clip}(\rho_{i,t},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_i\big)
\right]
\;+\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\big),
\label{eq:loss}\\[0.2em]
\text{where}\quad
\rho_{i,t} &=\frac{\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})}.
\nonumber
\end{align}
The critical observation, made mechanical by Eq.~\eqref{eq:adv}, is that whenever
$\sigma_g = 0$ every $A_i = 0$, so the whole group contributes nothing to the
\emph{surrogate} term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}; the KL-penalty term still applies, so
the update is a pure pull toward the reference rather than reward-driven learning.

\section{Parallel Runner and a Real Logging Defect}
\label{sec:runner}
To sweep configurations (e.g.\ $G \in \{2,4,8,16\}$ or three seeds $\times$ two arms)
within a fixed wall-clock budget, \texttt{parallel\_sweep.py} and \texttt{campaign.py}
launch runs concurrently. An early threaded runner hit a real bug: Weights \& Biases
keeps a thread-unsafe global \texttt{run} object, so with four threads calling
\texttt{wandb.init(reinit=True)} the summary writes for later runs failed (``Run is
finished'') even though per-step logs survived. The fix, used by the multi-seed
\texttt{campaign.py}, is one \emph{process} per run rather than one thread. We report
this because it is exactly the kind of silent logging failure the benchmark is meant
to surface (see the P3 caveat in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).

\section{The Colab White-Box Path (P1)}
\label{sec:colab}
P1 needs per-layer gradient norms, which the managed Tinker back-end does not expose.
It therefore runs on a Colab L4 GPU (\texttt{p1\_layer\_profile.py}):
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with LoRA adapters on the $q/k/v/o$ projections, five

\section{P2 --- Gradient Waste is Massive and Easy-Driven}
On real reward tensors for four methods (40 steps each, $16$ prompts $\times\,8$
samples, binary verifiable reward on GSM8K), the mean zero-variance fraction is
$\zvf = 0.72$--$0.77$: roughly three-quarters of every batch contributes no
gradient. The collapse is \emph{easy-driven}, not chance: about $65$--$71\%$ of
prompts are \emph{all-correct} (already solved) versus only ${\sim}5\%$ all-wrong.
We then tested whether a re-baselining trick could recover gradient from collapsed
groups. A naive cross-prompt baseline (advantage against the global mean) gives
$|A|\approx0.21$, but that ``signal'' is just a proxy for prompt
easiness/difficulty --- not a learning signal --- which is precisely what
RLOO~\cite{ahmadian2024rloo} already captures. A difficulty-isocline baseline
correctly recovers $|A|\approx0.012$--$0.02$, i.e.\ essentially zero, confirming that
\emph{no recoverable gradient exists} in these groups. \textbf{Honest redirect:}
re-baselining is the wrong lever; the data point instead to difficulty-targeted
sampling / curriculum, motivating the experiments below.

\section{Curriculum --- Eliminating Waste is Not a Free Lunch}
A curriculum that trains only on mixed-variance (non-collapsed) groups does exactly
what it is designed to do: it drives the zero-loss fraction from $0.50$ to $0.00$.
But it pays $4.81\times$ the sampling cost (oversampling to \emph{find} non-collapsed
groups) and delivers an \emph{identical} held-out gain to the baseline ($+0.05$ vs
$+0.05$, held-out $n=20$, one seed, both gains equal to one extra correct answer).
Eliminating the visible gradient waste does not, by itself, buy accuracy: the
sampling overhead offsets the gradient-density benefit.

re-tested the curriculum claim with statistical power. Across three seeds at $G{=}4$
(Table~\ref{tab:campaign}), the baseline mean held-out gain is $+0.028$ (sd $0.10$)
and the curriculum mean is $-0.028$ (sd $0.14$). With only three seeds per arm and
outcomes quantised to ${\sim}0.083$ (one held-out example of $12$), we deliberately
report \emph{descriptive} statistics rather than a parametric test: the arms overlap
almost entirely, the between-arm difference (${\sim}0.056$) is smaller than the
within-arm spread, and the sign of the curriculum effect flips across seeds
($+0.167,\,-0.083,\,-0.167$). We therefore treat the two arms as indistinguishable
on this setup. The promising single-seed
curriculum result ($+0.167$ at seed 0) was noise --- seeds 1 and 2 were negative. The
naive ``drop collapsed groups and oversample'' lever is therefore \emph{not worth}
its $5$--$6\times$ cost. Held-out gains on this setup are within noise throughout
($|\text{gain}|\le0.167$, i.e.\ at most $1$--$2$ of $12$ held-out examples), which is
fully consistent with the ``diagnostic benchmark, not a GRPO-wins leaderboard''
framing.

\section{P1 --- Step-1 Predictability Holds at Toy Scale but Collapses at 3B}
\label{sec:p1}
\input{tikz/fig4.tex}
The white-box layer profile at toy scale (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 28 layers, 5 GRPO-style
steps, a hardcoded arithmetic set) initially looked like the clearest positive in
the portfolio: the top-$k$ highest-gradient layers after a \emph{single} step were
identical to the top-$k$ over all steps
(\texttt{step1\_predicts\_final\_topk\_overlap} $=1.0$), suggesting one could decide
what to freeze after one update. \textbf{This did not survive scaling.} A powered
re-test (Qwen2.5-3B, 36 layers, 10 steps, \emph{real} GSM8K, seeds $\{0,1\}$) drops
the step-1$\to$final overlap to $0.11$ on both seeds --- i.e.\ ${\approx}$chance, with
only layer~0 persisting. The mid-late band that dominates by end-of-training
\emph{emerges} over steps and is invisible at step~1, so the apparent predictability
was an artifact of the toy setup. Adaptation is only \emph{moderately} concentrated:
the top $25\%$ of layers carry ${\sim}48\%$ of the total gradient norm
(\texttt{concentration\_top25pct\_share} $=0.476$ at 1.5B, $0.39$ at 3B), not the
$>90\%$ that a ``freeze almost everything'' story would need.

\section{P8 --- Telemetry Beats Reward-Only for Integrity}
Using the per-step telemetry rather than reward alone, the integrity auditor reaches
AUROC $0.84$, versus $0.43$ for a reward-only baseline. This supports the premise
that internal training signal (ZVF trajectory, entropy, KL drift) carries
information about run integrity that the reward curve does not. The result is
recomputed from the raw \texttt{n2\_reward\_tensor\_resume} tensors by
\texttt{p8\_detector.py}: four reward-optimisation algorithms each contribute $40$
per-step rows ($N=160$); the positive ``surrogate'' class is three GRPO variants
reported as GRPO ($120$ rows) and the negative class is genuine GRPO ($40$ rows), a
$3{:}1$ imbalance. Features are derived entirely from the raw reward/length/advantage
arrays (\texttt{zvf}, \texttt{reward\_mean}, \texttt{reward\_std},
\texttt{frac\_all\_zero}, \texttt{frac\_all\_one}, \texttt{mean\_len},
\texttt{cv\_len}, \texttt{len\_reward\_corr}, \texttt{adv\_abs\_mean},
\texttt{adv\_std}, \texttt{lag1\_autocorr}). A logistic-regression detector
(median-imputed, standardised, $5$-fold stratified CV) reaches
$\mathrm{AUROC}=0.838\pm0.010$, versus $0.426\pm0.027$ for a reward-only auditor
(below chance). \textbf{Overfitting caveat:} with $N=160$ rows, $11$ features, and a
$3{:}1$ class imbalance, the AUROC is a cross-validated estimate on a small sample and
is susceptible to optimism; it should be read as a \emph{proof-of-concept} that
internal telemetry is informative, not as a deployable detector. A larger, multi-setup
evaluation on a genuinely held-out set (diverse base models/tasks, thousands of steps)
is required before the auditor is trusted. Remaining provenance caveat: the base
model/task are not recorded in the tensor rows, so provenance is incomplete --- exactly
the gap the P5 protocol is designed to close.

\section{Cross-Study Synthesis}
Read together, the eight studies tell a coherent story rather than eight isolated
results. P2 establishes that the dominant, reproducible phenomenon is gradient waste
($\zvf\approx0.72$--$0.77$), and that it is \emph{easy-driven} (the collapsed groups
are mostly already-solved prompts). That immediately rules out the tempting fix ---
re-baselining --- because P2 also shows the ``recovered'' signal is just a difficulty
proxy with no learning content. The natural next lever, a curriculum that removes the
waste, is exactly what the curriculum and campaign studies test; both return honest
nulls, and the campaign shows \emph{why} single-seed enthusiasm is dangerous (the
$+0.167$ seed-0 result reverses at seeds 1--2). P3 tells the same cautionary tale for
group size: an apparent $G{=}4$ sweet spot evaporates under replication. P1 extends
the pattern to a structural claim --- step-1 predictability of layer adaptation ---
which likewise fails to survive scaling. The one lever that \emph{does} survive is P8:
using the internal telemetry (including the ZVF trajectory) to audit run integrity
beats a reward-only baseline decisively. The synthesis, then, is that on this
compute scale the \emph{measurement} is trustworthy and valuable while most
\emph{algorithmic} levers are noise-limited --- which is what justifies steering
Phase-2 toward the systems/provenance direction (P5) rather than chasing another
GRPO variant.

\section{Rigour and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:rigor}
The methodology is deliberately adversarial toward its own results.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed with matched baselines.} Every headline learning claim is
  re-tested across $\ge 3$ seeds against a baseline that differs only in the lever
  under study. This is what converted the single-seed ``curriculum wins / $G{=}4$ is
  best'' story into the honest null above.
  \item \textbf{Held-out evaluation.} Gains are measured on held-out prompts, and we
  report the held-out $n$ ($8$--$20$) alongside every gain so the reader can see that
  magnitudes are noise-limited.
  \item \textbf{Recompute-from-tensors.} Published diagnostics are recomputable from
  stored raw tensors (e.g.\ \texttt{p2\_collapse\_analysis.py}), not merely read back
  from a logged summary.
  \item \textbf{Independent verification.} Findings were re-checked by two frontier
  models used as adversarial auditors. They returned a \textbf{SUSPECT} verdict on
  the early single-seed P2/P3/curriculum conclusions --- correctly identifying them as
  underpowered --- which directly triggered the powered campaign re-test. The
  recomputed mechanics (ZVF $0.72$--$0.77$, all-correct $0.65$--$0.71$, naive
  $|A|\approx0.21$, curriculum $0.50\to0.00$ at $4.81\times$) all reproduced exactly.
\end{itemize}
Known threats we do \emph{not} hide: small held-out sets; a partially-recorded P3
sweep artefact (G8/G16 summaries lost to the W\&B thread-safety bug); a deterministic
prompt schedule shared across the four P2 methods; and an invalidated
\texttt{zero\_loss\_frac} in the first campaign batch (since fixed). Re-running P3
under the process-per-run launcher yields corrected G8/G16 held-out gains of
$+0.083$ (both from $0.750\to0.833$, single seed), superseding the thread-bug-affected
values --- but these remain single-seed point estimates pending multi-seed
replication.

\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusions}
This phase delivered a working unified GRPO/RLVR benchmark, a per-step diagnostic
telemetry layer, and eight studies whose honest verdict is: the algorithmic levers we
could test on this setup (naive curriculum, integer group-size sweeps) do \emph{not}
produce reliable held-out gains, while the large and reproducible effect is the
\emph{gradient waste} itself ($\zvf \approx 0.72$--$0.77$). Reporting those negatives
--- rather than the cherry-picked single-seed positives --- is the contribution: it is
what makes the portfolio defensible and the benchmark trustworthy. The single
clearest positive is P8: internal telemetry carries integrity information that the
reward curve does not (AUROC $0.84$ vs.\ $0.43$).

\paragraph{Where the original value is.} The strongest \emph{original} bet is
\textbf{P5, a GRPO provenance / reporting standard}, which the literature scan
confirms as open white space (no GRPO-specific machine-readable datasheet or
rollout-provenance protocol exists). The earlier second bet --- \textbf{P1 predictive
layer-freezing} --- is now weakened: its step-1 predictability held at 1.5B but
collapsed to chance ($0.11$) on the scaled 3B multi-seed re-test.

\section{Future Work (Phase-2 Plan)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Token-budget-optimal curriculum.} Replace naive ``filter collapsed
  groups'' with an allocation that trades oversampling against training under an
  explicit token budget with staleness bounds, tested multi-seed from the start. The
  Phase-1 null tells us the lever must be \emph{budget-aware}, not just
  waste-eliminating.
  \item \textbf{Re-examine P1.} The scaled 3B / multi-seed re-test shows step-1
  predictability does \emph{not} hold; the open question is whether the \emph{emerged}
  mid-late band supports any FLOP-saving freeze schedule when read off late-training
  gradients rather than step~1.
  \item \textbf{The P5 protocol.} Specify and implement the GRPO post-training
  datasheet plus cryptographic rollout provenance, and dogfood it on the harness's own
  runs so every benchmark result ships with a verifiable provenance record.
  \item \textbf{Adequately powered evaluation.} The most important methodological
  upgrade for Phase-2 is statistical power: move from held-out $n=8$--$20$ to the full
  GSM8K test split (or a large stratified subset), run $\ge 5$ seeds, and report
  confidence intervals on every headline metric, so that a genuine effect can be
  distinguished from the noise floor that dominates the Phase-1 nulls.
  \item \textbf{Cross-framework divergence study.} Execute the identical
  task/reward/decoding/seed configuration on TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF, and Tinker and publish
  the divergence table --- the concrete demonstration of the attribution claim that
  Phase-1 designs but does not yet run.
  \item \textbf{Harden the P8 auditor.} Re-train and evaluate the integrity detector on a
  much larger, multi-model, multi-task corpus with a held-out test split (not CV-only) to
  establish whether the AUROC $0.84$ proof-of-concept generalises.
  \item \textbf{Publication.} Consolidate the diagnostic findings and the P5 standard
  into a paper draft targeting a reputed peer-reviewed venue, as required by the
  programme guidelines.
\end{enumerate}
