2025–2026 Novelty Survey on GRPO and RLVR for LLM Post-Training Survey frame and top-line conclusions I surveyed recent primary sources spanning arXiv, OpenReview, ACL Anthology, and official proceedings pages, with emphasis on 2025–2026 work touching GRPO, RLVR, and closely adjacent LLM post-training methods. The strongest concentration of papers is on algorithmic pathologies and optimizer tweaks—especially zero-advantage collapse, group size, exploration allocation, and length bias—while the weakest coverage is on documentation standards, machine-readable run schemas, and integrity/audit benchmarks for training runs themselves. A second pattern is that most empirical evidence still comes from math-heavy RLVR and a relatively narrow set of base models, especially Qwen-family checkpoints, so claims that appear “general” are often only lightly validated across domains, verifiers, or hardware stacks. 1 The highest-value originality opportunities are therefore not in “another minor GRPO variant for math reasoning,” unless it comes with a strong theoretical or systems angle. The truly open areas are the ones where the literature is missing shared artifacts: a GRPO-specific reporting standard, a machine-readable RLVR run schema/registry, and a labeled integrity benchmark for compromised RL training runs. Finds RLVR parameter trajectories are extremely low-rank and uses that to extrapolate beyond observed training prefixes with very low additional cost. 8 What now looks established Across several papers, RL post-training curves are not arbitrary: they often show a small number of recognizable phases—slow start, rapid gain, plateau—and a large share of their variance is predictable from early training. Larger models usually give better returns under a fixed compute budget, and both step allocation and rollout allocation matter, not just raw GPU-hours. At the same time, the literature has not converged on one universal law: some papers emphasize sigmoidal reward curves, some emphasize prescriptive compute-allocation laws, and some emphasize near-linear or low-rank trajectory geometry.
