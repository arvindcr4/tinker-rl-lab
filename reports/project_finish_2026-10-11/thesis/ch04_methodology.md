# 4. Proposed Methodology

This chapter sets out the methodology of the work rather than its results. It presents, in order: the design commitment to attribution and the four de-confound pillars that operationalise it (§4.1); the per-step telemetry that makes a run's internal state observable at the granularity at which the mechanism acts (§4.2); the formalisation of the Zero-Variance Fraction as a descriptive diagnostic rather than a metric (§4.3); the seven-study RL portfolio P1–P7 and the question each study asks (§4.4); and the design of the E1–E14 evaluation campaign, including its single trained actor, pinned native evaluators, frozen sampling, receipt model, terminal-state taxonomy and decontamination handling (§4.5). Throughout, the reporting discipline is part of the method: figures are tied to named evidence, unavailable primary artefacts are distinguished from surviving summaries (Appendix B §B.11), and lanes without an admissible result are recorded as blocked.

## 4.1 The Design Commitment to Attribution

The premise of the project is that an RL-for-LLM result is not identified by the name of its algorithm. Two runs both labelled GRPO may differ in loss form, reference-policy handling, sampler, precision, base-checkpoint identity, data order and group-size schedule, and the label alone does not say which of those differences produced the outcome. The project's response is a design commitment rather than a post-hoc covariate adjustment: hold the experimental stack fixed except the intended contrast, then disclose any coupled changes that prevent attribution to that factor alone (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). This commitment is the methodological spine of the whole thesis, and it has a practical corollary that shapes every experiment reported later: a study is defined by the single axis it varies and is only interpretable to the extent that every other axis was genuinely pinned.

The commitment is implemented in a unified launcher, `platform_local/unified/launcher.py`, which consumes a single configuration object and dispatches it to thin per-framework adapters — `trl_integrations/`, `verl/`, `platform_modal/openrlhf/`, `platform_hybrid/skyrl/` and `platform_tinker/atropos/` — with the reference GRPO loop implemented once in `platform_tinker/tinkerrl/grpo.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The reward grader and the decoding path are shared code. Shared grading and decoding configuration are intended controls. They are not sufficient to identify a framework effect: the executed framework comparison retains a Base-versus-Instruct checkpoint difference and unresolved managed settings (§5.2), and completed-run parity exists only for Tinker and TRL. That comparison therefore describes the recorded stacks, not an isolated causal effect of framework. The commitment also bounds what a result may claim. A completed run supports a statement about that stack, at that revision, with that grader; it does not license a statement about "GRPO" in the abstract, and the project's reporting standard (§4.4, P5) is built around that distinction.

Four pillars organise the commitment, with the actual controls and coupled changes stated for each contrast (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/submissions/mtech-final-review/build_review_pptx.py, slide 7):

Pillar 1's target estimand is the effect of changing the advantage estimator while fixing the relevant treatment components. The original Qwen2.5-0.5B addition comparison does not identify that estimand: evaluation comes from the training generator, item pairing is broken, overlap differs between arms, and performance is saturated. It is excluded as estimator evidence, including as a bounded null (§6.2.3). The disjoint-test GSM8K v1 experiment improves the split design but changes unique prompt exposure and uses a minimal cold-start critic under a binding cap. The existing v2 snapshot improves cap and critic preparation but remains incomplete; Table 6.2 is provisional, and the prompt-exposure control is not yet available. A definitive comparison would separately hold prompts fixed and match realised generated-token budgets, use a documented token-level critic/GAE baseline alongside the minimalist baseline, and freeze identical disjoint evaluation items before training. Those are unexecuted requirements, not repairs performed by this manuscript revision.

Pillar 2 measures the reward contrast actually present in a rollout group; it is formalised below. Pillar 3 relates measured contrast to group size and cost without promoting contaminated arithmetic accuracy or untrained G ≥ 32 projections. Pillar 4 separates length censoring, held-out numerical accuracy and semantic reasoning quality. A result is admitted only for the estimand its design actually supports.

The headline configuration of the internal report sources is LoRA rank 32, Adam with β₁ = 0.9 and β₂ = 0.95, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴, and the seed set {42, 123, 456, 789, 1024} (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). It is not held fixed across the programme. The canonical dispatch matrix uses rank 16 and learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁶, the stack probe uses 1 × 10⁻⁵, the P4 scale extension uses rank 4, and the Tinker runners default to 3 × 10⁻⁵; Table 4.2 distinguishes reported settings, inspected candidate implementations and unresolved historical execution bindings. The instrumented 79-run roster described in that source spans the Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Llama-3.x, DeepSeek-V3.1 and Nemotron-120B families, from 0.6B to approximately 671B total parameters, including mixture-of-experts anchors at the frontier end of that range (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex); the wider P1 cross-scale roster adds a 1T anchor (Kimi-K2) and so spans 0.6B to 1T (Chapter 6 §6.1; source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). The statistical apparatus is declared in the shared methods source but was not pre-registered (Chapter 9 §9.2; the analysis log records 206 iterations). It consists of Welch t-tests and Mann–Whitney U tests for two-sample contrasts; Cohen's d with Hedges–Olkin intervals; 95% bootstrap confidence intervals with 10,000 resamples; rliable aggregate metrics (interquartile mean, median and optimality gap); and Bonferroni and Benjamini–Hochberg corrections over a declared test family (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Power was computed rather than assumed: for a two-sided, unpaired, equal-variance Student $t$-test at $\alpha = 0.05$ and 80% power, the minimum detectable standardised effect for five seeds against five (df = 8) is $d_{\min} = 2.024$, improving to $d_{\min} = 1.530$ when a five-seed group is compared against a pooled group of fifteen (pooled df = 18; a Welch test has fewer degrees of freedom and a larger $d_{\min}$; seed-paired designs use their own df) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). Those figures are a standing reminder of how coarse a five-seed design is, and smaller effects have lower detection probability, rather than being impossible to detect. Nonsignificance does not establish why an individual test failed to reject.

Two limitations are structural. First, the framework roster is a design inventory: only Tinker and TRL have completed runs in the historical four-row comparison, and they used different checkpoints. Second, marginal one-way effects from the unbalanced historical 42-record corpus cannot partition framework, algorithm, model and scale. The opening effect-size chart is replaced by an evidence-availability view; no partial-effect estimate is invented without a reconstructable design matrix and an explicit estimand. The historical twenty-test family and its source figures remain audit history, not confirmatory findings (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex; §6.1).

## 4.2 Per-Step Telemetry

Attribution is only as good as the run's observability. The intended per-step telemetry record contains the Zero-Variance Fraction; gradient utilisation; the collapse decomposition that separates all-correct from all-wrong zero-variance groups; and, alongside them, reward, loss, entropy, completion length and the KL divergence to the reference policy when one exists and that measurement is exposed; actual availability is runner-specific (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). Records are written to JSONL and mirrored to Weights & Biases, so a run can be analysed locally without a network dependency and reviewed remotely without a local environment. The reason ZVF and its decomposition are logged as separate fields rather than collapsed into a single starvation number is that the same aggregate fraction can describe opposite situations: a group of completions that are all correct and a group that are all wrong both contribute zero within-group variance, and the remedy for each is different. Logging the decomposition preserves that distinction at the point of collection instead of trying to recover it afterwards.

For the standardised group-relative form, the reporting convention in this revision is:

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g + \varepsilon_A},\qquad \mu_g = \frac{1}{G}\sum_j r_j,\qquad \sigma_g = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G}\sum_j (r_j - \mu_g)^2}$$

Here $\sigma_g^2=v_{0,g}=G^{-1}\sum_i(r_{g,i}-\mu_g)^2$ is population variance (`ddof=0`), and $\varepsilon_A$ is a denominator stabiliser in reward units. This is a notation and reporting convention, not a claim that all historical runners used one implementation. Some centre without dividing by standard deviation; some diagnostics use sample variance; the PPO comparator whitens across its batch; managed settings may be unknown. Table 4.2 and the convention ledger below distinguish those cases. Exact ties give zero group-relative advantages in both centred and standardised forms. KL, critic/auxiliary losses, weight decay and optimiser state remain separate; a zero advantage is not proof of zero parameter change.

Two further design choices distinguish this telemetry from conventional training curves. The first is a design goal to retain raw per-group reward tensors alongside summaries. Availability is incomplete and is stated per study. Where tensors survive, a published number can be recomputed: any downstream diagnostic, such as `p2_collapse_analysis.py`, can reproduce a reported statistic from the stored tensors rather than trusting a logged summary (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The convention has been exercised: study P2 recomputes three stored GSM8K group tensors (Qwen3-8B sampling runs, G = 8) from their raw values rather than reading the figures back from a log line, and the recomputed values are reported in Chapter 6 §6.3.1 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The second is that the logging layer is treated as part of the apparatus and is expected to fail. An early Weights & Biases integration used the library's thread-unsafe global run object from multiple worker threads, which produced repeated "Run is finished" summary-write failures; the fix was to give each run its own process in `campaign.py` (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex). The defect is recorded because telemetry that silently drops writes is indistinguishable from telemetry showing a healthy run, and the remedy — process-level isolation per run — is a structural property of the campaign rather than a patch.

## 4.3 Formalising the Zero-Variance Fraction

**Executed-treatment boundary.** Algorithm names below denote the inspected objective variants, not interchangeable implementations of every published algorithm. Table 4.2 records ratio granularity, advantage and KL/reference differences. Where historical executed-source identity is unavailable, that binding remains unknown; a historical checkpoint-only refresh policy, if executed, would introduce behaviour-policy lag. The current repaired reference refreshes its sampler every step; that code does not establish the historical actor’s policy.

Define the exact-tie statistic independently of any tolerance:

$$\mathrm{ZVF}_{0,t}=\frac{1}{|B_t|}\sum_{g\in B_t}\mathbf{1}[r_{g,1}=\cdots=r_{g,G}].$$

For finite rewards and $G\ge2$, the thresholded diagnostic in this revision is

$$\mathrm{ZVF}^{(0)}_{\tau,t}=\frac{1}{|B_t|}\sum_{g\in B_t}\mathbf{1}[v_{0,g}\le\tau],\qquad v_{0,g}=\frac1G\sum_i(r_{g,i}-\mu_g)^2,$$

with $\tau=10^{-6}$ in squared reward units when rewards are normalised to $[0,1]$. Superscript $(0)$ identifies `ddof=0`; it is not an iteration index. The threshold $\tau$ is distinct from the advantage stabiliser $\varepsilon_A$. Near-ties counted by $\mathrm{ZVF}^{(0)}_\tau$ can have nonzero advantages. Exact binary ties at the small group sizes used here are classified identically by the historical tiny thresholds, but continuous or shaped rewards require the full convention.

The following reference is a source-only specification for new reporting. Historical scores and code paths have not been rewritten to match it.

\Needspace{18\baselineskip}

```
import numpy as np

def zvf(rewards, tau=1e-6):
    r = np.asarray(rewards, dtype=np.float64)
    if r.ndim != 2 or r.shape[0] == 0 or r.shape[1] < 2:
        raise ValueError("nonempty groups with G >= 2 required")
    if not np.isfinite(r).all() or not np.isfinite(tau) or tau < 0:
        raise ValueError("finite rewards / tau >= 0 required")
    exact = np.all(r == r[:, :1], axis=1)
    near = r.var(axis=1, ddof=0) <= tau
    return {"zvf_exact": float(exact.mean()),
            "zvf_var_ddof0": float(near.mean()), "tau": tau}
```

For $v_{1,g}=Gv_{0,g}/(G-1)$ (sample variance), exact ties are invariant, but equal positive cutoffs satisfy $\mathrm{ZVF}^{(1)}_\tau\le\mathrm{ZVF}^{(0)}_\tau$. Equal classification requires $\tau_1=G\tau_0/(G-1)$. Switching only the advantage denominator gives

$$\frac{A_i^{(1)}}{A_i^{(0)}}=\frac{\sqrt{v_{0,g}}+\varepsilon_A}{\sqrt{G/(G-1)}\sqrt{v_{0,g}}+\varepsilon_A}$$

for nonzero numerators. This tends to $\sqrt{(G-1)/G}$ when the stabiliser is negligible, or 0.7071 at G = 2. No recorded learning-rate compensation for a convention switch was established. This advantage scaling is not an exact scaling law for Adam parameter updates. The stored sweep is therefore interpreted under its executed normalisation, not under a silently substituted sample variance.

Gradient utilisation is the corresponding complement, $\mathrm{GU}_{0,t}=1-\mathrm{ZVF}_{0,t}$ for exact ties and $\mathrm{GU}^{(0)}_{\tau,t}=1-\mathrm{ZVF}^{(0)}_{\tau,t}$ for a threshold (source: platform_hybrid/paper/neurips_2026_variants/paper_P8_workshop.tex). The complement is a descriptive accounting identity, not a claim about optimiser behaviour. Three facts in the table govern how ZVF is interpreted. First, an exact-tie group has zero reward-relative surrogate gradient only where the runner actually centres advantages within that group. Near-ties counted by $\mathrm{ZVF}^{(0)}_{\tau}$ need not have exactly zero advantages. TRL's dump records β = 0.04 and an on-device reference model, its launcher leaves β unspecified, the PPO roster inherits adaptive-KL defaults, and the managed dump has β = null. The Phase-1 four-method run divides by no σ even in its "grpo" arm. The scope must consequently be checked per row, including auxiliary losses, masks and source-binding gaps, rather than inferred from the algorithm label.

Budget matching is defined per experiment rather than inferred from a common step count. The primary arithmetic G sweep uses 16 prompts per step for 40 steps, so G = 2, 4, 8 and 16 require 1,280, 2,560, 5,120 and 10,240 generated completions respectively; it is a fixed-prompt, fixed-step sweep, not a matched-token comparison. The original PPO/GRPO comparison fixes 128 completions per step but changes prompt exposure; the later GSM8K comparison fixes 64 completions per step with the same issue. The separate Qwen3-8B panel fixes 2,560 rollouts while changing optimiser steps, and the curriculum re-test explicitly fixes a 30k-token budget. Equal rollout counts do not establish equal generated tokens, wall-clock cost or optimisation opportunity. Each result is interpreted against the budget it actually controls (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_groupsize_zvf_sweep.py; platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_samestack_ppo_grpo.py; platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_samestack_gsm8k_cot.py; §6.3.3; §6.4.3).

Three properties make the definition more than a restatement of the training loop. First, it is non-tautological under a simple null: if completions were independent Bernoulli draws with per-prompt success probability $p_g$, then $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ZVF}_t] = \mathbb{E}_{p_g}[p_g^K + (1-p_g)^K]$, a quantity that depends on the model's accuracy distribution and the group size rather than on the observed batch (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). The identity $\mathrm{pass@}G - p^G = 1 - h_G(p)$ follows algebraically for the model expectations under the same i.i.d.-Bernoulli model and is therefore reported as an algebraic remark rather than as evidence (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Second, it is checked against that null on instrumented runs: a Qwen2.5-0.5B sweep over G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}, three seeds and 40 steps — twelve runs and 480 logged steps — compares per-step observed ZVF with the Bernoulli-null prediction $p^G + (1-p)^G$ (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). Because the null for each step is computed from the same step's mean reward, a high correlation is partly mechanical, so the comparison is designed as a shape check rather than a validation, and the baseline used is the per-step average of the closed form (results in Chapter 6 §6.3.3). Third, at larger scale the same instrument is run on Qwen3-8B against GSM8K test problems through the Tinker managed API at G = 8, temperature 1.0, over 200 problems and three seeds (sampling only, no training; these are the same three stored tensors whose ZVF P2 recomputes), with the zero-variance groups decomposed into all-correct and all-wrong and compared with the same null (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex; results in Chapter 6 §6.3.3). Deviations from the null in either direction are not read as evidence about dependence between completions.

That the quantity is portable across libraries is a separate, weaker claim, and the project states its limits explicitly. A normaliser at `platform_modal/scripts/zvf_compute_cross_framework.py` maps each framework's reward and mask fields onto the common definition — TRL's `rewards` with `batch_size / group_size` and `completion_mask`; Tinker's `rollout.reward` with `rollout.group_id` and `rollout.eos_mask`; OpenRLHF's `reward_score` with `prompt_index` and `attention_mask`; veRL's `data_source/reward` with `uid` and `response_mask` — and emits JSONL records of the form `{step, zvf, batch_reward_mean, n_groups, K}` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). The scope correction attached to that parser is that it normalises reward logs and does not make its runner canonical GRPO; because the project does not bundle raw per-group rollout JSONL for every framework, no fresh four-way pointwise ZVF overlay is claimed, only the narrower implementation-level portability claim (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). The threshold ε is fixed in advance, and its sensitivity is reported with the P2 results in Chapter 6 §6.3.1 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf_pipeline_spec.tex). Boundary conditions are declared where the diagnostic does not apply: dense or continuous reward signals, K = 1, SFT-saturated baselines and non-outcome-reward RL all fall outside the setting in which the definition is meaningful (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex).

The methodological point of this section is that ZVF is a diagnostic and not a metric. The design commits to that position before the results rather than inferring it from them, and it takes four precautions. First, because the statistic is unsigned, a high value is read as consistent with either a solved or an unsolved task and never as a score in one direction; the heterogeneous 368-record export is audited in Chapter 7 §7.1 and §7.3 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex). Second, a pooled association between ZVF and performance is always broken down by task type before it is read, because the task mix of a pool can set the sign of the association (results in Chapter 6 §6.3.4). Third, the historical pooled association is retained only as an audited source result: it mixes synthetic cells and heterogeneous outcomes, including late training reward, and cannot validate held-out prediction (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex; results in Chapter 6 §6.3.4). Fourth, no step-level p-value is computed on the 480 autocorrelated steps of the instrumented sweep, because treating correlated within-run steps as independent samples would manufacture significance (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). One further boundary is recorded for the same reason: a variance-mitigation table in the internal report sources is a declared simulation projection and not evidence, and its rows are read as synthetic rather than as independent library deployments (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex; §6.3.2). The result of these choices is a diagnostic that is useful for describing where within a batch the learning signal is and is not, and that is explicitly not proposed as a quantity to be maximised, ranked or reported as a headline score.

## 4.4 The Seven Core RL Studies

Semester 3 delivered the shared code and the initial experimental foundation; the Semester 4 programme — the research direction, the expanded experiments, the analysis and the P1–P8 internal reports — is the present author's solo continuation, and the roster records 206 analysis iterations behind the numbers reported in this thesis (source: platform_hybrid/sem 4 work/README.md). The core portfolio comprises seven RL studies, each documented in an unpublished internal report with its own question and evidence boundary; the titles below are working titles. Their results are in Chapters 6–7. The unrelated supervised side study is archived separately in Appendix F. The questions and boundaries are stated here because they are methodological commitments: a study's question determines which comparisons it may make, and the boundary states what it declines to conclude.

P1 asks whether post-training outcomes admit a useful cross-scale law. Its historical roster is too heterogeneous to answer: interrupted three-to-five-step traces, single-seed frontier records, and scale-dependent recipes do not identify asymptotic performance. The revised analysis does not fit the interrupted anchors or interpret an unsuccessful fit as evidence that scaling is absent. P1 is retained as a design and identifiability audit, with matched-horizon multi-seed comparisons left open (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex; Chapter 6 §6.2).

P2, "The Zero-Variance Fraction: A Descriptive Diagnostic for Signal Starvation in GRPO", asks how much of a training batch carries usable group-relative signal, and whether that fraction predicts the final outcome (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). The study characterises ZVF descriptively, treats the identity of §4.3 as algebraic rather than empirical, audits a historical pooled association whose mixed synthetic membership and heterogeneous targets cannot establish held-out prediction, and marks the variance-mitigation table as a simulation projection; the retired roster item that previously carried a causal framing now lives under `archive/absorbed/R02_main_zvf/` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 6 §6.3.

P3 measures group-relative contrast across G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}. Its arithmetic training telemetry can describe how contrast changes under the executed fixed-prompt recipe. Its saturated in-distribution evaluation does not identify an accuracy optimum, and untrained G ≥ 32 token-budget grids are excluded from empirical claims. The all-pairs preference reading is an interpretation, not a proved identity with DPO (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_abstract.tex; Chapter 6 §6.4).

P4 asks about length dynamics and generalisation, but its present evidence does not test uncensored verbosity. The 200-token GSM8K cap leaves little upward length headroom and can penalise truncation before the final answer. The 1,024-token extension also begins with first-five-step means near its ceiling, lacks held-out accuracy outputs, and has incomplete historical source binding; invalid original Dr. GRPO-labelled arms are excluded. The valid retained records support logged capped training trajectories only, with no causal attribution to token mask or normalisation. No new runs were performed for this revision (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/tinker-runs/; Chapter 6 §6.5).

P5, "Report the Stack, Not the Label: RL-for-LLM Results Are Stack-Conditioned", asks how much of a reported result is attributable to the stack rather than to the algorithm label, and what a reportable stack must contain (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). Three exhibits carry the argument: a nominally matched-configuration backend audit, a same-label "DAPO" comparison between true dynamic sampling on an open trainer and an asymmetric-clip surrogate on a closed stack, and a twelve-cell head-to-head of four labelled algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO and GSPO; three seeds × 15 steps) on one fixed stack, distinct from the single-seed 40-step N2 run of GRPO, AERO, GIFT and AReaL (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex); their results are in Chapter 7 §7.1. The study proposes an eight-item minimum reportable stack (seven run-manifest fields and one evaluation item) — loss form; reference-policy and KL handling; sampler, backend and precision including base-checkpoint identity by revision or hash; per-step ZVF and gradient-utilisation trajectory; group-size schedule; a disjoint held-out split; decontamination plus a parser-robustness probe; and held-out pass@k curves alongside pass@1 — and formalises an RL-reproducibility threat model with flip-risk grades R0–R5 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_abstract.tex). This is the standard this thesis is written to.

P6, "GRPO-Registry: A Machine-Readable Catalog of Group-Relative RL Stacks and Their Variant Deltas", asks whether stacks can be compared mechanically rather than through prose (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). It defines stack records carrying the seven manifest fields of the MIN-REPORT-RL standard, a per-component variant-delta status, three variant-delta records verified against the DAPO, Dr. GRPO and GSPO papers, twenty seed stack entries (28 on disk at this revision; §7.2), and a reference CLI with a 0–100 MIN-REPORT auditor badge and a `stackdiff` command that maps a pair of entries to an R0–R5 flip-risk verdict (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). The study is explicitly a resource study rather than a second ranking, and it cites P5's seventeen-fold exhibit only to motivate the schema (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). Results are in Chapter 7 §7.2.

P7, "From Diagnostic to Controller", asks whether a reward-contrast diagnostic can guide group allocation. Under independent Bernoulli rewards, $h_G(p)=p^G+(1-p)^G$ is an exact tie-probability identity. The historical score $S=p(1-p)(1-h_G(p))$ is a heuristic, not the expected gradient magnitude; the exact mean-absolute-centred-advantage identity does not require the extra $1-h_G$ factor (§7.3). Gradient norms additionally depend on score vectors, masking, normalisation and the optimiser. The 368-record export has unresolved provenance composition, the archived gradient-correlation scalar lacks raw gradients, and retrospective controller rankings are withdrawn. The retained contribution is the implemented allocation rule and its assumptions. A prospective static-G versus adaptive-G comparison with matched realised tokens, frozen evaluation and execution receipts remains unrun (source: `platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_abstract.tex`; Chapter 7 §7.3).

The historical non-RL side study is retained only as archived supplementary material in Appendix F. It is not part of the seven-study RL argument or any comparative evidence in this chapter.

## 4.5 The Design of the E1–E14 Evaluation Campaign

The E1–E14 campaign is a pipeline and evidence-governance demonstration with incomplete historical training provenance. It is not a verified held-out capability campaign and does not establish RL efficacy. The reported actor is Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with the historical seed809 adapter, trained for 40 steps; its lost execution-source/dirty-diff binding and missing consumed-row inventory mean NFR-9 is Not Met. Public model identity can be checked independently of those gaps, but cannot repair them (Chapter 8 §8.1.1; Table 3.3). The recorded training-source names are API-Bank-RLVR and SWE-Gym. Absence of benchmark names from that list is weaker than verified content disjointness; E10 explicitly records `heldout_claim: false`. Native grading and scoped receipts can still support particular evaluation records, with the limitations in Chapter 8, without certifying the training run (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

The choice of a 40-step actor is reported as the available lightweight training snapshot. No contemporaneous evidence in this review establishes whether cost, quota or an efficacy target dictated that horizon, so no causal rationale is invented. The paired small-scale trained-versus-base results are inconclusive. This chapter claims only the observable evaluation workflow and its failures, not that 40 steps were adequate training or that every lane followed every proposed gate.

Evaluators are the suites' own scorers, used natively rather than reimplemented, and they are pinned by revision. The surviving native runners live under `zvf-program/flagship/`, including `e1_swe_bench_pro_full_eval.py`, `e11_verilog_eval_decontamination.py`, `eval_pavlov_sdab.py`, `eval_pavlov_xlam.py`, `mle_bench_eval.py` and `eval_apex_agents.py` (source: zvf-program/flagship/). The evaluation-side revisions pinned for the decontamination audit are LAB-Bench `998a8e0a40cf116c80e1b0e7a805ebb5fb9fa838`, AgentDojo `089ed468cf3ed0322acc66b0211f26d9d90dbf60` and Omni-MATH `40ba231d8f16e29ecd40e6407e2c8640145a8f62`, with the training-side envelope pinned at API-Bank-RLVR `bf67c42626f02c305514b1df16dcabc5fc616333` (597 rows, MIT) and SWE-Gym `bb94ed9e39bbeb96a7fcbfb533b80f25a7fd59cb` (2,438 train rows, MIT) (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Using the native scorer is a substantive choice and not a convenience: when the Omni-MATH lane produced two rows whose judged outputs were truncated before any equivalence verdict, the native scorer's omission of missing-verdict rows was adjudicated as correct official behaviour rather than a parser bug, and the lane closed with no re-judge because re-judging would deviate from the official scorer and generate new evidence that could have a small nonzero effect on the score; retaining the historical native result was a protocol choice (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

For the training-selection protocol, sampling is frozen rather than merely recorded: temperature 0.1, top-p 0.95, one sample per row, a maximum of 128 response tokens, with the service seed fixed where the provider supports it and otherwise recorded in the receipt (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Prompt and response limits are 1,200 and 128 tokens respectively, and the same source requires the strict verifier implementation to be hashed before paid work, with a perfect call defined as a verifier score of exactly 1.0 and no parser, target, split or verifier change permitted mid-campaign (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Freezing the sampler addresses the failure mode in which a benchmark score quietly improves because decoding became more favourable between runs; it also fixes the comparison between the base reference and the adapter, which is evaluated side by side on untouched manifests (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The 128-token setting belongs to that protocol only. The E1–E14 lanes were decoded under each suite's own settings, which range from 1,024 to 8,192 output tokens at temperatures 0 to 0.2, and are recorded per lane in the serving table of Chapter 8 §8.1.2. The actor's own training run also departed from the protocol in several fields (§8.1.1).

The prospective receipt model is freeze-before-paid-work, and it is the part of the campaign design that carries the most methodological weight. Before any paid phase a run must record an immutable base-model revision, tokenizer revision, container or environment digest and adapter revision; the selection slice and the disjoint holdout are re-created from a pinned dataset revision and sealed, are write-once, are not mounted in training, and the word "holdout" is reserved for a suite or manifest only once its split, task-ID, license, runtime and decontamination receipts are complete (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The selection slice is used for arm decisions and is therefore unsuitable for confirmatory claims; the final evaluation manifests are never used to choose an arm, alter a prompt, or re-run a failed suite with a more favourable split (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). Tracking is verified before spend rather than after: an online Weights & Biases run must be initialised and its Tinker run identifier recorded before the first paid step, every step records loss, reward, configuration, seed, split identity and cumulative cost, and every checkpoint is published to Hugging Face with a unique repository or revision URL, with a failure of any of these stopping the run fail-closed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). If a receipt is missing, the phase is blocked rather than silently shortened (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). At handoff the protocol requires four separate evidence classes to be reported apart: completed receipts; operational and provenance receipts; prospective gates and budget projections; and admissible final results, the last existing only if every gate passed (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

That last distinction has teeth in practice, and the campaign's disposition record shows it. The portfolio-level protocol defines a predeclared equal-domain macro average over sixteen domains, a conjunctive coverage gate, an integrity gate, a global improvement gate requiring the lower bound of a one-sided 95% interval for the portfolio difference to exceed zero, a domain no-regression guard with a predeclared five-percentage-point tolerance, and a safety guard bounding any violation increase by two percentage points — and it states that a cost cap may not be met by dropping domains or suites, and that a suite or domain which is missing, underpowered, contaminated or outside the cost cap must be labelled incomplete or blocked, "not negative and not positive" (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md). The portfolio training phase was never launched, so the campaign does not report a portfolio result; the recorded outcome is a set of per-lane evaluations of the one adapter, and the protocol's own instruction that a partial subset is not a portfolio result and may not be relabelled as one is honoured throughout (source: zvf-program/flagship/PAVLOV_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL_2026-08-09.md).

Lane dispositions are recorded in a seven-state taxonomy rather than as pass or fail: COMPLETE, for lanes with verified full-coverage results and no open gate (E8 public, E10 benign, E11); COMPLETE_TERMINAL_NOTE, for a lane complete under the native protocol with a recorded terminal note (E14 replacement); CLOSED_EXTERNAL, for lanes with no local or paid path, closed by written closure records (E3, E7, E8-private, E10-private, E12, E14-private); PENDING_QUOTA, for lanes gated on cloud quota resolution alone (E6, E9); AMENDMENT_ACCEPTED_LAUNCH_PENDING (E2, E13); REBUILD_READY_LAUNCH_PENDING (E1 wave10, E5 successor27); and CLOSED_PARTIAL (E4) (source: withheld historical campaign chronology). Those are the states of the 19 September ledger. The three pending states were emptied on 27 September, when the E1, E2, E5, E6, E9 and E13 replacement scopes were run to completion (§8.4). This report assigns them COMPLETE on coverage, with two qualifications recorded against the label: E6 still has 108 tasks awaiting a judge, and E1's coverage spans two contracts reported separately. The taxonomy exists so that a lane which has not been measured is not silently absorbed into a percentage. Carried alongside it is the project's evidence rule, stated in the ledger of record and repeated in every results artefact: original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled (source: withheld historical campaign chronology). The rule is not cosmetic. The original-contract E1 lane and its replacement scope are different suites with different task populations, and a table that added them would produce a number that corresponds to no benchmark.

Decontamination is handled as a declared deterministic procedure with its own stated limits. The check hashes complete UTF-8 fields and then normalised fields with SHA-256; for fields of at least eight tokens it hashes each consecutive eight-token sequence and computes exact set Jaccard similarity for every training and evaluation pair sharing a shingle; and it tests whether an entire normalised evaluation field is a token-bounded contiguous substring of a training field (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Against the pinned training envelope, the per-suite inventories were 1,967 LAB-Bench tasks over 13,113 text fields with 5,105 eligible for eight-token near checks, 97 AgentDojo tasks over 71,966 fields with 9,382 eligible, and 4,428 Omni-MATH tasks over 13,284 fields with 9,394 eligible; the count of near pairs at or above 0.80 was zero in all three suites, and the maximum eight-gram Jaccard similarity was 0 for LAB-Bench and AgentDojo and 0.13414634146341464 for Omni-MATH, below the declared 0.80 threshold, with all 1,338 candidate pairs sharing an eight-gram examined (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). Short exact matches of zero to three normalised tokens were retained and enumerated rather than dropped — the longest examples being seven characters in the LAB and Omni inventories and at most seventeen raw characters in AgentDojo, such as "Paris" and "Conference Room A" — and the audit states plainly that these collisions do not demonstrate duplication of a task but do make an unqualified "zero overlap" claim incorrect (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit's scope limits are recorded with equal care: it does not reconstruct which training records were consumed: 512 is the size of the training pool recorded in the E11 decontamination receipt (`selected_training_row_count`), from which the 40-step run drew 80 prompts at two per step, and the transform and consumed-row inventory that would identify them were not recorded; it establishes a content comparison against the verified historical source envelope rather than proving global pretraining cleanliness, semantic disjointness, character-edit disjointness or image cleanliness; ASCII normalisation loses mathematical punctuation and non-ASCII text; and fields shorter than eight tokens fall outside the near-match and containment rule (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md). The audit itself is receipted: it re-verifies Weights & Biases run `withheld-id` against `training_identity_verified.json`, matches the 23,733 original source-field document hashes to the historical E11 receipt, adds 2,507 decoded nested API-Bank strings to reach 26,240 source documents, and records 43,977,748 bytes downloaded against a 500 MiB cap, with the checker not having enumerated the roughly 2.58 billion possible document pairs (26,240 training documents × 98,363 evaluation fields). The near-duplicate check covered only the 23,881 evaluation fields eligible for eight-token checks; what was not enumerated is the all-pairs comparison, including shorter fields and character-edit overlap (source: outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/decontamination/README.md).

The proposed gate and the executed path must also be distinguished. The 27 September replacements used thin native runners under a separate authorisation rather than the reconstructed fail-closed controllers (§5.9). Their receipts record that deviation; these runs are not evidence that the prospective controllers enforced their guarantees.

The campaign records incomplete execution explicitly. Some September lanes remained blocked by infrastructure or readiness conditions and were completed on 27 September after changes to the execution environment. The per-lane protocol deviations and results remain separate (§8.4). Private account state, access routes and operational approvals are omitted from the public derivative.

The campaign also carries its integrity accounting in the same register. Eleven of eleven deterministic checks passed on 2026-09-19, covering exact divisions, receipt presence, sealed-artifact hash equality and lost-source absence probes (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A judgment layer was added for classification, faithfulness and value-ordering questions and then partly invalidated by an external service outage: both blocker-classification batteries returned degenerate distributions after the incident, their receipts are retained as incident evidence, and the per-lane blocker column therefore rests on document evidence and not on model judgment (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A design rule was banked from the healthy window and is followed in this thesis: arithmetic and string lookups stay in code, and model judgment is reserved for classification, faithfulness and value ordering, because a parity probe was answered incorrectly even while the service was healthy (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md).

What the campaign does not claim follows directly from the design. It does not claim improvement over any baseline anywhere, because no lane carries a test establishing it. The one trained-versus-base comparison is the paired small-scale rerun of Table 8.6, with 5 to 151 identical items per lane, and its outcome is reported in Chapter 8; the campaign's role in this thesis is to fix the evaluation protocol, the receipt discipline and the disposition taxonomy that any future improvement claim would have to satisfy. It does not claim that the fourteen suites constitute a portfolio result. It does not pool original-contract with replacement-scope figures, and it does not average across suites. The single-actor campaign does not rank model families; its separately paired base comparison remains small-sample and inconclusive. And where the record is incomplete — a lost execution source, an unavailable external benchmark scope — the incompleteness is written into the ledger rather than resolved by inference.


## 4.6 C1 Frozen Policy Diagnostic and Reference Review

C1 completed 4,096 fixed-order screening questions with eight independently seeded responses per question. Eligibility required all eight outputs to be parseable, uncapped and assigned zero exact-match reward by frozen parser v2. After the full screen, the first 64 of 87 eligible questions received 24 additional responses each. Selection was truncated deterministically; the 23 other eligible questions were not followed up. The primary endpoint is whether at least one of the 24 fresh responses receives parser-defined exact-match reward one. No question was removed or relabeled after review. [S1, S13]

The new workload contains 32,768 screening responses and 1,536 fresh follow-up responses, totaling 34,304 new draws. A disjoint historical smoke cohort contributes 1,024 retained draws imported once, giving 35,328 recorded unique draws. The historical smoke is an operational quality record, not part of the primary denominator. The 512 initial responses for the selected 64 questions are already included within screening, and must not be counted again. [S1, S10]

The model and tokenizer were Qwen/Qwen3-8B at revision `b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218`, with BF16 and thinking disabled. The dataset was openai/gsm8k, main configuration, train split, revision `740312add88f781978c0658806c59bc2815b9866`. Generation used temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, min-p 0, a 1,024-token generation cap and 4,096-token context. The recorded execution used one NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB, vLLM 0.11.0, PyTorch 2.8.0 and Transformers 4.57.1, with eager execution enabled and prefix caching disabled. Fixed request seeds do not imply bitwise reproducibility across hardware or allocations. [S12, S13]

Screening had 504 parser failures and 19 cap hits among 32,768 outputs. By design the selected initial groups had neither. Follow-up had ten parser failures and four cap hits among 1,536 outputs. Those quality counts do not certify semantic correctness. The primary denominator remains 64 regardless of later quality flags. Descriptive prefixes of eight, sixteen and twenty-four fresh draws yield 9/64, 10/64 and 11/64 parser events. They are nested observations, not independent comparisons or evidence of an optimal rollout budget. [S1]

The Wilson interval uses a question-level binomial working model within the selected cohort. It is not design-based or population coverage: both outcome-dependent eligibility and fixed-order truncation restrict the estimand. The observed 87/4,096 eligibility count similarly describes this fixed screen, not benchmark-wide mathematical failure prevalence.

The first review solved the questions before comparing reference answers, then inspected the required model outputs. A second reference review was separately masked from the first judgments and model outputs. Final reconciliation compared the exact question, reference and annotations, retaining disagreements rather than forcing consensus. All stages were assistant-assisted. Separate masking and repeat review do not provide independent human validation or rule out shared model biases. [S2, S6]

The first output audit covers all 64 cases, all 512 initial responses and 143 required fresh responses: 655 responses in total, including all 38 primary-success outputs. The selection rule for the other 105 fresh responses is not recovered, so the 143 form a selected audit set rather than a random sample; whether the follow-up cap hits are a subset of the parser failures is likewise not recorded in the retained public ledger. The final mechanical coverage receipt verifies those identities and stored logic; it is not an independent mathematical adjudication. Six initially missing reasoning-caveat fields were clarified in a separately attributed supplement without altering the original fields, scores or verdicts. No coverage or annotation defect remains unresolved in the final receipt. [S7]

For the eleven event questions, reconciliation read all 88 initial outputs and all 38 primary-success fresh outputs in full. All 126 explicit final-number extractions are faithful and uncapped. Two unit-sensitive non-events add sixteen inspected initial outputs. This is not a claim to have validated reasoning in every one of the 1,536 fresh responses. The first audit identified five frozen-v2 extraction false negatives and no extraction false positives within its reviewed set; these selected-case observations do not establish population parser precision. [S2, S3, S5, S7]

The conservative witness rule requires a reference value supported in both rounds without material ambiguity, eight faithful uncapped initial claims independently judged wrong, and at least one faithful uncapped fresh primary-success claim independently judged numerically correct. Sound final arithmetic and sound visible reasoning are recorded separately. No post-review filtering changes the 64-question denominator.

Operational smoke provenance (S10) is not supplied as a scientific source in this derivative. This design is distinct from the historical V2 diagnostic.
