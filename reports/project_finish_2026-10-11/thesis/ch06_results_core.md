# 6. Results I: Core GRPO Studies (P1–P4)

This chapter reports the core studies P1–P4: cross-scale training traces, the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF), group-size comparisons and capped response-length behaviour. Its strongest retained observations concern diagnostic arithmetic and explicitly scoped stored-run comparisons. Interrupted traces, synthetic projections and evaluation-contaminated comparisons do not establish empirical scaling, prediction or algorithm-ranking results.

## 6.1 Scope, Sources and Evidence Boundary

The four pillars share one methodological commitment, which is the organising principle of the whole project: each comparison names the intended contrast, the controls actually held fixed and any coupled changes that limit attribution. Where checkpoint, prompt exposure, sampling or budget differ, the result describes those executed configurations rather than an isolated algorithm effect. The reader should treat this chapter as the primary result surface for P1–P4; the corresponding historical LaTeX sources are preserved, while this chapter distinguishes their reported outputs from corrections recomputed from the named data and inspected analysis code.

Each study draws on a different evidence base, and the differences matter. P1 historically describes a 70+ run roster across libraries and model families, but its fitted anchor subset includes interrupted and single-seed records and is not a verified common-budget population (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex). P2's retained measured evidence includes three stored sampling tensors and separately identified measured sweeps. The five-seed, nine-row mitigation panel is wholly synthetic and is excluded from empirical prediction and intervention claims (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex). P3's measured sweep covers G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}; every G = 32 and above number in the pillar is a joint-fit reconstruction, not a trained arm (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p3_intro.tex). P4's controlled comparison is sixteen runs across two algorithms and two tasks; its roster-level audit is drawn from a separate, larger pool that mixes models, tasks and libraries (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias.tex).

Historical marginal one-way ANOVAs were reported for 42 experiments, with library/framework η² = 0.546, algorithm η² = 0.558, model family η² = 0.471 and scale η² = 0.322. Their sum, 1.897, is not a partition of variance. A verified row-level membership and crossed-factor census for that exact historical subset is unavailable in this audit; these values therefore do not support the claim that framework dominates algorithm or estimate independent factor contributions. They are retained only as a locator for the historical analysis, alongside the unsupported single-seed comparisons in its twenty-test family (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/_shared_methods.tex). The case for explicit configuration reporting rests on the observable mismatches described in Chapters 4–5, not on this decomposition.

Throughout this chapter, "statistically indistinguishable" means that the stated difference test did not detect a difference at its declared level; it does not mean demonstrated equivalence. Equivalence is reported only when both one-sided tests reject differences outside a stated margin. Failure to establish equivalence and failure to detect a difference are separate outcomes, and can occur together.

## 6.2 P1 — Cross-Library and Cross-Scale Evidence Limits

P1 does not identify a post-training scaling law. Absolute training reward combines pretrained starting capability with adaptation, whereas a benefit estimand would compare matched pre- and post-training accuracy at a common budget, $\Delta(N,B)=\mathrm{Acc}_{post}(N,B)-\mathrm{Acc}_{pre}(N)$. The historical pool supplies neither that common endpoint nor independent variation of model size and training recipe.

### 6.2.1 Trace inventory and exclusions

Of twelve cross-scale anchors, five terminate after only three to five logged steps: Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3-30B-MoE, Qwen3-30B-MoE-Instruct, Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-235B-MoE. The other seven provide twenty to thirty steps. Frontier anchors are single-seed, and learning rate, LoRA rank and model identity covary with scale. The interrupted traces are execution records, not completed learning curves or comparable terminal outcomes (sources: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/scaling_laws.tex; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p1_abstract.tex).

The historical cross-scale power-law regressions, fitted saturation rates, changepoints and compute-adjusted proxies are excluded from this chapter's evidentiary results. Removing five short traces does not repair the remaining recipe and starting-capability confounding, so no replacement scaling fit is presented. In particular, a zero-intercept saturation fit to an already capable pretrained policy cannot identify a learning rate merely because its fitted parameter reaches an optimiser boundary. The source analyses remain archived; their numerical slopes and fit-derived intervals are not evidence for either a scaling law or its absence.

### 6.2.2 What the trace audit establishes

The audit establishes missing design support, rather than a statistical null about scale. Some recorded training rewards fall or fluctuate, including the single Nemotron-120B trace, but short within-run observations cannot identify a general learning-phase taxonomy. The earlier dense-versus-MoE contrast also depends on a misclassified model and is withdrawn. Categorical agreement does not repair this: where both labelling procedures assign every trace the same category, Cohen's κ is undefined because both observed and chance agreement equal one (source: platform_modal/scripts/scaling_law_iter17.py). A common-budget, replicated pre/post comparison is still required to answer the original scaling question.

### 6.2.3 Saturated arithmetic as an evaluation-design audit

The historical Qwen2.5-0.5B arithmetic PPO/GRPO comparison reports mean evaluation accuracies of 0.992 and 0.990 respectively across five seeds. These values are retained only to describe the stored records, not as an isolated estimator comparison. Each arm drew its 200 evaluation problems from the stateful training generator after a different number of training draws. Within seeds, the evaluation sets share only 6, 6, 4, 7 and 3 items. Evaluation items previously drawn for training number 12, 16, 13, 15 and 13 for GRPO, versus 90, 86, 80, 83 and 74 for PPO. The arms also expose different numbers of distinct training prompts per step (16 versus 128), and both lie near the accuracy ceiling (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/samestack_ppo_grpo.json; historical evaluation replay documented in Appendix E).

Consequently, the historical difference tests and equivalence tests are not evidence that the algorithms agree, differ or have equal performance. Pairing aggregate scores by seed does not restore missing item pairing or remove unequal training overlap. Later runner code contains a fixed-disjoint evaluation repair, but that code change does not repair these stored runs. The unsaturated v1/v2 studies in §6.5.5 have separate evaluation contracts; Table 6.2 remains provisional.

The PPO arm retains its correct implementation description: a sequence-level clipped policy-ratio objective with a learned value baseline. Absence of generalised advantage estimation does not make a clipped PPO surrogate REINFORCE. The critic's training quality and the exposure mismatch limit the comparison independently of that name.

## 6.3 P2 — The Zero-Variance Fraction

The second pillar asks a mechanistic question: when GRPO stops learning, is the cause visible in the reward statistics of the groups it samples? The diagnostic proposed is the Zero-Variance Fraction.

### 6.3.1 Definition and measurement

For a prompt x sampled from the training distribution, GRPO emits G completions and scores each with a verifier. Centring those rewards within the group produces the advantage signal. If all G completions receive the same reward — all correct, or all incorrect — every centred reward is zero and the group contributes no contrastive signal to that step's update. The exact-tie fraction records how often this occurs; the declared tolerance rule in §4.3 also counts near-ties. They agree for the binary rewards below. Gradient utilisation is defined descriptively as GU = 1 − ZVF (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex).

What a zero-variance group does to the update depends on the loss form. The source states the general case: in an objective with a KL penalty or auxiliary loss, a zero-variance group does not make the total gradient zero, and ZVF measures only the fraction of groups contributing zero centred reward contrast (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex). An exact-tie group contributes zero to a verified group-centred policy surrogate, but the full update can include KL, critic or other auxiliary losses and optimiser state. Table 4.2 contains both nonzero-KL configuration evidence and unknown managed settings; neither may be relabelled zero. Near-ties counted by the ε = 10⁻⁶ diagnostic need not have exactly zero policy gradients. ZVF therefore describes reward contrast, not measured useful compute or the whole update.

The diagnostic arithmetic is checked by recomputation. The thesis defines its variance convention in §4.3. The retained historical tolerance/jitter recomputation uses sample variance (ddof = 1) ≤ 10⁻⁶, whereas historical exact-tie checks and PCD use population variance (ddof = 0). Its numerical results below are left under that historical convention, not silently converted to the thesis convention. The Modal training runners standardise rewards with NumPy's population standard deviation and log their own near-zero-standard-deviation rule. These conventions coincide on the binary exact ties here, but are not interchangeable on continuous or jittered rewards (sources: platform_modal/scripts/partial_correlation_zvf.py; platform_modal/scripts/pcd_vs_zvf.py; platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_groupsize_zvf_sweep.py). Three stored GSM8K group tensors (Qwen3-8B, G = 8, sampling only, 200 problems per seed) were re-read from disk and their ZVF recomputed from the raw per-group rewards; the recomputed values are 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155, pooled 0.1583, matching the reported values exactly (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_abstract.tex). This is the mechanical check that the quantity in the tables is the quantity the code computes. The earlier ≤0.4% ε-sensitivity statement did not distinguish exact ties from thresholded variance and is replaced here by a reproducible sweep over these same 600 groups. With independent seeded δ ~ U(0,10⁻⁴) jitter, exact ties fall from 95/600 to 0/600. At sample-variance ε = 10⁻⁹, 71/600 groups remain below threshold; at ε = 10⁻⁸ through 10⁻², including the declared 10⁻⁶, all 95/600 remain. Unperturbed binary rewards retain 95/600 across this range. At G = 8, jitter alone has sample variance at most 2.86 × 10⁻⁹, so it cannot cross 10⁻⁶ in an originally tied group. PCD changes by +2.984 × 10⁻⁷. The example diagnoses exact-tie sensitivity, not failure of tolerance-based ZVF; it does not establish robustness to larger perturbations or other reward scales (sources: platform_modal/scripts/pcd_vs_zvf.py; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/pcd_vs_zvf_tolerance_analysis.json; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/pcd_vs_zvf_epsilon_sensitivity.tsv).

### 6.3.2 Measured evidence and excluded projections

The nine-method, five-seed mitigation panel is entirely synthetic, including its vanilla-GRPO row. The labels AERO, CPPO, NGRPO, SCAFGRPO, MCGRPO, GIFT, AREAL and ES identify historical simulation rules in this artifact, not verified independent deployments. Its rankings, synthetic accuracies and collapse counts are excluded from the empirical results. The historical 60-row range/autocorrelation analysis contains these 45 simulated rows, so its pooled magnitudes and warning lead times are also excluded; no measured-only early-warning estimate is supplied by that analysis (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/variance_mitigation.tsv; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex). The measured evidence below is identified separately by source, task and unit. Published CPPO, NGRPO, Scaf-GRPO, AERO, MC-GRPO, GIFT, AReaL and evolution-strategy methods have their own definitions [@lin2025cppo; @nan2025ngrpo; @zhang2025scafgrpo; @zhang2026aero; @kim2026mcgrpo; @wang2025gift; @fu2025areal; @qiu2025es]. Their existence does not authenticate these proxy implementations; AReaL is a training system and evolution strategies are not intrinsically GRPO variants.

### 6.3.3 The all-correct wall and the mastery–incapacity aliasing

The mechanism behind large ZVF values on easy tasks is a saturation effect that the project calls the all-correct wall. A matched-budget panel was run at 2,560 rollouts per arm — G = 2 over 160 steps against G = 16 over 20 steps — on Qwen3-8B with GSM8K, LoRA rank 4 and batch size 8. The late G = 2 arms reach a mean training reward of approximately 1.0 with ZVF between 0.75 and 1.0; the G = 16 arms hold ZVF at or below 0.25 at the same rollout budget (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/zvf.tex). The reading is that when the policy solves the task, nearly every group becomes all-correct, nearly every centred reward is zero, and the estimator loses its contrast even though the reward signal is at its maximum. Signal starvation in this regime is driven by ease, not by difficulty — which is the opposite of the intuition that motivates variance-mitigation work.

This produces the pillar's most important structural caveat. ZVF is unsigned and symmetric: an all-wrong group ($K_x = 0$) and an all-correct group ($K_x = G$) score identically. The statistic therefore cannot distinguish mastery from incapacity, and this aliasing is a theoretical limitation on its interpretation; the historical pooled correlation does not separately test the claimed held-out association (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p2_conclusion.tex). Under an i.i.d.-Bernoulli model the closed form is $\mathrm{ZVF}(p_x, G) = h_G(p_x) = p_x^G + (1-p_x)^G$, and it follows in one line. If the G rewards of a group are independent Bernoulli(p) draws, the group has zero variance exactly when all G rewards are equal. That happens when all are 1, with probability $p^G$, or all are 0, with probability $(1-p)^G$. The two events are disjoint, so $P(\text{zero variance}) = p^G + (1-p)^G$. At $p = \tfrac12$ this is $h_G = 2^{1-G}$, so $\ln h_G$ falls by $\ln 2$ for each unit increase in G. The project's source document for this closed form and the aliasing analysis is headed as reasoning contributions and proposed theorems from ChatGPT Pro Extended and Gemini Deep Think, "NOT experiments this paper ran" (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/frontier_synthesis_zvf.tex). That provenance is kept on record, and AI assistance is declared in the front matter, but the result does not rest on it: the derivation above is elementary and is the argument this thesis relies on. What the project itself measured is the empirical ZVF against the closed form, evaluated at the per-step training reward rate because ZVF is a training-batch quantity. Recomputed from the per-step training rewards in `platform_hybrid/experiments/results/groupsize_zvf_sweep.json` (3 seeds × 40 steps per arm), the per-step-average null is 0.828, 0.709, 0.597 and 0.522. The measured mean ZVF is 0.838, 0.764, 0.691 and 0.631, so the residuals are +0.010, +0.055, +0.094 and +0.109: measured ZVF sits *above* the null at every G, which is compatible with heterogeneous prompt difficulty. Jensen's inequality bounds expected degeneracy at true latent success probabilities, not the sign of a finite-sample residual against an estimated plug-in baseline [@nie2026starvation]. Gradient utilisation rises across the same range from 0.162 to 0.369. The magnitude and interpretation of the gap depend on the baseline; averaging over heterogeneous prompt success probabilities is part of the formal null in §4.3. Evaluating the closed form at the pooled training rate understates the mean of the per-step plug-in predictions, because $h_G$ is convex: $h_G(\overline p_t) \le \overline{h_G(p_t)}$. The per-step-average plug-in baseline is $0.60$ at $G = 8$, rather than the $0.33$ obtained at the pooled mean. A held-out rate is a different quantity and has no corresponding ordering guarantee. Even the per-step baseline uses a batch-average estimate instead of the unknown per-prompt probabilities in §4.3, so prompt-difficulty heterogeneity and estimation error remain. Against the per-step-average baseline the measured ZVF at $G = 8$ ($0.69$) \emph{exceeds} the null by approximately $0.09$. The change from the pooled baseline to the per-step baseline is the Jensen effect; it does not by itself explain the remaining 0.09 residual. The source consequently "do[es] not read it as strong evidence that the independence null is a lower bound" (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). This chapter uses only the per-step-average baseline. It is a weak consistency check on shape rather than evidence about dependence.

The formalisation's own summary of the same sweep, twelve runs and 480 logged steps, reports per-step observed ZVF correlating at r = 0.92 with the Bernoulli-null prediction $p^G + (1-p)^G$, with mean ZVF of 0.84, 0.76, 0.69 and 0.63 at G = 2, 4, 8 and 16 respectively (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). That correlation is a shape check, not a validation: the null for each step is computed from the same step's mean reward, so a high r is partly mechanical. At larger scale the same instrument was run on Qwen3-8B against GSM8K test problems through the Tinker managed API at G = 8, temperature 1.0, over 200 problems and three seeds (sampling only, no training; these are the three stored tensors of §6.3.1), yielding a mean ZVF of 0.16 composed of 12.7% all-correct groups, 3.2% all-wrong groups and 84% mixed groups; the raw correlation with the mechanical null is approximately r = 0.93 with rank ρ ≈ 0.65, and the i.i.d. null over-predicts (approximately 0.28 against the observed 0.16) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/appendix_zvf_formalization.tex). The direction of the deviation from the null is therefore opposite in the two runs: above the null on the 0.5B sweep, below it on this Qwen3-8B sampling run. The source does not state which baseline produced the 0.28, and neither deviation is read as evidence about dependence between completions.

A recomputation on the three stored Qwen3-8B tensors (600 groups, pooled success rate 0.694) separates the candidate explanations and does not reproduce the 0.28. At the pooled rate the homogeneous binomial null is $h_8(0.694) = 0.054$, against 0.158 observed, so the null *under*-predicts by a factor of about three. A beta-binomial fitted to the per-group success counts ($\alpha = 3.61$, $\beta = 1.62$, intraclass correlation 0.16) predicts ZVF 0.170, in an exploratory fit. The repeated prompts across seeds and boundary at zero overdispersion require an explicitly calibrated inferential procedure; no likelihood-ratio rejection is claimed here. The fitted agreement shows that prompt-difficulty heterogeneity can explain the excess, without identifying it separately from conditional dependence or other latent variation: to a good approximation ZVF is the mean of $h_G(p_i)$ over prompts with heterogeneous success probabilities $p_i$, which is the Jensen argument above applied across prompts rather than across steps. A per-group plug-in residual of +0.1224 that appears in a P2 working note is withdrawn: it is non-negative by construction (the same sign-guaranteed quantity as the withdrawn "anti-herding" δ_div of §7.2, whose expectation is +0.105 under i.i.d. rollouts) and is not evidence of dependence (recomputation: 16 October 2026 audit of `results/tinker_gsm8k_zvf_s{42,123,456}.json`). Contemporaneous work studies the same quantity under other names — the Advantage Collapse Rate [@he2026avspo] and gradient starvation in binary-reward GRPO [@nie2026starvation], the latter proving that heterogeneous prompt difficulty pushes the degeneracy rate above the i.i.d. Bernoulli prediction — so ZVF is not claimed here as a new diagnostic; the contribution is its recomputation from stored tensors and the reporting discipline around it.

### 6.3.4 Predictive validity remains unestablished

The historical residual and collapse analyses mix provenance and endpoints. Eighty source rows are grouped into 23 cells; six cells lack ZVF and are excluded, leaving 17 finite cells, nine of which are synthetic. The outcome field mixes late training reward, sampling and other source metrics rather than a common held-out endpoint. Residualising only the outcome is semipartial adjustment and does not turn these records into an independent predictive test. The historical coefficients, sample-size-based intervals and held-out interpretation are therefore excluded from inferential claims (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/zvf_summary.tsv; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/zvf_iter26_residual.tsv; reports/project_finish_2026-10-11/colab_validation/result.json).

The 52-row risk-index evaluation is also withdrawn as validation evidence. Its 45 variance-mitigation rows are simulated, its seven collapse anchors receive analysis-imputed feature values, and all 41 safe rows are synthetic. The resulting AUROCs and Hanley–McNeil intervals are removed rather than presented as empirical discrimination. A post-hoc fusion rule cannot be validated against class features supplied by the same analysis. No replacement measured-only AUROC, threshold or confidence interval is asserted (source: platform_modal/scripts/zvf_diagnostic_iter130.py).

The retained finding is definitional and directly checkable: ZVF records observed reward homogeneity and is symmetric between all-correct and all-wrong groups. Predictive or intervention value needs a separately identified measured cohort, a common future endpoint, fixed feature and threshold choices, and held-out runs. Neither ZVF nor PCD has met that prospective comparison in the evidence reported here.

## 6.4 P3 — Group Size and Contrast Density

The third pillar studies G, which plays two roles simultaneously in GRPO: it is a variance-reduction knob, like a Monte-Carlo baseline, and it is a preference-density knob, since centring rewards within a group induces implicit pairwise contrasts — nonzero only for mixed-reward groups. Practitioners often treat larger G as strictly better by analogy to variance reduction, which conflates the two roles and ignores cost. The measured evidence does not justify a group-size optimum. The arithmetic accuracy endpoints are saturated in-distribution draws, so their non-monotone point estimates cannot establish non-monotonic trainability.

### 6.4.1 The measured arithmetic sweep

The sweep covers G ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}, with three seeds per level and 40 steps on Qwen2.5-0.5B two-digit addition. Mean recorded evaluation accuracies (± seed SE) are 0.9817 ± 0.0044, 0.9883 ± 0.0017, 0.9900 ± 0.0029 and 0.9783 ± 0.0060 respectively. The 200 evaluation problems are fresh draws from the training generator, not a guaranteed disjoint test split. Near-ceiling scores and only three seeds leave little sensitivity to modest differences; these records do not establish an optimal G, a genuine inverted-U response or algorithmic equivalence (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/groupsize_zvf_sweep.json).

Mean observed ZVF falls from 0.838 to 0.764, 0.691 and 0.631 across the same G levels. This describes changing reward-group statistics, not a proved gain in held-out learning or useful compute. The runner fixes prompts and optimiser steps, so larger groups consume more completions. Its advantages use population-standard-deviation normalisation, its response log probabilities are length-normalised, and its loss is averaged over the full completion batch. The inspected runner uses Adam at 10⁻⁴ across G with no G-specific learning-rate compensation; the stored sweep records omit the executed learning rate and immutable source binding. This is a coupled sampling-budget comparison, not an equal-token intervention (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_groupsize_zvf_sweep.py).

### 6.4.2 Excluded reconstructions and attribution claims

The historical G ≥ 32 and million-token budget grids are joint-fit reconstructions, not executed training arms. Their retention curves, fitted asymptotes, critical-budget intervals and adaptive-saving percentages are excluded from the main empirical results. They cannot establish equivalence to a literature reference, locate a group-size optimum or forecast savings. The measured matched-rollout panel in §6.3.3 and the small matched-token checks below have their own, narrower budgets and must not be extended into that unexecuted grid (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/group_size_g4_vs_g32_broader_scale.tsv; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/group_size_iter23.tex).

The four-axis factorisation does not repair the arithmetic comparison. Its algorithm component inherits the different evaluation items and unequal training overlap of §6.2.3, while the group-size component inherits saturation and changing completion expenditure. Those shares are descriptive functions of the supplied records, not estimates of independently manipulated causes. The appropriate follow-on evidence is the separately contracted unsaturated comparison in §6.5.5, whose v2 seed and exposure-matched analysis remains incomplete.

### 6.4.3 Small curriculum and matched-token checks

The exploratory curriculum, loss-form and layer-freeze checks did not establish a reliable held-out improvement. Their small samples and differing budgets prevent a general conclusion of ineffectiveness.

A difficulty curriculum drives the reported zero-gradient fraction from 0.50 to 0.00 among accepted batches, at approximately 4.81 times the sampling cost. Rejected candidates still consume sampling resources. Both arms move from 0.80 to 0.85 on twenty held-out items in a single eight-step seed: one additional correct item each. A later three-seed, matched 30k-token comparison reports +0.028 in both arms. Equality of these point estimates does not establish equivalence or rule out a practically important effect. The source's independent verification flags the original curriculum claim and a single-seed G = 4 “win” of +0.125 as SUSPECT; the latter is one held-out item and is not evidence of a group-size optimum (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/curriculum_opening/FINDINGS.md; platform_hybrid/paper/sections/robustness_smallscale_nulls.tex).

The loss-form comparison records gains of +0.125 for the standard sum, +0.000 for mean-normalisation and +0.042 for the surprise formulation, with only 12–20 held-out items per arm. These coarse point estimates do not establish a ranking or equality. The standard arm's recorded length change is −16 tokens; neither shortening nor a small accuracy difference tests the general uncensored length-bias mechanism (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/robustness_smallscale_nulls.tex).

The layer-ranking check begins with overlap 1.0 in a 1.5B hardcoded-arithmetic toy, then records approximately 0.11 in two 3B GSM8K seeds. A later four-seed pass gives mean overlap 0.083 ± 0.048 (mean ± SD), while top-quarter concentration remains approximately 0.39. Its reference is 0.25 for independent uniform top-nine sets among 36 layers. The target is the aggregate across all steps, including step 1, rather than an independent final-step target. Model size, task and recipe changed together, so this is failure to reproduce the toy ranking in the tested configuration, not an isolated effect of scale or a general result about freezing (§5.5; source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p1_layerfreeze/scaled_4seed_result.json).

The matched 30k-token check has a defined budget; the other small comparisons do not jointly form a matched-cost intervention study. The diagnostic's prescriptive value remains unresolved, and P2 is retained as a monitoring instrument rather than a validated optimisation objective.

## 6.5 P4 — Length Bias and Held-Out Generalisation

The fourth pillar tests the most widely repeated specific criticism of GRPO: that it rewards verbosity, inflating completion length in a way that Dr. GRPO corrects. The available capped runs do not identify unconstrained length bias; their observed lengths and rewards are reported within that limitation.

### 6.5.1 Observed lengths under a binding cap

Sixteen controlled runs were analysed: ten on an arithmetic task with Qwen2.5-0.5B over 40 steps, and six on GSM8K chain-of-thought with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct over 30 steps, split evenly between GRPO and Dr. GRPO. The arithmetic half is saturated: recorded evaluation accuracy is 0.987 for GRPO and 0.992 for Dr. GRPO (paired p = 0.35, five seeds), and completions average 4.7 tokens, so its score differences and short responses have little headroom (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/drgrpo_vs_grpo.json). Only the GSM8K-CoT half has headroom. At these 30–40-step horizons, neither algorithm exhibits the verbosity-trap signature on either task: the pillar's length-bias flag, which fires when completion length grows while reward stays flat or falls, is zero on every one of the sixteen runs (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias.tex). That flag is not the roster audit's peak-then-decay rule of §6.5.2. Recomputed from the per-step logs, the peak-then-decay rule (peak before 65% of training, last-10 mean reward below 90% of peak) fires on none of the ten arithmetic runs but on five of the six GSM8K-CoT runs, three GRPO and two Dr. GRPO, because the reward trace on that task is noisy and peaks early; length still falls in all five (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/drgrpo_vs_grpo.json; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/drgrpo_gsm8k_cot_full.json).

The summary correlations are as follows, given as mean ± SD across seeds (n = 5 arithmetic, n = 3 GSM8K-CoT). On arithmetic, the rank correlation between training step and completion length is −0.405 ± 0.176 for GRPO and −0.307 ± 0.181 for Dr. GRPO; on GSM8K-CoT it is −0.622 ± 0.301 and −0.355 ± 0.255. The step-versus-reward correlations are +0.709 ± 0.082 and +0.753 ± 0.074 on arithmetic, and +0.046 ± 0.162 and +0.025 ± 0.091 on GSM8K-CoT. The length-versus-reward correlations are −0.419 ± 0.155 and −0.293 ± 0.182 on arithmetic, and −0.501 ± 0.258 and −0.618 ± 0.118 on GSM8K-CoT. Mean reward over the last ten steps is 0.988 for both algorithms on arithmetic, and 0.265 for GRPO against 0.252 for Dr. GRPO on GSM8K-CoT (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias.tex). The direction of the length trend is negative everywhere: all sixteen length slopes are negative, with the arithmetic GRPO slope at −0.029 tokens per step and the GSM8K GRPO slope at −0.481, and the count of runs with a positive horizon is zero (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias.tex). On the harder task, GRPO's final-ten-step mean completion length is 184.4 tokens against Dr. GRPO's 189.3 — a difference of +2.7% in the direction opposite to the critique (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias.tex).

The GSM8K result is heavily cap-censored. Initial mean completions are about 194 of the allowed 200 tokens, leaving little upward range. A correctness reward requiring an extractable final answer can penalise cap truncation and thereby create downward pressure on observed length; the surviving logs do not identify how much of the decline is caused by this mechanism. The absence of observed expansion therefore does not falsify normalisation-induced length bias or show that the unconstrained policies would not expand. A matched comparison with demonstrably low truncation, correctness-stratified lengths and actual gradient weights remains unresolved (sources: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex; platform_hybrid/experiments/results/drgrpo_gsm8k_cot_full.json).

### 6.5.2 The roster-level verbosity-trap audit

Set against the controlled null is a descriptive audit over the whole experiment roster, and here the picture differs. The audit defines four statistics per trace with at least five steps: an instability index σ(r_tail)/|μ(r_tail)| over the final 50% of training; a monotonicity score, the fraction of consecutive step-pairs with strictly improving reward; a regression severity, the largest normalised drop from peak; and a binary verbosity-trap flag, set when the peak occurs before 65% of training and the terminal reward falls below 90% of peak (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex).

On that definition, the eleven GRPO runs in the roster have a mean instability index of 0.267 ± 0.335, against 0.785 ± 0.297 (mean ± spread across runs; the source does not label the ±, and its size relative to the mean implies an SD) for the seventeen PPO-labelled runs. Four of the eleven GRPO runs, 36% (Wilson 95% CI [15%, 65%], one run per configuration), carry the flag on this definition, including both Qwen3-8B runs and the Nemotron-120B run. That count is reported here as a description of the roster, not as evidence of length bias, because the flag does not isolate the behaviour it is named for. Three properties of the rule prevent the stronger reading. Its thresholds are post hoc: neither the 65% peak position nor the 0.90 terminal-to-peak ratio is pre-registered anywhere in the repository, and the rule cannot be traced to a commit that predates the traces it judges. It is not specific to length: the metric it tests is in-training reward, and the project's own source reads the flag as "a generic instability detector" rather than a length measure, so a run can carry it without any change in response length. And its base rate is high: applied to the wider trace pool rather than this eleven-run subset, the rule fires on the majority of runs — including PPO-labelled runs and the Dr. GRPO control in the one matched comparison — so 36% sits at or below the rate the rule produces on traces with no decay at all. The project's own length data point the other way: under the 200-token cap, length drifts from 194.4 to 183.5 tokens, i.e. shorter. The run-level audit therefore does not establish length inflation, and the capped comparison does not establish its unconstrained absence. The Nemotron run records the highest instability index in the pool at 1.194, with a regression severity of 1.0 and a terminal-to-peak reward ratio of 0.57. The two DeepSeek-V3.1 GRPO runs, by contrast, show markedly lower instability, with a mean of 0.143 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex; the same rates appear in platform_tinker/reports/combined/combined_capstone.tex, where the trap rates are given as GRPO 36% and PPO 71%). The PPO rate of 12/17 has a Wilson 95% CI of [47%, 87%]. These are descriptive uncertainty intervals for heterogeneous roster counts; their overlap is not a formal test of a rate difference, and the pool composition prevents an algorithm-level stability comparison.

Two caveats on that comparison must travel with it. First, the PPO pool is dominated by classic-RL library runs (SB3, CleanRL, Tianshou) that failed to train at all, with last-10 rewards below 5%; a near-zero reward trajectory mechanically produces a high instability index. Restricted to LLM-native PPO, the PPO Llama instability is 0.105, lower than GRPO Llama's 0.228, and the source therefore explicitly declines to claim that GRPO is more or less stable than PPO at the algorithmic level (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex; platform_tinker/reports/combined/combined_capstone.tex). Second, the audit's reward metric is the in-training reward, not a held-out one, and the audit pool is single-seed per configuration: each Tinker configuration was run once and is reported descriptively. The only multi-seed matched base-versus-RL control on Qwen3-8B is the five-seed comparison discussed below.

An earlier draft of this report's summary attributed to P4 the claim that Dr. GRPO is the only algorithm without the verbosity-trap pattern. That claim could not be located in any P4 source. What the sources state is that 36% of the audit's GRPO runs carry the flag; the audit does not report a per-algorithm flag rate for Dr. GRPO, and in the controlled sixteen-run comparison the length-bias flag is zero for both algorithms while the peak-then-decay rule fires on both (three of three GRPO and two of three Dr. GRPO runs on GSM8K-CoT). The finding recorded here is therefore the sourced one: the peak-then-decay pattern is not specific to GRPO, it appears in both arms of the controlled comparison without any length inflation, and the project makes no claim that Dr. GRPO is uniquely free of it. The narrowest related statement the sources do support is a length-drift one — that Dr. GRPO is the only algorithm whose length keeps drifting upward after the initial compression phase (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/length_bias_iter48.tex) — which is a different statistic and is not the verbosity-trap flag.

### 6.5.3 Exploratory length analyses

Post-hoc sliding-window, rank-correlation and length–reward analyses describe local variation in the same sixteen runs. They do not create additional independent replications or remove the generation cap. With five arithmetic seeds and three GSM8K seeds, estimates are sensitive to individual runs; within-run windows are autocorrelated. Appendix E records the statistical corrections and the historical exploratory test family. None supplies a multiplicity-surviving confirmatory result about length bias.

The historical coefficient-product percentages labelled “proportion mediated” are not causal mediation estimates. Products and ratios of marginal and partial correlations are not common-scale path coefficients, and same-step observational reward, length and ZVF do not establish a causal ordering. Local re-expansion flags likewise describe the chosen rule rather than prevalence of an unconstrained verbosity failure (sources: platform_modal/scripts/length_bias_mechanism.py; platform_modal/scripts/length_bias_iter24.py).

### 6.5.4 Held-out generalisation and the scale extension

The pillar's held-out results are the part of the chapter closest to a conventional capability claim, and they are reported with corresponding care. The only multi-seed matched base-versus-RL control on Qwen3-8B is a five-seed comparison on Qwen3-8B-Instruct, which moves from 82.0% to 83.3% with p = 0.26 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex). Stated as a mean improvement over the pre-RL checkpoint, the gain is +0.013 with a paired p = 0.256 at n = 5 — small and not statistically significant, and a matched pre-RL control for the Qwen3-8B-Base checkpoint is not available (sources: `platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_results_intro.tex`, `platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_intro.tex`). In summary, on this near-ceiling configuration, GRPO does not produce a held-out improvement that can be separated from noise at the available seed budget, and that GRPO and Dr. GRPO produce held-out gains that are indistinguishable from each other at that budget (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p4_abstract.tex).

On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct GSM8K-CoT, the stored mean pre/post changes are +6.2 percentage points for GRPO and +5.0 for Dr. GRPO over three seeds and the same 200 test items. These are observed score changes with an unresolved measurement problem: greedy pre-training evaluation of the same base checkpoint yields two correctness vectors, scoring 40/200 and 41/200 but disagreeing on seven items. Thus item discordance can arise before any training; the one-item aggregate difference does not bound the noise at one item. The retained records do not identify whether the cause is batching, padding, numerical kernels, model mode, sampling or another execution difference. No specific cause or deterministic repair is claimed without repeated unchanged-checkpoint evaluations and execution receipts. The historical seed-level and McNemar tests therefore do not isolate training benefit from evaluation variability. The separately implemented v2 comparison below is reported under its own contract (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/drgrpo_gsm8k_cot_full.json).

The raised-cap Qwen3-8B extension comprises six completed 30-step logs: GRPO and a centred-advantage arm labelled `drgrpo`, seeds 42, 123 and 456, LoRA rank 4, G = 8, batch 4, learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁵ and temperature 1.0. The source reports a 1,024-token cap. First-five-step to last-ten-step mean lengths are, in seed order, GRPO 1004.05 → 904.98, 980.51 → 943.61 and 995.73 → 900.09 tokens; the centred arm records 999.43 → 931.38, 972.38 → 902.04 and 1000.21 → 878.23. These are rollout-window means, not step-zero or unchanged-base evaluations. Every early window is close to the cap; exact truncation fractions cannot be recovered from mean lengths alone. No held-out accuracy, per-completion correctness-stratified length or token-mask receipt is present in these six files (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/tinker-runs/results/p4uncap_grpo_s42.json and corresponding s123/s456 and drgrpo files).

The extension cannot confirm canonical Dr. GRPO or an uncapped length-bias null. The historically adjacent `live_zvf_probe.py` source at commit 1959effe sums full-sequence log probabilities, supplies an extra final token-zero target and refreshes the sampler only at save intervals; `--loss drgrpo` changes reward standardisation, without introducing canonical fixed-token loss normalisation. Later code repairs are not retroactive execution evidence. The six receipts do not bind an immutable executed source, and earlier Dr. GRPO-labelled attempts explicitly retained as `invalid_actually_grpo` must not be pooled with them. The supportable result is only recorded length compression and training reward under a raised but still potentially binding cap; the intended held-out and controlled-length comparison remains missing.

The arithmetic late-efficiency statistic is likewise exploratory: it uses training-reward gain per unit length change on five seeds, under a short-response ceiling. It does not supply a held-out benefit or resolve the cap-censoring problem (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/length_bias_iter136_step_coupling.tsv).

### 6.5.5 Same-stack rerun on the unsaturated configuration

The historical arithmetic comparisons of §6.2.3 and §6.4.1 sit near accuracy 0.99 and have the evaluation and budget limitations stated there. To test the contrasts where headroom exists, they were rerun on 2 October 2026 (v1) on the configuration of §6.5.4: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GSM8K with a boxed-answer correctness reward and a 200-token chain-of-thought cap, LoRA rank 16 on the attention projections, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁵, 30 steps, two inner epochs of the clipped surrogate with per-response length normalisation, on one A10G per run (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/modal/modal_samestack_gsm8k_cot.py). Three arms share that stack and a fixed count of 64 generations per step, but differ in advantage estimation, critic training and distinct-prompt exposure: GRPO with G = 8 (8 prompts × 8 completions), GRPO with G = 2 (32 × 2), and PPO with a learned value head on the prompt-end hidden state (64 × 1, value loss 0.5 × MSE, batch-normalised advantage). Each run evaluates the same 200 GSM8K *test* items greedily before and after training, so the evaluation set is disjoint from the training split. Five paired seeds were run per arm; the unit is the seed, and the analysis, including a ±2-point equivalence margin, was fixed in the script before launch (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/samestack_gsm8k_cot.json).

Two design features of v1 prevent its PPO contrast from being read as an estimator comparison. First, the 200-token cap was binding: mean training completions are 190.16 (G8), 191.62 (G2) and 194.16 (PPO) tokens under cap 200, with PPO last-ten length 196.19, and the starting model scores only 0.200 under this cap, so the endpoint is performance under a binding completion budget; the fraction of the gain attributable to learning to finish within that budget was not identified (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/samestack_scope_check.json, regenerated by platform_hybrid/experiments/scripts/samestack_scope_check.py from samestack_gsm8k_cot_full.json). The 64-prompt PPO batch also saw eight times as many distinct prompts per step as GRPO at G = 8. Fixed generation counts are not measured equality of generated tokens or total compute.

## 6.6 Cross-Pillar Observations and Limitations

The core studies establish no general algorithm ranking or scaling law. The short and interrupted scale traces cannot identify post-training scaling. The saturated arithmetic PPO/GRPO records have unequal training overlap and different evaluation items, so they do not estimate an isolated estimator effect. The G sweep changes sampling expenditure and has little accuracy headroom. Synthetic mitigation panels, imputed-feature risk scores and unexecuted large-budget reconstructions are excluded from empirical support.

ZVF has a stronger but narrower footing: its arithmetic is reproducible from stored rewards, and its unsigned definition necessarily aliases all-correct with all-wrong groups. That does not establish incremental prediction, useful gradients or a beneficial control policy. Capped response-length compression likewise does not identify the policies' unconstrained length behaviour. The raised-cap P4 extension adds training logs, not the missing held-out or censoring controls.

The unsaturated v2 comparison in Table 6.2 is the principal interim algorithm comparison. Its numerical snapshot remains unchanged and provisional, with incomplete planned seeds and an exposure-matched arm. The earlier v1 gap is superseded; the available v2 differences are protocol-specific and cannot establish final equivalence or identify which jointly changed component explains the change. Completing the declared comparison and its provenance is an empirical task, not a conclusion supplied by this source-only revision.
