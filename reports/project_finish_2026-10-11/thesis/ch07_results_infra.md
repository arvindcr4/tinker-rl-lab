# 7. Results II: Reporting, Registry and Controller (P5–P7)

The three internal reports covered in this chapter form a chain rather than a set of independent results. P5 makes a claim about the epistemic status of reinforcement-learning results for language models and proposes a checkable reporting standard to match it. P6 turns the standard into a machine-readable artifact with a validator, a badge and a diff tool. P7 takes the diagnostic that the standard requires authors to publish — the zero-variance fraction (ZVF) — and asks whether it is mechanistically predictable and ready to control rollout allocation. Each report is explicit about where its claim stops, and this chapter reports their evidence in the same register.

## 7.1 A Minimum Reporting Standard for Stack-Conditioned Results (P5)

P5 proposes reporting the configuration needed to interpret an RL result. This is an engineering contribution, not proof that framework explains a particular share of outcome variance. The inspected comparisons expose concrete ambiguities: the managed Tinker/TRL exhibit associates 0.856 and 0.050 last-10 training rewards with different Base/Instruct checkpoints, as well as different loss, parser, prompt, sampling and optimiser settings. It cannot assign the gap to backend or checkpoint. Near-zero reward at the start of a run does not diagnose a parser or template failure without the corresponding outputs and scoring checks (sources: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/framework_comparison.json; platform_hybrid/experiments/master_results.json).

A smaller managed head-to-head contains twelve records, four labels and three seeds each, on Qwen3.5-4B/GSM8K. Mean last-10 reward spans 0.710–0.744 over fifteen steps. This is a short-horizon configuration comparison; the small band does not establish canonical implementation equivalence or equivalence of performance (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/tinker-runs/results/week_h2h/h2h2_*.json). An open dynamic-sampling arm reporting zero ZVF and a managed asymmetric-clip surrogate sharing the DAPO label are likewise different recipes on different tasks/models. Discarding homogeneous groups makes accepted-batch ZVF zero by construction and does not make collection free.

### Reporting requirements and demonstrated tooling

The standard has eight items: (1) loss form, including ratio, clipping, token mask, advantage normalisation and sampling filters; (2) reference policy and KL handling; (3) sampler, backend, precision and checkpoint identity; (4) per-step reward/ZVF telemetry; (5) group-size and escalation schedule; (6) a disjoint evaluation split; (7) contamination and parser checks; and (8) held-out pass@k with uncertainty. The first seven describe the run; the eighth describes evaluation. This list is justified by the identifiable failure modes, without claiming its optimality (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p5_stack.tex).

The badge audit reports zero gold, zero silver, 99 bronze and four wood badges over its 103 manifests. That is evidence about declared coverage. In the historical 98 sampling manifests every top-level field is present, yet key KL and loss details are inapplicable or unavailable; applying a training-reporting rubric to these records cannot certify actual RL implementation. The reported cell-level random-forest fits, variance shares and fingerprint-distance correlations remain exploratory analyses of this sampling grid. They are excluded as evidence of predictive sufficiency or transfer to independently trained runs. Shared configuration, identifiers and outcome-derived fields require grouped holdouts and a separately bound feature definition before such a claim could be assessed.

The exact-tie jitter example provides a concrete reason to publish definitions: tiny reward perturbations remove all 95 exact ties among 600 groups, while the historical sample-variance tolerance of 10⁻⁶ retains all 95 and PCD changes negligibly (§6.3.1). It does not establish that PCD is a superior controller or that a reporting badge predicts learning.

## 7.2 The GRPO-Registry (P6)

**Validation boundary.** Structural schema validity, internal agreement, source binding, executed-code fidelity and outcome prediction are distinct properties. Seven eligible declarations can agree without proving execution honesty; comparisons sharing entries also need dependence-aware uncertainty. The badge reports coverage. The cell-level predictive estimates below do not establish transfer: saved feature definitions, grouped folds and independent-run/configuration holdouts are needed to rule out shared-configuration or outcome-derived information and test incremental value beyond reward, G and task.

P6's premise is that the deltas defining GRPO variants — DAPO's asymmetric clip, dynamic sampling and token-level loss; Dr. GRPO's dropped length and standard-deviation normalisation; GSPO's sequence-level importance ratio [@zheng2025gspo] — live in paper appendices and framework defaults rather than in any machine-readable form (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). The registry is the response, and it ships as three artifacts at `registry/`: `schema.json`, `entries/*.json` and `query.py` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/paper_P6_registry.tex).

The schema defines two record types under a single `oneOf`, both carrying `schema_version` (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_schema.tex). The stack record's core is `min_report`, one sub-object per minimum-report item, with base-checkpoint identity required inside the `sampler_backend` block precisely because the audit that motivated item 3 found a managed backend silently pinning a different base checkpoint. Two design decisions carry weight. The first is the distinction between an unreported field and a field reported as absent: a null means the author said nothing, whereas an explicit `false`, `0.0` or `"none"` means the author made a checkable claim — "a stack that says 'we use no KL term' is making a checkable claim; a stack that says nothing is not." The second is that item 8, the held-out pass@k requirement, is attached under outcomes as an evaluation-report field rather than folded into the run manifest, preserving the eight-item standard's split between what describes a run and what describes a result. Per-component delta status is recorded on the stack side in `variant_deltas_applied`, with each entry's status drawn from {implemented, surrogate, absent, unknown}. The shipped entry for the managed DAPO run makes the point concretely: `clip_higher` is recorded as `surrogate` with the note that the asymmetric clip was set through the user-facing config while the underlying managed loss form is unverifiable, `dynamic_sampling` is recorded as `absent`, and `token_level_loss` is `unknown` because it is managed by the runtime. An optional `ci_method` object (method, bootstrap count, seed, confidence level, source) is populated on the seven derived entries with `n_boot = 2000` and a percentile 95% interval, and left null on the remaining twenty-four.

Three variant-delta records were verified against their source papers: DAPO with five components (clip higher at ε_low = 0.2 and ε_high = 0.28, dynamic sampling, token-level loss, overlong reward shaping, KL removed); Dr. GRPO with two removals (the 1/|o_i| response-length normalisation in the loss and the division by the within-group reward standard deviation in the advantage); and GSPO with a length-normalised sequence-likelihood importance ratio and sequence-level clipping.

The seed population described in the P6 report is twenty stack records in five families, all of which pass validation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_population.tex). The `registry/entries/` directory at this revision holds 28 stack records and 20 variant-delta files, so the population counts, badge spans and coverage percentages below describe the earlier 20-record snapshot rather than the current directory, where the badge spans 43–93 (mean 62.68; §7.2). Family A holds four framework config dumps (Qwen3-8B, GSM8K, seed 42) scoring badges 74, 60, 60 and 62; family B holds the four open Colab trainer arms at toy scale, scoring 96, 96, 96 and 96; family C is the managed head-to-head at 57, 62, 62 and 60; family D contributes three variant records from a same-stack four-method run at 67 each; and family E contributes five method records from a five-seed risk-index batch at 43 each. The family sizes are therefore 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 5 = 20. The finding worth stating plainly is why the ordering looks the way it does. The config dumps were assembled for a reviewer-requested fairness audit and score only 60 to 74: they nail items 2, 3 and 5, expose item 1 only partially, and cover items 6 and 7 not at all. The open Colab arms score 96 not because the runs are better but because an open backward pass, coupled with explicit declarations that no KL term and no decontamination check were used, makes every field reportable. Reproducibility supplements of the kind commonly shipped with RL papers do not automatically satisfy MIN-REPORT.

The historical registry snapshot contains 31 records: twenty stack declarations and eleven variant-delta records, all parsing successfully with no broken delta pointers. These are structural checks, not execution certification. Among ten DAPO-labelled declarations, the delta statuses are distributed as shown below; unknown is a reporting state, not a verified implementation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex).

The registry was stress-tested rather than asserted. A batch of adversarial perturbations with B = 4000 bootstrap resamples — 13 categories × 31 entries × 10 mutations = 4,030 generated, of which 800 were no-ops for their entry and were skipped, leaving 3,230 applied (source: platform_hybrid/experiments/results/p5p8/registry_stress_per_run.tsv and registry_stress_summary.json) — moved the overall robustness statistic from 0.864 [0.756, 0.878] before the corresponding schema bumps to 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] after them, closing three silent-failure categories: a missing identifier pattern check (0.645 to 1.000), MIN-REPORT leaves declared in `properties` but not in `required` (0.000 to 1.000, fixed by promoting key presence to a schema invariant), and free-form delta references (0.000 to 1.000, fixed by replacing `delta_id` with an enum generated from the entry filenames) (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex, §Stress Test). A separate consistency check cross-references the variant-delta records against the MIN-REPORT blocks: of 31 triples from 13 entries, 7 are MATCH, 0 are MISMATCH, 0 are MISSING_REPORT, 5 are SURROGATE_OBS and 19 are NOT_APPLICABLE, giving an internal-consistency match rate of 7/7 = 1.000 with a Wilson 95% CI of [0.65, 1.00] (a percentile bootstrap of 7/7 is degenerate at [1, 1]).

A separately recorded same-stack comparison supplies an illustrative case; it does not validate the schema causally. Re-executing four GRPO-family methods (GRPO, AERO, GIFT, AREAL) on a single identical stack — managed sampler, G = 8, seed 0, 40 steps, 16 prompts per step — with the full per-prompt reward tensor logged at every step, produces reward deltas of magnitude ≤ 2 percentage points. Two of the three show within-run intervals that exclude zero on the side of a *decrease* (AERO at −0.014 [−0.023, −0.005] and AREAL at −0.020 [−0.032, −0.008]). Those intervals are paired bootstrap intervals over the last 10 of 40 steps of a single seed-0 run per method, so they carry no seed variance and are not replication. GIFT's delta is +0.016 [−0.007, +0.040]. Exhaustively enumerated sign-flip calculations over the same 10 paired steps, Holm-adjusted across the three contrasts, give p = 0.047 for AERO, 0.047 for AREAL and 0.23 for GIFT; when prompt-level variation is included (160 paired prompt-steps), the AERO decrease is no longer distinguishable from zero (Holm p = 0.20) (source: outputs/PES_Phase2_Third_Review_2026-09-24/thesis_review_2026-09-27/stats_followup/m16_summary.json). The observed magnitudes describe these traces only. Inferential exactness requires exchangeability or sign-symmetry assumptions, which enumeration alone does not establish for sequential steps. Nested prompt-steps are not additional training replications. Whether any difference exceeds seed-to-seed noise was not tested, so neither the claim that all differences are within noise nor its negation is established. The largest same-stack effects are not on reward at all: AERO and AREAL lengthen completions by approximately 31 tokens and tighten the length coefficient of variation, and GIFT's loss shift of −1.67×10⁴ is a baseline constant (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_measured_evidence.tex). A later registry extension measured an "anti-herding" residual $\delta_{\mathrm{div}} = \mathrm{ZVF}^{\mathrm{iid}} - \mathrm{ZVF}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ on the same tensors; this analysis is **withdrawn**. The source reported a stack-mean of 0.049 with a range of [0.039, 0.053]; only GIFT showed a borderline-significant reduction against GRPO (p = 0.066, CI [−0.021, +0.001]), which it read as resolving a synthesis range of [0.13, 0.23] downwards (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_registry_health.tex, §Contrastive Yield and Anti-Herding). The reading does not survive: with $\mathrm{ZVF}^{\mathrm{iid}}$ computed by plugging each group's own success rate into $h_G$, a positive $\delta_{\mathrm{div}}$ is guaranteed by construction — its expectation is +0.105 even under i.i.d. rollouts with no herding at all — so neither its sign nor its size is evidence of a diversity effect, and the associated iso-G table is withdrawn with it.

The reference CLI, `registry/query.py`, implements four verbs using only the standard library: `list`; `query --item reference_kl` returning FULL, PARTIAL or UNREPORTED; `badge`; and `stackdiff`, which maps a pair of entries onto the R0–R5 ladder and reports the highest rung triggered — R0 identical manifests, R1 nuisance-only, R2 runtime differences, R3 loss-form or KL-handling differences, R4 differing variant-delta sets, R5 unauditable because a differing item is null on at least one side (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_query_auditor.tex). The badge formula weights the seven items equally and is outcome-blind: $\mathrm{badge}(s) = \lfloor 100 \cdot \tfrac{1}{7} \sum_i c_i(s) \rfloor$. `stackdiff` prints a warning at R4 and above that outcome differences must not be attributed to the shared algorithm label, and the P6 report is careful to describe the ladder as "a risk scale, not an effect-size scale." Applied to the shipped entries, `stackdiff` flags the open-versus-closed "DAPO" pair as R5 from manifests alone, and across the twenty seed entries the MIN-REPORT badge spanned 43–96 (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex); across the 28 current stack entries, `query.py badge` gives 43–93 with mean 62.68. Two boundaries deserve restating. The registry reports stated provenance and conflicts; it does not reconcile unresolved exact-ID or model conflicts, nor does it validate nominal algorithm comparisons (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p6_abstract.tex). And the badge is coarse relative to the corpus — twenty entries collapse to ten distinct fingerprints, and twenty of twenty-three sub-fields carry at most two values. The version of `stackdiff` described in the P6 report did not compare the model field, so the Base-versus-Instruct checkpoint difference behind the 17× exhibit was invisible to it; its R5 rung meant only that a differing item was null on one side, and it fired on 366 of the 378 pairs among the 28 current stack entries; and it shipped without unit tests. It was revised on 16 October 2026, and the revised tool is the one evaluated here. It compares a fixed catalogue of 48 fields and assigns a level only from fields known on both sides that differ, so missing information no longer raises the verdict. R5 is now reserved for outcome-critical fields — execution mode, checkpoint identity and variant, reward definition, prompt and chat template, dataset and evaluation protocol — and missing information is reported separately as a coverage fraction, the worst severity that could still be hidden, and an UNDERSPECIFIED flag when coverage is below 0.8 or an R5 field is unknown. Across the 378 pairs of the 28 stack entries the distribution is R0 39, R2 1, R3 10, R4 26 and R5 302; 342 pairs are UNDERSPECIFIED, with median coverage 0.19, because 16 of the 28 entries are still hand-entered rather than generated from a run configuration. Among the 78 pairs whose checkpoint is known and identical on both sides, the distribution is R0 39, R2 1, R3 10, R4 26 and R5 2, so the R5 rung is driven mainly by checkpoint differences rather than by missing data. On the 17× exhibit the revised tool returns R5 on checkpoint identity and variant (Qwen3-8B-Base against Qwen3-8B), reward definition, and prompt and chat template, at coverage 0.58 — which is the confound the exhibit was missing. On the two "DAPO" records it returns R5 on checkpoint, reward, template, dataset and evaluation metric at coverage 0.87, confirming that the pair is not comparable. The tool ships with 29 passing unit tests (`tests/test_registry_stackdiff.py`). It remains a reporting check: an R5 verdict says which recorded fields differ, not how much they move the outcome, and its predictive value for outcome gaps is untested. The committed 11 October version, whose output on the DAPO pair (KL coefficient 0.0 against null, R5 unauditable, exit code 5) is quoted in earlier drafts, requires its immutable source commit and registry snapshot to reproduce; moving git `HEAD` is not a revision identifier. The exact historical source/dirty-diff binding remains unresolved in this review copy.

## 7.3 From Diagnostic to Controller (P7)

P7 investigates whether reward-group statistics can guide rollout allocation. The mathematical identities, stored-tensor replay and small online experiment are distinct evidence classes. None establishes a validated adaptive policy.

The theory is compact. Fix a prompt with latent per-rollout success probability p and a group of G conditionally independent Bernoulli rewards. Write $h_G(p) = p^G + (1-p)^G$ for the probability that the group is degenerate, and model the usable signal per prompt as $S(p,G) = p(1-p)(1-h_G(p))$. This product is a heuristic allocation objective, not an identity for expected advantage magnitude. For unnormalised centered binary advantages, $M_G=2\hat p(1-\hat p)$ and $E[M_G\mid p]=2(G-1)p(1-p)/G$ already include homogeneous groups' zero contributions. Conditioning on a mixed group divides this expectation by $1-h_G(p)$ rather than multiplying by it. Reward contrast also omits score-vector magnitudes and cancellation, so it does not determine gradient magnitude. Both factors vanish at the endpoints and peak at p = ½, so this proposed signal proxy is largest at intermediate latent success probability and vanishes at the endpoints; it is not a measured law of learning speed (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_theory.tex). Three consequences follow, each paired with the measurement that could have falsified it. T1 states that the expected ZVF indicator is exactly $h_G(p)$. T2 states that $h_G$ is strictly decreasing in G for 0 < p < 1, with exponential decay rate −ln max(p, 1−p). T3 states that with unnormalised group-centred advantages the mean absolute advantage in a group is exactly 2p̂(1−p̂).

The measured arithmetic sweep's mean ZVF declines with G (§6.4.1), but the larger 368-record audit is not a verified independent replication cohort (§7.1). Plugging a group's own observed success rate into the null does not validate latent-p predictions: the observed indicator is identically one at empirical p̂ = 0 or 1 and zero in the interior. Likewise, reward-contrast identities do not establish gradient magnitudes or learning gains. The retained scalar gradient correlation lacks the underlying per-group gradient data needed for recomputation (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_theory.tex).

PCD is a magnitude-aware descriptive statistic, not a solution to sign ambiguity: it too is symmetric under p ↔ 1−p. Mean reward and the counts of all-correct, all-wrong and mixed groups remain necessary. A proposed allocation rule combining these signals still needs prospective testing.

The source reports one toy open-trainer seed per arm with unequal sampling expenditure. Fixed GRPO and its centred-advantage comparison use 120 rollouts; dynamic sampling uses 174 and adaptive G uses 186. Their recorded held-out gains are respectively +0.500, +0.575, +0.550 and +0.575. These are small-task configuration outcomes, not equal-budget causal estimates or replicated controller superiority (source: platform_hybrid/paper/sections/p7_controller.tex). In particular, zero ZVF among accepted dynamic-sampling batches is purchased by rejecting already sampled candidates.

The remaining controller results are retrospective accounting or model-based projections. Resampling an observed all-zero or all-one group cannot produce an unseen outcome; its zero-rescue result is a mathematical property of that plug-in procedure, not empirical evidence that additional fresh model responses cannot restore contrast. Beta-posterior and hindsight alternatives change those assumptions and objectives, so their cost/restoration rankings cannot be treated as measured policy gains. Their detailed numerical rankings are excluded from the main results. The invalidated N10 outputs are excluded; their implementation discrepancies belong to the audit history, and no corrected replication is claimed here.

The historical name `Dualformer-Auto` denotes an internal difficulty-gated group-size rule, not an implementation of the published Dualformer method [@su2025dualformer], which learns fast/slow reasoning modes from randomized traces. Its shrink-only cost ratio must not be combined with an escalation-only contrast-efficiency statistic. The latter script computes $1000R/(8F)$, where R is model-predicted restored contrast and F counts fired prompts, assigning eight extra completions to each G = 8 → 16 fire; a step-level fire first expands to sixteen prompt fires. This denominator is gross positive escalation expenditure, not a signed net rollout change. It supplies no valid efficiency ranking for a rule whose net expenditure decreases, and the cross-axis ranking is withdrawn (sources: platform_modal/scripts/p5p8/p7_costeff_boot.py, lines 159–162; platform_modal/scripts/p5p8/p7_postpred_costeff.py, lines 42–44).

Fresh-rollout evidence addresses a different, narrower question. The historical V2 diagnostic (§7.4) and C1 (§7.5) directly observe additional responses from frozen policies rather than bootstrap-recycling eight rewards. They demonstrate that initial homogeneity need not persist under their respective contracts, with parser and numeric-correctness distinctions kept explicit. Their selected cohorts, checkpoints and parsers differ from the historical controller tensors; their rates cannot be transferred into a rescue estimate for those runs or treated as controller benefit. A matched-budget, multi-seed online comparison with a frozen trigger and held-out endpoint remains unresolved.

## 7.4 Historical V2 Frozen-Policy Fresh-Rollout Diagnostic

**Format-induced contrast is distinct from numeric contrast.** A prospectively frozen diagnostic completed on 2 October 2026 found that 33 of 461 initially homogeneous eight-response groups became mixed after 24 additional responses: 7.16%, with a prompt-level Wilson 95% interval of 5.14-9.88%. Twenty of those first reward changes were parser failures whose visible final number still agreed with the gold answer. The remaining 13/461 (2.82%) were assistant-audited numeric-correctness contrasts: two wrong-to-correct recoveries and eleven correct-to-wrong changes. Thus the raw 7.16% measures variation in this diagnostic's binary reward, not a useful learning-signal rate or an accuracy gain (sources: outputs/rescue_colab_v2_2026-10-02/analysis_final/summary.json; outputs/rescue_colab_v2_2026-10-02/audit/completed_primary_event_audit.json).

The main cohort contained 512 GSM8K **train** prompts, each with 32 ordered responses from a frozen public Qwen/Qwen3-8B checkpoint in BF16 non-thinking mode. All 16,384 main responses completed. A disjoint 32-prompt smoke cohort was excluded from every main estimate. Each draw was a fresh response to the same original prompt, with a unique request seed, rather than a continuation of a previous answer. The primary endpoint was predeclared: among prompts whose first eight binary rewards all agreed, count those with both rewards in the combined 32. The strict parser used exact rational matching; parser failures received reward zero, while cap-hit outputs retained their exact-match reward and were separately flagged. Neither kind of output was removed from the primary denominator. Appendix B §B.12 gives the pinned revisions, decoder, receipt chain and audit method (source: outputs/rescue_colab_v2_2026-10-02/analysis_final/validation.json).

They do not reconstruct a historical checkpoint, demonstrate training or controller benefit, identify a group-size optimum, or estimate held-out capability. ## 7.5 C1 Results with Reference and Unit Boundaries

Benchmark reference errors, ambiguous questions and equivalent units limit what this diagnostic can establish. Its frozen parser-defined result is **11/64 questions (17.19%; Wilson 95% interval 9.88-28.21%)**. Assistant-assisted reconciliation corroborates **three numeric recovery witnesses on the same 64-question denominator**. This 3/64 count (4.69%) describes the support found under a conservative review rule; it does not replace the frozen endpoint or estimate a population recovery rate. The other eight parser events include three convention-dependent interpretations, four materially ambiguous cases and one match to an incorrect reference answer. [S1-S4]

The study contains no training update. It does not measure useful reinforcement-learning gradients, reasoning improvement, controller superiority, held-out accuracy or general model capability. The prompts are public GSM8K training questions and are fresh only to this campaign. Historical V2 evidence remains separate and unpooled.
