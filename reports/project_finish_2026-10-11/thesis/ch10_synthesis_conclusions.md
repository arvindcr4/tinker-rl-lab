# 9. Cross-Study Synthesis, Threats to Validity, Conclusions and Future Work

## 9.1 Cross-Study Synthesis

This report presents two bodies of evidence with different instruments. The P1–P7 corpus examines short-horizon GRPO-style post-training and produces a diagnostic layer, machine-readable registry and controller audit. The E1–E14 campaign is an infrastructure and harness audit of one lightly trained checkpoint, with incomplete execution provenance and later exploratory protocol overrides. It does not test training efficacy. The common methodological commitment is attribution: separate measured outcomes from reconstructions, preserve denominators and nulls, and narrow a claim whenever the data cannot isolate its cause.

The retained contribution is strongest where the evidence can be recomputed without inferring an unobserved training process. Three stored Qwen3-8B reward tensors reproduce ZVF values 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155, pooled to 0.1583. Exact ties, sample-variance thresholds and population-variance conventions are kept distinct. The binomial identity explains why both all-correct and all-wrong groups can be homogeneous; fitted prompt-difficulty heterogeneity is compatible with the observed excess over a homogeneous null, without identifying dependence or useful-gradient loss. ZVF is therefore an instrument for a specified reward tensor, not a new diagnostic or a validated predictor of training benefit (§6.3) [@he2026avspo; @nie2026starvation].

Several earlier headline interpretations do not survive source inspection. P1's heterogeneous scale anchors include interrupted traces and confounded recipes; their fits cannot establish either a scaling law or a scaling null. The nine-method mitigation panel is synthetic, and the risk-index panel combines simulated rows with imputed collapse features; neither validates an intervention or predictor. The arithmetic PPO/GRPO comparison uses different evaluation items and unequal overlap with training, so its difference and equivalence tests do not estimate an isolated algorithm effect. Larger-budget group-size points are reconstructions rather than trained arms. These exclusions are not negative experimental results (§§6.2–6.4).

The capped P4 records describe observed lengths but do not test unconstrained verbosity. A 200-token cap leaves little room for expansion; the raised-cap extension also begins close to its limit and lacks a held-out evaluation or immutable executed-source binding. Separately, nominally unchanged pre-training evaluations disagree on seven of 200 items, so the historical pre/post tests do not isolate learning from evaluation variability. The late-efficiency statistic is exploratory training-metric evidence. Correcting its test does not convert it into a held-out capability result (§6.5).

The separately contracted v2 unsaturated comparison is the principal interim algorithm comparison, with its numerical snapshot preserved in Table 6.2. It remains provisional because planned PPO seeds and the exposure-matched arm are incomplete. Joint changes to critic initialization, generation cap, scoring and evaluation scope prevent attributing the v1–v2 difference to a single repair. The five-arm variant audit likewise supplies no multiplicity-adjusted rejection or established equivalence. A completed, adequately powered estimator comparison remains open.

P5 and P6 contribute a reporting specification and implemented checks. The nominal 17× training-reward contrast changes checkpoint, reward, template and other settings; it cannot attribute an effect to backend. The retained 98-cell mega subset is sampling-only, not 98 training runs, and cannot validate claims about training variance or outcome prediction. The 368 service records include failed/crashed records and lack a complete training/simulation classification; the historical 790-run total has no recovered exact membership. These counts are distinct provenance populations (§7.1). The registry distinguishes missing declarations from explicit absence, exposes method-label ambiguity and implements a 48-field comparison. Across its 28 current stack entries, 342 of 378 pairs remain underspecified. This is useful reporting infrastructure, without validation of predictive sufficiency, execution honesty or causal risk (§7.2).

P7's controller audit supplies a design warning rather than an allocation recommendation. In the counterfactual trace, 1,723 of 1,867 escalation events occur on observed all-correct groups. That share largely follows from the reward level, and no controller executed in that replay. Plugging observed success rates of zero or one into a Bernoulli model forces zero predicted rescue; it does not establish latent certainty. Historical N10 scripts do not implement one frozen controller and are excluded as replications. The shrink-only rule's gross-added-rollout efficiency is undefined when its denominator is zero; avoided-rollout efficiency is a different endpoint. Neither the heuristic nor the toy gradient correlation establishes a useful-update or held-out benefit (§7.3; Appendix E).

Fresh frozen-policy sampling supplies a narrower empirical result. Historical V2 observes 33/461 initially homogeneous groups becoming reward-mixed, but 20 first changes are format-induced parser failures. The 13 reviewed numeric contrasts comprise two wrong-to-correct recoveries and eleven correct-to-wrong changes. C1 retains its separate 11/64 parser-defined endpoint; assistant-assisted reconciliation supports three numerical recovery witnesses and a 22-case union of specified reference defects. The first-review/second-review cross-tab accounts for all 64 cases and thirteen category disagreements (§§7.4–7.5; Appendix G). Neither study measures gradients, training improvement or a controller effect, and neither is pooled with the other.

The synthetic card-fraud study P8 remains outside the RL contribution (Appendix F). Its tree and SFT language-model AUCs use different held-out subsets and do not establish a matched performance ranking, deployment calibration or production economics.

The E1–E14 campaign supplies a bounded harness audit rather than a broad post-training result. The reported actor budget is 40 steps, 80 prompt draws and 320 completions; the historical scalar-loss threshold was met on 35 steps, without enough telemetry to count zero gradients or parameter updates. Exact training source, consumed prompts and convergence evidence remain missing. The public base-model identity is verified (Appendix B §B.14), but public model availability does not authenticate historical execution. Private recovery supports selected receipt fields and arithmetic without making those receipts independently available in the public package.

Under these limitations, the headline campaign outcomes remain LAB-Bench 450/1,967, AgentDojo benign utility 88/97, VerilogEval 129/312 across 156 paired designs, SWE-bench Pro 2/731, and Omni-MATH native judged-row accuracy 2,271/4,426 (51.31%), with a separate all-disposition diagnostic of 2,271/4,428 (51.29%). The denominator-label correction is explained in §8.2. These are end-to-end protocol scores. Their failure analyses expose 593 malformed SWE-bench Pro patches and 150 VerilogEval extraction failures; cap-related observational differences do not isolate a causal budget effect. Six later replacement scopes were attempted under exploratory self-issued protocol overrides, outside the original prospective gates (Tables 8.3–8.4). They are neither original-contract completions nor evidence that independent governance succeeded. Appendix C preserves the scope and status of each lane.

In the same-engine paired pilot (Table 8.6), comparisons with an applicable test or interval provide no conventional evidence of a nonzero effect. The E4 paired scores remain descriptive, without a test or interval; the pilot's small samples and differing inferential units do not establish equivalence. E12 adds a direct self-evaluation bias risk because its judge is the base-model family being compared; no independent regrade is available. E4's reported full-rerun zero is excluded from empirical conclusions because its secondary date conflicts with the pending-state ledger and the primary job receipt is missing. The retained bridge source documents omitted explicit stops and lossy history conversion; source inspection and offline protocol tests do not recover that historical run or attribute a benchmark failure to the defect (§8.5).

The historical ledger is useful as a record of decisions and unresolved conflicts, not a certificate of universal reproducibility. Its eleven named checks have a bounded historical scope; missing public dependencies prevent treating their recorded success as a fresh checkout-level validation. The 27 September self-issued override and unreconciled E4 history are governance limitations in the result, not exceptions to hide.

## 9.2 Rigour and Threats to Validity

Held-out *n* is reported alongside every gain, and failed runs are counted rather than hidden: failed attempts remain part of the stated limitations.

**Statistical power: single-seed Tinker experiments.** Most Tinker configurations ran once, because cost precluded repetition, and those single-seed runs carry no variance estimates or significance tests. The exceptions are stated where they occur. They are the three-seed-per-arm managed head-to-head (Chapter 7 §7.1), the five-seed Qwen3-8B held-out control (p = 0.26, Chapter 6 §6.5.4) and the Qwen3-8B scale extension. Henderson et al. (2018) [@henderson2018deep] motivate reporting variability across random seeds; no universal seed-count threshold guarantees power. A follow-up needs a prespecified effect size and power rationale. The seed counts are: one seed for most Tinker runs and all P1 anchors; three for the P3 sweep, the P4 GSM8K-CoT and scale-extension arms and the managed head-to-head; five for the same-stack PPO/GRPO contrast, the P4 arithmetic arms and the Qwen3-8B pre/post control; and eight for the variant audit (P11). The limitations document says the ten-seed bar was met for the TRL baseline and the GSM8K evaluation and then that the evaluation was cut to five seeds; the run records in Appendix A show five seeds for both the TRL Modal campaign (Table A.3) and the GSM8K pre/post evaluation (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1; Table 1.1). The 30B MoE scaling analysis uses three seeds; the bootstrap confidence intervals assume independent observations, so any positive correlation from shared initialisation or data ordering would make the reported intervals too narrow, and the true intervals wider; and secondary analyses carry uncorrected p-values. An open runner can improve inspectability, but does not by itself certify an executed configuration or remove leakage and measurement error; inferential support is assessed comparison by comparison, while single-seed Tinker figures remain descriptive (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2).

**Horizons of 30–50 steps.** The Tinker runs are early-training snapshots; long-horizon effects such as reward hacking, catastrophic forgetting and late-stage policy collapse are unlikely to manifest, and the source conclusion is direct: "Asymptotic claims are unsupported" (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.1). Short horizons are only one limitation. P1 lacks a common-budget, replicated pre/post design, so it supplies no scaling null; P4's observed compression under a 200-token cap does not identify unconstrained length bias.

**Closed Tinker internals.** The Tinker SDK exposes a custom loss path, a limited optimiser surface and standard LoRA hyperparameters, but not the exact server-side GRPO loss formulation, the reward normalisation or baseline-subtraction scheme, minibatch or gradient-accumulation strategy, hardware configuration, or system-level telemetry (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.1). This applies unevenly: the inspected custom-loss Tinker reference loops define their loss in client code (REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline, Table 4.2), while the actor's missing original execution source limits verification of its historical loss path (Appendix D), whereas the runners that use Tinker's built-in `importance_sampling` loss leave its server-side details unrecorded. The consequence is best quoted: Tinker results measure "the **platform's** implementation of GRPO, not an abstract algorithmic specification". The illustrative case in the limitations document compares a pooled last-10 training reward of 99.9% for Tinker GRPO with 73.4% for TRL GRPO on GSM8K. Those figures are training rewards, not held-out accuracy, and they pool Tinker runs on 4B–235B models against TRL on Qwen2.5-0.5B, so model size and implementation are confounded; the source itself attributes part of the gap to the model difference (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §6.2; platform_tinker/reports/combined/combined_capstone.tex). The only same-model pair, Qwen3-8B, used the Base checkpoint on Tinker and the Instruct checkpoint on TRL (Table A.2), so it does not remove the confound either. The threat propagates into the campaign, whose actor was trained on Tinker. The SDK is also a moving target: Phase 1 pinned `tinker==0.16.1`, while the September 2026 bridge required `tinker==0.30.0` after the older SDK produced a no-longer-supported error (source: withheld operational serving record).

**Per-suite native denominators are not comparable.** Every E1–E14 result is expressed on its own suite's denominator, and those denominators differ in size, in what they count and in what they exclude. SWE-bench Pro is 2/731, whose denominator retains 14 generation failures and 4 artifact losses alongside 713 native evaluations, 593 of which ran on the unpatched base because the submitted patch was corrupt for `git apply` (§8.3); APEX-Agents is 7/480; E9 MLE-bench is 40/75 competitions natively graded, stated as 53.33% coverage with a null suite score, because coverage and score are different quantities; E1's replacement SWE-bench Multilingual is 1/190 on one contract and 4/110 on another, never summed to a 300-task figure; E6 WebArena is 90/812 with 108 tasks ungraded, so its denominator counts items that were never graded; E13 BALROG is a mean of six per-environment means rather than a rate over episodes; and E14's native judged-row accuracy is 2,271/4,426 (51.31%) and its separate all-disposition diagnostic is 2,271/4,428 (51.29%), with the denominator erratum explained in §8.2 (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md). A 41.35% on one denominator and a 0.274% on another do not license a comparison, an average or a ranking, and none is offered; the rule holds within lanes too, and the ledger states it flatly: "N/A means unverified; no pooled results" (source: withheld historical campaign chronology).

**No cross-suite aggregate.** No mean, median or composite index over suites appears anywhere in this report or in the campaign materials it draws on: an aggregate over native denominators would be a number with no referent, mixing suites that measure different capabilities at different difficulties under different scoring rules. The cost is that the campaign has no single headline; the three strictly complete scopes, the terminal-complete Omni-MATH scope, the six exploratory protocol-override replacement scopes attempted on 27 September and the two original-contract scores are the most the record supports.

**The E10 benign-utility scope says nothing about adversarial robustness.** The E10 lane's completed evidence is a benign-utility evaluation of 97/97 completed tasks with utility 88/97 = 0.907 under AgentDojo's benign scope; its original contract, private AgentHarm tasks, is externally blocked and carries a closure record with a named reopen condition. The completeness result therefore carries no adversarial-robustness measurement, and this report makes no such claim for the trained actor. The lane has a dual-use dimension, though the limitations analysis finds the marginal uplift of the released artefacts small, because the base model's capability is overwhelmingly pre-existing: the pre-RL Qwen3-8B-Instruct checkpoint already scores 82.0% on held-out GSM8K, and full GRPO adds only +1.3 points to 83.3%, not significant at p = 0.26. The 82.0% is the same Instruct checkpoint before RL, not a separate base model (sources: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §2.2, §2.3; platform_hybrid/paper/archive/absorbed/U01_main_compendium/main.tex).

**The retained E11 result came through the original sampler.** The 129/312 full-run receipt attributes its samples to the managed Tinker sampler rather than a locally served copy. Private execution and repository access identities are omitted, so this public package cannot replay the original route. A different serving stack would be a protocol change with unmeasured same-weights, different-sampler divergence. The separate four-task step-40 pilot has different source and decontamination limitations and is not substituted for the full run.

**Known evidence loss in the finish-era recovery source.** The record is explicit: the historical source-loss incident recorded in `withheld historical source-loss chronology` removed the execution sources of the finish-era recovery and build code — the E1 wave-10 controller, the E9 restored dependencies and the E6 validator — while sealed requests, validation receipts and test logs survive with matching hashes, and the replacement lanes' native runners survive under `zvf-program/flagship/` (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §6). The lost-source policy was applied rather than waived: a replacement constrained by the retained regression fixtures (full historical behavioral equivalence is unverified) was written into a tracked path under `zvf-program/e1_wave10/`, where it verifies 10/10 targeted regressions offline (`test_process_group.py` 4/4, `test_cleanup_regressions.py` 6/6) (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §9.5). Two consequences follow: a sealed request without its execution source is not a rerun, so the E9 MLDevBench runtime recipe was never reconstructed (the 27 September run built a fresh environment on Google Cloud instead); and the policy response — execution sources henceforth live under tracked paths — cannot restore what was already lost. A related item: the Jev (TypeSafe) judgment harness lost reliability from roughly 13:10Z on 2026-09-19, both blocker-classification batteries returning degenerate distributions and a controlled yes-probe falling from about 1.0 to 0.3; the receipts are retained as incident evidence and the batteries must be re-run once the health check passes, so the ledger's first-uncleared-blocker column stands on document evidence rather than judgment output (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §8). The retained incident account does not identify a cause: no version-pinned before/after API payloads, bridge diff or prompt comparison survives here to distinguish an external model update from a local bridge/prompt regression. The approximate 13:10Z onset is an incident-report timestamp, not proof of either mechanism.

**Scope and confound inventory.** Several Phase-1 threats remain live. Many early runs logged train-set rewards only, so time-series comparisons spanning old and new runs must account for the instrumentation change. Initial phases used LoRA at ranks 8–64 exclusively; historical baseline comparisons use default hyperparameters from the Tinker cookbook and the TRL documentation; and no full learning-rate × rank × batch × group-size grid was swept. Tinker, Modal, Colab and the PES A100 node use different accelerators, memory hierarchies and training stacks, so observed differences in reward dynamics are not purely algorithmic, and the work was not pre-registered. The historical model-scale roster is heterogeneous, includes interrupted and single-seed traces, and supplies no reliable extrapolation interval (Chapter 6 §6.2); benchmark coverage is narrow and biased toward verifiable rewards such as exact match, unit tests and schema validity, with no human preference data, so findings should not be extended to reward-model-based RLHF; and the work was carried out at one institution in Bengaluru, so benchmark choices and evaluation design reflect that context (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §7.2, §7.3).

The resource accounting is itself a threat. The report estimates roughly 60 A100 GPU-hours of in-kind PES University compute and projects environmental use of about 776 kWh and about 296 kg CO₂-eq; the dominant uncertainty is the Tinker GPU-hour count, inferred because the platform exposes no hardware telemetry, giving a pessimistic Tinker contribution of about 540 kg against an optimistic one of about 130 kg. The mitigation offered is reproducibility by artefact (source: LIMITATIONS_AND_IMPACT.md §3, §4).

## 9.3 Conclusions

This report set out to build a multi-framework training harness for GRPO-style post-training of large language models and to study that post-training under a single methodological rule: hold the stack fixed, change one factor, report what happens. The harness dispatches one configuration to several back-ends, but only Tinker and TRL produced completed runs, on different base checkpoints, so it is not a completed multi-framework benchmark. The four research questions of §1.4 have the following answers.

- **RQ1 (ZVF).** Yes, it is recomputable: the three stored Qwen3-8B tensors reproduce 0.130, 0.190 and 0.155 exactly. Its predictive validity remains unestablished: the historical outcome and collapse correlations mix synthetic and measured records, and the outcome is not a common held-out measure (§6.3; Table 1.1). Its magnitude is regime-dependent, from 0.16 to 0.84 across selected measured cohort means.
- **RQ2 (identifiable algorithm and group-size contrasts).** Partly answered. The arithmetic estimator comparison is excluded because leakage, unequal exposure and broken item pairing prevent isolated attribution; a ceiling is an additional limitation, not its only defect. Capped length records do not identify unconstrained length bias, and inconsistent unchanged-base evaluations limit historical pre/post claims. The unsaturated v2 and five-arm variant comparisons retain the scoped limits below. <!-- SAMESTACK-V2 NUMBERS (headline only): provisional; source of truth is Table 6.2. -->
The separately contracted same-stack rerun on the unsaturated Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K-CoT configuration supplies an interim comparison. Its first version (v1: 200-token cap, cold critic, 200 test items, five seeds) showed GRPO at G = 8 exceeding PPO by +5.0 percentage points (+0.050; 95% CI [+1.5, +8.5] points), but its PPO arm finished below its starting accuracy (0.195 against 0.200) and the sign-flip p after Holm adjustment was 0.19. The corrected v2 rerun (512-token cap, warm-started critic, full 1,319-item test split, independent re-grade agreeing on every item) supersedes it: GRPO G8 − PPO is +0.76 percentage points (+0.0076; 95% CI [−0.34, +1.85] points) over seven paired seeds, an interim TOST calculation at ±2 points (±0.02) (p = 0.016), not a final equivalence conclusion; G8 − G2 is +0.79 points (+0.0079; [+0.02, +1.56]) over ten seeds, not significant after Holm adjustment (p = 0.14); and G2 − PPO is +0.04 points (+0.0004), from a base accuracy of 0.654. Under the revised protocol the interim difference test does not detect a GRPO advantage over PPO. Critic training, generation cap, scoring and evaluation scope changed together, so the v1–v2 comparison does not isolate any one repair. The figures are provisional while three PPO seeds and the pre-specified exposure-matched PPO arm complete (§6.5.5, Table 6.2).
- **RQ3 (manifest-level flip risk).** Yes, as a reporting check. The revised `stackdiff` flags both exhibits from their records alone — the 17× pair at R5 on checkpoint, reward and template (coverage 0.58) and the "DAPO" pair at R5 on checkpoint, reward, template, dataset and evaluation metric (coverage 0.87) — and reports missing information separately; 342 of the 378 pairs are underspecified because 16 entries are hand-entered (§7.2). Whether its flags predict outcome differences was not tested.
- **RQ4 (governance-first evaluation).** Partly answered as an audit: the ledger separates scopes and records failures, but missing public receipts, the E4 chronology conflict and self-issued protocol overrides limit reproducibility and prospective governance. It does not yield a post-training delta: the actor had 40 reported training steps, and the paired Table 8.6 comparisons provide no conventional evidence of a nonzero effect where a test or interval is reported, while E4 remains descriptive without either.

The analytical contribution is a verified diagnostic calculation, a reporting specification and a set of explicit evidence boundaries. ZVF is recomputable but predictive validity is unestablished; the homogeneous-group identity explains structure without validating an allocation policy. The surviving short-horizon contrasts do not establish a general algorithm ranking, group-size optimum or uncensored length-bias result. Interrupted scaling fits, synthetic/imputed validation panels and evaluation-contaminated estimator comparisons are excluded from scientific support, rather than retained as nulls. The reporting standard and registry remain implemented artifacts, whose utility for predicting independent outcomes still needs testing. The novelty claim is bounded by the reviewed literature, not proof that no equivalent specification exists (§7.1).

The E1–E14 campaign contributes infrastructure and harness findings with explicit governance limitations: the actor received 40 steps of critic-free policy-gradient training (§8.1.1), so its scores describe a checkpoint rather than a training method. Its historical ledger reports results on three strictly complete scopes (two replacements and one original contract), with Omni-MATH terminal-complete; full-coverage original-contract scores on two (E1 and E11); and explicit lane states and remaining gates (Table C.3). Original-contract and replacement-scope numbers are never pooled, no cross-suite aggregate is computed, and no improvement over the base model is claimed for this campaign: the paired Table 8.6 comparisons provide no conventional evidence of a nonzero effect where a test or interval is reported, while E4 remains descriptive without either. The controlled Chapter 6 gains are separate, stack-specific findings. VerilogEval's 129/312 = 41.35% pass@1 at full coverage is an end-to-end harness score. Of its 183 failures, 150 are extraction failures, plausibly related to the 1,024-token cap with thinking enabled; missing per-sample finish reasons prevent a definitive causal attribution. On a 50-item subset the same adapter scores 33/50 with thinking off at 4,096 tokens against 23/50 in this run, but engine and temperature also changed. That comparison does not isolate the cap's effect or replace the full-run score. Operational source bindings remain outside this public derivative.

The report's overall conclusion is about method rather than algorithms. With these instruments, some measurements are directly inspectable while several algorithmic questions remain unidentified or underpowered; the stack is a material part of the result, so a label without its stack is not a reproducible experimental specification; and the most durable artefacts produced here are the ones that make other people's results checkable — the ZVF/PCD diagnostic layer, the eight-item minimum reportable stack (seven manifest fields and one evaluation item), the registry and its CLI, and a campaign ledger with named receipts and gates, with recovered primary receipts preserved separately from the frozen checkout and remaining raw-evidence and provenance limits stated explicitly (Appendix B §B.11).

### C1 adds numerical witnesses and exposes reference limits

The C1 extension makes the evidence boundary more precise. Its frozen score is 11/64 on a deterministic first-64 subset, with a question-level Wilson 95% working-model interval of 9.88-28.21%. Assistant-assisted reconciliation corroborates three numerical recovery witnesses on that unchanged denominator. Three further events retain convention-dependent interpretations, four are materially ambiguous and one matches an incorrect checkpoint reference after eight correct initial answers. The frozen score is preserved, not retrospectively repaired. Selection on parser zeros can enrich the reference faults and representation mismatches that prevent a direct mathematical-recovery interpretation (Appendix G, sources S1-S5).

These three witnesses show that eight observed wrong numerical answers do not force future numerical answers to remain wrong for this frozen checkpoint, task and decoder. They do not validate the reasoning in a successful response: Santana's earliest successful number was reached by an incorrect argument. They do not measure a gradient, training update, held-out capability or controller benefit. Reference auditing and unit-aware evaluation therefore belong in the measurement design before a future intervention is interpreted. The full 64-case audit retains thirteen category disagreements and provides no whole-benchmark error-prevalence estimate. Historical V2 remains separate and unpooled.

The separately frozen parser-v3 candidate passes only its narrow four-change extraction check in 2,048 prospectively selected future outputs. Preservation of 2,025 old-valid objects and agreement on four new scalar extractions do not establish task correctness or broad parser precision. The candidate remains undeployed; the C1 endpoint stays on parser v2 (Appendix G, sources S8-S9).

## 9.4 Future Work

The work leaves three kinds of open question: evaluation lanes whose measurement is incomplete, analytical claims that the present evidence could not settle, and methodological tools that have been built but not yet tested at scale.

**Closing the remaining evaluation gaps.** The six replacement lanes that were launch-pending or quota-blocked on 21 September were attempted under exploratory protocol overrides on 27 September 2026 with the reported lane outcomes (Chapter 8 §8.4). Two measurement gaps remain in them. The 108 WebArena tasks that need an LLM judge are ungraded because the judge was unavailable, and the native judge model has since been retired, so a regrade would record a judge substitution (source: `outputs/finish_pending_2026-09-27/E6/result.json`). SWE-bench Multilingual has been attempted on all 300 tasks, but under two serving contracts, so a single 300-task figure would need the first 110 tasks rerun under the 27 September contract (32,768-token context and the re-implemented source collector). Six original-contract scopes are recorded `CLOSED_EXTERNAL` with written closure records: E3 (SDAB private bundle), E7 (BinaryAudit private payload), E8-original (LifeSciBench package), E10-original (AgentHarm private tasks), E12 (AppBench deployment) and E14-original (FrontierMath hosted evaluation). A seventh original contract, E13's OpenReward held-out games, is also blocked on external access but has no closure record, because the lane continues through its BALROG replacement scope (Table C.3). A new evaluation would require the unavailable benchmark scope (source: outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md §5; withheld historical campaign chronology). A full BankerToolBench score for E4 would be a new-runtime experiment; the recovered pending-state ledger recorded a grader-only lower bound of approximately an undisclosed operational estimate before launch. **Settling the analytical questions.** Five Phase-1 directions remain open, each answering a threat identified above (source: platform_tinker/reports/esa_phase1/Phase1_Project_Report_ZVF.tex):

- adequately powered evaluation, moving from the Phase-1 probes' held-out *n* of 8–20 (the multi-seed controlled comparisons already use 200–500 items, Chapter 1 §1.5) to the full GSM8K test split or a large stratified subset at five or more seeds;
- a token-budget-optimal curriculum with staleness bounds, tested multi-seed;
- testing whether the mid-to-late band in the per-layer gradient profile supports a FLOP-saving freeze schedule, even though step-1 predictability did not survive scaling;
- hardening the Phase-1 method-trace classifier on grouped run-level and temporal splits rather than row-wise cross-validation;
- consolidating the diagnostic findings and the reporting standard for publication.

Three more follow from the Chapter 6 results. The first is a length-confounded regime, with the completion cap lifted and longer horizons, in which a Dr. GRPO length advantage would be expected to appear if it exists. **Testing the methodological tools.** Three extensions come from the Semester-4 work:

- a pre-registered controller bakeoff against static G = 16 and naive boundary heuristics, the test P7 declines to claim in advance of;
- a cross-framework divergence study that runs an identical task, reward, decoding and seed configuration across TRL, veRL, OpenRLHF and Tinker, turning P5's attribution argument from an audit of existing runs into a designed experiment;
- dogfooding the registry schema on the campaign's own runs, so that every future benchmark result ships with a verifiable provenance record.

Two commitments carry forward, because the threats above are structural rather than incidental.
