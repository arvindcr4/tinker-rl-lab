AI tools were used extensively in this work, including autonomous coding
agents that edited the repository, ran analyses and launched cloud jobs. The
uses recorded in the project repository are listed below.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Agentic coding and writing.} Anthropic Claude models through
Claude Code (including multi-agent teams and background subagents) and OpenAI
Codex wrote and revised a large share of the code, analysis scripts and documents,
including drafts and revisions of this report and the defence materials. These

claude-flow and MiniMax agents.
\item \textbf{Parallel agent sessions.} Several agent sessions worked on the
repository at the same time under a recorded ownership log: xAI Grok CLI
(defence preparation and statistical audit), Muse CLI (code consolidation and
test suites), Claude Code (audits and experiment replication) and Google
Antigravity with Gemini models (coordination and test maintenance), coordinated
with the \texttt{squad} multi-agent tool. Gemini 3.1 Pro also reviewed build
and verification code through the \texttt{agy} command-line tool.
\item \textbf{Experiment execution.} Agents prepared and launched training and
evaluation jobs on Modal, Tinker and Colab, including the replication run
reported in Section 6.5.5, and pulled their results. The resulting
measurements come from those executed runs.
\item \textbf{Literature work.} Agent pipelines (hyperresearch and similar)
searched, fetched and summarised literature, and checked references against
arXiv and Crossref. Citations in this report were checked against the cited
sources, but not every reference was read in full by the author.
\item \textbf{Frontier-model synthesis.} ChatGPT Pro Extended and Gemini Deep
Think produced proposed derivations and reasoning recorded in
\texttt{platform\_hybrid/paper/sections/frontier\_synthesis\_*.tex}, which those
documents mark as not being experiments. Where this report uses such a result,
it states its own derivation (Chapter 6 \S6.3.3).
\item \textbf{Independent verification and review.} An LLM verification pass
(Kimi, 6 July 2026) flagged two single-seed results as \textsc{suspect}; both
were re-tested and reported as nulls (Chapter 6 \S6.4.3). The pre-defence
self-review of 27 September 2026, the October evaluation review and the
16 October 2026 publication-readiness audit were carried out by AI agents.
\item \textbf{Evaluation triage.} The Jev (TypeSafe) judgment harness was used
in the E1--E14 ledger only for classification, faithfulness and value
ordering, never for arithmetic (Chapter 4 \S4.5).
\end{itemize}

No AI tool fabricated experimental data: every reported measurement comes from
an executed run or evaluation recorded in the repository, and simulated values
are labelled as simulations. AI assistance does not transfer responsibility.
The research direction, the choice of claims, their final wording and any
remaining errors are the author's responsibility.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}

This report studies group-relative reinforcement-learning post-training of
large language models through an explicit record of the training and
evaluation stack. Controlled comparisons seek to isolate the factor under
test; remaining differences and missing evidence limit attribution.
It makes four contributions.

\textbf{(1) A recomputable signal-starvation diagnostic and a loss-form
audit.} The Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) is logged per step and recomputed
from stored reward tensors, with a closed-form Bernoulli null. Reading the
runners' code distinguishes custom REINFORCE group-mean loops from clipped
surrogates, nonzero-KL configuration records and unknown managed settings.

\textbf{(2) Stack-conditioned reporting.} A minimum reporting standard and a
machine-readable stack registry with a \texttt{stackdiff} tool that flags
label-flip risk between two runs from their records alone.

\textbf{(3) Calibrated negative results.} No reliable scaling law is recovered
from reward curves across twelve fitted anchors. A same-stack PPO/GRPO
contrast (0.992 against 0.990, $p = 0.374$, five seeds) and a group-size sweep
find no detectable difference on saturated, in-distribution arithmetic draws;
neither establishes equivalence or an optimum. On an unsaturated GSM8K rerun, an initial 5.0-point GRPO advantage over an
untuned-critic PPO under a binding 200-token cap did not survive a corrected
rerun (512-token cap, warm-started critic, 1,319 test items): GRPO minus PPO
is $+0.76$ percentage points ($+0.0076$; 95\% CI $[-0.34, +1.85]$ points), with an interim TOST at $\pm 2$ points ($\pm 0.02$), not a final equivalence claim
(provisional; seven PPO seeds), and $G = 8$ versus $G = 2$ is not significant
after multiplicity adjustment. ZVF, studied concurrently as advantage collapse, is recomputable, but its
predictive validity is unestablished: the historical pooled association mixes
simulation and measured records with inconsistent outcome definitions. An adaptive group-size controller
derived from ZVF reports zero rescue under a plug-in counterfactual model;
92\% of its escalations target observed all-correct groups, a share the
reward level alone predicts, without proving latent certainty or
impossibility of recovery with fresh rollouts.

\textbf{(4) An evaluation-governance protocol.} One lightly trained actor
(Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with a LoRA adapter, 40 training steps) is evaluated on
fourteen suites absent from its recorded training-suite list under native graders
at pinned revisions. Content-level decontamination remains unverified. Receipts,
a no-pooling rule and a per-lane serving audit expose a serving defect in a
zero-scoring run; the defect's contribution to that score is unquantified.
Complete scopes include VerilogEval
($129/312 = 41.35\%$), LAB-Bench ($0.2288$) and AgentDojo benign utility
($88/97$); six replacement scopes completed on 27 September 2026
(Chapter~8, Table~8.C); seven original contracts remain externally blocked. A
paired trained-versus-base rerun on 5--151 identical items per lane finds no
significant difference in any lane (inconclusive at those sizes). No
claim of campaign-level improvement over the base model is made.


\textbf{C1 diagnostic extension.} Benchmark reference errors, ambiguous wording and equivalent units limit the interpretation of a new frozen-policy study on public GSM8K train. The first 64 of 87 eligible questions from 4,096 screened questions received 24 fresh responses after eight valid, uncapped parser-zero responses. The frozen endpoint is 11/64 (17.19\%; Wilson 95\% interval 9.88--28.21\%). Assistant-assisted reconciliation corroborates three numerical witnesses on the same denominator, while preserving convention-dependent and ambiguous cases and one wrong-reference match. This is conditional diagnostic evidence, not a population estimate, reasoning validation or training gain. A separate, undeployed parser candidate preserves 2,025 previously valid parse objects and admits four unambiguous extractions in a predefined future subset of 2,048 outputs. It does not alter C1's frozen parser-v2 score.

starvation, stack-conditioned results, benchmark evaluation, reproducibility,
evaluation governance, attribution.
