# 8. The E1–E14 Evaluation Campaign

This chapter reports the evaluation of a single Tinker-trained actor against fourteen benchmark suites outside its recorded training-suite list, recorded lane by lane under sealed reservations, immutable receipts and explicit terminal states. Item-level decontamination is not established. Lanes that did not finish and lanes that could not start are reported too, because the methodological aim is to make the boundary between what was measured, what was only reported, and what can still be verified explicit. The current repository does not preserve every cited artifact, so it cannot support an unqualified claim that every figure has a surviving, hash-pinned evidence chain. Lane-by-lane detail that does not change any figure is collected in Appendix D.

Two reporting rules govern everything below, and they are worth stating before any number appears. First, original-contract results and replacement-scope results are never pooled and never averaged across suites; a replacement scope is a different suite, a different interface and often a different grader, and combining such figures would produce a number with no referent. Second, no figure in this chapter is quoted to a precision beyond what its source receipt records, and no comparison against a baseline, a sibling suite, or the untrained base model is asserted for the campaign lanes of Sections 8.2–8.6. Those lanes have no matched base-model arm. The one paired baseline comparison in this chapter is the small-scale trained-versus-base rerun of Section 8.7 (Table 8.B): 14 lanes, 5 to 151 identical items each, one vLLM engine for both arms. It finds no significant difference in any lane, and this chapter therefore claims no improvement of any kind over the base model. Where a lane's record itself contains an unresolved discrepancy, the discrepancy is stated.

The consolidated ledger of record for the campaign is `outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md`, which supersedes the working tables of 2026-09-05 and 2026-09-12; the per-lane terminal-state ledger, `withheld historical campaign chronology`, was recovered from the original Mac checkout on 3 October 2026 with updates through 21 September, while remaining absent from the frozen repository; and selected arithmetic of the ledger was historically re-verified by deterministic code checks recorded in `outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json`, which reports eleven of eleven named checks passing with zero failures (Appendix B lists their scope; they do not cover every figure in this chapter).

**Evidence availability (private recovery audit, 3 October 2026).** Six original files covering all five previously missing basenames were recovered from the Mac checkout: the E10 native receipt, E8 diagnostics, training-identity verification, E14 terminal note and two distinct `Pending_Experiments.md` snapshots. All remain absent from the frozen repository, but their exact UTF-8 bytes and SHA-256 hashes are preserved in the withheld source archive described in Appendix B §B.11. A separately retained source audit records bounded read-only checks of underlying E8/E10/E14 artifacts on the Mac: 29 exact reference-path hash matches, 1,979 E8 archive/local member checks, 525 E10 archive-member checks and E14 stored-disposition inspection. Exported E8 grade records and E10 selected trace records permitted independent tally checks in the retained private evidence audit; those underlying rows are not included in this public document package; E14 exports aggregate traversal results and two exclusion records, not all underlying rows. This verifies stored evidence and arithmetic within that scope, without rerunning models or scorers, rejudging correctness, or reconstructing the missing training logs. It supersedes the earlier missing-primary disclosure for these files only; the frozen-checkout absence record remains valid. E14's recovered note contains a denominator inconsistency, and the recovered finish ledger does not substantiate the later E4 rerun; both are disclosed below.

## 8.1 The evaluated actor and the one-actor design

A single actor was evaluated across the whole campaign. The base model is `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` at revision `995ad96eacd98c81ed38be0c5b274b04031597b0`, and the trained parameter set is the LoRA adapter `campaign-adapter` at commit `withheld-adapter-revision`, served in bfloat16 under the identifier `campaign-actor-bf16` (source: `outputs/E1_E14_FINAL_RESULTS_2026-09-19.md`; the same identity pair is verifiable in the surviving E11 full receipt, which also preserves the training sampler path [operational identifier withheld]: source `outputs/modal_e1_e14/2026-08-16/e11_full_receipt.json`; the corroborating E10 receipt, `outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/agentdojo_native_receipt.json`, is now preserved in the withheld source archive and binds the same base/adapter identity). The public package does not contain the original execution route or operational repository access identities. It therefore cannot reproduce the original sampler execution from these materials alone; no current provider-retention or account-status claim is made here.

The one-actor design is deliberate. No lane received a separate checkpoint, per-suite prompt engineering beyond its native harness, or tuning against its own evaluation set: the recovered training-identity record lists only API-Bank-RLVR and SWE-Gym (§8.1.1), while the recovered E10 receipt explicitly reports `TRAINING_INVENTORY_ABSENT` and makes no held-out claim. The E10 contract's declaration of AgentDojo training is explicitly marked as not evidence of training consumption; neither record proves content-level decontamination. Differences between suites therefore reflect the interaction of one fixed policy with fourteen different interfaces, observation spaces, tool conventions and graders, and they are not evidence about what a specialised actor could achieve. The campaign also cannot support any statement about seed variance. Inference conditions were frozen per lane and recorded in that lane's own receipt (§8.1.2).

### 8.1.2 Serving conditions per lane

The E4 zero (§8.5) prompted identification of a serving bridge that sent no stop sequences. The defect can cause failed agent trajectories, but its contribution to the full 100-trial zero is not identified by the available comparison. The same bridge, `zvf-program/flagship/modal_tinker_openai_bridge.py`, served several other lanes, so Table 8.2 records for every lane how the actor was served and whether that defect could apply. Its `/v1/responses` route hard-codes `stop=None` and thinking on. Its `/v1/chat/completions` route sends stop sequences only if the client supplies them, and the agent clients used here (opencode, mini-swe-agent and the APEX client) do not. ## 8.2 Suites with strictly complete scopes

The historical ledger and its check log report three strictly complete scopes: E8, E10 and E11 (source: `outputs/verification/LEDGER_CODE_CHECK_2026-09-19.json`, check `strict_completed_suites_are_E8_E10_E11`). E8 and E10 are replacement scopes. E11 is an original-contract full-suite result that also meets the strict-completeness check, because the suite evaluated is VerilogEval itself; the ledger lists it both among its replacement scopes and among its original-contract results, but it is one run and one result. A fourth, E14, is recorded terminal-complete under its native protocol in the recovered terminal note, subject to its denominator inconsistency and the raw-output limits below. The six replacement scopes completed on 27 September postdate that check and are reported separately in §8.4.

**E8 --- LAB-Bench, public split.** The recovered `outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/labbench_final_diagnostics.json` records all 1,967 expected items evaluated across eight categories, with 450 correct and 1,517 incorrect responses. Those counts give 450/1967 = 0.2288 (Wilson 95% CI [0.211, 0.248], unit = question, n = 1,967). It also records 1,259 unparsed responses (`native_answer_parse_none`) and 1,086 length-truncated finishes. The supplementary validation found the native receipt at its recorded hash and checked 1,979 archive members against both declared and local hashes. Its 1,967 unique exported grade records independently corroborate the correct/incorrect totals, category counts and 1,086 length finishes. The 1,259 parse-none count remains a field of the byte-preserved diagnostic: the exported grade records contain no parsed-answer field for a separate recount. The reported score is an end-to-end harness score with substantial formatting and length limitations. The conditional descriptive sensitivity remains 450/708 = 0.636 (Wilson 95% CI [0.600, 0.670]), not the headline or a direct measure of domain knowledge; its response-level classification has not been independently regraded. Per-category figures and evidence limits are in Appendix D.

**E10 --- AgentDojo, benign utility scope.** The recovered `outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/agentdojo_native_receipt.json` records status `COMPLETE_NATIVE_BENIGN_UTILITY`, 97 completed episodes, 88 utility passes, AgentDojo v1.2.2 and decontamination status `TRAINING_INVENTORY_ABSENT`. Its score is 88/97 = 0.9072 (Wilson 95% CI [0.833, 0.950], unit = episode, n = 97). The receipt binds the benchmark version but contains no upstream commit field; the previously attributed commit `089ed468cf3ed0322acc66b0211f26d9d90dbf60` is therefore not verified by this receipt. Its scope is native default benign utility only: `security_evaluation` and `heldout_claim` are false, while `attack` and `defense` are null. Completion and utility are distinct: 97 completed episodes do not mean 97 correct episodes. The supplementary validation checks 525 archived member pins and maps all 97 selected episodes to unique exact-hash traces (94 original and 3 explicit continuation), whose stored utility fields independently tally to 88 passes and zero handled errors. It excludes extra historical partial traces from that mapping. This is stored-trace corroboration, not a rerun of AgentDojo or an independent judgement of task correctness. The earlier 94-episode attempt and reported collector repair retain their separate missing-source limitations in Appendix D.

**E11 --- VerilogEval, two native framings.** All 312 pinned prompt instances were evaluated under the upstream NVlabs/verilog-eval harness, but they represent 156 underlying designs, each appearing in code-completion (`code-complete-iccad2023`) and specification-to-RTL framing. The descriptive result remains 129 passes of 312 = 0.4135, decomposed into 67/156 = 0.4295 for code-completion and 62/156 = 0.3974 for specification-to-RTL. Because paired framings share a design, a Wilson interval treating all 312 outcomes as independent is inappropriate. Resampling the 156 design pairs together gives a 95% percentile cluster-bootstrap interval of [0.343, 0.487] (200,000 replicates, seed 20261002). This describes design-composition uncertainty under an exchangeable-design assumption for this checkpoint and these fixed sampled generations; it is not training-seed, decoding-seed or protocol uncertainty. The observed full pinned-benchmark fraction itself remains descriptive. The surviving receipt records 150 extraction failures included in the denominator, so 129/312 is an end-to-end harness score: 150 of its 183 failures are extraction failures. The generation budget is a plausible contributor to those failures, but its effect cannot be separated from format and serving conditions (corrected; see Table E.1). The run used a 1,024-token cap with the chat template's default thinking mode on, and the receipt's 312,622 response tokens give a mean of about 1,002 per sample, which is near the configured cap but does not identify individual cap hits or the point at which reasoning ended. On the 50 items of the 26 September small-scale rerun (§8.7), the receipt's verdicts give 23/50, while the same trained adapter with thinking off and a 4,096-token cap scores 33/50 with no extraction failures (11 items pass only in the second run and 1 only in the first; exact McNemar p ≈ 0.006). That comparison also changes engine and temperature (managed sampler at 0.2 against vLLM at 0) and covers 50 of 312 prompts, so it describes a joint protocol difference on that subset, not the direction or size of a budget-only effect or a corrected score. The extractor also accepted a module found inside an unterminated reasoning block, and per-sample response text was not kept, so some of the 162 extracted candidates, and possibly some passes, may be drafts from the reasoning rather than final answers. The parseable-only descriptive sensitivity is 129/162 = 0.796, hard to interpret for that reason; no interval is given because the receipt's aggregate extraction count does not provide the per-design extraction indicators needed for a clustered conditional interval (sources: `outputs/modal_e1_e14/2026-08-16/e11_full_receipt.json`; `outputs/e1_e14_small_scale_2026-09-26/E11/paired.json`; bootstrap script and paired-outcome artifact `platform_hybrid/experiments/scripts/e11_design_cluster_bootstrap.py` and `.json`). One prompt, `verilog_eval/spec-to-rtl/Prob099_m2014_q6c`, fails against its own test bench under both simulators; excluding it yields 129/311 = 0.4148, but no immutable justification for the exclusion was captured, so the raw 129/312 is canonical and 129/311 is a noncanonical sensitivity only (sources: `outputs/e11_verilog_eval/e11_verilog_eval_rerun_receipt.json`, `outputs/e11_verilog_eval/lane_status_2026-08-09.md`). Pair counts, the bootstrap method, harness revisions, verdict rule and amendment are in Appendix D.

**E14 --- Omni-MATH replacement scope, receipt-recorded terminal-complete.** The surviving ledger and historical arithmetic check record 4,426 accepted rows of 4,428 and official accuracy 0.5131043831902395, consistent with 2,271/4,426. Retaining 2,271 passes but counting both unjudged rows as failures gives 2,271/4,428 = 51.29 per cent (Wilson 95% CI [0.498, 0.528]), 0.02 percentage points below the judged-row rate. The recovered `outputs/PES_Phase2_Review_2026-09-12/finish/e14_terminal_note_2026-09-19.json` records truncated judge outputs before an Equivalence Judgement section, two recorded skip reasons, no re-judge and terminal completion at 4,428 dispositions. The note nevertheless labels `2271/4428` as `0.5131043831902395`, a denominator inconsistency: that decimal equals 2,271/4,426, not 2,271/4,428 (corrected; see Table E.1). The original note is preserved unchanged, and this thesis retains both clearly labelled arithmetic conventions. The supplementary Mac-side traversal found the stored disposition, judged-row and new-judge-response files with 4,428 valid JSON rows apiece. It reports 4,426 accepted dispositions, 2,271 correct and two skipped, and exports both exclusion records with missing Equivalence Judgement headings. It also records the native score's explicit 4,426 denominator and the separate all-disposition diagnostic. The full E14 rows are not exported here, so the cloud check verifies the exported arithmetic and exclusions rather than independently recounting all 4,428 rows or rejudging any mathematics. The headline is strongly shaped by the 2,048-token actor cap (corrected reading; see Table E.1). Recounting the stored dispositions, the 2,982 responses that hit the cap are correct in 1,029 cases (34.5%), against 1,242 of the 1,446 uncapped responses (85.9%). The split is confounded by difficulty, since harder problems both run longer and fail more, so it does not identify what a larger budget would score; it does mean 51.29% should not be read as the actor's Omni-MATH capability independent of its generation budget (source: `outputs/public_portfolio_2026-09-05/native_finish_v5/e14_official_collector/official_result01/native_dispositions.jsonl`).

The 2/731 therefore mostly measures whether the actor could write arithmetically consistent diff headers, not whether it could solve the issues, and it is not a measure of problem-solving ability (source: parse of the stored `candidates.json` beside the receipt; the runner `zvf-program/flagship/modal_e1_swe_bench_pro_full.py` now repairs hunk headers before submission and records apply status, but the stored score was produced without that repair).

## 8.7 Small-scale reruns of all fourteen lanes

This section adds a separate, smaller set of runs, made on 26 September 2026, to give every one of the fourteen lanes a measured number under one fixed protocol, including the lanes that never produced a score. None of these figures is pooled with, averaged against, or substituted for a result reported earlier in this chapter.

The protocol fixes one actor family, a fixed item-selection seed (20260926), temperature 0 and a non-thinking chat template, and scores each lane with its native grader where one could be run. Lanes whose original benchmark is externally blocked were run on a public substitute, and each substitute's gap from the original suite is recorded in its lane file. Items that errored or timed out are counted as failures in the denominator (source: `outputs/e1_e14_small_scale_2026-09-26/PROTOCOL.md`).

Two arms were run. The first is the base model, `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` with no adapter, sampled on Tinker (Table 8.A). These are small samples. Most lanes score between five and eighty items, so a single item moves a lane's rate by several percentage points and the confidence intervals are wide. In Table 8.B, b and c count items solved only by the trained actor and only by the base model respectively, and the CI is a paired bootstrap interval on the difference. Four lane-specific caveats matter. For E12 the 151 rubric items come from only six generated applications, so its McNemar p-value overstates the evidence; the exact sign-flip test over the six per-app differences gives p = 0.25, and the grader is an LLM judge that is the base model itself, so the E12 rates are judge-dependent. For E4, only `btb-19b3361c` scores above zero in either arm (trained 0.397, base 0.557; 0.409 in Table 8.A), so no interval is valid. The paired differences are not combined across lanes, and none of the seventeen paired tests excludes zero. At these sizes that is an inconclusive result, not evidence of no effect: with 5 to 10 items, an exact McNemar test needs at least six discordant items, all in one direction, to reach p < 0.05. ## 8.8 Summary of the campaign at the point of writing

By the 19 September ledger the campaign had established two full-suite results and one complete replacement scope with a score: E1 at 2/731 = 0.274 per cent on SWE-bench Pro, E11 at 129/312 = 41.35 per cent pass@1 (design-cluster bootstrap 95% interval [34.3, 48.7], n = 156 designs) across its two framings, and E8 at 450/1967 = 0.2288 ([0.211, 0.248]) on the LAB-Bench public split, alongside a complete benign-utility evaluation of E10 at 88 of 97 episodes (0.907, [0.833, 0.950]), a terminal-complete E14 replacement scope at 2,271/4,428 = 51.29 per cent accuracy, and five original-contract partials (E2, E4, E5, E7, E9), none of which is a suite score. Three of those scores have substantial formatting, extraction or truncation limitations: 593 of E1's 713 submitted patches were corrupt for `git apply`, E11's 150 extraction failures occurred under a 1,024-token cap with thinking on, with causal attribution unresolved, and E14's capped responses are correct at 34.5% against 85.9% uncapped (§8.2, §8.3). On 27 September six further replacement scopes were completed under a self-issued override whose deviations are recorded per lane (Tables 8.C and 8.D). Seven original contracts remain externally blocked, and no lane now waits on a launch or a cloud quota. The campaign did not establish a multi-seed estimate, a held-out security result, a BankerToolBench suite score, a graded result for the 108 WebArena tasks that need an LLM judge, or a baseline comparison for the campaign lanes themselves; the only trained-versus-base comparison, Table 8.B, is inconclusive in every lane. The E8 diagnostic, E10 native receipt, training-identity verification, E14 terminal note and both pending-state snapshots are recovered in the private package while remaining absent from the frozen repository.
