# Appendix A. Historical Run Registry and Coverage Reconciliation

This appendix records the historical run inventories used by the report and
the later reconciliation of those inventories. It is not an exhaustive
current account history. Provider logging identities, independent scientific
executions, checkpoint revisions, and claim-to-run links are different units
and must not be added as though they were interchangeable. Run identifier,
library and platform, base model, algorithm, group size, dataset, seed, steps,
and terminal status are retained where a primary source records them; unknown
fields remain unknown. A claim without supporting primary evidence must be
qualified or withdrawn rather than inferred from an inventory count.

Historical inventories differ in scope and counting units. Provider-ledger entries, opening-study records, claim links and independent scientific trials are not interchangeable. This public derivative omits all account-wide totals, project breakdowns and exact provider identities. Only scientific study configurations, denominators, outcomes and limitations are retained; it makes no complete-coverage claim.

## A.5 Completeness limitations

The historical registry is an index of its retained snapshot and is incomplete as a scientific dataset. Many records lack model, dataset, algorithm, group size, steps or a usable outcome. Missing fields remain unknown; names and provider state are not used to infer them. The public version does not include the account-wide field-coverage table or private per-identity export.

**Table A.9 — Scientific metadata limitations.**

| Required scientific field | Interpretation of missing data |
|---|---|
| Model and dataset | Do not infer identity from a display name or tokenizer family |
| Algorithm and group size | An unrecorded configuration remains unknown |
| Training steps | Provider state does not establish completed optimization |
| Outcome | A logged record without a metric is not a scored scientific result |

: Metadata limitations in the historical retained registry; private account-level field counts are omitted.

Four further limits apply.

1. **Aggregate presentation.** This appendix names 105 runs individually: the 4
   cross-framework runs of Table A.2, the 20 Modal campaign runs of A.3, the 12
   sweep runs (in 4 rows) of A.4, the 46 opening runs (in 16 rows) of A.6, and
   the 23 E-lane rows of A.8 — plus the 18 claim rows of Table A.5, which index
   claims rather than runs.
   Other historical inventory entries are not published in this derivative.

2. **Non-runs inside the count.** Three entries are not training results:
   `verl-dryrun` and `openrlhf-dryrun` are seeded deterministic fallbacks, and
   the E4 base-model rerun performs zero training steps; to these add
   §A.3.1's P1 gradient probe, which takes no optimizer steps at all. They are
   counted because they are runs in the registry's sense, and flagged so no
   reader treats them as measurements.

3. **Identity conflicts.** Historical source-identity disagreements remain a limitation; private provider reconciliation and its counts are withheld.

4. **Snapshot scope.** Historical source inventories differ in scope. They are neither current live accounts nor counts of independent experiments, and this public package does not provide a complete inventory replay.

None of this changes a reported result, because every result in Chapters 6–8
is bound to named artefacts rather than to the registry as a whole. It does
bound what the registry can be used for: it establishes provenance and
identity, shows that the run population is far larger than the reported
claims, and shows that those claims rest on a small, explicitly tiered subset
with the P1–P8 empirical rows at Tier C or X and P11 at Tier C under the
stated per-run step threshold. The four Tier X P1–P8 rows must not be cited
as supporting evidence.
