\begin{abstract}
% =============================================================================
Reproducibility failures in deep RL are long-standing
\cite{henderson2018deep,hu2024openrlhf}: small stack choices can dominate
reported gains. The \grpo{}-family literature is especially exposed, because
each variant typically ships in its own trainer, sampler, and evaluation
harness. This paper contributes \audit{}, a single-stack internally frozen audit
protocol in which DAPO, GSPO, Dr.\grpo{}, and AERO are re-implemented as
declared overrides on one shared language-model trainer, with seed-paired
inference, exact noncentral paired-$t$ power, Benjamini--Hochberg correction
across four comparisons (both applied through a disclosed post-execution
correction of the aggregator), and a fail-closed artifact gate (local, W\&B,
private-Hub checkpoint, stack fingerprint, treatment fingerprint, 500-row
held-out). All 40 arm--seed units were historically reported to pass an
earlier gate after documented recovery and replay steps, but the current
strengthened contract validates zero units, because the stored manifests lack
run-configuration fields it now requires; no current verdict or numerical
effect is certified. No metric-compatible published delta was registered, so
survival fractions are not computed; the executable estimands are controlled
arm--GRPO deltas and equivalence/difference verdicts. A recomputation from the
checked-in seed records (all four comparisons \verdict{inconclusive}) is
reported below as historical, uncertified evidence, not a current empirical
claim.
A small open-source
telemetry pilot (effective $n{=}1$ per arm) is reported only as infrastructure
evidence. The current contribution is the protocol and its fail-closed
evidence boundary, not a certified cost observation or algorithm ranking.
\end{abstract}
\begin{sloppypar}\noindent\textbf{Current admission boundary.} The current local
\path{results/audit.json} reports \texttt{PREREGISTERED-NOT-RUN}, zero

\section{Introduction: Why a Single-Stack Audit}
% =============================================================================

A typical GRPO-family paper compares a new variant against ``GRPO''
\cite{shao2024deepseekmath} by running each in a different codebase. The new
variant may change the clip rule, the advantage normalization, or the
group-size schedule; the baseline may use a different token mask, KL
placement, sampler precision, or reward parser
\cite{minreportrl2026, zvfaudit2026}. The result is a comparison of two stacks
$S_A$ and $S_B$ that happen to be tagged ``A'' and ``B''. The algorithmic delta
and the stack delta are inseparable. Code-level choices are known to rival
algorithmic ones in policy-gradient RL \cite{engstrom2020implementation}, and
few-seed comparisons need interval estimates rather than point rankings
\cite{agarwal2021deep}; this audit applies both lessons to the GRPO family.

Throughout, the zero-variance fraction (\zvf{}) of a training step is the
fraction of its prompt groups whose $G$ binary rewards are all equal (so the
group-relative advantage is identically zero), and gradient utilization is
$\gu{}=1-\zvf{}$.

The \minreport{} position paper \cite{minreportrl2026} proposes an eight-item
standard so that future papers expose the stack: seven run-manifest fields
(loss form; reference-policy/KL handling; sampler, backend and precision,
including base-checkpoint identity; the per-step \zvf{}/\gu{} trajectory; the
group-size schedule; a disjoint held-out split; and decontamination plus a
parser-robustness probe) and one evaluation item (held-out pass@$k$ alongside
pass@1). The \grpoReg{} catalog \cite{grpoRegistry2026} records each variant's
self-declared delta relative to a baseline. This paper closes the loop: it
specifies a controlled, single-stack re-implementation protocol that asks,
for each variant, \emph{what controlled delta remains when the stack is held
fixed?} When a metric-compatible published effect is available, the protocol
can optionally report a survival fraction relative to that effect. The
executed case study below has \texttt{published\_delta=null} for every arm, so
it reports controlled deltas, exact-power equivalence/difference verdicts, and
secondary cost telemetry---not a fraction of a claimed gain.

\paragraph{What survival is not.} Failure to reproduce a gain does not show
that a variant is ``fake,'' nor does it identify the original stack as the
cause. It says only that this audit did not recover the gain under its declared
model, task, horizon, and implementation. Conversely, a positive result would
establish an effect under this stack, not universal robustness.

\paragraph{A live specimen.} While assembling this audit's own pilot, our
trainer shipped exactly the failure the protocol targets: a
\texttt{-{}-loss drgrpo} flag whose commit message claimed it toggled the
advantage normalization, but which was never wired into the loss --- so a
six-arm panel (GRPO vs.\ Dr.GRPO, three seeds each, Qwen3-8B/GSM8K, run on a
separate managed-training stack, not the E1 trainer below) silently compared
GRPO with GRPO, and its committed ``finding'' stood until the runner was read
line by line. No output-level check caught it: rewards, lengths, and \zvf{}
traces all looked plausible. The panel was invalidated, the artifacts
preserved under explicit \texttt{.invalid\_actually\_grpo.json} names, and the
panel was rerun with the flag wired (reported in the companion theory paper).
A shared trainer removes infrastructure differences by construction, but not
wiring defects: stack identity cannot be certified from results; it must be
certified from the stack (\S\ref{sec:stackdiff}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item A concrete single-stack audit protocol with shared-stack constraints,
    arm definitions, and pre-registration rules (\S\ref{sec:design}).
  \item A controlled-verdict logic and statistical protocol that treat seed as
    the independent unit (\S\ref{sec:verdict}--\ref{sec:stats}).
  \item Integration with \texttt{grpo-stackdiff} to verify stack identity
    across arms before any result is trusted (\S\ref{sec:stackdiff}).
  \item A small open-source pilot (\S\ref{sec:pilot}) that exercises the
    protocol on four arms and shows that the required telemetry is obtainable
    from a standard GRPO trainer.
  \item A 40-unit execution record and its admission history: an earlier gate
    accepted all 40 arm--seed units (after one evaluation resumption, two
    post-acceptance finalizer/manifest corrections, and six evaluation
    replays, all disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:execution}); the current
    strengthened contract admits none. A post-execution finite-sample
    correction of the aggregator changed the one earlier disappearance
    verdict to \verdict{inconclusive}; a recomputation from the seed records
    finds all four comparisons inconclusive, as uncertified historical
    evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Audit Design}
\label{sec:design}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Shared stack.} Every arm uses the same:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item base checkpoint and tokenizer revision,
  \item LoRA target modules, rank, alpha, and dropout (if LoRA is used),
  \item optimizer, learning-rate schedule, and gradient clipping,
  \item rollout engine (vLLM), decoding parameters, sampler/trainer precision,
  \item reference-policy and KL handling (placement, coefficient, schedule,
    estimator),
  \item token mask and advantage normalization (unless the variant explicitly
    changes one of these),
  \item reward parser and held-out evaluation harness,
  \item prompt distribution and train/test split.
\end{itemize}
The only permitted differences are the variant's declared algorithmic deltas.

\paragraph{Arms.} The primary arms are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{GRPO (baseline):} vanilla per-token GRPO with the shared stack.
  \item \textbf{DAPO:} asymmetric clip-higher + dynamic sampling
    \cite{yu2025dapo}.
  \item \textbf{GSPO:} sequence-level importance-sampling ratio
    \cite{qwen2025gspo}.
  \item \textbf{Dr.\grpo{}:} removal of within-group standard-deviation
    normalization \cite{tong2025drgrpo}. The executed arm sets only this
    lever (\texttt{scale\_rewards=none}) and keeps the shared loss aggregation
    (\texttt{loss\_type=grpo}), so Dr.\grpo{}'s second change, removing
    length-dependent normalization, is \emph{not} part of this arm.
  \item \textbf{AERO:} adaptive rollout allocation, selective rejection, and
    posterior-guided avoidance of zero-advantage groups \cite{zhang2026aero}.
\end{itemize}
Optional secondary arms (CPPO \cite{lin2025cppo}, NGRPO
\cite{nan2025ngrpo}, Scaf-\grpo{} \cite{zhang2025scaffgrpo}) are added if compute
permits; each would be implemented as a declared hook on the same shared trainer.
M-\grpo{} \cite{hong2025mgrpo} is audited separately on an agentic task with a
hierarchical trajectory schema; pooling it with arithmetic arms would violate
the stack-lock requirement. Its separate frozen contract is
\path{zvf-program/audit/preregistration_mgrpo.json}.

\begin{sloppypar}
\paragraph{Frozen protocol.} The full audit is locked to Qwen3-8B, GSM8K,
30 training steps, batch size 4, 1,024-token completions, a fixed held-out set
of 500 prompts, and eight paired seeds
$\{11,23,37,53,71,89,107,131\}$. The checkpoint is the fixed final step, not
the best training-reward checkpoint. The primary estimand is the seed-paired
held-out delta relative to GRPO; training reward, \zvf/\gu{}, realized rollouts,
and wall-clock time are secondary. The machine-readable record is
\texttt{audit/preregistration.json} (frozen 2026-07-14; see ``Protocol timing''
in Section~\ref{sec:limits} for why we call it internally frozen rather than
preregistered). Its rule is that any change after a scored run requires a new
version and is not pooled with this study; the one post-execution change, to
the aggregator's statistics (Section~\ref{sec:stats}), is instead disclosed
with both the original and corrected verdicts. Freezing the protocol prevents
the selection-by-reward bias that the audit is designed to diagnose
\cite{zvfaudit2026}.
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Locked shared-stack values.} The checked-in runner
(\path{zvf-program/colab-experiments/e1_grpo_confirmatory.py}; TRL
\texttt{GRPOConfig}) sets: $G{=}4$ completions per prompt and 4 prompts per
step (16 completions per optimizer step for fixed-$G$ arms); LoRA $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}32$, dropout 0, all linear modules; AdamW (fused), learning rate
$10^{-6}$, linear schedule, no warmup, weight decay 0, gradient-norm clip 1.0,
bf16; no KL term ($\beta{=}0$); one policy iteration per batch; clip
$\epsilon{=}0.2$ (DAPO: $\epsilon_{\text{high}}{=}0.28$); sampling temperature
1.0, top-$p$ 1.0, no top-$k$; Qwen3 chat template with thinking enabled. The
per-arm treatment overrides and runtime parameters are recorded in each unit
manifest (\path{zvf-program/audit/results/full/manifests/}); the shared values
above are bound by the recorded stack fingerprint rather than restated in the
manifest. The 30-step, 120-prompt-group horizon is a short-horizon protocol
audit chosen for compute, not a convergence regime: with about 69\% of GRPO
groups flat, fixed-$G$ arms receive roughly 37 informative groups per run, and
a 0.01 margin on 500 held-out items is five problems.

\paragraph{Hook interface (schematic).} Conceptually, a variant is a small
hook object with the following interface:
\begin{verbatim}
class VariantHook:
    def on_group_rewards(self, rewards: Tensor) -> Tensor:
        # optionally transform per-prompt group rewards
        return rewards
    def on_advantage(self, advantage: Tensor) -> Tensor:
        # optionally transform advantages
        return advantage
    def on_loss(self, loss_terms: Tensor, mask: Tensor) -> Tensor:
        # optionally modify loss terms
        return loss_terms
    def group_size_rule(self, step: int, zvf: float) -> int:
        # return G for this step; default is fixed G
        return self.G
\end{verbatim}
This interface is a simplified schematic, not the executed code: it cannot
express DAPO's intra-step reject-and-refill loop, ratio-level clipping, GSPO's
sequence-level ratio, or AERO's probe-and-rescue rollouts. In the executed E1
runner the levers are implemented as (i)~TRL \texttt{GRPOConfig} overrides
(\texttt{epsilon\_high}, \texttt{importance\_sampling\_level=sequence},
\texttt{scale\_rewards=none}), and (ii)~two trainer subclasses: a DAPO trainer
that filters zero-variance groups and refills (at most 10 generation batches
per step), and an AERO trainer with a custom rollout function (3 exploration
rollouts per prompt plus a global rescue budget of 4 per step, Beta(1,1)
posterior state, and retention of one zero-advantage rollout). All arms run the
same trainer code under one stack fingerprint, which removes infrastructure
differences by construction but does not by itself exclude wiring defects;
that is what the fingerprints and \texttt{grpo-stackdiff} check
(\S\ref{sec:stackdiff}) are for.

\section{Controlled Verdicts}
\label{sec:verdict}
% =============================================================================

For each variant we report two numbers when they are metric-compatible:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Published $\Delta$:} the gain claimed in the original paper,
    used quantitatively only when model, task, split, and metric match the
    controlled audit. Otherwise it is contextual metadata.
  \item \textbf{Controlled $\Delta$:} the gain in the shared stack vs.\ the
    shared GRPO baseline, with a 95\% confidence interval across seeds.
\end{itemize}
The audit verdict is then:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.74\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Rule}\\
\midrule
\verdict{Reverses} & Controlled 95\% CI is entirely negative \emph{and} the
  BH-adjusted two-sided paired-$t$ test rejects.\\
\verdict{Disappears} & The 90\% CI lies inside the frozen equivalence margin
  $[-m,+m]$ and the exact 80\%-power MDE does not exceed $m$.\\
\verdict{Retains} & Controlled 95\% CI is entirely positive, the BH-adjusted
  paired-$t$ test rejects, and the CI lower bound is at least $50\%$ of a
  metric-compatible published $\Delta$.\\
\verdict{Survives} & As \verdict{retains}, but the published effect is not
  metric-compatible or the half-gain rule fails.\\
\verdict{Inconclusive} & Every other outcome, including inadequate power.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Rules are evaluated in the listed order and the first match is the verdict, so
the five categories are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (this is the order
implemented in \path{aggregate_audit.py}). The $50\%$ retention threshold is a
frozen convention, not a comparison between incommensurate benchmark deltas.
The equivalence margin is $m=0.01$ in absolute held-out success. Sensitivity
analyses at $m=0.005$ and $m=0.02$ are secondary.

\begin{sloppypar}
\paragraph{Executable estimand in this case study.} The frozen
preregistration stores \texttt{published\_delta=null} for every arm. Therefore
the \verdict{retains} rule and any fraction-of-published-gain (``survival'')
estimand are not evaluated here and \verdict{retains} is structurally
unreachable. The executable outputs are the controlled arm--GRPO deltas and
the \verdict{survives}, \verdict{disappears}, \verdict{reverses}, or
\verdict{inconclusive} rules defined above. Historical notes that quote DAPO as
\verdict{disappears} at $m=0.01$ are superseded by the exact-power correction
(\S\ref{sec:stats}); that label does not appear as a primary result in this
manuscript.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:stats}
% =============================================================================

The independent unit is a seed, not a training step. Training rewards are
autocorrelated within a trace, so inferential claims are made only across the
seed sweep. With paired differences $d_i$ (arm minus GRPO held-out score on
seed $i$, $i=1,\dots,S$), primary deltas use paired percentile-bootstrap 90\%
and 95\% confidence intervals over seeds ($B=10{,}000$ resamples of the $S$
differences, RNG seed 20260714, linearly interpolated percentiles).
Sign-based \verdict{survives}, \verdict{retains}, and \verdict{reverses}
decisions additionally require rejection by a two-sided paired-$t$ test
($\nu=S-1$) after Benjamini--Hochberg correction across the four arm--GRPO
comparisons at FDR 0.05. Single-seed arms are descriptive only. At $S=8$ a
percentile bootstrap is narrower than the exact paired-$t_7$ interval (its
spread scales with $\sqrt{(S-1)/S}$ and it cannot exceed the observed
extremes), so Section~\ref{sec:execution} also reports paired-$t$ intervals;
no verdict below depends on the choice.

The chosen seed count is $S=8$ per core arm. The achieved minimum detectable
effect is
\[
\mathrm{MDE}_{80}=\lambda_{\nu}\,\frac{s_d}{\sqrt S},\qquad
s_d=\Big(\tfrac{1}{S-1}\textstyle\sum_i(d_i-\bar d)^2\Big)^{1/2},
\]
where $\lambda_\nu$ is the noncentrality at which a two-sided level-$0.05$
paired-$t$ test with $\nu=S-1$ degrees of freedom has power 0.80
($\lambda_7\approx3.27$, versus the large-sample $z_{0.975}+z_{0.80}\approx2.80$).
It is an achieved (post hoc) quantity computed from the observed $s_d$, not a
design-stage power calculation. If the MDE exceeds the equivalence margin $m$,
the audit cannot claim disappearance and reports \verdict{inconclusive}.

\paragraph{Post-execution statistical correction.} The first aggregator used
the large-sample normal critical value for MDE and contained, but did not call,
the multiplicity routine. Those choices were unsafe at eight seeds. The
corrected aggregator evaluates exact noncentral-$t$ power, emits all four raw
$p$-values and Benjamini--Hochberg decisions, and requires the adjusted decision
for every sign-based verdict. The correction changed only the analysis code:
the run records, held-out scores, bootstrap intervals, and equivalence margin
are those of the first aggregation. This correction is disclosed
because it changes DAPO from \texttt{DISAPPEARS} to \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE}.
(Under the normal multiplier DAPO's MDE was $\approx0.0087\le0.01$; under the
exact multiplier it is \HistDAPOMDE{}.)

\section{Using \texttt{grpo-stackdiff} to Verify Stack Identity}
\label{sec:stackdiff}
% =============================================================================

Before interpreting any controlled comparison, we verify that the arms are
stack-matched. The implemented registry CLI command
\path{platform_hybrid/registry/query.py}~\texttt{stackdiff} \cite{minreportrl2026}
takes two \minreport{} registry records and reports
whether any unapproved lever differs. The audit proceeds only if every pairwise
comparison returns \texttt{STACK\_MATCHED} (or \texttt{STACK\_MATERIAL} for
levers the protocol explicitly tolerates).

\begin{sloppypar}
If a hook inadvertently changes a non-target lever---for example, a dynamic
sampling rule that also changes the effective tokenizer chat template because
it filters on string prefixes---\texttt{grpo-stackdiff} flags it as a
DISTRIBUTIONAL or SEMANTIC\_OBJECTIVE diff. The hook must then be rewritten so
that only the declared delta remains. This check is the difference between a
``single-trainer'' audit and a genuinely ``single-stack'' audit.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Historical Execution Record and Current Admission Status}
\label{sec:execution}
% =============================================================================

The locked output schema contains one row per arm and seed plus two generated
tables: (i) controlled held-out delta, paired CIs, achieved MDE, and verdict;
and (ii) last-10 reward, mean \zvf/\gu{}, collapse rate, realized rollout count,
and wall-clock time. The aggregator refuses to emit a verdict unless all eight
core seeds are admitted. On 2026-07-20 an earlier version of the admission gate
accepted all eight records for each of GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, Dr.\grpo{}, and AERO,
each with 500 held-out examples, after the recovery and replay steps listed at
the end of this section. The current strengthened contract admits none of the
40 records (front matter), so everything below is a \emph{historical,
uncertified} recomputation.

\paragraph{Provenance of the numbers below.} All values are recomputed from the
checked-in seed records \path{zvf-program/audit/results/full/<arm>-seed-<s>.json}
by \path{tools/review_ledger/P11_reproducibility_audit_recompute.py}, which
calls the corrected aggregator's own bootstrap, exact-MDE, paired-$t$, and
Benjamini--Hochberg functions (output:
\path{platform_hybrid/experiments/results/review_2026-10-18/P11_reproducibility_audit/e1_recompute.json}).
All 40 held-out scores match the finished W\&B runs named in the records
(read-only export; 0 mismatches). The GRPO held-out exact matches for seeds 11,
23, 37, 53, 71, 89, 107, and 131 are 0.650, 0.636, 0.634, 0.634, 0.634, 0.620,
0.634, and 0.622 (mean 0.633); DAPO achieves 0.648, 0.624, 0.628, 0.636,
0.634, 0.636, 0.634, and 0.632; GSPO 0.640, 0.638, 0.642, 0.632, 0.638, 0.646,
0.640, and 0.628; Dr.\grpo{} 0.642, 0.626, 0.622, 0.636, 0.620, 0.628, 0.628,
and 0.646; and AERO 0.630, 0.628, 0.636, 0.632, 0.632, 0.630, 0.630, and 0.640.

\section{Open-Source Pilot}
\label{sec:pilot}
% =============================================================================

We ran a small pilot on a single T4 GPU to check that the required telemetry
can be extracted from a standard GRPO trainer. The pilot is \emph{not} the
full-scale audit. It ran seeds $\{0,1\}$, but the checked-in artifact
(\path{zvf-program/colab-experiments/results/e3_open_audit.json}) stores only
the two-seed mean per arm, as computed by
\path{zvf-program/colab-experiments/e3_open_audit.py}; the per-seed records
were not saved. For inference each arm is therefore effective $n=1$ (no
between-seed variance is available): the numbers below are a telemetry smoke
test, not replicated evidence.

\paragraph{Setup.} Model: Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with full-model updates;
ten training steps; three prompts per batch; initial group size
$G_0=4$; maximum generation length 40 tokens; and a seed-specific held-out set
of 20 generated integer-addition prompts per seed (\texttt{--heldout-n 20}).
The stored held-out value for each arm is the mean over the two seeds of the
within-arm change in greedy held-out accuracy (post-training minus
pre-training, each on 20 prompts), which explains its 0.025 granularity.
Rollout and \zvf{} entries are likewise two-seed means. Arms are GRPO,
Dr.\grpo{}, DAPO, and
an adaptive-$G$ GRPO baseline. The shared implementation keeps tokenizer,
sampler, reward parser, prompt generator, optimizer settings, and evaluation
procedure fixed across arms. These small synthetic held-out sets make the
pilot a telemetry smoke test, not a language-model capability benchmark.

\paragraph{Pilot results.} Table~\ref{tab:pilot} shows the stored within-arm
held-out change (post minus pre accuracy on the synthetic set---a change
relative to the arm's own starting point, not a delta versus GRPO), \zvf{},

zero \zvf{} but spends more rollouts (174 vs.\ 120), while adaptive-$G$ matches

\section{Limitations and Extensions}
\label{sec:limits}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Scope.} The audit tests one shared stack, model, task, and short
training horizon. A positive verdict would not imply transfer to a different
stack, and a negative verdict would not isolate the cause of the original
reported gain. The present all-inconclusive outcome supports neither claim.
The setting is also far from the regimes in which DAPO, GSPO, and Dr.\grpo{}
reported their gains (competition mathematics such as AIME and MATH-500,
long chain-of-thought, hundreds of steps): here every arm ends near 0.63 on the
500-item GSM8K held-out set and about 69\% of GRPO training groups are flat, so variants that act on
clipping, normalization, or flat groups have little room and few informative
groups to act on in 30 steps.

\paragraph{Replay stability.} Six legacy units (GRPO seeds 11, 89, and 107;
DAPO seed 131; GSPO seeds 11 and 71) were replayed to add completion hashes.
Per the preservation note
(\path{zvf-program/audit/results/full/legacy-unhashed-2026-07-19/README.md}),
replay re-ran evaluation only, from the exact checkpoint-30 tree. Because two
rows changed outcome, the replay evidently regenerated completions rather than
re-hashing stored text. Five units reproduced their held-out score exactly;
GRPO seed 11 moved from 0.646 to 0.650 because two of 500 rows changed from an
unparsed completion to a correct completion, which indicates that held-out
generation is not bit-reproducible on this stack. Using the pre-replay 0.646
instead shifts every arm's paired delta by $+0.0005$ (DAPO
\HistDAPOPreReplayDelta{}) and changes DAPO's exact MDE to
\HistDAPOPreReplayMDE{}, still above the 0.01 margin, so no verdict changes.
The 0.004 replay shift is larger than the 0.001 DAPO point difference: effects
smaller than the observed replay shift are not robust to evaluator replay.

\paragraph{Protocol timing.} The checked-in amendment records only the date
2026-07-15, the same date on which the first confirmatory unit completed; no
independent timestamped receipt establishes which came first. We therefore
describe the study as following an internally frozen protocol, not as an
externally time-stamped preregistration or registered report.

\paragraph{Faithfulness of re-implementation.} A variant may depend on an
unreported implementation detail. If a careful re-implementation cannot
reproduce the original result from the paper, that is itself a finding about
reporting quality, but it is not a finding about the method's intrinsic value.
We report both readings and publish every arm's full \minreport{} block.

\paragraph{Extensions.} The protocol generalizes to any family of methods that
are small deltas on a base loop. The same hook interface can accommodate
future variants; the same \texttt{grpo-stackdiff} check can verify that each
new hook changes only its declared levers.

\section{Conclusion}
% =============================================================================

The \grpo{} family needs a way to separate algorithmic gains from stack
effects. The \minreport{} standard tells authors what to report; the
\grpoReg{} catalog records each variant's declared delta; and the \audit{}
protocol reported here specifies how to hold the stack fixed so that those
deltas can be tested fairly. The 40-unit execution record exists and its
held-out scores match the finished W\&B runs, but the current strengthened
admission contract admits none of its units, so no verdict is certified. The
historical statistical answer is also indecisive: at eight paired seeds, none
of DAPO, GSPO, Dr.\grpo{}, or AERO is distinguishable from or equivalent to
the shared GRPO baseline at the primary margin. That outcome is itself the
point of a fail-closed audit: complete-looking artifacts do not manufacture
admitted or statistical evidence.
The protocol, telemetry pilot, machine-readable frozen protocol, execution
record, and \texttt{grpo-stackdiff} verification step make it operational to
ask of a GRPO-family result not ``did it win?'' but ``what controlled delta,
at what cost, did the evidence actually resolve?''
