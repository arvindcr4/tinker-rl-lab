\begin{frame}{The program at a glance}
  \textbf{Core object:} the Zero-Variance Fraction (ZVF) --- the fraction of
  GRPO prompt-groups whose identical rewards contribute \emph{zero gradient}.
  \vspace{4pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{P-Method} — adaptive ZVF-aware rollout controller
          \tier{pilot: GO}
    \item \textbf{P-Theory} — calibrated estimator (T1), wasted-compute floor
          (T2), optimal group size $G^\star$ (T3) \tier{all 3 validated on real data}
    \item \textbf{P-OSS} — \texttt{zvf-triage} library, 73 tests green
          \tier{shippable}
    \item \textbf{P-Position} — MIN-REPORT-RL: now an \textbf{8-item}
          minimum-reportable-stack standard \tier{draft compiles}
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{4pt}
  The field converged on our phenomenon: the TMLR agentic-RL survey
  (arXiv:2509.02547) canonizes it as \emph{advantage collapse}; DAPO, GRESO,
  EDGE-GRPO, DARS all intervene on it --- \textbf{none measures it}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Position: controllers acting on an uncalibrated signal}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item 20+ GRPO variants now catalogued in one survey table; a striking
          fraction filter, reweight, or reallocate on the dead-group fraction
    \item Every one acts on an \emph{implicit point estimate} of the quantity
          whose sampling distribution (T1), compute cost (T2), and optimal
          set-point (T3) this program supplies
    \item Our theory is not a competitor to DAPO/GRESO/DARS --- it is the
          \textbf{missing measurement layer} any of them needs to become a
          calibrated controller
    \item Sharper implication (survey \S6.4): zero-variance groups are the
          all-fail/all-pass cells --- controlling ZVF concentrates gradient
          budget exactly where RL installs \emph{new} capability
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Stage-1: pre-registered validation protocol (now complete)}
  \textbf{Data:} one sampling pool --- Qwen3-8B, GSM8K, \textbf{512 prompts
  $\times$ 32 rollouts} (Tinker). All analyses are offline resampling; decision
  rules fixed before scoring.
  \vspace{4pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item E-T1: does the closed-form CI achieve nominal coverage?
    \item E-T2: does the wasted-compute floor hold, and how tight?
    \item E-T3a: does the closed-form $G^\star$ match the empirical optimum?
    \item E-B: does bandit reallocation recover the oracle static policy?
    \item Item-8 protocol: pass@\{1,8,32\} everywhere, base and post-RL,
          pinned config
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{4pt}
  \good{Verdicts below were confirmed at $n{=}350$ (billing-truncated,
  honestly stamped) and re-confirmed unchanged at $n{=}512$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{T1 --- the estimator is calibrated; the failure mode is an
  estimand mismatch}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Batching regime & Coverage target & 95\% CI coverage \\
    \midrule
    shuffled (iid), Wald, $m{\ge}64$ & global ZVF & \good{0.93--0.96} \\
    shuffled, Wilson, all $(m,K)$    & global ZVF & \good{0.95--0.98} \\
    difficulty-sorted (curriculum)   & global ZVF & \warn{0.07--0.22} \\
    difficulty-sorted                & \textbf{local} ZVF & \good{0.944} \\
    stratified batches               & global ZVF & \good{0.996} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Adopted: \textbf{Wilson interval}, $m\ge 64$ groups
    \item Curriculum ordering does NOT break calibration --- it silently
          changes the estimand to the curriculum-stage ZVF (which is what a
          stage-local controller acts on anyway)
    \item Global inference under curriculum $\Rightarrow$ stratified batch
          composition (validated) \tier{Tier C, n=512 pool}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{T2 --- the wasted-compute floor holds, and it is essentially exact}
  Floor: $\;$ rollouts $\ge G\cdot\lceil \ln\delta / \ln \mathrm{ZVF} \rceil$
  before one informative (mixed-reward) group, w.p. $1-\delta$.
  \vspace{4pt}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Stratum ($\hat p$) & ZVF$(G{=}8)$ & floor $(\delta{=}0.1)$ & observed & tightness \\
    \midrule
    0.01 (hardest) & 0.886 & \textbf{160 rollouts} & holds & 1.00 \\
    0.11 & 0.384 & 24 & holds & 1.00 \\
    0.21--0.69 (4 strata) & 0.004--0.121 & 8--16 & holds & 1.00 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Headline datum: at $\hat p \approx 0.01$, \textbf{160 rollouts are
          provably burned} before a single usable gradient
    \item 6/6 strata, tightness 1.00--1.05 \tier{Tier C, n=512 pool}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{T3 --- theory says the optimal group size is $G^\star = 2$;
  the field uses 8--16}
  Objective (eq.~efficiency): $J(G)=\frac{1}{G}\,
  \mathbb{E}_{p\sim\hat\phi}[(1-p^G-(1-p)^G)\,p(1-p)]$
  \vspace{4pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Closed-form argmax: $G^\star=2$ in \textbf{every} difficulty stratum
          and pooled
    \item Empirical $J$-argmax: 2--3 (exact agreement 3--4 of 5 strata,
          adjacent grid point otherwise)
    \item Corroboration from training: the $G{=}2$ adapter matched
          $G{=}16$'s held-out pass@32 gain at $8\times$ fewer
          rollouts per step (next slides)
    \item Named open question (E-T3b): does small-$G$ advantage-estimate
          quality erode the predicted efficiency in training?
          \tier{Tier C; theorem still proof-sketch}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Pilot for the controller pillar: bandit reallocation is GO}
  Offline simulation on the real pool; arms = difficulty strata; reward =
  group is informative (usable gradient). $G{=}8$, 250 groups/sim, 500 sims.
  \vspace{4pt}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Policy & informative groups / 1k rollouts & vs.\ oracle \\
    \midrule
    static-oracle (needs truth) & 124.3 & 100\% \\
    \textbf{Thompson-GU (ours)} & \textbf{120.7} & \good{97\%} \\
    uniform & 94.5 & 76\% \\
    hardest-first (DARS caricature) & \warn{13.7} & \warn{11\%} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty $\ne$ signal: hardest-first wastes $\sim$89\% of the
          gradient budget (hardest $\approx$ all-fail $\approx$ zero variance)
    \item Caveat: frozen-pool simulation; training-time pilot must confirm
          under distribution shift
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{We measured the stack effect the position paper warns about}
  Same model, same 200 problems, same config (seed 42, $T{=}1.0$) ---
  \textbf{only the sampler/backend differs}:
  \vspace{4pt}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Backend & pass@1 & pass@8 & pass@32 \\
    \midrule
    Tinker sampler & 30.4\% & 79.7\% & 91.0\% \\
    vLLM 0.24 (Modal A100) & 30.6\% & 77.8\% & 89.0\% \\
    \midrule
    $\Delta$ & \good{+0.2} & $-1.9$ & \warn{$-2.0$} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Point estimates are backend-robust; \textbf{frontier metrics are
          not} (individual problems move up to $|11|/32$ solutions)
    \item Eval-seed spread (3 seeds): $\sim$4 pts at pass@1 --- the noise
          bands all later comparisons are judged against
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
